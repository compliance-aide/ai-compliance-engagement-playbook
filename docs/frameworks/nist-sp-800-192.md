# NIST SP 800-192 access-control-policy-verification engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST verification methods, an access-control policy, an authorization decision, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-192](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/192/final) and the organization’s approved security, architecture, privacy, legal, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Operate a controlled engagement for verification and testing of in-scope access-control policies and models. Connect policy intent and safety requirements to model scope, implementation and configuration evidence, verification and test plans, assumptions and limitations, results, discrepancy handling, remediation, changes, incidents, and retained workpapers. A model, test result, configuration extract, or report is evidence to assess; none alone proves policy intent is complete, implementation enforces it correctly, access is appropriate, secure operation is achieved, or compliance.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, security, architecture, identity and access-management operations, application and data owners, privacy and legal where relevant, quality or validation, change-management, incident-response, and records-management roles. Operators maintain scoped policy and model inventories, approved requirements and safety-property records, implementation and configuration baselines, verification and test plans, assumptions and limitations, results and discrepancy logs, exceptions, remediation, incidents, and review evidence. Reconcile policies, models, implementations, owners, and dependencies quarterly; review failed tests, policy or configuration changes, exceptions, incidents, and unresolved risks at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material policy, system, provider, threat, or publication changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples intent-to-model-to-test-to-remediation traceability; auditors test supplied evidence without granting access, changing access-control configuration, approving exceptions, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied requirements, models, test results, discrepancies, remediation, and review evidence, identify missing owners or stale decisions, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot define access policy, grant or revoke access, change authorization configuration, approve an exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** At engagement start, retain the retrieved version and applicable use terms for the official [NIST SP 800-192 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/192/final), plus the approved local policy, architecture, privacy, and system decisions used to scope verification. The packages are original evidence-planning guidance, not reproduced verification methods, access-control policy, an authorization decision, or a compliance conclusion.

### 1. Policy-intent, model-scope, and safety-property package

- **Request and owner:** Security, architecture, identity and access-management, application, data, privacy, and legal owners provide approved policy-intent references, in-scope system/data boundary, stated access-control model and assumptions, safety-property objectives, role/attribute vocabulary, dependency map, named owners, and scope exclusions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected policy objective through the stated model boundary, system/data dependency, owner, assumption, and explicitly excluded condition. This can test whether the proposed verification scope is intelligible and bounded; it cannot define policy intent, determine legal applicability, or prove access is appropriate.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may identify missing owner, assumption, or boundary references in supplied material. Humans approve policy, model scope, safety properties, and exclusions. Refresh after a policy, data classification, architecture, role-model, or legal/privacy change.

### 2. Implementation-to-verification package

- **Request and owner:** IAM operations, application/platform engineering, security, quality/validation, and change owners provide approved configuration/build baselines, model-to-implementation mapping records, verification/test plan, authorized test inputs, environment boundary, assumptions, test outputs, and change approvals.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected safety-property check from approved test plan through authorized environment and input, versioned model/configuration baseline, result, and stated limitation. This can assess whether the check is reproducible from supplied evidence; it cannot grant access, change a configuration, prove all enforcement paths, or prove a policy is complete.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare supplied result metadata and flag absent baseline or limitation records. Humans authorize tests, manage environments, interpret results, and approve changes. Recollect after a failed test, code/configuration change, identity-provider change, or material integration change.

### 3. Discrepancy, remediation, and governance package

- **Request and owner:** Security, IAM, application, data, risk, incident, change, and independent-review owners provide discrepancy log, access-impact assessment, containment/escalation record where relevant, remediation plan, owner and due date, retest evidence, exception/risk decision, and management-review record.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected discrepancy from recorded result through human triage, authorized remediation, retest or documented rationale, remaining limitation, and closure decision. This can support a reviewable follow-through record; it cannot decide user access, accept risk, certify the model, or establish comprehensive operating effectiveness.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index discrepancies and flag overdue actions but cannot alter access or make closure decisions. Humans approve remediation, exceptions, closure, and risk acceptance. Review quarterly and after a failed test, access-control incident, material policy/model/configuration change, or publication update.
