# California CCPA/CPRA — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not California legal advice. Consult current
> [California regulations](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/) and qualified counsel.

## Engagement focus

Confirm applicability and roles, maintain a data/use/disclosure map and
consumer-notice/choice workflow, monitor rights and vendor-change evidence,
assess current regulatory requirements before material change, exercise incident
and request escalation, and track management-approved remediation.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Operators verify facts and implement approved notices and contract positions.
Independent reviewers test evidence integrity and escalation governance. AI
assembles review packets and detects stale notices; it cannot decide applicability,
consumer outcomes, data classifications, or filings. Review quarterly and after
regulatory or business changes.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the current [California Privacy Protection Agency regulations](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/) and qualified California counsel; reviewed 2026-07-31. This original plan does not decide business/consumer scope, exemptions, whether an activity is a sale or sharing, or a consumer-request outcome.

### 1. Applicability, data-use, and party inventory

- **Request and owner:** A human-approved applicability record; inventory of California consumer data categories, purposes, sources, disclosures, service providers/contractors/third parties, and stated retention rules, maintained by privacy, product, and vendor owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected data use to its system source, purpose record, recipient, and accountable owner. This can support a fact base for counsel's decision; it cannot establish an exemption, a sale/share classification, or legal scope.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved inventories and flag new recipients or uses. Counsel and privacy leaders approve classifications. Refresh for a new product use, vendor, advertising/analytics flow, or California-consumer channel.

### 2. Notice, choice, and consumer-request operation

- **Request and owner:** Published notice/version history, preference and opt-out handling tests where applicable, request-intake and response records, verification procedures, and escalation log, owned by privacy and customer operations.
- **Validate and limit:** Independently trace a selected request or preference event to its intake, system action, timestamp, and review record while minimizing consumer data. This can support operational traceability; it cannot decide a request outcome, identity determination, deadline, or statutory exception.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may calculate internal service-level warnings and create redacted traceability, but cannot respond autonomously to consumers or alter preferences. Refresh after a notice, interface, request-process, or regulatory change.

### 3. Contract, retention, and incident evidence

- **Request and owner:** Provider/contractor responsibility records, retention/deletion outputs, security or incident exercises, actual incident decision records, and remediation approval from vendor, records-management, security, and privacy owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected vendor or deletion/incident event to source records, authority, and corrective action. This can support governance evidence; it cannot prove contract sufficiency, legal compliance, breach notice duties, or provider performance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may maintain a source-linked evidence index and flag expired reviews. Humans approve contracts, risk treatment, filings, notices, and closure. Refresh after a vendor, retention, incident, or annual governance change.
