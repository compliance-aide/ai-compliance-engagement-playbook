# NAIC Own Risk and Solvency Assessment engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NAIC guidance, an actuarial opinion, or a solvency conclusion. Confirm state-specific applicability through the [NAIC ORSA topic page](https://content.naic.org/insurance-topics/own-risk-and-solvency-assessment).

## Engagement focus

Maintain a current enterprise-risk record: material risk inventory, ownership, risk and capital decision inputs, scenario evidence, business-plan changes, governance discussions, and the controlled drafting and review history for any required summary reporting.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Insurer leadership owns risk and capital decisions; risk, finance, actuarial, and business teams maintain their inputs; independent reviewers challenge evidence and governance. AI compiles approved records, identifies incomplete ownership or stale assumptions, and drafts internal questions, but cannot quantify capital adequacy, make actuarial judgments, or file a report. Reassess after material strategy changes, schedule management review, and retain human sign-off for confidential reporting.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [NAIC ORSA topic page](https://content.naic.org/insurance-topics/own-risk-and-solvency-assessment), the controlling domiciliary-state adoption and filing materials, and qualified actuarial and insurance counsel; checked 2026-07-31. This is original engagement language, not an ORSA instruction, actuarial opinion, solvency determination, or filing advice.

### 1. Insurer, legal-entity, and risk-capital boundary

- **Request and owner:** Request the legal-entity and domicile register, approved engagement boundary, material-risk inventory, business-plan assumptions, governance map, and capital-planning inputs from the enterprise-risk, finance, actuarial, and legal owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected material risk from legal entity and documented owner through its risk record, planning assumption, and management-review agenda. This supports a bounded evidence trail; it cannot decide statutory applicability, materiality, or capital adequacy.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved registers and flag absent ownership or stale dates. Designated leaders and qualified actuaries retain risk, capital, and filing decisions. Refresh for an acquisition, reorganization, material strategy change, new domicile requirement, or changed planning horizon.

### 2. Scenario, governance, and action evidence

- **Request and owner:** Request approved scenario or stress-analysis workpapers, model-governance records, management and board risk discussions, challenge notes, action owners, and remediation tracking from actuarial, risk, finance, and corporate-secretary owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Select a documented scenario and trace it to source assumptions, accountable review, challenge or decision record, and follow-up action. This establishes provenance of a recorded process; it cannot validate a model, endorse an assumption, or conclude solvency.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved, access-controlled workpapers and identify inconsistent version dates; it may not calculate, alter, or approve capital analysis. Reperform after a material loss, model change, risk appetite change, or governance decision.

### 3. Confidential reporting and annual readiness record

- **Request and owner:** Request the reporting calendar, controlled draft lineage, approval log, retention/access register, exception decisions, regulator correspondence where applicable, and source-change log from the reporting lead, legal, records, and executive owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected draft assertion or exception to its approved evidence, authorized reviewer, and disposition. This supports readiness and accountability; it cannot authorize a filing, determine confidentiality treatment, or make a regulatory representation.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a redacted evidence index and surface unresolved actions. Authorized humans approve report content, submission, risk acceptance, and retention decisions. Revisit annually and after a regulatory request, reporting change, or material exception.
