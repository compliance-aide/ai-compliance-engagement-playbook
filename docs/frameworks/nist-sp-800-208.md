# NIST SP 800-208 stateful hash-based signature engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST signature-scheme content, implementation instructions, a cryptographic approval, a validation claim, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-208](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/208/final) and the organization’s approved security, cryptography, architecture, legal, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Operate a tightly governed lifecycle for approved stateful hash-based signature uses. Maintain traceability among signing purpose, signer or service identity, authorized state boundary, key and state custody, module or provider, backup and recovery approach, signature-use records, change approvals, anomaly handling, and lifecycle evidence. A successful signature, state record, or provider claim is evidence to assess; it does not prove state is unique and protected, no misuse occurred, signer authority, legal effect, or compliance.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, security, cryptography or platform engineering, architecture, identity, application and service owners, legal, procurement, supplier-management, change-management, incident-response, business-continuity, and records-management roles. Operators maintain an in-scope use inventory, authorized signer and service records, state-management design and custody evidence, approved module or provider baselines, signature-use and anomaly records, backup and recovery tests, change approvals, exception decisions, and supplier assurance. Reconcile uses, state owners, and deployed versions quarterly; review anomalies, recovery readiness, access, provider changes, exceptions, and unresolved risks at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material system, state-management, provider, cryptographic, or threat changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples use-to-state-to-recovery traceability; auditors test evidence without signing, accessing private keys, changing state, restoring production, approving exceptions, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied inventory, custody, lifecycle, recovery, and review evidence, identify missing ownership or overdue validation, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot design or implement cryptography, sign or access private keys, create or alter signature state, restore production state, determine signer authority, approve an exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.
