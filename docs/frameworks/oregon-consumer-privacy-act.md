# Oregon Consumer Privacy Act engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not Oregon statutory text or legal advice. Confirm current requirements through the [Oregon legislative privacy materials](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2023I1/Downloads/CommitteeMeetingDocument/285470).

## Engagement focus

Maintain a year-round record of personal-data processing, owner decisions, consumer requests, sensitive-data handling, vendor evidence, notices, assessments, incidents, and product or policy changes.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Privacy and product owners maintain the operating record; security and procurement supply evidence; counsel confirms applicability; independent reviewers test evidence. AI can organize approved artifacts and identify stale decisions, but cannot resolve rights requests, interpret law, or authorize disclosures. Review material changes before release and retain human approvals and rationale.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [Oregon legislative privacy materials](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2023I1/Downloads/CommitteeMeetingDocument/285470), checked 2026-07-31, and qualified Oregon privacy/legal review. This is original engagement guidance, not statutory text; it does not decide controller/processor role, applicability, consumer status, consent, opt-out result, assessment need, or disclosure duty.

### 1. Processing, purpose, sensitive-data, and notice package

- **Request and owner:** Processing and data-flow inventory; purpose, collection-source, data-category, sensitive-data, retention, recipient, and system records; notice/version history; and accountable product, privacy, data, engineering, and procurement owners. Identify the product, jurisdictional scope assumptions, provider boundary, and period covered.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected declared processing flow from collection experience through system inventory, documented purpose, data categories, retention setting, recipient, and notice version; inspect evidence provenance and restricted access. This supports a factual operating map, not a legal processing classification, consumer-status finding, consent determination, or proof that notice is sufficient.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare approved release metadata and inventories, then flag declared-versus-observed gaps. Human legal, privacy, and product owners decide classifications, scope, and release approval. Refresh before a new collection, sensitive-data use, recipient, purpose, or material interface change.

### 2. Consumer-rights, preference, and appeal-operation package

- **Request and owner:** Request, verification, preference/opt-out, appeal, and response workflow descriptions; de-identified queue and outcome indexes; system-action logs; staff training; quality sampling; and corrective-action records from privacy operations, support, engineering, and legal owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a de-identified test request, preference, or appeal through intake, routing, verification step, system action, human decision, response approval, and retained case record. This supports workflow traceability; it cannot authenticate a requester, decide an appeal, determine opt-out applicability, or establish that a response meets law.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare de-identified sample indexes and identify stalled or incomplete records; it cannot infer identity, resolve a request, or contact a consumer. Authorized humans make request, appeal, and communications decisions. Trigger on complaint trends, workflow defects, new channels, or product changes.

### 3. Assessment, processor, and change-governance package

- **Request and owner:** Human-approved risk/assessment inputs where counsel identifies relevance; processor/subprocessor diligence, instructions, and change records; security-test references; incident and remediation records; management review; and official-source monitoring from privacy, security, procurement, and leadership owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected provider onboarding, high-impact change, assessment input, incident, or remediation item to source evidence, accountable authority, documented limitation, corrective action, and retest. This supports governed change oversight; it cannot decide a required assessment, accept risk, determine processor performance, or conclude notification duties.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved materials and flag expired reviews. Humans approve contractual terms, risk decisions, external communications, and closure. Refresh for a processor change, potentially high-impact processing change, incident, source update, or annual scope review.
