# UK Cyber Essentials — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not a scheme certificate. Lock the engagement
> version and route using current [NCSC scheme resources](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/cyberessentials/resources).

## Engagement focus

Establish applicant and technical scope, including provider responsibilities.
Keep an in-scope inventory and configuration/change evidence; conduct readiness
checks against the current official material; resolve or explicitly govern
deviations; and use the authorized route for formal assessment where sought.

## Roles and annual rhythm

System owners maintain scope and evidence. Independent reviewers and authorized
scheme parties make assessment decisions. AI may keep the scope ledger, change watch, and question log;
it drafts only from approved evidence and cannot submit, approve scope, or claim a certificate.
Monitor change throughout the year and plan renewal from current scheme rules.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the current [NCSC Cyber Essentials
resources](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/cyberessentials/resources) as the official
source; at review on 2026-07-31 the published requirements were version 3.3,
effective 2026-04-27. This guide uses original engagement language and links
out rather than reproducing scheme questions or requirements. A named scheme
owner must confirm the applicable route, scope boundary, and current edition.

**Plan status:** Independently reviewed after source and skeptical review;
three recorded corrections cover all five scheme themes, cloud/end-user scope,
and provider commitment evidence.

### 1. Applicant scope and responsibility ledger

- **Request and owner:** The applicant's scope statement, business-unit,
  network and location boundary, asset/service inventory (including end-user
  devices and cloud services), and provider or inherited-service responsibility
  record, maintained by the technical scope owner. Where a provider implements
  a practice, request the relevant contract, incorporated security statement,
  or trust-centre record and identify which party operates each planned theme.
- **Validate and limit:** Independently reconcile a selected in-scope service
  and endpoint to the ledger, network/service inventory, and responsible
  party; record omissions and access limits. This can support a bounded
  readiness scope. It cannot prove that a certification body will accept the
  scope or that every relevant asset is included. A human must obtain the
  certification body's agreement before using a separately managed subset;
  cloud services must be explicitly considered rather than silently excluded.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize the reconciliation and flag mismatches;
  the named applicant owner approves the boundary. Revisit on a material
  service, network, provider, location, or ownership change and before renewal.

### 2. Configuration and supported-software operating record

- **Request and owner:** Time-bounded, read-only exports or signed reports
  showing device/software population, supported-version posture, update
  handling, secure-configuration baseline evidence, and approved exceptions,
  from endpoint, platform, and change-management owners. The evidence index
  must separately identify the five scheme themes—network boundary/firewalls,
  secure configuration, security-update management, user-access control, and
  malware protection—without reproducing the scheme's question set.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a human-approved selection from the scope
  ledger to the export, configuration record, update status, and exception
  register; preserve source time, population coverage, and report limitations.
  This can support a readiness assessment of recorded posture. It cannot prove
  continuous operation or replace scheme assessment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare inventories and draft a gap list but may
  not alter configuration or close an exception. Recollect after significant
  platform, software, baseline, or update-process change.

### 3. Access, boundary, and malware-protection evidence

- **Request and owner:** Read-only administration records for accounts and
  privileged access, network-boundary/firewall configuration evidence, and
  malware-protection status or alert-handling records, from identity, network,
  and security-operations owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect access and boundary records against the
  approved scope and trace a documented operational event or review to its
  owner and date. This can support that evidence exists for the declared
  operating practices; it does not prove every account is appropriate, that a
  control is effective in all circumstances, or that the applicant is
  certified.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may create a non-destructive evidence index and
  escalate stale or contradictory records. Humans approve any access decision,
  risk acceptance, remediation, or scheme response. Recheck after identity,
  boundary, provider, or security-tool changes and during renewal preparation.

### 4. Readiness response and deviation workpaper

- **Request and owner:** A version-pinned log that maps proposed readiness
  responses to the evidence above, together with deviations, compensating
  measures, owner approvals, expiry dates, and remediation status, maintained
  by the engagement lead.
- **Validate and limit:** An independent reviewer traces each proposed response
  to current evidence and records unsupported statements. The workpaper can
  support a human readiness decision; it cannot be submitted by AI or treated
  as a declaration, certificate, or formal assessment result.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may draft traceability and identify unanswered items;
  the authorized applicant and scheme parties alone approve, submit, assess,
  or certify. Refresh it when the official resource changes, a material
  exception expires, or the annual renewal cycle starts.
