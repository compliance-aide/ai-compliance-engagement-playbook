# NSPM-33 research-security readiness — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not federal research policy or a grant-certification conclusion. Confirm current implementation materials through the [White House research-security guidance](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/010422-NSPM-33-Implementation-Guidance.pdf).

## Engagement focus

Maintain research scope, researcher disclosures, external affiliations, foreign-travel and export-control coordination, cyber and insider-risk program evidence, training records, conflict review, and grant-response approvals.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Research leadership, legal, export-control, security, and authorized institutional officials decide disclosures and certifications; independent reviewers test evidence. AI inventories approved records and flags omissions, but cannot determine conflicts, certify, or submit grants. Review before submissions and annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [NSPM-33 implementation guidance](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/010422-NSPM-33-Implementation-Guidance.pdf), current sponsor/agency instructions, and institutional research-security, export-control, and legal authorities; checked 2026-07-31. This original readiness aid does not decide a disclosure, conflict, export-control issue, certification, or grant submission.

### 1. Institution, award, researcher, and program scope

- **Request and owner:** Request the applicable sponsor or institutional requirement, award/submission boundary, designated research-security roles, researcher population, approved external-activity/disclosure workflow, and system/data boundaries from research administration, principal-investigator, legal, export-control, and security owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected award or researcher record to the declared scope, accountable owner, approved workflow, and relevant review deadline. This supports engagement scoping; it cannot determine a conflict, foreign affiliation, disclosure content, or sponsor applicability.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may inventory approved metadata and flag missing owners or dates. Authorized institutional officials make disclosure and eligibility decisions. Refresh before a submission, new award, new researcher role, material outside activity, or sponsor-policy change.

### 2. Disclosure, travel, cyber, and training operation

- **Request and owner:** Request approved disclosure-review records, travel/export-control coordination metadata, cyber-program and access evidence, insider-risk coordination records, training completion, and approved exceptions from research security, export control, HR, IT, and training owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Select an approved record and trace it to a responsible owner, dated review, required follow-up, and exception/remediation record. This shows a documented workflow generated evidence; it cannot judge disclosure completeness, authorize travel, or determine export-control treatment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize sanitized records and identify timing gaps; it may not access sensitive research data, decide a conflict, or approve a disclosure. Recollect after a travel, access, affiliation, system, or training-process change.

### 3. Submission, certification, and annual oversight record

- **Request and owner:** Request the pre-submission checklist, authorized approval/certification record, source-version watch, audit/review findings, corrective-action register, and annual leadership review from research administration, legal, and institutional officials.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected proposed submission statement or finding to its evidence, human authority, follow-up action, and closure. This supports a human readiness review; it cannot certify, submit a grant, make a sponsor representation, or close a legal issue.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may draft a non-authoritative evidence index and surface unresolved actions. Authorized humans approve certifications, submissions, remediation, and risk decisions. Revisit annually and after a sponsor update, review finding, material exception, or upcoming submission.
