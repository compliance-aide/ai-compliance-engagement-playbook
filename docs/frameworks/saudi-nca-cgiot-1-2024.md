# Saudi NCA Cybersecurity Guidelines for Internet of Things engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NCA guideline text, a product-safety determination, a regulatory interpretation, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through the [Saudi National Cybersecurity Authority Internet of Things cybersecurity guidelines](https://nca.gov.sa/en/regulatory-documents/guidelines-list/cglot/).

## Engagement focus

Maintain an IoT cybersecurity engagement record that connects device and service inventory, intended operating context, accountable product and service owners, supplier and software dependencies, authorized evidence, security events, remediation, and independently reviewable assurance. Keep safety, product design, and regulatory decisions with accountable human owners rather than converting collected evidence into an automated approval.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Executive, product, engineering, security, privacy, safety, and legal authorities retain accountable decisions; device, platform, and supplier owners preserve evidence; independent reviewers test traceability and readiness. AI may organize authorized records, identify stale evidence, surface dependency changes, and draft review questions, but cannot make product-safety or regulatory determinations, approve a product release or supplier, accept risk, alter production services, issue an assessment, or claim compliance. Review annually and before material device, software, data, connectivity, supplier, safety, incident, or regulatory changes.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Retain the official [NCA Internet of Things cybersecurity guidelines page](https://nca.gov.sa/en/regulatory-documents/guidelines-list/cglot/), retrieved 2026-07-31, and have an authorized entity representative confirm the applicable guideline version, status, and permitted use. This is original evidence-planning language, not NCA guideline text, a product-safety conclusion, a product release decision, or a conformity claim. Keep device identifiers, telemetry, and supplier material in authorized repositories.

### 1. Device, service, and operating-context package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain the approved device, gateway, platform, and connected-service inventory; intended operating context; accountable product, service, safety, security, and privacy owners; supported lifecycle state; connectivity model; and open applicability questions from product, engineering, security, safety, legal, and service owners. Define the product/service population, deployment boundary, and evidence period.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected device/service family to an approved owner, version/lifecycle record, connectivity and service dependency, intended environment, and any authorized scope decision. Use sanitized metadata where device identifiers or diagrams are restricted. This supports an accountable engagement boundary; it cannot establish product safety, regulatory scope, or full inventory coverage.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare authorized inventory metadata and flag missing owners, versions, or review dates. Human product, safety, legal, and security owners decide scope and escalation. Trigger on a new device/service, material deployment or connectivity change, lifecycle transition, or annual review.

### 2. Software, update, and operational-security package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain approved architecture and software-component references, release/change approvals, update and vulnerability-handling records, identity/access references, monitoring or event records, test summaries, and approved exceptions from engineering, product security, platform operations, and service owners. Preserve technical outputs under approved access controls.
- **Validate and limit:** For a selected release, component, or event, inspect the trail from accountable approval through version/change evidence, time-bounded operational or test reference, exception or issue record, and corrective-action link. This supports traceability of the claimed process; it cannot prove secure design, vulnerability absence, safe operation, or guideline conformance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index permitted metadata and identify stale update, test, or owner links; it cannot publish releases, change device settings, approve exceptions, or alter evidence. Named humans decide release and remediation action. Trigger on a release, vulnerability report, failed test, security event, or supplier-component change.

### 3. Supplier, incident, and lifecycle-assurance package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain supplier/component responsibility records, support and end-of-life plans, incident/escalation references, risk and exception approvals, independent-review observations, remediation/retest evidence, and management-review records from procurement, engineering, product security, operations, and leadership.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected supplier dependency, lifecycle event, or material finding to its product boundary, accountable owner, approved decision, support or incident reference, corrective action, and retest or documented closure rationale. This supports reviewable lifecycle governance; it cannot establish supplier performance, risk acceptance, regulatory compliance, or authorization for external communication.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a non-authoritative action register and flag expiring support or overdue actions. Independent reviewers challenge traceability; named humans approve residual risk, closure, and external communications. Trigger on supplier change, end-of-support event, incident, source update, or annual readiness cycle.
