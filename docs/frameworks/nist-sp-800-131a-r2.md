# NIST SP 800-131A Revision 2 cryptographic-transition engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST transition guidance, an algorithm-selection decision, a migration authorization, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-131A Revision 2](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/131/a/r2/final) and the organization’s approved cryptography, security, architecture, legal, procurement, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Run a governed transition program for in-scope cryptographic algorithms, key lengths, implementations, and dependent services. Connect the transition rationale and scoped inventory to approved target-state decisions, system and protocol dependencies, data and interoperability impacts, supplier posture, migration sequencing, implementation changes, validation, exceptions, residual-risk decisions, incident response, decommissioning, and evidence retention. A discovery record, vendor statement, migration plan, test result, or configuration extract is evidence to assess; none alone proves an approved transition path, successful cutover, suitable cryptography, secure operation, or compliance.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, security, cryptography or platform engineering, architecture, application and data owners, key-management operations, procurement, supplier-management, legal and privacy where relevant, change-management, incident-response, and records-management roles. Operators maintain a scoped cryptographic inventory, approved transition decisions and milestones, dependency and data-impact records, target-state and migration designs, supplier commitments, implementation and validation evidence, exception and residual-risk records, rollback and incident materials, decommissioning evidence, and retained decision history. Reconcile inventory coverage, dependencies, owners, milestones, and supplier posture quarterly; review emerging cryptographic risks, missed milestones, configuration changes, exceptions, incidents, and unresolved risks at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material algorithm, key-length, provider, product, threat, or publication changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples discovery-to-decision-to-transition-to-review traceability; auditors test supplied evidence without selecting algorithms, changing cryptographic configuration, approving exceptions, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied inventory, dependency, milestone, supplier, test, and review evidence, identify missing owners or overdue transition decisions, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot select cryptographic algorithms or key lengths, change configurations, authorize a migration or exception, access or derive keys, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.
