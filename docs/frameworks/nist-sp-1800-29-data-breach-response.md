# NIST SP 1800-29 data-breach response engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST practice-guide content, an incident determination, a notification decision, a compliance claim, or professional advice. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 1800-29](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/1800/29/final) and the organization's approved security, privacy, legal, incident-response, financial, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round capability for detecting, responding to, and recovering from suspected data confidentiality incidents. Connect critical data paths, detection sources, alert triage, escalation criteria, response roles, evidence preservation, containment options, recovery dependencies, external parties, lessons learned, and improvement work. A detection, alert, ticket, log, or tool output is an input to accountable investigation; it does not prove a breach, establish impact, or determine reporting obligations.

Maintain an evidence trail that shows what was observed, when it was assessed, which assumptions guided response, which actions were authorized, what information was preserved, how restoration was verified, and which follow-up work remains. Coordinate with applicable privacy, legal, communications, insurance, supplier, and business authorities without allowing the engagement record to substitute for their decisions. Use exercises and retrospective reviews to improve response readiness before an event.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Incident-response leadership, security operations, technology, data, privacy, legal, business, communications, finance, insurance, supplier, recovery, records, and executive owners establish authority and make accountable decisions. Operators maintain detection coverage records, contact lists, playbooks, exercise evidence, alert and case records, preservation logs, approved actions, recovery validation, supplier coordination, lessons learned, remediation plans, and review history. Reconcile material detection and response dependencies quarterly, exercise relevant scenarios during the year, review material events promptly, and complete an annual readiness review.

Independent reviewers sample an exercise or closed case from detection evidence through triage, authorized action, preservation, recovery validation, and lessons learned, testing traceability and decision records. Auditors evaluate supplied evidence without declaring an incident or breach, directing response, authorizing containment, deciding notification, accepting risk, making legal or financial conclusions, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize alerts and supplied evidence, flag missing timestamps or owners, draft case summaries and review questions, and identify stale playbook elements. AI cannot determine breach status, direct incident response, authorize communications or notifications, approve recovery, make legal or financial conclusions, or attest to conformance.
