# New York SHIELD Act — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not New York legal advice or a breach-notification conclusion. Confirm current guidance at the [New York Attorney General SHIELD Act resource](https://ag.ny.gov/index.php/resources/organizations/data-breach-reporting/shield-act).

## Engagement focus

Maintain private-information inventory, safeguards evidence, vendor records, incident detection and decision logs, investigation artifacts, notification authority, remediation, and annual review evidence.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Legal, privacy, security, and executive owners decide incident response and notifications; independent reviewers test the record chain. AI organizes approved evidence and highlights missing decisions, but cannot determine a breach, notify anyone, or make legal representations. Review quarterly and annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Plan status:** Draft; independent source and skeptical review required before reliance.

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the current [New York Attorney General SHIELD Act resource](https://ag.ny.gov/index.php/resources/organizations/data-breach-reporting/shield-act) and the [General Business Law text](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/899-BB); checked 2026-07-31. These are official public references. This original plan requests operational artifact categories, not statutory text, customer private information, or a legal conclusion. Counsel confirms applicability, effective-law changes, and notification duties.

### 1. Private-information boundary and safeguard ownership

- **Request and owner:** A minimized data inventory and flow map identifying systems and suppliers that handle relevant private information, plus assigned administrative, technical, and physical safeguard owners, from privacy, security, and architecture leads.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a sample data flow to an accountable owner, a system record, and a current safeguard or risk-treatment record without placing personal data in the workpaper. This supports a bounded evidence scope; it cannot determine statutory coverage or prove complete discovery.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare approved metadata for unowned flows. Privacy/legal humans approve scope. Refresh after a new data use, product, processor, acquisition, or material architecture change.

### 2. Provider and incident-decision record

- **Request and owner:** Supplier due-diligence, security responsibility, change-review, and incident-escalation records, together with incident intake, investigation chronology, and decision authority records, owned by vendor management, security operations, privacy, and counsel.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace selected supplier and incident records to dates, owners, escalation path, and retained decisions. This can show an observable operating chain; it cannot establish contract sufficiency, determine a breach, or decide notice recipients or timing.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved records and flag missing ownership or dates; authorized humans decide investigations, notification, and remediation. Recollect after a supplier change, security event, or material vendor finding.

### 3. Safeguard operation, remediation, and annual challenge

- **Request and owner:** Time-bounded access, training, vulnerability/remediation, incident-exercise, and management-review evidence from their accountable operational owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample from the declared population and trace records to source evidence, completion date, exception, and follow-up. This supports that declared processes leave evidence; it cannot prove continuous effectiveness or compliance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may produce a redacted evidence index and surface stale items. Humans approve exceptions and closure. Refresh after a failed test, significant finding, incident, or annual review.
