# Costa Rica personal-data-protection engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not Costa Rican law or a legal conclusion. Confirm current law, regulations, and authority guidance through [Prodhab’s legislation page](https://www.prodhab.go.cr/acercade/normativa/).

## Engagement focus

Maintain an accountable processing record: data purposes, owner decisions, collection and sharing evidence, database records where applicable, service-provider handling, individual requests, incidents, and changes.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Privacy and business owners maintain processing evidence; security and supplier owners provide operating records; Costa Rican counsel confirms scope and legal duties; independent reviewers test traceability. AI organizes approved artifacts and drafts gap questions, but cannot interpret legal requirements, register databases, decide individual-rights outcomes, or submit complaints or notifications. Review records at material processing change and through a recurring annual cycle with human approval.

## Tailored evidence plan

### 1. Processing, database, and accountability record

- **Request and owner:** Privacy, business, product, data, and records owners provide a dated register of processing activities and databases; purposes; data categories; collection channels; systems; owners; recipients/service providers; retention/disposal context; policy/notice versions; and scope questions for counsel.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected activity or database to its collection route, purpose record, system, owner, recipient/provider, notice reference, and review date. This supports a factual accountability record; it cannot decide controller obligations, database registration, consent, applicability, or lawful processing.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved records and identify absent owners, unsupported purposes, or changed recipients. Costa Rican counsel and designated human owners decide scope and actions. Refresh for a new data use, database, collection channel, provider, data transfer, or material product change.

### 2. Notice, request, and safeguards workflow package

- **Request and owner:** Privacy, customer-operations, security, and records owners provide approved notice/version history, rights-request and identity-verification procedures, de-identified case logs, access/security-operation references, retention/disposal execution evidence, incident/exercise records, and corrective-action tracking.
- **Validate and limit:** Independently trace a de-identified request, retention event, or incident exercise through intake, assigned owner, documented human decision, system action, escalation, and follow-up. This can evidence recorded workflow operation; it cannot decide an individual's rights result, verify identity, establish security effectiveness, or determine notification/reporting duties.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index authorized metadata and flag missing timestamps or ownership; it cannot resolve requests, contact people, change records, or submit a notice. Humans and counsel approve decisions and communications. Recheck after an incident, complaint trend, policy/interface change, or failed workflow test.

### 3. Provider, exception, and annual legal-review package

- **Request and owner:** Procurement, legal, privacy, security, and leadership owners provide provider/data-recipient records, contract/instruction references, assurance inputs, exception and remediation logs, source-change watch, legal-review record, management decisions, and retest evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected provider change, exception, or remediation item to the factual record, accountable human authority, limitation, due date, and retest/follow-up. This supports reviewable governance; it cannot prove provider performance, accept risk, determine legal sufficiency, or issue an audit conclusion.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a non-authoritative evidence packet and flag stale approvals. Human legal, privacy, security, and executive authorities approve contracts, risk treatment, registrations, filings, external statements, and closure. Refresh after a provider change, incident, legal-source update, or annual review.
