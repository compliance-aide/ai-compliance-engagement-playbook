# CISA BOD 18-02 high-value-assets engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not Binding Operational Directive text, a high-value-asset designation, a federal applicability determination, an authorization decision, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [CISA’s cybersecurity directives](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/directives) and applicable agency, component, system-owner, and authority direction.

## Engagement focus

For applicable Federal Civilian Executive Branch environments, maintain a heightened-assurance record for assets designated or evaluated as high value. Connect criticality context, designation decisions, accountable owners, security assessment and monitoring evidence, remediation, reporting, authorization handoffs, and service dependencies. Preserve the distinction between an asset’s business importance and an authorized determination, security conclusion, or accepted residual risk.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable agency or component executive, asset-owner, mission or business owner, security, platform, application, risk, authorization support, incident-command, reporting, and supplier-management roles. Operators maintain designation and scope records, criticality evidence, assessment and monitoring artifacts, remediation and exception records, reporting artifacts, supplier evidence, and closure status. Review material high-value-asset populations, ownership, assessment assumptions, open findings, and exceptions quarterly; reassess after material mission, service, architecture, supplier, threat, or scope changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples designation through treatment evidence; auditors test the evidence trail without designating assets, authorizing systems, accepting risk, approving remediation, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied designation and assessment evidence, flag missing ownership or stale review records, relate documented high-value assets to services and remediation, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot designate an asset, authorize operation, approve a remediation plan, accept risk, determine directive applicability, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [CISA cybersecurity directives index](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/directives), applicable agency designation process, and component direction at engagement start; checked 2026-07-31. This plan is original operational guidance, not a high-value-asset designation, authorization, or compliance conclusion.

### 1. Candidate, designation, and mission-dependency package

- **Request and owner:** Mission/business, asset, and security owners provide candidate or designated-asset registers, approved designation decisions, mission/criticality rationale, accountable owners, dependency maps, scope boundaries, and review history.
- **Validate and limit:** Independently trace a selected recorded asset from register through mission context, accountable owner, documented designation or non-designation decision, dependencies, and review date. This cannot designate an asset, validate every dependency, or decide agency scope.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize supplied registers and flag missing owners, stale rationale, or dependency conflicts. Authorized agency humans decide designation and mission priority. Refresh after material mission, ownership, service, architecture, or dependency change.

### 2. Assessment, monitoring, and treatment package

- **Request and owner:** Security, system, platform, incident, and risk owners provide assessment workpapers, monitoring outputs, findings, remediation plans, validation/retest evidence, incident handoffs, exception requests, and supplier evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample one recorded finding or assessment observation from source artifact through asset linkage, owner assignment, treatment decision, test/retest result, and open or closed status. This does not authorize operation, validate all safeguards, or approve remediation.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may identify unlinked findings, stale treatment dates, and absent retest records. Humans set treatment priorities, authorize changes, accept exceptions, and close actions. Refresh after an assessment, incident, failed retest, supplier change, or material threat change.

### 3. Reporting, escalation, and independent-assurance package

- **Request and owner:** Program leadership, reporting owners, and independent reviewers provide reporting inputs, metric/source lineage, escalation records, management decisions, evidence limitations, review samples, and corrective-action follow-up.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected reported measure or escalation from source population through calculation/assumption record, review challenge, accountable decision, limitation, and follow-up date. This cannot submit a federal report, certify reporting accuracy, or attest for management.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile supplied values to their cited records and flag incomplete provenance. Authorized humans approve reports, escalations, risk decisions, and external representations. Refresh before reporting cycles, after a material data-quality issue, and during annual independent review.
