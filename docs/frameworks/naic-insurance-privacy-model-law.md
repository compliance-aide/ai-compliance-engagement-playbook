# NAIC insurance privacy model-law engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NAIC text or state-law interpretation. Confirm applicable requirements through the [NAIC data privacy and insurance overview](https://content.naic.org/insurance-topics/data-privacy-and-insurance) and the relevant state regulator.

## Engagement focus

Maintain an evidence trail for insurance-data governance: data uses and disclosures, consumer interactions, vendor handling, retention decisions, complaints, incidents, and changes to privacy-facing practices.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Privacy and business owners maintain notices and decision records; security and vendor owners supply operating evidence; legal counsel confirms jurisdictional interpretation; independent reviewers test traceability. AI inventories approved data-flow evidence, finds aging vendor or notice reviews, and drafts questions, but cannot determine legal obligations, approve consumer notices, or decide individual-rights outcomes. Review material data-practice changes before release, perform recurring evidence reviews, and keep human approvals with their rationale.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [NAIC data privacy and insurance overview](https://content.naic.org/insurance-topics/data-privacy-and-insurance), the enacted law and regulator material for each in-scope insurer, and qualified state insurance counsel; checked 2026-07-31. This original planning aid does not reproduce model language or decide applicability, disclosure permissibility, notice content, or a consumer outcome.

### 1. Insurance-data use and jurisdiction record

- **Request and owner:** Licensed-entity/state matrix, insurance-data inventory, collection/use/disclosure map, notice/version register, accountable owner, retention record, and recipient/vendor register from privacy and business owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected data use or disclosure to its entity, source, purpose record, owner, notice version, recipient, and retention entry. This supports factual traceability; it cannot determine whether a use or disclosure is authorized.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved metadata and flag an unowned use. Counsel and privacy owners decide scope and interpretation. Refresh before a new product, data source, recipient, purpose, or state expansion.

### 2. Consumer, provider, and security-operation evidence

- **Request and owner:** Redacted consumer contact/request and complaint records, identity-handling procedure, provider instructions/oversight, access and disclosure logs, security-event inputs, and training records from privacy, service, supplier, and security owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected request, complaint, or provider exchange to intake, authorized handling, source record, escalation, and closure. This supports operating evidence; it cannot decide an individual request or a notification obligation.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may make a redacted index and flag stale actions; it may not resolve consumer cases or contact a regulator. Refresh after a complaint trend, supplier change, incident, or workflow change.

### 3. Exception, change, and management review record

- **Request and owner:** Human-approved exception/risk decisions, privacy-change review inputs, remediation/retest records, regulator correspondence, source-change watch, and management-review decisions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected exception or change to authority, evidence, expiry, remediation, and retest. This supports governance; it cannot accept risk, decide legal interpretation, or submit a filing.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag expirations and prepare review packets. Counsel and accountable leaders approve exceptions, external communications, and closure. Revisit annually and after a legal, product, or incident change.
