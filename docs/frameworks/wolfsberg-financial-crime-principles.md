# Wolfsberg financial-crime principles — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not Wolfsberg content, law, or endorsement. Check the [Wolfsberg resource library](https://wolfsberg-group.org/resources).

## Engagement focus

Define voluntary use case and local-law boundary; maintain governance, data, monitoring, investigation, third-party, outcome, automation, override, and remediation evidence.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Humans make alert, risk, customer, and regulatory decisions; independent audit challenges effectiveness. AI supports evidence and planning, but cannot autonomously disposition alerts or assert compliance. Review quarterly and annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the current [Wolfsberg resource library](https://wolfsberg-group.org/resources); checked 2026-07-31. These voluntary principles must be bounded to the institution's products, jurisdictions, and applicable law. This original plan is not Wolfsberg content, legal advice, a customer-risk decision, a suspicious-activity decision, or an assertion of compliance or effectiveness. Legal, compliance, and accountable business leaders decide adoption, local-law requirements, and external reporting.

### 1. Financial-crime governance, risk, and use-case record

- **Request and owner:** Approved voluntary-use-case statement, legal/jurisdictional applicability analysis, enterprise and product risk inputs, governance/committee records, designated accountability, policy decisions, and review calendar from financial-crime compliance, legal, risk, product, and business owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected program objective or risk factor to its input source, accountable owner, documented governance decision, and review date. This can support a transparent planning boundary; it cannot determine legal obligations, set customer risk, or establish program effectiveness.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved non-sensitive metadata and flag missing owners or stale risk reviews. Humans approve methodology, program scope, and risk decisions. Refresh after a new product, geography, customer segment, legal change, or material typology/risk event.

### 2. Customer, transaction-monitoring, and investigation evidence

- **Request and owner:** Approved customer-risk and due-diligence process records, monitoring configuration/change approvals, data-lineage and quality evidence, access-controlled alert/case workflow metrics, quality-assurance results, investigation-hand-off records, and retention controls from compliance operations, technology, data, and records owners.
- **Validate and limit:** In an authorized secure environment, trace a human-selected anonymized or access-controlled case from source trigger through review steps, human decision, escalation, and retention record. This can support workflow traceability for the sample; it cannot disposition an alert, make a customer decision, prove complete detection, or make a filing determination.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may perform read-only completeness checks on approved metadata and flag late or missing handoffs. Authorized humans make all alert, investigation, customer, and filing decisions. Recollect after monitoring/data-model changes, material quality issues, significant alerts, or retention-cycle events.

### 3. Independent testing, third-party, and remediation workpaper

- **Request and owner:** Independent-test plan/results, training records, third-party/technology oversight evidence, override and exception register, remediation plans, management responses, and retest evidence from compliance, procurement, HR, technology, and independent-review owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected finding, override, or exception to its source, accountable approver, rationale, mitigation, expiry, and retest. This can support accountable program improvement; it cannot accept financial-crime risk, approve remediation closure, or replace independent testing.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index restricted evidence and identify stale actions without exposing sensitive case content. Humans approve exceptions, remediation closure, external communications, and public claims. Review quarterly, challenge annually, and revisit after material findings, provider changes, or legal developments.
