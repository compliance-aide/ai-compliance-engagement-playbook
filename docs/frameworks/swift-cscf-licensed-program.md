# SWIFT Customer Security Controls Framework — licensed-program guide

> Original operational guidance, not SWIFT content or an attestation. Confirm the applicable cycle in [SWIFT’s official program materials](https://www.swift.com/swift_resource/252448).

## Engagement focus

Maintain SWIFT footprint, accountable owner, version-change, implementation, evidence, independent-assessment, attestation-readiness, finding, remediation, and retest records.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Operators own truthful evidence; independent reviewers determine scope independently. AI may organize evidence and changes, but cannot assess, assert compliance, submit attestation, or access/alter production assets. Review continuously and annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use [SWIFT's official program materials](https://www.swift.com/swift_resource/252448), the applicable customer security cycle, authorized program content, and organization-approved scope records; checked 2026-07-31. This is original readiness planning, not CSCSF control text, an assessment, an attestation, or a SWIFT submission. Do not copy licensed or restricted program content into this guide or AI prompts.

### 1. SWIFT footprint, scope, and cycle-governance package

- **Request and owner:** SWIFT service, security, architecture, operations, compliance, and executive owners provide current environment/endpoint inventory, approved scope rationale, accountable owner matrix, applicable-cycle/version confirmation, material dependency map, assessment calendar, and attestation-delegation record.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected in-scope service or component to an accountable owner, approved boundary record, current cycle reference, dependency context, and review date. This supports readiness traceability; it cannot determine program applicability, assessment scope, or attestation eligibility.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize approved metadata and identify stale scope records or unowned dependencies. Humans decide scope, program applicability, and attestation authority. Refresh after SWIFT architecture, connectivity, endpoint, service-provider, ownership, or cycle change.

### 2. Implementation-evidence, test, and finding package

- **Request and owner:** Security, platform, network, endpoint, identity, application, and operations owners provide organization-owned implementation evidence index, configuration/change approvals, access-review records, vulnerability/remediation records, test or exercise records, finding register, and retest evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected declared practice or finding to a dated evidence reference, accountable system owner, approved change/test record, stated limitation, corrective action, and retest or management review. This can support internal readiness; it cannot assess against CSCSF criteria, prove continuous effectiveness, or assert compliance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile evidence references, dates, owners, and open actions but cannot access or change production assets, assess controls, or decide finding closure. Humans authorize testing, validate evidence, and approve remediation. Recollect after material change, failed test, incident, vulnerability, or overdue remediation.

### 3. Independent review, attestation-readiness, and lifecycle package

- **Request and owner:** Independent-review, compliance, executive, and SWIFT-service owners provide review-scope record, reviewer independence record, readiness observations, disagreement/escalation log, management decisions, submission-preparation approvals, and post-cycle improvement actions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected readiness observation or submission-preparation item to source evidence, independent reviewer, documented limitation, accountable approver, date, and follow-up. This can evidence governance of preparation; it cannot make, sign, or submit an attestation.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a status ledger and flag incomplete approvals. Independent reviewers retain their judgment; authorized humans approve conclusions, representations, and any submission. Review continuously, before the applicable cycle deadline, and after material scope or program changes.
