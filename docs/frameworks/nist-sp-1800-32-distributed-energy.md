# NIST SP 1800-32 distributed-energy cybersecurity engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST practice-guide content, a grid-control design, a safety determination, a compliance claim, or professional advice. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 1800-32](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/1800/32/final) and the organization's approved engineering, safety, cybersecurity, regulatory, legal, financial, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round cybersecurity engagement for distributed energy resources and their information exchanges with distribution-grid operations. Connect the DER assets, operational purpose, owners, communications paths, data flows, identities, network boundaries, cloud or analytics dependencies, vendor responsibilities, monitoring, protective safeguards, incidents, and recovery dependencies. Treat reference architectures and technical evidence as inputs to local engineering and safety decisions, not as automatically deployable solutions.

Make the operational impact of cybersecurity actions explicit. Preserve decisions about data integrity, communications protection, authentication and access, monitoring, patching, supplier changes, exceptions, and contingency operations. An alert, integrity signal, or architecture assessment should prompt accountable review; it does not itself determine operational safety, dispatch, grid control, or compliance.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Energy operations, grid engineering, DER engineering, safety, cybersecurity, network, identity, data, supplier, procurement, regulatory, legal, incident-response, recovery, and records owners establish scope and make accountable decisions. Operators maintain asset and data-flow inventories, approved architectures, configuration and access evidence, monitoring records, supplier materials, change records, exceptions, incident cases, exercises, and review history. Reconcile material DER assets, owners, connectivity, and supplier dependencies quarterly; review changes and anomalies promptly; exercise contingency scenarios during the year; and conduct an annual safety-aware governance review.

Independent reviewers sample a DER communication path from approved purpose and architecture through access and monitoring evidence, change control, and operational review, testing traceability. Auditors evaluate supplied evidence without operating grid equipment, changing configuration, authorizing dispatch, determining safety, approving suppliers, accepting risk, making regulatory conclusions, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize supplied records, flag stale ownership or evidence, summarize data flows, draft workpapers, and prepare review questions. AI cannot operate or control DERs, grant access, change settings, determine safety, approve operational actions, accept risk, or attest to conformance.
