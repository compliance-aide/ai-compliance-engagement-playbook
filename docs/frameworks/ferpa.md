# FERPA — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not legal advice or a student-record disclosure
> decision. Check the current [FERPA regulations](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/subtitle-A/part-99).

## Engagement focus

Establish in-scope institution, programs, records, and authorities. Maintain a
record taxonomy, access model, disclosure-authority register, vendor terms, and
request/disclosure process. Review changes, route rights requests and disclosures
to authorized privacy decision-makers, and validate annual notice and policy
processes without exposing student-level data.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Operators own policy execution, access records, notices, vendors, and request
handling. Independent privacy reviewers evaluate decision paths and evidence.
AI handles approved redacted metadata and missing-approval checks; it cannot
decide disclosure, infer consent, authenticate requestors, or handle identifiable
education records outside an authorized environment.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the current [FERPA regulations](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/subtitle-A/part-99), applicable institution policy, and qualified education-privacy counsel; checked 2026-07-31. This original planning aid does not determine an education-record status, a disclosure exception, consent, directory-information treatment, or an individual outcome.

### 1. Institutional scope, record, and authority map

- **Request and owner:** Institution/program/school-official scope record, education-record and system inventory, record custodian register, role/access map, annual-notice version, directory-information decision record where applicable, and applicable policy references from registrar, privacy, and technology owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected record workflow to the system, custodian, authorized role, policy/version, and applicable scope record while using redacted metadata. This supports factual traceability; it cannot decide whether a record or party fits a regulatory definition.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved inventory metadata and flag missing owners or outdated notices. Authorized institutional and legal owners decide classification and authority. Refresh for a new program, system, data sharing arrangement, or policy change.

### 2. Access, request, disclosure, and provider evidence

- **Request and owner:** Redacted access and disclosure logs, request-intake/escalation record, identity-handling procedure, consent/authority artifacts where used, school-official/provider due-diligence and contract records, training evidence, and retention/disposal records from registrar, privacy, procurement, and security owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected access or disclosure route to the requesting role, documented authorization path, source system, reviewer, recipient, and closure. This supports evidence of a process; it cannot authenticate a requester, decide an exception, or approve a disclosure.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved redacted logs and flag incomplete fields or expiring provider reviews; it may not make a record available or contact an individual. Refresh after a provider change, complaint, suspected disclosure, or workflow change.

### 3. Incident, exception, and annual notice review

- **Request and owner:** Incident/escalation timeline, human disclosure/exception decision log, approved communications, complaint/remediation records, access-review results, annual-notice/policy review, source-change watch, and management decisions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected exception, incident, or notice decision to facts, authorized approver, dated evidence, remediation, and retest. This supports accountable governance; it cannot determine notice duty, accept risk, or make a submission.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a redacted review packet and flag stale actions. Humans approve risk acceptance, external communications, and closure. Revisit annually and after a material disclosure, incident, official-source change, or institutional restructuring.
