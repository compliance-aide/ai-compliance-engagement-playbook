# Saudi NCA CSCC-1:2019 critical-systems cybersecurity engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NCA control text, an assessment, operational instruction, or a compliance claim. Confirm the current program through the [NCA Critical Systems Cybersecurity Controls page](https://nca.gov.sa/en/regulatory-documents/controls-list/cscc/).

## Engagement focus

Maintain a critical-system engagement record: authoritative system scope, service and process dependencies, accountable operational and safety owners, privileged-access evidence, configuration and change history, detection and incident evidence, resilience and recovery proof, supplier support, and remediation decisions. Treat criticality and any relationship with general NCA controls as accountable human scope decisions, not an inference an AI can make from asset labels.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Critical-system sponsors and operational owners define service obligations and authorize consequential actions; cybersecurity teams retain monitoring and assurance evidence; engineering maintains change and recovery records; safety, legal, and risk functions challenge material decisions; independent reviewers test evidence traceability. AI may organize approved records, compare versions, identify incomplete provenance, and draft review questions, but cannot designate critical systems, operate systems, authorize access or change, make safety decisions, score an assessment, submit to NCA, or claim compliance. Review whenever system, service, supplier, or incident risk materially changes and complete an accountable annual review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Retain the official [NCA Critical Systems Cybersecurity Controls page](https://nca.gov.sa/en/regulatory-documents/controls-list/cscc/), retrieved 2026-07-31, with the CSCC-1:2019 source/version, current status, and permitted use confirmed by authorized entity leadership before engagement reliance. This plan uses original operational language only; it does not reproduce NCA controls, decide criticality, prescribe operations, score an assessment, or make a conformity claim. Keep system and safety evidence in authorized repositories.

### 1. Critical-system designation, service, and safety-boundary package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain the human-approved critical-system scope or open designation questions, service/process dependency inventory, operational and safety owners, system boundary, consequence context, supplier/support relationships, and legal/regulatory applicability record from critical-system sponsors, operations, safety, cybersecurity, and legal owners. State the in-scope system population and excluded boundaries before review.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected system to its sponsor, operational service obligation, boundary and dependency record, safety/operational owner, and authorized designation or escalation record. Use summarized metadata where detailed engineering evidence is restricted. This supports accountable scope provenance; it cannot designate a system as critical, establish safety, settle regulatory scope, or show complete population coverage.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare approved inventories and flag absent owner, dependency, or review fields. Human sponsors, safety authorities, and counsel decide criticality and escalation. Trigger on system commissioning/decommissioning, material service or consequence change, supplier change, acquisition, or annual review.

### 2. Privileged operation, engineering-change, and detection package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain authorized privileged-access approvals/reviews, configuration and engineering-change references, segregation or operational-authorization records, monitoring/detection and test references, incident records, and exception evidence from operations, engineering, cybersecurity, and system owners. Set restricted-access and safe-observation constraints before viewing production evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Select a material access, change, or event and inspect the evidence chain from accountable approval through time-bounded operational record, monitoring/detection reference, exception or incident handling, and remediation/retest. Do not execute commands or modify a critical system. This supports traceability of claimed governance; it cannot prove safe operation, detection coverage, absence of compromise, or CSCC conformance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize approved metadata and identify missing approvals, timestamps, or retest links; it cannot operate systems, authorize access/change, or alter evidence. Human operational and cybersecurity owners decide action and escalation. Trigger on privileged access change, material engineering change, alert/incident, failed test, or annual evidence renewal.

### 3. Resilience, supplier support, and accountable-remediation package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain resilience/recovery plan and exercise references, supplier support and maintenance responsibility records, incident coordination records, risk and exception decisions, independent-review observations, corrective actions, retest evidence, and management-review minutes from engineering, operations, suppliers, cybersecurity, safety, and leadership.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected recovery exercise, supplier dependency, or material finding to the relevant system boundary, accountable owner, approved decision, exercise or response evidence, corrective action, and retest or documented closure rationale. Preserve unresolved safety, supplier, and timing limitations. This supports a reviewable resilience trail; it cannot demonstrate recovery under real conditions, supplier performance, safety assurance, or regulatory compliance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a non-authoritative evidence queue and flag overdue owners or expiring exceptions. Independent reviewers challenge traceability; named humans approve remediation, residual-risk decisions, and external communications. Trigger on an exercise result, supplier support change, incident, overdue corrective action, source update, or annual readiness cycle.
