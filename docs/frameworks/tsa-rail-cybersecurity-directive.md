# TSA rail cybersecurity directive engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not TSA directive text, a determination of coverage, or an operational-security assessment. Confirm current applicability and directive version through the [TSA rail cybersecurity directive notice](https://www.tsa.gov/sites/default/files/tsa-security-directive-1580_82-2022-01c-and-memo-508c.pdf).

## Engagement focus

Maintain an owner-approved record of covered rail operations, critical cyber-system scope, plans and exercises, evidence of operational safeguards, material changes, incidents, testing, remedial actions, and official communications.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Rail owner/operators and accountable security leaders determine coverage and operational priorities; safety and operations teams retain evidence; independent reviewers test documentation and exercise traceability. AI organizes approved records and prepares non-sensitive questions, but cannot determine directive applicability, approve an operational plan, perform safety actions, or submit material to TSA. Review after material system or threat changes and retain human decisions for every external representation.


## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [TSA rail cybersecurity directive notice](https://www.tsa.gov/sites/default/files/tsa-security-directive-1580_82-2022-01c-and-memo-508c.pdf), checked 2026-07-31, and have rail legal/security leadership confirm the current directive, applicability, sensitive-security-information handling, and authorized reporting channel. This plan is original readiness guidance, not directive text, a coverage decision, or a TSA submission.

### 1. Rail-operation boundary and authority package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain approved rail operation and critical-system boundary, site and operational-technology inventory, accountable security/operations contacts, safety interfaces, sensitive-information handling constraints, and counsel-led coverage questions from rail leadership, safety, operations, security, and legal owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected system or location to an accountable owner, operational purpose, dependency, approved boundary, and sensitivity classification. This supports engagement scoping; it cannot determine directive applicability, operational criticality, or safety acceptability.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile sanitized inventory metadata and flag missing owners or review dates. Authorized rail and legal leaders decide scope and access. Trigger on a route/site/system change, acquisition, new supplier, directive update, or annual review.

### 2. Cyber-operation, exercise, and remedial-action package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain approved operating-plan references, access/change/monitoring records, incident and escalation chronology, exercise or test records, supplier responsibilities, exceptions, findings, and corrective-action evidence from security, operations, engineering, and vendor owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected event, exercise, or finding from source record through accountable owner, approved action, operational/safety coordination, and retest or documented closure. This supports process traceability; it cannot prove effectiveness, direct a safety response, or determine whether an event requires a report.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index authorized non-sensitive records and flag missing evidence links; it may not access sensitive systems, direct rail operations, or make notification decisions. Human security and operations leads decide response. Trigger on incident, exercise failure, control failure, or supplier/technology change.

### 3. External-communication and annual-readiness package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain authorized communication drafts, submission-authority records, legal reviews, management oversight records, retention references, source-change checks, and unresolved-exception register from legal, security, and accountable executives.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample a proposed external communication or exception through its evidence basis, approved authority, review record, and action follow-up. This supports readiness discipline; it cannot submit to TSA, represent compliance, or close a regulatory matter.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a source-linked internal action queue and identify stale approvals. Named humans approve risk decisions and all external communications. Review after a directive change, incident, material finding, or annual readiness cycle.
