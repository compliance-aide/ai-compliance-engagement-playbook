# BMA operational cyber-risk code — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not BMA text, legal advice or a supervisory conclusion.

## Source and applicability

Start from [BMA banking policy and guidance](https://www.bma.bm/documents-centre/documents-policy-and-guidance/documents-banking)
and the relevant licensed-sector page. Checked 2026-09-04: official search identifies
the September 2022 Operational Cyber Risk Management Code of Conduct for banks,
deposit companies, corporate service providers, trust companies, money service
businesses, investment businesses and fund administration providers. The linked
PDF could not be fully retrieved in this check; detailed paragraph verification
remains pending. Do not equate an indexed excerpt with full source review.

BMA also publishes separate insurance and digital-asset cyber materials in its
[policy collection](https://www.bma.bm/documents-centre/documents-policy-and-guidance).
Resolve the licensed entity and applicable instrument before applying requirements
or reporting clocks. A group-wide label is insufficient. See the
[author record](../refresh-reviews/bma-operational-cyber-risk-code.md).

## Engagement focus

Trace material services through risk, safeguards, providers, actual operating
observations and governance decisions. Record the approved proportionality rationale
rather than allowing AI to omit work because an entity appears small.

## Roles

Board and senior management own governance and risk decisions. Compliance/counsel
resolve scope and reporting duties. Security/risk owners coordinate evidence;
operators perform authorised tests and changes; service owners maintain provider
responsibilities. Independent auditors/reviewers challenge evidence. AI may reconcile
metadata and draft workpapers; it cannot accept risk, decide proportionality,
authorise production changes, file with BMA or make a compliance claim.

## Before starting

Obtain licence/entity inventory, applicable code/version, approved governance and
risk method, service/asset/provider maps, evidence permissions, incident authorities,
assurance plan and QA test scope. Missing code text blocks paragraph-level conclusions
while permitted inventory work continues. Use the [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md).

## Ordered workflow

| Step | Action and owner | Output and exit check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Resolve entity scope | Compliance maps each licence to the governing code and related obligations. | Versioned applicability record and unresolved source gaps. |
| 2. Map material services | Operations/security reconcile assets, information, providers and dependencies. | Service-to-owner map with missing dependencies visible. |
| 3. Approve risk/evidence plan | Risk/security propose scenarios, safeguards and methods; management approves proportionality decisions. | Each scoped claim has evidence owner, expected observation and coverage rationale. |
| 4. Observe safeguards | Authorised operators collect actual configuration, monitoring and change records and execute approved QA tests. | Results match the service, version and period; plans are distinguished from implementation. |
| 5. Exercise response/recovery | Incident/service owners run approved fictional incident and recovery scenarios, including provider failure. | Observed escalation and recovery results with limitations and adverse paths. |
| 6. Correct and retest | Operators implement approved changes; reviewer retests the same affected claim. | Finding/change/retest trail; unresolved risks remain open. |
| 7. Review and hand off | Management reviews findings and exceptions; compliance prepares any separately authorised supervisory work. | Decisions, protected evidence and next queue; draft reporting is distinct from submission. |

## Evidence and test plan

| Evidence and custodian | Check | Expected observation | Failure or limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Licence/source register, compliance | Trace service/entity to applicable instrument and version. | Scope decision is supported. | Insurance or digital-asset duties cannot be copied indiscriminately. |
| Risk/asset/provider map, security | Follow a material service across dependencies to safeguards and owners. | Gaps and shared responsibilities are explicit. | Provider outsourcing is not evidence that risk disappeared. |
| Monitoring/recovery results, operations | Compare approved expected behavior with observed QA results. | Failed paths and coverage limits remain recorded. | A tabletop cannot prove live recovery performance. |
| Exception/retest records, risk/audit | Follow finding through authorised treatment, expiry and independent challenge. | Closure evidence addresses the original issue. | An expired exception or completed ticket cannot prove remediation. |

## Failure branches and decisions

Unknown provider ownership remains a gap, not an assumed inherited safeguard.
Conflicting source versions require compliance resolution before a conclusion.
Unexpected operational effects stop the affected test for its safety owner; retain
observations and use approved recovery. Actual incidents escalate promptly through
the incident authority; legal reporting clocks come from the applicable instrument.
After interruption, verify actual state and existing receipts before repeating work.

## Cadence and renewal

Use the approved assurance and regulatory calendar. Quarterly evidence housekeeping
and annual independent readiness review are operating defaults, not asserted BMA
frequencies. Reassess after licence, service, supplier, threat, incident or source
changes, and before relying on an expiring exception.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver applicability, service/dependency and risk records, evidence/test coverage,
observations, exceptions and retests, governance decisions and next queue. Name the
full-code retrieval gap and untested services. Independent source, engagement and
skeptical review and named human publication approval remain pending. Original
workflow and links only; no restricted source tables or real financial evidence.
