# Bermuda Monetary Authority operational cyber-risk code engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not Bermuda Monetary Authority text, a supervisory determination, legal advice, or a compliance claim. Confirm current applicability through the [BMA operational cyber-risk code publication](https://www.bma.bm/viewPDF/documents/2022-09-26-17-20-08-Operational-Cyber-Risk-Management-Code-of-Conduct.pdf) and qualified regulatory counsel.

## Engagement focus

Maintain an operational-cyber-risk record that connects licensed-entity scope, board and management ownership, technology and information assets, material services and providers, cyber-risk assessments, safeguards, monitoring and testing evidence, incidents, remediation, governance decisions, and review dates. Preserve proportionality reasoning, sources, scope, and limitations with material evidence. Separate facts, recommendations, and human decisions so reviewers can test how a conclusion was reached.

## Roles and annual rhythm

The board, senior management, risk, security, technology, operations, legal, compliance, procurement, and service owners retain accountable decisions. Security owners coordinate evidence and assurance; service owners maintain provider context; incident authorities decide escalation and communication; and auditors evaluate evidence in their independent role. Independent reviewers sample assets and services through ownership, risk assessment, safeguards, oversight, observation, remediation, and retesting, challenging unowned providers, stale risk context, untested recovery assumptions, and closure without support.

AI may organize authorized metadata, identify gaps in ownership or review dates, compare approved plans with retained evidence, draft factual questions, and prepare non-authoritative workpapers. AI cannot determine regulated scope, decide proportionality, authorize a safeguard or exception, make a supervisory filing, accept risk, alter evidence, or claim compliance. Reassess before material service, provider, system, threat, incident, business, or regulatory changes; review evidence routinely and conduct an annual independent readiness review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the current [BMA operational cyber-risk code publication](https://www.bma.bm/viewPDF/documents/2022-09-26-17-20-08-Operational-Cyber-Risk-Management-Code-of-Conduct.pdf); checked 2026-07-31. This is original planning language and does not reproduce the code, provide legal advice, determine licensed-entity scope, or assert compliance. Board, management, legal/compliance, and designated incident authorities retain decisions on proportionality, risk acceptance, reporting, and safeguards.

### 1. Entity scope, governance, and cyber-risk record

- **Request and owner:** Licensed-entity/service inventory, board and management accountability records, information and technology asset context, risk-assessment method/results, material-provider context, and dated governance decisions from compliance, risk, security, technology, and business owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected material service or asset to its owner, risk context, provider/dependency record, and management or board review. This can support transparent planning evidence; it cannot determine regulated scope, establish proportionality, or prove all operational-cyber risks are known.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved ownership and review metadata and flag missing records. Humans approve governance decisions and risk treatments. Refresh following material changes to licensed activities, services, systems, providers, or threat conditions.

### 2. Safeguard operation, monitoring, and response evidence

- **Request and owner:** Approved safeguard standards and implementation records, privileged-access/change evidence, monitoring and test outputs, vulnerability/remediation records, incident-response evidence, continuity/recovery exercises, and provider-security evidence from security, technology, operations, and service owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a human-selected observation, test, or incident through source evidence, accountable response, escalation, corrective action, and retest. This can support a limited evidence trail; it cannot prove sustained operating effectiveness, authorize a safeguard, or decide incident notification.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may perform read-only completeness checks and flag late owner actions; it cannot alter systems or initiate response actions. Authorized humans approve changes, make incident/escalation decisions, and direct recovery. Recollect after a material incident, test failure, significant vulnerability, or provider change.

### 3. Independent challenge, exception, and remediation workpaper

- **Request and owner:** Assurance/test plans and results, exception register, management risk decisions, remediation plans, audit observations, and closure/retest records from risk, internal audit, security, and management owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected exception or finding to rationale, accountable approver, mitigation, expiry, supporting evidence, and independent retest/challenge. This can support accountable oversight; it cannot accept risk, close remediation, or replace independent assurance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compile a restricted evidence index and identify nearing expiries. Humans approve exceptions, resourcing, closure, filings, and external representations. Review routinely, challenge annually, and reassess after material findings or regulatory updates.
