# Bermuda Monetary Authority operational cyber-risk code engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not Bermuda Monetary Authority text, a supervisory determination, legal advice, or a compliance claim. Confirm current applicability through the [BMA operational cyber-risk code publication](https://www.bma.bm/viewPDF/documents/2022-09-26-17-20-08-Operational-Cyber-Risk-Management-Code-of-Conduct.pdf) and qualified regulatory counsel.

## Engagement focus

Maintain an operational-cyber-risk record that connects licensed-entity scope, board and management ownership, technology and information assets, material services and providers, cyber-risk assessments, safeguards, monitoring and testing evidence, incidents, remediation, governance decisions, and review dates. Preserve proportionality reasoning, sources, scope, and limitations with material evidence. Separate facts, recommendations, and human decisions so reviewers can test how a conclusion was reached.

## Roles and annual rhythm

The board, senior management, risk, security, technology, operations, legal, compliance, procurement, and service owners retain accountable decisions. Security owners coordinate evidence and assurance; service owners maintain provider context; incident authorities decide escalation and communication; and auditors evaluate evidence in their independent role. Independent reviewers sample assets and services through ownership, risk assessment, safeguards, oversight, observation, remediation, and retesting, challenging unowned providers, stale risk context, untested recovery assumptions, and closure without support.

AI may organize authorized metadata, identify gaps in ownership or review dates, compare approved plans with retained evidence, draft factual questions, and prepare non-authoritative workpapers. AI cannot determine regulated scope, decide proportionality, authorize a safeguard or exception, make a supervisory filing, accept risk, alter evidence, or claim compliance. Reassess before material service, provider, system, threat, incident, business, or regulatory changes; review evidence routinely and conduct an annual independent readiness review.
