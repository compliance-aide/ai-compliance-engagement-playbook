# Australia Ransomware Payment Reporting Rules 2025 engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not Australian legislation, legal advice, an incident-response direction, a payment decision, or a compliance claim. Confirm current requirements through the [Cyber Security (Ransomware Payment Reporting) Rules 2025](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2025L00278/asmade/versions) and applicable legal and authority guidance.

## Engagement focus

Maintain a reporting-readiness record for ransomware events that may bring the organization within the rules: entity and business scope, critical-infrastructure and turnover analysis, decision ownership, incident facts, demand and payment evidence, communications, reporting-clock triggers, report preparation records, legal review, insurer and law-enforcement coordination, and post-event improvements. Keep the legal applicability, payment decision, notification decision, and factual reporting approval as separate accountable decisions. Preserve the source and timestamp for material facts; do not let a draft or automated summary replace incident command or legal review.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, incident-command, legal, finance, security, records, communications, insurer, and supplier-management owners. Operators maintain incident intake, escalation, evidence-preservation, contact, report-preparation, and exercise records. Review entity-scope assumptions, reporting contacts, incident playbooks, evidence handling, insurer and supplier dependencies, and notification pathways quarterly; exercise the reporting workflow at least annually. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples readiness records and completed exercises; auditors test the evidence trail without directing an incident, deciding payment, determining legal applicability, approving a report, or assuming management responsibility.

AI may organize supplied incident evidence, maintain a time-stamped fact register, flag missing ownership or document gaps, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot direct incident response, decide a payment or report, determine legal applicability, approve external communications, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the current [Cyber Security (Ransomware Payment Reporting) Rules 2025](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2025L00278/asmade/versions), checked 2026-07-31, and current legal/authority advice. This is original readiness planning language, not legislation, legal advice, incident direction, or a report. Incident command, legal, finance, and authorized executives retain all scope, payment, reporting, and communication decisions.

### 1. Applicability and reporting-readiness record

- **Request and owner:** Legal, compliance, executive, and records owners provide entity/group context, turnover and sector assumptions, dated applicability analyses, reporting contacts, delegated authorities, insurer and supplier escalation contacts, and playbook version history.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected readiness assumption to its source, named owner, approval/review date, and incident-playbook location. This supports prepared escalation; it cannot determine applicability, calculate a reporting deadline, or authorize a payment or notification.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index authorized contact and playbook metadata and flag expired reviews. Humans decide scope, delegation, and legal interpretation. Refresh after an entity, business, provider, insurer, authority-contact, or legal change and at least quarterly.

### 2. Time-stamped incident and decision-evidence package

- **Request and owner:** Incident command, security, finance, legal, and records owners provide incident timeline records, demand/payment facts and preservation references, communications, approvals, insurer/law-enforcement coordination records, report drafts, and factual-source register.
- **Validate and limit:** Follow a selected material incident fact from source/time record through validation, accountable decision, communication or report draft, and retained approval. This supports factual traceability; it cannot decide what occurred, validate every fact, direct response, or approve a submission.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may summarize supplied facts with source/time labels and flag missing decision owners; it cannot send communications or make incident decisions. Recollect continuously during an incident and after a new demand, payment-related event, or material fact change.

### 3. Exercise, independent challenge, and remediation workpaper

- **Request and owner:** Security, legal, finance, records, and assurance owners provide tabletop/exercise scenarios and outputs, reporting workflow observations, exception log, improvement actions, accountable due dates, and closure/retest evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected exercise observation or readiness gap to its owner, corrective action, decision rationale, completion evidence, and independent challenge or retest. This supports preparedness improvement; it cannot prove incident performance, accept residual risk, or close an action without human approval.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compile a restricted exercise evidence list and flag overdue actions. Humans approve exercises, risk decisions, closures, and all external reports. Exercise annually and reassess after an incident, legal update, material supplier change, or failed test.
