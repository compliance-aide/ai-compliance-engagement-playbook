# NIST SP 800-167 application-allowlisting engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST guidance, a software-approval decision, a production-change authorization, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-167](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/167/final) and the organization’s approved security, architecture, change, and endpoint-management decisions.

## Engagement focus

Operate application allowlisting as a controlled endpoint safeguard that connects authorized software scope, ownership, validation, deployment, exception handling, monitoring, change control, and recovery considerations. Maintain a defensible record of what is authorized and why, while treating actual software approval, emergency changes, and production enforcement as accountable human decisions rather than an automated or AI conclusion.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, security, endpoint, platform, application, change-management, service-owner, risk, supplier-management, and business-owner roles. Operators maintain endpoint scope, authorized-application records, validation and deployment evidence, exception approvals, monitoring results, change records, support and recovery procedures, supplier evidence, and remediation status. Review material application scope, exception assumptions, and enforcement coverage quarterly; test representative authorization, exception, rollback, and recovery paths at least annually and after material endpoint, application, supplier, or architecture changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples authorization and exception evidence; auditors test the evidence trail without approving software, authorizing production changes, accepting risk, directing enforcement, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied authorization and exception evidence, flag missing ownership or stale reviews, trace approved applications to recorded service scope, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot approve software, alter endpoint policies, authorize a production change, accept risk or an exception, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.
