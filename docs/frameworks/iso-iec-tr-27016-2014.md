# ISO/IEC TR 27016:2014 security-economics engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not ISO or IEC text, an investment recommendation, a risk-acceptance decision, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through the [ISO/IEC TR 27016:2014 publication record](https://www.iso.org/standard/43756.html).

## Engagement focus

Keep security investment decisions traceable to business context rather than treating a budget amount as evidence of protection. Maintain an economics record that connects decision objectives, assets and services affected, accountable owners, credible options, resource assumptions, expected outcomes, uncertainty and limitations, authorized evidence, risk and dependency context, approval, implementation status, measurement, and revisit triggers. Separate an option analysis from the accountable decision to fund, defer, accept risk, or discontinue work.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Executive leadership, finance, security, risk, technology, procurement, service, privacy, legal, and internal-audit owners retain their decisions. Business and security owners frame options and evidence; finance stewards cost and resource assumptions; risk owners govern acceptance within authority; and executive decision-makers approve priorities. Independent reviewers sample material decisions to test traceability, data provenance, assumption disclosure, treatment of alternatives, outcome measures, and exception governance without substituting their own investment judgment.

AI may organize authorized options and evidence, identify stale assumptions, compare approved measures with reported outcomes, draft questions, and prepare non-authoritative workpapers. AI cannot estimate business value as a fact, set budget, choose an investment, accept risk, initiate procurement, approve a program, or claim compliance. Reassess on material business, threat, incident, technology, supplier, or resource changes; conduct quarterly decision-portfolio reviews and an annual independent readiness review.
