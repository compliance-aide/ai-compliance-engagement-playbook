# NIST SP 800-228 cloud-native API protection engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST API-protection content, an API security design, a production authorization, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-228](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/228/upd1/final) and the organization's approved security, architecture, privacy, legal, operational, and change decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round engagement for protecting in-scope APIs in cloud-native systems. Maintain the relationship among business purpose, API inventory, owners, consumers, data flows, schemas, identity and authorization design, gateways and service paths, runtime dependencies, interfaces, deployment environments, observability, supplier components, vulnerabilities, and retirement. Cover both design-time and runtime evidence while recognizing that API risk changes with every material change in data, consumer, service, identity, or exposure.

Treat an API specification, gateway configuration, code review, test result, log sample, scanner output, deployment record, or supplier statement as evidence to evaluate in context. None alone proves that an interface is secure, correctly authorized, safely exposed, or operating as intended. Preserve the decision trail from risk analysis and approved design through implementation, release, monitoring, incident response, deprecation, and corrective work.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Product, API, security, architecture, engineering, operations, identity, privacy, legal, data, supplier, change, incident-response, and records owners establish scope and make accountable decisions. Operators maintain API and dependency inventories, approved specifications and designs, authorization and data-flow traceability, test and deployment evidence, gateway and runtime configuration records, monitoring and vulnerability evidence, exceptions, incidents, and deprecation records. Reconcile APIs, owners, consumers, data uses, dependencies, and exposure at least quarterly; review material changes and high-risk findings before release; and conduct management review annually and after significant architecture, threat, supplier, or regulatory changes.

Independent reviewers sample an API from approved purpose and design through implementation, release, observed operation, and remediation. Auditors assess supplied evidence without accessing protected data, changing API or gateway settings, authorizing releases, approving exceptions, accepting risk, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize specifications and evidence, identify missing ownership or stale reviews, compare supplied records with approved decisions, draft workpapers, and prepare review questions. AI cannot design or approve APIs, change configurations, grant access, release software, accept risk, make legal or privacy conclusions, or attest to conformance.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [NIST SP 800-228 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/228/upd1/final), retaining the retrieved version and applicable use terms; checked 2026-07-31. This is original evidence planning, not reproduced NIST content, an API-security design, production authorization, penetration test, or conformance conclusion.

### 1. API inventory, consumer, and data-flow package

- **Request and owner:** Product, API, data, privacy, architecture, and security owners provide API/service inventory, business purpose, consumer and exposure classification, schema/data-flow reference, service/dependency map, and accountable ownership.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one API from declared purpose through its consumer class, data-flow/schema reference, service path, and named owners. This checks the supplied inventory's linkage; it cannot establish that all consumers, data, or exposure are known or appropriate.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile inventories and flag missing owners, consumer classes, or data-flow references. Humans determine data treatment, exposure, and scope. Refresh after endpoint, consumer, data, schema, service, or exposure change.

### 2. Identity, authorization, and release package

- **Request and owner:** Identity, API, engineering, platform, and change owners provide approved identity/authorization design records, non-secret gateway/service configuration references, code and deployment traceability, test references, access-review evidence, and approved exceptions.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample an interface from approved authorization design through implementation/deployment identifiers, controlled configuration reference, and access-review evidence. This can assess evidence continuity; it cannot grant access, test authorization behavior, alter gateway settings, or approve release.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may identify missing design-to-release links or aging access reviews and draft questions. Humans approve authorization, releases, changes, and exceptions. Recollect before material exposure or authorization change and after a release or access-control incident.

### 3. Runtime observation, vulnerability, and deprecation package

- **Request and owner:** Operations, security, API, supplier, incident-response, records, and risk owners provide approved monitoring coverage references, alert/incident records, vulnerability and dependency notices, remediation/change records, deprecation/retirement plans, and management-review evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one operational event, finding, or deprecation from observed record through owner assignment, remediation or exception decision, and closure/next-review evidence. This does not prove continuous monitoring, vulnerability absence, secure runtime operation, or acceptable risk.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may group supplied events, flag stale remediation or unsupported dependencies, and draft review questions. Humans investigate, approve remediation and exceptions, notify affected parties, and accept risk. Review quarterly and after incident, material finding, supplier change, or deprecation event.
