# Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not Illinois legal advice or a litigation conclusion. Confirm current statutory text through the [Illinois General Assembly BIPA record](https://ilga.gov/Legislation/ILCS/Articles?ActID=3004&Chapter=CIVIL+LIABILITIES&ChapterID=57&MajorTopic=RIGHTS+AND+REMEDIES).

## Engagement focus

Maintain biometric-use inventory, purpose and notice records, consent authority, retention and destruction decisions, vendor and disclosure records, access controls, incident evidence, and remediation history.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Privacy, legal, security, HR, and product owners determine applicability and rights handling; independent reviewers test evidence. AI maps approved records and flags gaps, but cannot collect biometrics, obtain consent, decide retention, or make legal conclusions. Review on change and annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [Illinois General Assembly BIPA record](https://ilga.gov/Legislation/ILCS/Articles?ActID=3004&Chapter=CIVIL+LIABILITIES&ChapterID=57&MajorTopic=RIGHTS+AND+REMEDIES), plus counsel’s current-law and litigation-risk assessment. Retrieve and record statute status before each engagement cycle. This is original operational planning, not Illinois legal advice, statutory text, or a litigation conclusion.

### 1. Biometric-use, person, and purpose boundary

- **Request and owner:** Request the human-approved inventory of biometric-enabled processes and related vendors, stated business purpose, collection/use channels, employee/consumer and Illinois nexus inputs, data-flow summary, and named product, HR, privacy, security, procurement, and legal owners. Define the actual service/process population and time period; never include biometric templates in the engagement repository.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected process to its approved purpose, data-flow and vendor record, accountable owner, product/release boundary, and counsel-reviewed applicability question. This can support an accountable scope and purpose record; it cannot determine whether a person or data type is covered, whether collection occurred, or legal applicability.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved process inventories with vendor and release metadata and flag missing ownership. Legal and privacy owners decide scope; product/HR owners authorize implementation. Trigger on a new biometric feature, collection channel, vendor, workforce use case, or annual legal review.

### 2. Notice, authorization, and disclosure decision trail

- **Request and owner:** Request approved notices, consent/authorization workflow designs and version records, authority-verification criteria, disclosure/recipient approval references, access/request logs, and exception escalations from privacy, legal, HR, product, and operations owners. Retain references and approved metadata rather than biometric data or signatures.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected collection or disclosure workflow from published/versioned notice through human-approved authorization path, recipient decision, system/audit reference, and exception handling. This can support evidence of the documented process; it cannot verify individual understanding, decide authorization validity, prove all collections followed the process, or establish compliance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare workflow versions and flag missing approval or audit-link metadata. It cannot collect biometric data, obtain consent, decide disclosures, or communicate rights outcomes. Authorized human privacy/legal owners make those decisions. Trigger on a form/workflow revision, new recipient, complaint, unusual access, or annual test.

### 3. Retention, destruction, vendor, and incident accountability

- **Request and owner:** Request retention and destruction decision records, system/vendor schedule references, deletion job/reconciliation evidence, supplier data-use and incident/escalation provisions, incident case references, corrective-action records, and management-review minutes from privacy, security, IT, procurement, and legal owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected retention-cycle or vendor exit to approved schedule inputs, accountable system/vendor owner, execution/reconciliation reference, exception decision, and follow-up review. This can support lifecycle traceability; it cannot prove irreversible deletion, supplier performance, incident absence, notification duty, or legal sufficiency.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may identify aging records and missing supplier/action links from approved metadata. Humans decide retention, destruction, supplier remedy, incident communications, and risk acceptance. Trigger on system or vendor change, schedule exception, incident, complaint, corrective-action due date, and annual review.
