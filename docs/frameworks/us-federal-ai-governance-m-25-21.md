# U.S. federal AI governance — M-25-21 engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not an agency determination. M-25-21 is the current policy; it replaced M-24-10. Consult the [official memo](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/M-25-21-Accelerating-Federal-Use-of-AI-through-Innovation-Governance-and-Public-Trust.pdf).

## Engagement focus

Maintain agency AI portfolio, executive governance, reporting calendar, impact reviews, acquisition/data safeguards, approvals, evaluations, exceptions, and material-deployment revalidation evidence.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Operators retain use-case and change records; independent reviewers test inventory completeness and exceptions. AI prepares records and identifies missing evidence, but cannot make agency determinations, grant exceptions, certify compliance, or submit reports. Review quarterly and annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [OMB M-25-21 memorandum](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/M-25-21-Accelerating-Federal-Use-of-AI-through-Innovation-Governance-and-Public-Trust.pdf), current agency implementation direction, and authorized counsel/policy interpretation; checked 2026-07-31. This original plan does not reproduce policy text, determine applicability, grant an exception, or prepare an authoritative government submission.

### 1. AI-use-case inventory and governance package

- **Request and owner:** Agency AI governance, component, program, data, privacy, civil-rights, security, procurement, and system owners provide the use-case inventory, accountable official, mission purpose, deployment stage, component boundary, review cadence, and governance meeting/decision records.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected use case from inventory through accountable owner, intended purpose, component boundary, documented governance review, and next review date. This tests traceability, not inventory completeness or agency applicability.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare supplied inventory fields and flag absent owners, dates, or review records. Authorized officials determine classification, governance scope, and portfolio decisions. Refresh before deployment and after material model, mission, data, vendor, or ownership change.

### 2. Impact, safeguard, and acquisition package

- **Request and owner:** Program, privacy, civil-rights, security, data, acquisition, legal, and vendor-management owners provide authorized impact/review artifacts, documented safeguards, data and model provenance references, evaluation/test records, contract or vendor obligations, and limitations.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample a use case from documented impact consideration through designated safeguard/owner, evaluation or review reference, acquisition/data dependency, limitation, and tracked treatment. This cannot assess impact, approve use, interpret a contract, or validate a model.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize authorized artifacts and identify missing provenance, review dates, or safeguard owners. Humans conduct required assessments, approve safeguards, and decide procurement/data handling. Refresh after material data, model, vendor, interface, or risk change.

### 3. Exception, reporting, and independent-review package

- **Request and owner:** Governance, legal, executive, reporting, risk, and independent-review owners provide exception requests/decisions, reporting source records, approvals, unresolved-gap register, corrective actions, and reviewer challenge workpapers.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected exception or reported portfolio item to source evidence, accountable official, decision date, limitation, and follow-up. This cannot grant an exception, conclude compliance, or submit a report.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may draft internal reconciliation questions and flag stale actions; it cannot make an agency determination or transmit reporting. Authorized officials approve exceptions, reports, and corrective-action closure. Recheck before a reporting event and annually.
