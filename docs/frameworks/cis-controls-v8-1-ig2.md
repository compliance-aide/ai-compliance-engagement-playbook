# CIS Controls v8.1 Implementation Group 2 engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not CIS Controls text, a safeguard selection, a security decision, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through CIS’s [Implementation Group 2 resource](https://www.cisecurity.org/controls/implementation-groups/ig2).

## Engagement focus

Maintain a scaled-security engagement record that connects enterprise scope, differentiated risk contexts, accountable technology and business owners, specialized operating evidence, prioritization decisions, exceptions, remediation, supplier dependencies, and independent review. Make the evidence process usable across more complex environments without converting AI-generated analysis into a decision about security design or implementation sufficiency.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Business leadership, IT, security, privacy, risk, procurement, and service owners retain accountable decisions; evidence owners preserve authorized records; independent reviewers test scope, prioritization rationale, evidence traceability, and unresolved exceptions. AI may organize authorized evidence, identify gaps, draft questions, and prepare non-authoritative workpapers, but cannot choose an implementation group, select or approve safeguards, determine sufficiency, accept risk, alter production configuration, initiate scans or changes, or claim compliance. Review operating evidence quarterly, reassess organizational fit annually, and reopen the plan before material asset, service, threat, incident, regulatory, business, or technology changes.

## Tailored evidence plan

### 1. Differentiated environment and dependency record

- **Request and owner:** A service and environment inventory that distinguishes business contexts, data sensitivity inputs, hosting/network boundaries, critical dependencies, accountable owners, and supplier relationships, maintained by architecture, service, security, and procurement owners. Include in-scope complex environments and changes since the last review.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected service or dependency through the recorded context, named owner, supplier/responsibility record, and human-reviewed priority rationale. This can support a differentiated evidence boundary; it cannot establish complete discovery, supplier performance, or the correct implementation group.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved service/dependency metadata and flag unowned or stale relationships. Humans decide scope, priority, and supplier treatment. Refresh after a new material service, environment, supplier, data flow, or business/risk change and annually.

### 2. Specialized operating-practice evidence

- **Request and owner:** Time-bounded evidence references for the approved complex-environment practices, including access/governance reviews, technical monitoring or validation metadata, change/release records, incident follow-up, recovery exercises, and service-provider oversight, supplied by accountable operational owners with population and access constraints.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect a human-approved cross-environment selection, tracing source records to the relevant service, period, owner, and follow-up action while preserving log, customer, and supplier confidentiality. This can support that declared practices have traceable operating evidence; it cannot prove continuous effectiveness, tool accuracy, or complete implementation.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index authorized metadata, compare dates, and surface missing evidence; it cannot initiate scans, alter production, or decide evidence sufficiency. Humans approve methods, changes, and conclusions. Recollect after an incident, failed test, major release, supplier change, or quarterly review.

### 3. Cross-owner remediation and risk-decision record

- **Request and owner:** Prioritized remediation items, dependency impacts, exception records, decision authority, compensating-context narrative in original language, due dates, and retest evidence from security, risk, service, and procurement owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected issue to its scope, accountable owners, supplier dependency if any, approved decision, expiry, and retest/renewal result. This can support governed remediation; it cannot accept risk, establish legal or contractual sufficiency, or replace independent review.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may identify overdue or conflicting ownership and assemble a read-only decision packet. Humans authorize risk treatment, supplier commitments, and closure. Trigger on an exception expiry, overdue remediation, material supplier change, or annual reassessment.
