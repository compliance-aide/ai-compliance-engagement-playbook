# Massachusetts 201 CMR 17.00 — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not Massachusetts legal advice or a regulatory conclusion. Confirm current text through the [Massachusetts regulation record](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/201-CMR-1700-standards-for-the-protection-of-personal-information-of-residents-of-the-commonwealth).

## Engagement focus

Maintain personal-information scope, written-security-program evidence, asset and access records, vendor safeguards, workforce training, incident records, encryption and lifecycle decisions, exceptions, and remediation history.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Legal, privacy, security, and accountable business owners make applicability and incident decisions; independent reviewers test evidence. AI inventories approved artifacts and identifies gaps, but cannot make legal determinations, approve safeguards, or notify authorities. Review quarterly and annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [Massachusetts 201 CMR 17.00 regulation record](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/201-CMR-1700-standards-for-the-protection-of-personal-information-of-residents-of-the-commonwealth). ### 1. Massachusetts-personal-information scope and written-program record

- **Request and owner:** A counsel-approved applicability record, data inventory identifying the approved population and locations of Massachusetts residents' personal information, system/process boundary, written information-security-program approval, and named privacy, security, and business owners. Cover the in-scope annual period and material changes.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace selected inventory entries to approved system/process owners and the written-program version in force at the time. Reconcile scope changes to a human decision record without exporting personal information. This supports a bounded management record; it cannot decide residency, legal applicability, or legal sufficiency.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare approved inventories, ownership, and version metadata for gaps. Counsel decides applicability and accountable leaders approve program scope. Refresh for a new data use, acquisition, system, material process change, or annual counsel review.

### 2. Access, safeguard, and service-provider decision trail

- **Request and owner:** Approved access-role inventory, account review and termination records, security configuration/exception references, supplier due-diligence and contractual-security records, training completion population, and accountable security, HR, procurement, and supplier owners. Bound evidence to systems/services handling the approved information population.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample a user lifecycle and supplier relationship from scope approval through access decision, review/termination or contract safeguard record, and documented exception where relevant. Protect credentials and contract-sensitive terms. This supports traceability of selected safeguards; it cannot prove every account, supplier, or safeguard operated effectively.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize metadata, identify missing owners/dates, and prepare sample questions. Humans decide access, exception acceptance, supplier suitability, and remediation. Trigger on role changes, supplier onboarding/change, access anomaly, or periodic review.

### 3. Incident, remediation, and annual-management review

- **Request and owner:** Sanitized incident and suspected-disclosure register, triage/forensic references, decision log, remediation/change records, post-incident review, and annual program-review record from security response, legal, privacy, and accountable executives. Include the entire defined reporting period, including no-incident attestation where appropriate.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace selected incidents or annual-review assertions to source case identifiers, responsible decision-makers, remediation evidence, and closure rationale. Do not place personal information or privileged analysis in this repository. This supports accountable chronology; it cannot determine breach status, notice duties, harm, or legal compliance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved case metadata and flag missing owner, decision, or closure links. Legal and designated incident leaders decide notification, investigation, risk, and closure. Trigger on a suspected incident, material finding, regulator/legal change, or annual executive review.
