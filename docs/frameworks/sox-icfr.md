# SOX Section 404 / ICFR — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not a management conclusion or auditor report.
> Confirm scope with the [SEC rules](https://www.sec.gov/files/404guide.shtml),
> auditor, and securities counsel.

## Engagement focus

Set the reporting boundary, materiality/risk method, accountable control owners,
and external-auditor coordination rules. Capture financial-process and technology
changes, evaluate their effects on management's documented rationale, remediate
deficiencies, retain evidence, and carry lessons into the next fiscal cycle.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Management owns evaluation, remediation, and its report. Independent auditors
perform separately required work. AI organizes controlled documentation, change
tracking, and evidence lineage; it cannot set materiality, conclude effectiveness,
classify a deficiency, sign certifications, or access MNPI without controls.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [SEC Section 404 guidance](https://www.sec.gov/files/404guide.shtml) with current issuer-specific rules, auditor direction, and securities-counsel advice; checked 2026-07-31. This is original engagement-planning language, not a management assessment, audit opinion, or certification. Handle MNPI under the issuer’s approved access and retention controls.

### 1. Financial-reporting boundary and control-accountability record

- **Request and owner:** Approved reporting boundary, significant-account/process inventory, reporting-system and interface map, control-owner roster, entity-level governance records, and management-approved scoping rationale from finance, IT, and controllership.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected significant reporting flow to its accountable owner, supporting systems, relevant process narrative, and approved scope decision. This can support a documented planning boundary; it cannot set materiality or establish completeness of the issuer’s ICFR scope.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile owner and system metadata in an authorized environment. Management approves scope and access to sensitive information. Refresh for acquisitions, divestitures, new reporting systems, material process changes, or fiscal-year planning.

### 2. Control-operation and change-impact evidence

- **Request and owner:** Period-specific control-performance records, review evidence, system access/change records, interface/reconciliation support, known exceptions, and change-impact assessments from process and technology control owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect a pre-approved selection for period, population, performer/reviewer, source lineage, exception, and follow-up. This can support that selected controls have retrievable operating evidence; it cannot conclude effectiveness, identify all deficiencies, or replace auditor procedures.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize read-only evidence and flag missing dates, approvals, or linkages; it may not classify a deficiency or modify financial systems. Recollect after an exception, significant system/process change, quarter close, or control redesign.

### 3. Deficiency, remediation, and reporting-decision record

- **Request and owner:** Management evaluation workpapers, deficiency/escalation log, remediation and retest evidence, audit-committee or governance records, and approved reporting-decision documentation from management and counsel-controlled stakeholders.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected issue to discovery, evidence, owner, management decision, corrective action, retest, and reporting consideration. This can support accountable issue governance; it cannot classify a deficiency, determine disclosure, or sign a management report.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may produce a restricted traceability index and identify overdue retests. Authorized humans decide classification, reporting, risk, and certification. Review at each reporting cycle and before management’s annual assessment.
