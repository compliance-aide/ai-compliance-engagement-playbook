# Japan APPI — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not Japanese legal advice or a regulatory conclusion. Confirm current material at the [Personal Information Protection Commission](https://www.ppc.go.jp/personalinfo/).

## Engagement focus

Maintain a data, process, vendor, purpose, notice, rights, retention, security, and overseas-transfer decision register. Exercise incident escalation and sample a live processing route through approved evidence.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Owners operate the evidence; independent reviewers test traceability. AI can classify evidence and draft questions, but cannot interpret Japanese law, decide applicability, release data, or make an audit conclusion. Review quarterly and refresh scope annually.


## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [Personal Information Protection Commission](https://www.ppc.go.jp/personalinfo/) materials and qualified Japanese privacy review; checked 2026-07-31. This original plan does not decide APPI applicability, business-operator role, purpose-change permissibility, third-party provision, foreign-country transfer conditions, reporting duty, or a principal's request outcome.

### 1. Purpose, data-use, and provision/transfer register

- **Request and owner:** Personal-data and processing register, purpose/publication or notice records, system/data-flow map, retained-personal-data ownership, third-party provision/vendor register, foreign-country transfer review inputs, and retention/disposal records from privacy, product, procurement, and records owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a collection-to-provision route to declared purpose, system, owner, recipient/vendor, stated transfer context, and retention. This supports a factual trace; it cannot decide purpose compatibility, consent, provision conditions, or foreign-transfer legality.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved inventory and flag new recipients or data routes. Privacy/legal humans decide classifications and actions. Refresh for a new use, purpose change, vendor, overseas route, or material system change.

### 2. Principal requests, safeguards, and incident-response evidence

- **Request and owner:** Disclosure/correction/use-suspension request procedures and logs, identity-verification procedure, access/security-operation evidence, vendor instructions, incident exercise/event timeline, and corrective actions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a redacted sample to source, date, owner, decision authority, escalation, and follow-up. This can support workflow traceability; it cannot decide a principal's request, prove safeguards effective, or determine reporting/notification duty.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may create a redacted evidence index and internal deadline prompts; it may not decide outcomes, contact principals, or submit notifications. Recollect after an incident, recurring request issue, major security change, or failed exercise.

### 3. Vendor, exception, and annual oversight record

- **Request and owner:** Vendor oversight/change records, third-party provision review inputs, exception/risk register, management-review minutes, remediation/retest, annual scope renewal, and PPC/source-change watch.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected exception, vendor change, or remediation item to human authority, facts, expiry, and retest. This can support accountable oversight; it cannot accept risk or provide a legal/audit conclusion.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag stale evidence and prepare a review packet. Named humans approve risk, contracts, communications, and closure. Revisit annually and after material recipient, transfer, processing, or regulatory change.
