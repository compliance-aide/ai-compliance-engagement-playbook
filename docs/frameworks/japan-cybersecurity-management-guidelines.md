# Japan cybersecurity management guidelines — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance for voluntary management practice, not a certification. Consult [IPA cyber-management practice resources](https://www.ipa.go.jp/security/economics/csm-practice.html).

## Engagement focus

Set board-sponsored objectives, decision rights, risk, asset, supplier, incident, investment, recovery, and measurable outcome evidence.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Operators run the program; independent reviewers challenge governance-to-practice evidence. AI synthesizes inventories and drafts decision materials, but cannot substitute for executive risk decisions or state conformance. Review quarterly and annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [IPA cyber-management practice resources](https://www.ipa.go.jp/security/economics/csm-practice.html); checked 2026-07-31. This original plan uses public source context only and does not reproduce guidance or decide whether an organization conforms to a voluntary practice.

### 1. Executive direction and investment evidence

- **Request and owner:** Board or executive cybersecurity objectives, designated decision rights, risk appetite inputs, budget/investment decisions, management-review minutes, and improvement commitments from executive, risk, finance, and security owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one stated objective to a named decision authority, funded action, risk input, success measure, and review date. This establishes an accountable management trail; it cannot judge investment adequacy or certify management practice.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved minutes and highlight missing owners or stale measures. Executives decide priorities, funding, and risk treatment. Refresh after a strategy, funding, leadership, or material-risk change.

### 2. Business-risk, supplier, and operational resilience record

- **Request and owner:** Critical-asset/service inventory, business-impact and cyber-risk assessments, supplier dependency reviews, security requirements, incident and recovery exercises, and action registers from business continuity, procurement, operations, and security owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample a critical service and trace its owner, dependency, scenario, exercise result, corrective action, and retest. This tests evidence continuity; it does not prove resilience or determine supplier assurance sufficiency.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved inventories and draft exercise questions; it may not rate suppliers, approve exceptions, or alter production. Recollect after an outsourcing, material service, incident, or failed exercise.

### 3. Measurement, exception, and improvement oversight

- **Request and owner:** Metrics definitions and trend records, exception/risk decisions, remediation evidence, internal-review workpapers, annual program review, and source-change watch from security governance and independent-review owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one adverse metric or exception to facts, human authority, expiry, remediation, and retest. This supports transparent oversight; it cannot accept risk or make an audit conclusion.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a redacted review packet and flag overdue actions. Named humans approve exceptions and closure. Revisit quarterly and annually, and when official source materials change.
