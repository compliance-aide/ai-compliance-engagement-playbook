# NIST SP 1800-45 water-utility OT remote-access engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST practice-guide content, an operational-technology architecture, a safety determination, a compliance claim, or professional advice. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 1800-45](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/1800/45/final) and the utility's approved safety, engineering, cybersecurity, regulatory, legal, financial, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round engagement for remote access to water and wastewater operational technology that protects service continuity and safety while retaining clear accountability. Connect the operational environment, facilities, processes, remote-access paths, identities, vendors, communications links, architecture decisions, safeguards, monitoring, emergency procedures, maintenance, and evidence. Treat representative architectures and technical configurations as inputs to local engineering and risk decisions, not as automatically deployable designs.

Maintain evidence for approved remote-access purpose, scope, users, authentication and authorization decisions, session control, segmentation and connectivity assumptions, supplier responsibilities, configuration and change records, monitoring, incident handling, backup access, and recovery. Make safety and operational constraints explicit; a cybersecurity change that affects process availability or operator response requires accountable operational and engineering approval.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Utility leadership, OT engineering, operations, safety, cybersecurity, identity, network, facilities, supplier, procurement, legal, regulatory, incident-response, recovery, and records owners establish scope and make accountable decisions. Operators maintain asset and remote-path inventories, approved architectures and access records, configuration and change evidence, supplier attestations, monitoring and session records, maintenance evidence, exercises, incidents, exceptions, and review history. Reconcile material remote-access paths and owners quarterly, review changes and anomalies promptly, exercise contingency access during the year, and conduct an annual governance and safety review.

Independent reviewers sample a remote-access path from approved purpose and architecture through access evidence, monitoring, change control, and operational review, testing traceability and safety-aware governance. Auditors evaluate supplied evidence without designing the system, authorizing remote access, changing OT configuration, directing operations, making safety or regulatory conclusions, accepting risk, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize supplied records, flag stale approvals or missing ownership, summarize evidence, draft workpapers, and prepare review questions. AI cannot design or operate OT, grant access, change configuration, determine safe operation, approve architecture or emergency actions, accept risk, or attest to conformance.
