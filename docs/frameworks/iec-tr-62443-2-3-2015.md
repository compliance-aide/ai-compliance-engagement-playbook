# IEC TR 62443-2-3:2015 IACS patch-management engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not IEC text, patch authorization or a safety decision.

## Source and applicability

The [IEC publication record](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/22811), checked 2026-09-04, describes patch-management activities for IACS asset owners and product suppliers, including patch information and installation. Full licensed report, exchange format and applicable sector requirements remain unverified for this draft. Confirm edition, authorized source access and contractual obligations before detailed evaluation; do not reproduce restricted text here.

## Engagement focus

Trace every relevant advisory from affected product/version through qualified evaluation, approved disposition, implementation and verified running state. Keep supplier availability, owner approval, installation and restored operation separate.

## Roles

Suppliers provide applicability and compatibility information. Asset, engineering, operations, safety and maintenance owners evaluate suitability and authorize work. Risk owners approve deferrals and compensating measures. An independent reviewer challenges coverage and closure evidence. AI may organize permitted records and draft questions; it cannot schedule or deploy patches, scan OT, set maintenance windows, approve exceptions, accept risk or declare safe operation.

## Before starting

Record assets, installed software/firmware, dependencies, support status and owners. Identify official advisory channels, approved evidence sources, safety constraints and escalation contacts. Obtain existing records without connecting to industrial equipment. A patch-management review does not authorize downloads to devices, test execution, restarts or production changes.

## Ordered workflow

1. **Reconcile advisory intake.** Preserve official supplier references, publication/revision dates, affected versions and available corrections. Account for every scoped advisory and inventory match; retain unknown versions as unresolved rather than unaffected.
2. **Determine applicability.** Qualified owners compare exact installed configurations, dependencies and supplier guidance. Record affected, unaffected and uncertain decisions with evidence. A scanner finding or absent CVE match alone is not the complete applicability decision.
3. **Evaluate treatment.** Review compatibility, operational consequences, safety, prerequisites and recovery feasibility. Record approved test environment, results and limitations. Supplier approval does not by itself establish fitness for the site's configuration.
4. **Authorize disposition.** Record patch, defer, replace or other approved treatment with owner, rationale, due date and review trigger. A deferral must preserve the exposure and compensating-measure evidence. Do not mark a deferred asset patched.
5. **Prepare controlled execution.** Authorized operators establish exact package identity, assets, sequence, maintenance window, approvals, stop criteria and recovery arrangements. Resolve prerequisites before execution; this documentation workflow performs no deployment.
6. **Verify actual outcome.** Read permitted implementation and post-change evidence, including running version, pending restart, relevant service checks and rollback status. A successful installer message is insufficient when the vulnerable process still runs. Record failures and partial rollout explicitly.
7. **Close or reopen.** Reconcile all affected assets, update inventories and retain remaining actions. Retest failed outcomes under approval. Reopen after revised advisories, rolled-back changes, new dependencies or failed compensating measures; preserve the original chronology.

## Evidence and test plan

Maintain complete scoped accounting for assets, versions, advisories, dispositions and implementation outcomes. Preserve all evidence supplied for assessment without silent trimming or sampling. State actual verification coverage separately from inventory completeness.

### 1. Asset, software, and advisory intake package

- **Request and owner:** OT asset inventory, approved software/firmware and supplier-dependency records, supplier advisory intake, accountable asset owners, and safety/process constraints, from OT operations and maintenance.
- **Validate and limit:** Reconcile all scoped advisories and trace each to the affected asset/service record, owner, supplier reference, and stated operational constraint. This supports intake completeness and traceability; it cannot establish that every affected asset is known or that a patch is suitable.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize authorized records and flag missing links. Humans decide scope and relevance. Refresh upon an advisory, inventory, supplier, or lifecycle change.

### 2. Human-authorized evaluation and disposition package

- **Request and owner:** Authorized test-environment evidence, compatibility and rollback considerations, patch/change decision records, compensating-measure rationale, maintenance-window approval, and named safety/operations review inputs.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace every scoped disposition to the responsible decision-makers, approved environment evidence, constraints, and next review date without scanning or changing OT. This supports governed decision records; it cannot validate engineering fitness, safety, or operational acceptability.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a read-only comparison packet and identify expired dispositions. OT, safety, operations, and risk authorities approve all actions. Reopen before a patch, exception expiry, or material process change.

### 3. Deployment follow-through and independent challenge package

- **Request and owner:** Approved work order/change record, implementation confirmation, post-change observation, unresolved issues, exception/remediation tracker, and independent review workpapers.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace every scoped completed or deferred action to authorization, accountable owner, evidence date, follow-up, and exception expiry. This supports follow-through; it cannot prove resilient operation or close an independent assessment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index evidence and flag overdue follow-up; it cannot schedule, deploy, or close a change. Review at approved intervals and reopen after an incident, rollback, or material maintenance change.

## Failure branches and decisions

- Installed version unknown: assign retrieval and retain applicability uncertainty.
- Patch unavailable for a supported operational need: obtain a qualified interim treatment and lifecycle decision, not a fictitious remediation date.
- Test failure or safety conflict: stop proposed deployment and escalate; do not weaken acceptance criteria to meet a deadline.
- Partial rollout: report each asset's actual state and keep outstanding assets visible.
- Rollback restores service but reintroduces the vulnerability: reopen exposure and the risk decision.

Use [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md) assertions: supported, not_supported, inconclusive, not_applicable and not_tested. These evidence statuses are distinct from patch task states and risk acceptance.

## Cadence and renewal

Monitor advisories and review dispositions according to approved risk and obligations. Trigger review on new advisories, evidence, incidents, inventory changes and exception expiry. Do not invent universal quarterly reviews or annual certification requirements.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver the advisory-to-asset register, applicability decisions, authorized dispositions, implementation evidence, residual exposure and open actions with owners and dates. Installation activity is supported; effective remediation is not yet supported. Route the restart and verification through operations approval rather than closing the vulnerability.
