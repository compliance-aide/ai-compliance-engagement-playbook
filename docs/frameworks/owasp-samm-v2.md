# OWASP SAMM v2 — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not an OWASP maturity rating. Consult the [OWASP SAMM project](https://owasp.org/www-project-samm/) under its applicable terms.

## Engagement focus

Set executive sponsorship and measurable secure-development outcomes. Baseline only processes actually operating, build a risk-led improvement roadmap with owners and dates, corroborate progress through lifecycle evidence, and re-baseline after material organizational or product change.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Program owners own roadmap delivery; engineering teams provide operating proof. Independent reviewers challenge whether claimed improvement is evidenced. AI can consolidate interviews, flag inconsistent claims, and track outcomes; it cannot assign official maturity, approve priorities, or turn plans into evidence. Review quarterly and re-baseline annually.


## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [OWASP SAMM project](https://owasp.org/www-project-samm/) under applicable terms; checked 2026-07-31. This plan is original implementation guidance and does not assign an official maturity rating or reproduce source material. Program leadership approves scope, baseline method, roadmap priorities, and external representations.

**Plan status:** Independently reviewed; see the [review receipt](../evidence-plan-reviews/owasp-samm-v2.md).

### 1. Product/SDLC boundary and baseline record

- **Request and owner:** Product and SDLC inventory, executive sponsorship record, operating-process baseline, accountable team/role map, measurement approach, and known scope exclusions from application-security and engineering leaders.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected claimed practice to lifecycle artifact, team owner, and stated baseline method. This can support a risk-led baseline of observable processes; it cannot establish maturity from interviews or plans alone.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize interviews and artifacts, flag inconsistent claims, and track evidence gaps. Humans approve baseline scope and results. Re-baseline after material organization, product, delivery-method, or threat change.

### 2. Lifecycle evidence and improvement-roadmap package

- **Request and owner:** Security-requirement, design/review, build/release, vulnerability/incident, training, metric, and roadmap records appropriate to the selected SDLC scope, from engineering and program owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a human-approved sample through lifecycle stages to operating evidence, measure, finding, and roadmap action. This can support progress evidence; it cannot turn a roadmap item into implemented practice or establish an official maturity level.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index artifacts and draft questions but cannot set priorities or close work. Refresh after major product/release-process, incident, toolchain, or team change.

### 3. Quarterly governance and annual re-baseline record

- **Request and owner:** Quarterly metric/review records, exception and remediation register, leadership decisions, evidence of completed roadmap work, retest/validation results, and annual re-baseline from program leadership.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected claimed improvement to baseline, owner, delivery evidence, and independent challenge. This can support accountable change; it cannot ratify maturity or certify a secure-development program.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag overdue actions and draft a review packet. Humans approve priorities, resources, risk, and external claims. Review quarterly and re-baseline annually.
