# NIST SP 800-144 — cloud-security engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not a modern complete cloud baseline or audit conclusion. See the [official NIST publication](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/144/final).

## Engagement focus

Maintain cloud portfolio, sensitivity, criticality, data flow, shared responsibility, identity, configuration, logging, encryption, exposure, portability, deletion, recovery, and provider evidence.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Humans approve architecture, contracts, and risk; independent reviewers test high-impact workloads. AI correlates inventory and evidence, but cannot approve decisions or draw audit conclusions. Review quarterly and annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [NIST SP 800-144 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/144/final), noting publication currency and use terms. The plan is original evidence planning, not a complete cloud baseline, contract interpretation, or cloud-security conclusion.

### 1. Cloud workload, data-flow, and responsibility package

- **Request and owner:** Cloud, application, data, security, privacy, and service owners provide cloud-service inventory, workload criticality, data classifications/flows, tenancy and region decisions, dependency maps, and shared-responsibility assignments.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a sampled high-impact workload to owner, provider/service, data flow, responsibility allocation, and dependency record. This assesses documented traceability; it cannot validate all configurations or determine legal applicability.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile inventory and flow records and flag unclear ownership. Humans determine classification, architecture, and applicability. Refresh after workload, data, region, provider, or dependency changes.

### 2. Provider assurance and control-operation package

- **Request and owner:** Procurement, supplier-management, cloud security, and service owners provide agreements, provider assurance materials, service commitments, identity/configuration/logging evidence, encryption/key-management responsibility records, and material exceptions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected responsibility to provider/customer assignment, evidence owner, operating artifact, limitation, and contract or service reference. This cannot interpret a contract, certify provider controls, or approve architecture.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize responsibility matrices and identify missing evidence. Humans approve supplier, architecture, access, and exception decisions. Recollect on provider service change, assurance expiry, or material control gap.

### 3. Resilience, exit, and exception package

- **Request and owner:** Operations, business-continuity, security, legal/records, and risk owners provide backup/recovery test evidence, incident records, portability/exit plans, deletion/retention responsibility records, exception register, corrective actions, and independent-review samples.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace sampled critical workload evidence from recovery or exit assumption through test, observed limitation, accountable decision, corrective action, and follow-up. This cannot perform recovery, delete data, accept risk, or prove portability.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may identify aging exceptions and draft scenario questions. Humans approve continuity, retention, risk, and closure decisions; independent reviewers challenge assumptions. Review quarterly and after outage, provider incident, or exit-plan change.
