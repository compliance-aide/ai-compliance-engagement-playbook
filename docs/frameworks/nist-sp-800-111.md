# NIST SP 800-111 end-user storage-encryption engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST text, a cryptographic selection, a device-management action, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through the [NIST SP 800-111 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/111/final).

## Engagement focus

Maintain a storage-encryption engagement record that connects device and media populations, business and data context, accountable owners, authorized protection approach, key or recovery-governance boundaries, enrollment evidence, configuration verification, exceptions, loss or disposal events, and review triggers. Do not treat an enabled setting as proof that all affected data, removable media, recovery paths, and end-of-life actions are appropriately covered.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Endpoint, security, identity, data, privacy, legal, operations, risk, and service owners retain accountable decisions. Endpoint owners maintain approved configuration and lifecycle records; security and key-management owners govern technical safeguards; data and privacy owners define handling context; and incident authorities decide lost-device or exposure response. Independent reviewers sample the path from device or media scope through authorized configuration, verification evidence, exception, recovery governance, and disposal to challenge missing boundaries or stale evidence.

AI may organize authorized device and evidence metadata, identify missing verification or exception fields, compare approved records, draft questions, and prepare non-authoritative workpapers. AI cannot select cryptography, access keys or recovery material, enable or disable encryption, modify devices, determine exposure, initiate a wipe, make notification decisions, accept risk, or claim compliance. Reassess before material device, media, data, key-management, incident, supplier, or lifecycle changes; review exceptions quarterly and complete an annual independent readiness review.
