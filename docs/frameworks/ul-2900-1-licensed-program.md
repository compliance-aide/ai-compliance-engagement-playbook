# UL 2900-1 licensed-program engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not UL standard text, evaluation criteria, or a certification conclusion. Confirm applicable program terms through the [UL 2900 standards overview](https://www.ul.com/insights/medical-device-cybersecurity-standards-and-services).

## Engagement focus

Maintain a connected-product lifecycle record: intended use, product and software boundaries, security ownership, design and release evidence, vulnerability handling, supplier dependencies, testing artifacts, customer communications, and change history.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Product and engineering owners define intended use and release decisions; security and quality teams retain evidence; authorized evaluators apply any licensed criteria; independent reviewers test internal traceability. AI may organize organization-owned lifecycle evidence and draft questions, but cannot access or reproduce licensed criteria, perform product testing, decide safety, or claim certification. Reassess at design and release changes and retain human approvals for every product statement.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [UL 2900 standards overview](https://www.ul.com/insights/medical-device-cybersecurity-standards-and-services), applicable authorized program agreement/materials, and the product's approved lifecycle records; checked 2026-07-31. This is original connected-product lifecycle planning, not UL standard text, evaluation criteria, a test report, or a certification claim. Keep licensed criteria and evaluator communications in their authorized systems.

### 1. Product boundary, intended use, and release-governance package

- **Request and owner:** Product, engineering, security, quality, regulatory, and release owners provide approved intended-use statement, product/software/hardware boundary, version and component inventory, data/interface context, accountable-owner map, release-decision record, and authorized evaluation-engagement record.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected release or product version to its approved boundary, intended-use owner, component/version record, human release decision, and review date. This can show controlled lifecycle traceability; it cannot determine safety, program applicability, or certification readiness.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize organization-owned metadata and flag unowned components or stale release records. Humans define intended use, release scope, evaluator engagement, and product statements. Refresh after product, interface, component, intended-use, supplier, or release-process change.

### 2. Secure design, verification, and vulnerability-response package

- **Request and owner:** Engineering, security, quality, and vulnerability-response owners provide threat/risk decision records, design/change reviews, software provenance/dependency records, test-plan and result references, defect/vulnerability intake and triage records, remediation approvals, and retest evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected design change or vulnerability through source/version context, human triage, approved test or review reference, stated limitation, remediation decision, and retest/follow-up. This can support internal process evidence; it cannot perform product testing, determine exploitability/safety, or apply UL evaluation criteria.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved lifecycle evidence and flag missing links, stale dependencies, or overdue remediation; it cannot test products or decide severity/closure. Humans authorize tests, accept risk, and approve remediation. Recollect after vulnerability disclosure, material design/dependency change, failed verification, or release decision.

### 3. Supplier, customer communication, and post-release package

- **Request and owner:** Supplier-management, product security, support, quality, legal, and communications owners provide supplier dependency/security records, update/support policy, security advisory/customer communication approvals, incident/escalation records, field feedback, change history, and independent-traceability review results.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample a post-release issue or supplier change to a component owner, approved communication/decision record, remediation path, release/update evidence, and closure review. This can show managed lifecycle follow-up; it cannot certify a product, guarantee customer protection, or authorize external statements.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may draft status questions and flag missing ownership or overdue actions. Humans approve supplier decisions, communications, safety conclusions, releases, and claims; independent reviewers test traceability. Review at release and after supplier, vulnerability, incident, customer-impact, or support-lifecycle change.
