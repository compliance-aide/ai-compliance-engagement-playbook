# NIST SP 1300 small-business cybersecurity engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST CSF content, a cybersecurity plan, a compliance claim, or professional advice. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 1300](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/1300/final) and the organization's approved business, security, privacy, legal, financial, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a practical year-round cybersecurity engagement for a small or medium-sized organization that is establishing or improving its risk-management practices. Start with the services, devices, accounts, information, people, suppliers, business priorities, likely disruption scenarios, and existing safeguards that matter most. Translate that understanding into a small, owned backlog of improvements, with simple evidence that shows what was decided, implemented, checked, deferred, or changed.

Keep the engagement proportionate. A concise inventory, access review, backup check, supplier record, incident contact list, training record, and recovery exercise can be more useful than a large unowned control spreadsheet. Link each work item to an accountable person, intended outcome, evidence location, due date, dependencies, assumptions, and review point. Preserve gaps and uncertainty rather than inferring that an undocumented practice is working.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Business leadership establishes priorities, funding, tolerances, and accountable owners. Security or technology owners coordinate the program; system and service owners maintain implementation evidence; workforce members follow approved practices and report concerns; privacy, legal, finance, suppliers, and advisors contribute within their authority. Operators maintain the asset and service view, identity and access records, backup and recovery evidence, supplier information, training records, issues, improvement backlog, decisions, and review history.

Review high-priority actions and material changes at least quarterly, test a small number of realistic recovery or incident scenarios during the year, review access and critical backups on a defined cadence, and perform an annual leadership review of priorities, evidence limitations, remaining risk, investment needs, and next-year work. Reassess promptly after a material incident, new service, supplier change, significant personnel change, acquisition, or business-model change.

Independent reviewers sample a priority from the business need through the implemented safeguard, available evidence, and leadership review, testing whether the record is coherent and current. Auditors evaluate supplied evidence without choosing safeguards, setting priorities, accepting risk, approving spending, operating systems, making legal or financial conclusions, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize supplied records, surface missing ownership or stale evidence, draft task lists and workpapers, summarize approved decisions, and prepare review questions. AI cannot determine adequate protection, set business priorities, select or approve safeguards, accept risk, authorize spending, make legal or financial conclusions, or attest to conformance.
