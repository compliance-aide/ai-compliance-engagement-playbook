# NIST SP 800-152 federal CKMS profile engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST profile content, a federal applicability determination, a procurement approval, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-152](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/152/final) and the organization’s approved federal, security, cryptography, architecture, procurement, legal, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

For a cryptographic key-management system with a federal-use scope, run a controlled implementation-and-operation engagement that connects the documented scope and applicability decisions to architecture, policies, components, key and metadata lifecycle, interfaces, acquisition, installation, configuration, operational evidence, change, incidents, and retirement. Preserve the rationale and approval trail for each profile-related decision. A design record, configuration extract, supplier statement, test result, or log is evidence to assess; none alone establishes federal applicability, correct operation, profile conformance, or compliance.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, federal program or system owner, security, cryptography or platform engineering, architecture, procurement, supplier-management, key-management operations, identity, application and data owners, legal and privacy where relevant, change-management, incident-response, and records-management roles. Operators maintain scope and applicability records, approved profile-decision evidence, architecture and policy baselines, component and configuration inventories, key and metadata lifecycle records, interface and deployment-test evidence, access and audit records, supplier materials, exceptions, incidents, and retirement evidence. Reconcile components, configurations, owners, interfaces, and suppliers quarterly; review access, lifecycle anomalies, deployment or provider changes, exceptions, incidents, and unresolved risks at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material system, provider, cryptographic, federal, or revision changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples the trace from profile decision through operation and review; auditors test supplied evidence without operating keys, changing configuration, authorizing procurement or deployment, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied scope, decision, implementation, configuration, supplier, and review evidence, identify stale decisions or missing ownership, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot determine federal applicability or profile conformance, design or operate a CKMS, access or derive keys, change configuration, authorize procurement or deployment, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.
