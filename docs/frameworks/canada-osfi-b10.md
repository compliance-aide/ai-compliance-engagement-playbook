# Canada OSFI Guideline B-10 — third-party risk engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not OSFI content or a supplier-risk conclusion. Confirm current guidance at the [OSFI third-party risk record](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/guidance/guidance-library/third-party-risk-management-guideline).

## Engagement focus

Maintain vendor inventory, criticality decisions, due-diligence evidence, contractual governance, subcontractor visibility, concentration and exit considerations, continuity evidence, monitoring, exceptions, and remediation history.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Business, procurement, risk, legal, and technology owners make supplier and risk decisions; independent reviewers challenge the evidence. AI organizes approved vendor artifacts and detects stale records, but cannot select suppliers, approve contracts, or accept risk. Review critical suppliers quarterly and annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the current [OSFI third-party risk record](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/guidance/guidance-library/third-party-risk-management-guideline); checked 2026-07-31. This is original planning language, not a reproduction of OSFI material, legal advice, a supervisory determination, or a conclusion that a third-party program is adequate. Legal and accountable business owners determine regulated scope, contract terms, notification duties, and risk acceptance.

### 1. Supplier scope, criticality, and approval record

- **Request and owner:** Current service-provider inventory, service and data-flow descriptions, materiality/criticality method and outcomes, dependency and concentration inputs, due-diligence results, accountable owners, and approval records from procurement, business, technology, risk, and legal owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a human-selected provider from the inventory through service dependency, criticality rationale, due-diligence source, accountable owner, and approval date. This can support a transparent third-party planning record; it cannot determine regulatory materiality, approve a supplier, or establish that all dependencies have been identified.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile authorized supplier metadata and identify absent owners, review dates, or dependency records. Humans approve criticality, onboarding, and residual-risk decisions. Refresh before onboarding a material provider and after a material service, subcontractor, concentration, ownership, or jurisdiction change.

### 2. Contract, resilience, and ongoing-monitoring evidence

- **Request and owner:** Executed-contract governance extracts, service-level and incident/escalation arrangements, subcontractor disclosures, continuity/exit artifacts, performance and control-monitoring results, and issue/escalation records from legal, vendor-management, service, and security owners.
- **Validate and limit:** For a selected critical provider, trace a contractual or operational expectation to evidence of monitoring, an identified gap or result, the responsible owner, and a dated response. This can support evidence traceability for the selected relationship; it cannot interpret enforceability, prove service resilience, or approve a contract variation.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index authorized artifacts and flag stale attestations or missed monitoring milestones; it may not negotiate terms, direct a provider, or make legal conclusions. Recollect after a significant service degradation, audit result, security event, or contract/subcontractor change.

### 3. Exception, exit, and independent-challenge workpaper

- **Request and owner:** Exception and risk-treatment log, concentration analysis, exit/transition plans and exercises, management decisions, remediation/retest evidence, and independent review outputs from risk, business-continuity, procurement, and independent-review owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected exception or exit dependency to approval authority, mitigation, expiry/review date, test or exercise evidence, and retest status. This can support accountable oversight; it cannot accept risk, certify exit readiness, or replace an independent review.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may assemble a source-linked evidence index and flag approaching expiries. Authorized humans approve exceptions, investment, exit activation, and external communications. Review critical-provider records quarterly, challenge the program annually, and refresh after a material finding or provider change.
