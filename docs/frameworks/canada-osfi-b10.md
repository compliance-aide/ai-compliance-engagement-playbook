# Canada OSFI Guideline B-10 — third-party risk engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not OSFI content, legal advice, supplier approval or a supervisory conclusion.

## Source and applicability

Use the current [B-10 guideline](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/guidance/guidance-library/third-party-risk-management-guideline) and [foreign-branch clarification](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/guidance/guidance-library/consequential-amendments-guidelines-b-10-b-13-related-foreign-branches). Checked 2026-09-04. OSFI expects federally regulated financial institutions to manage all third-party arrangements proportionately to risk and criticality while retaining accountability. Do not limit intake to critical outsourced IT vendors: strategic and smaller arrangements also need a recorded disposition. The branch clarification addresses Canadian operations subject to applicable requirements and legal obligations; legal/compliance owners confirm the entity-specific position.

B-10 recognizes non-negotiable or absent contracts without removing the need to manage risk. An inability to change terms is therefore a decision input, not an exemption. Legal and compliance owners identify applicable records, location, access and notification requirements; this guide does not infer them from a vendor's country.

## Engagement focus

Connect each arrangement to the business operation it supports, accountable owners, risk and criticality rationale, due diligence, contractual or other safeguards, subcontractor dependencies, monitoring and a workable response to disruption or exit. Review aggregate dependencies as well as individual providers.

## Roles

Business owners own the service need and operating relationship. Procurement maintains arrangement and contract records. Risk owners challenge classification, concentration and treatment. Security, privacy, continuity and records specialists review relevant evidence. Legal approves interpretations and contract terms; authorized management makes supplier, exception and investment decisions. Independent reviewers challenge the program. AI reconciles permitted records and drafts questions; it cannot select a supplier, accept risk, sign contracts or activate an exit.

## Before starting

Follow the [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md). Name the regulated entity, arrangement, owner, period, decision and permitted evidence location. Create work items with inputs, outputs and exit checks. Obtain the approved risk framework, inventory, classification method, applicable direction and authority roster. Missing records remain named dependencies; do not invent a low-risk rating to move work forward. Keep contracts and confidential provider evidence in approved systems, not this repository.

## Ordered workflow

| Step | Action and owner | Output and exit check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Reconcile intake | Procurement and business owners compare purchasing, service, technology and strategic-arrangement records to the third-party inventory. Identify duplicates, omissions and shared providers. | Arrangement inventory with owner and service dependencies; no unsupported exclusion because spend is low. |
| 2. Assess risk and criticality | Business/risk owners apply the approved method, considering operational impact and relevant dependencies. Document the proportionate diligence and oversight needed. | Approved rationale and review plan; AI may prepare inputs but does not assign the final rating. |
| 3. Perform due diligence | Assigned specialists gather evidence appropriate to the arrangement, scope and period. Risk owner examines weaknesses, subcontractors and concentration across arrangements. | Evidence-linked diligence record, unresolved questions and decision options. One provider report does not settle aggregate dependence. |
| 4. Establish safeguards | Legal, procurement and service owners compare required safeguards with agreed terms and operational arrangements. Where terms cannot be negotiated, record limitations and proposed alternative risk treatment. | Exact contract/mitigation decision packet. Draft terms or desired rights are not executed safeguards. |
| 5. Prepare resilience and exit | Business/continuity owners establish proportionate disruption and transition arrangements, dependencies, resources and evidence of feasibility. | Approved plans or documented rationale for reduced treatment; assumptions and untested dependencies remain visible. |
| 6. Make the onboarding decision | Coordinator presents diligence, safeguards, unresolved risks and conditions to the authorized management owner. Record and read back the actual decision. | Separate prepared, approved and operational states. Approval conditions are assigned before reliance is claimed. |
| 7. Monitor and remedy | Service owners compare actual performance and control evidence with expectations. Route incidents, changes and threshold breaches to risk/specialist owners; assign correction and retest. | Monitoring/finding/action trail with escalation dates. Ticket closure alone does not prove recovery or effective correction. |
| 8. Renew, change or exit | Owners reassess changed services, ownership, subcontractors, geography and concentration before the next decision. Execute exit only under applicable approval, then verify transition and residual obligations. | Recorded decision, verified outcome and continuing records/data obligations with owners. |

## Evidence and test plan

These original examples guide workpapers; the institution's approved methodology determines coverage and intensity. Preserve the complete evidence corpus and do not trim material sent to an assessment scorer.

| Package and owner | Verification task | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inventory and classification — procurement/risk | Trace an arrangement from business dependency to classification rationale, diligence and approval; reconcile omissions against all available intake records. | Selected traces cannot prove full inventory coverage. Record missing feeds and unresolved arrangements. |
| Contract and operation — legal/service owner | Compare an agreed expectation with actual monitoring evidence, incident contacts and recorded action on a breach. | Contract wording alone does not establish enforceability or operational performance. |
| Subcontractors and concentration — risk/provider owner | Connect disclosed dependencies across arrangements and compare failure assumptions with actual alternatives. | Different prime suppliers can share one underlying dependency; unavailable disclosure remains uncertainty. |
| Continuity and exit — business/continuity owners | Walk an approved disruption or transition exercise through resources, data access, dependencies, results and corrective work. | A plan or simulated result is not actual recovery. Do not terminate service or incur costs to test it without authorization. |
| Exceptions and remediation — risk/reviewer | Trace a limitation to its decision authority, mitigation, review date and verified retest or open status. | Accepted risk does not establish that a failed safeguard now works. |

Record evidence source, collector, date, covered period, arrangement, expected/observed result, limitation, reviewer and follow-up. Identify selected test populations and untested coverage separately from the full inventory.

## Failure branches and decisions

- **Unrecorded arrangement:** add an intake gap with a business owner and seek classification; do not silently exclude it.
- **Missing or stale diligence:** identify the exact scope/period needed and the accountable request owner. Leave the decision limitation visible.
- **Non-negotiable terms:** prepare the limitation and alternatives for legal/risk review. Do not fabricate contractual rights or presume an exemption.
- **Concentrated dependency or infeasible exit:** preserve the exposure, options and management decision question; a second reseller is not automatically diversification.
- **Significant disruption:** route immediately to the institution's incident and compliance owners to determine obligations and authorized communications. Do not substitute an invented reporting timer or send regulatory notices without authorization.
- **Failed test or overdue condition:** retain the adverse result, escalate to its decision owner and obtain a retest or explicit next decision.
- **Interrupted update or uncertain write:** save the last completed step and versions; read back the destination before retrying. Do not infer contract execution or provider notification from a click.

## Cadence and renewal

Use the approved risk-based oversight schedule, contract dates and actual conditions. Any quarterly or annual internal review is an owner-selected practice unless a specific applicable requirement establishes it; the earlier guide's blanket cadence is not a universal B-10 rule. Reassess after material arrangement changes, incidents, adverse diligence and changing concentration. Record who monitors each trigger.

## Completion and handoff

A decision packet contains the scoped inventory record, approved risk rationale, diligence evidence, safeguards and limitations, proportionate resilience/exit evidence, open actions, exact decision questions and monitoring owners. Mark omissions. Approval, contract execution and actual exit are separate milestones requiring their own authority and readback. Handoff specifies next action, owner, due date and dependency.
