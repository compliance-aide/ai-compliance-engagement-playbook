# NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NAIC model-law text, state law advice, or an enforcement conclusion. Confirm adoption and local duties through the [NAIC cybersecurity overview](https://content.naic.org/insurance-topics/cybersecurity) and the insurer's domiciliary-state requirements.

## Engagement focus

Maintain a year-round insurer record of information-security governance, data and system context, risk assessment, supplier oversight, cybersecurity-event handling, investigations, notices, decisions, and remediation.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Licensed-entity leadership determines applicable state obligations; security and privacy owners maintain evidence; legal counsel directs notice decisions; independent reviewers test the engagement record. AI organizes approved artifacts, tracks state-specific questions and deadlines, and prepares review packets, but cannot determine legal applicability, make a notification decision, or communicate with regulators or affected persons. Recheck state adoption and organizational scope periodically and obtain human approval for all incident communications.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [NAIC cybersecurity overview](https://content.naic.org/insurance-topics/cybersecurity), the enacted law and regulator materials for each in-scope licensed entity, and qualified state insurance counsel; checked 2026-07-31. This original planning aid does not determine adoption, insurance-license scope, event notice, or a legal conclusion.

### 1. Entity, data, system, and risk context

- **Request and owner:** Domicile and licensed-entity register, state applicability analysis, information-system and nonpublic-information inventory, accountable security roles, approved risk-assessment inputs, and remediation register from legal, security, and business owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected system or information set to licensed entity, owner, inventory record, risk input, and approved follow-up. This supports an evidence trail; it cannot decide state applicability or the sufficiency of an assessment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved inventories and flag missing owners. Legal and accountable leaders decide applicability and risk disposition. Refresh for a new entity, system, material data use, acquisition, or state-law change.

### 2. Safeguards, provider, and workforce operation

- **Request and owner:** Access-review, security-monitoring, vulnerability/remediation, change, training, service-provider due-diligence, contract/instruction, and oversight records from security, procurement, and business owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected control operation or provider to the approved owner, dated artifact, exception, corrective action, and retest. This supports evidence that a declared process generated records; it cannot certify safeguard effectiveness or contract adequacy.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index redacted evidence and flag overdue reviews; it may not approve a supplier or accept a security exception. Refresh after a provider, control, incident, or material environment change.

### 3. Event, decision, and annual governance record

- **Request and owner:** Event timeline, investigation inputs, legal decision log, approved communications, regulator correspondence where applicable, post-event remediation, annual report/review record, and source-change watch from incident, legal, and leadership owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected event or exception to facts, human authority, approved communication, corrective action, and closure evidence. This supports accountability; it cannot determine notice, make a filing, or conclude compliance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may assemble a redacted review packet and surface aging actions. Humans approve communications, filings, risk acceptance, and closure. Revisit annually and after an event, regulator action, or law change.
