# IEC TR 60601-4-5:2021 medical-device security engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not IEC technical-report text, device testing criteria, clinical advice, or a conformity claim. Confirm the current publication through the [IEC TR 60601-4-5:2021 record](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/64703).

## Engagement focus

Maintain an evidence-backed security lifecycle for medical electrical equipment, medical electrical systems, and medical-device software that exchange data or operate in medical IT networks. Keep intended use, interfaces, deployment environment, security ownership, supplier dependencies, vulnerability handling, update decisions, operational incidents, and end-of-support decisions traceable to each product version.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Product and clinical-safety owners define intended use and approve safety-related decisions; engineering keeps version, test, and change evidence; security coordinates vulnerability and incident work; healthcare delivery partners document their operating environment; independent reviewers assess evidence traceability. AI may organize approved records, detect missing provenance, and draft review questions, but cannot evaluate clinical safety, test a device, authorize a release, alter a clinical environment, or make a conformance claim. Reassess at material product, interface, supplier, vulnerability, and service changes; require accountable human approval before a customer-facing, regulatory, or safety statement.
