# COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not COSO framework content, a controls evaluation, or an effectiveness conclusion. Confirm current materials and permitted use through the [COSO internal-control guidance page](https://www.coso.org/internal-control).

## Engagement focus

Maintain a traceable record of objectives, accountable owners, control activities, evidence of operation, change impacts, monitoring, deficiencies, remediation, and governance decisions across the reporting period.

## Roles

Management owns design and operation; process owners retain evidence; internal audit or independent reviewers evaluate the evidence trail; governance bodies decide remediation and risk matters. AI maps approved evidence to objectives, identifies stale ownership or gaps, and prepares questions, but cannot evaluate effectiveness, accept risk, or issue an audit conclusion. Perform periodic monitoring, preserve review results, and retain human judgments.

## Source and applicability

The [COSO source page](https://www.coso.org/internal-control) identifies the 2013 framework and distinguishes supplemental sustainability, automation and 2026 generative-AI guidance. Select supplements from the actual engagement scope; a supplement is not automatically a replacement edition. Record publication, version, access rights, intended use and the human reviewer responsible for interpretation. Obtain authorized framework access before claiming complete principle coverage; this guide does not reproduce the licensed framework or its assessment criteria.

Define whether the engagement addresses operations, reporting, compliance or a documented combination. Management approves entity boundaries, processes, reporting period and intended conclusion. Do not silently reduce a broad internal-control engagement to financial reporting, cybersecurity or an inventory of policies.

## Before starting

Follow the [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md). Create a scope record and one work item per approved objective/control evaluation unit. Each needs owner, source reference, process/system boundary, period, expected operation, evidence location and reviewer. Keep proprietary framework material and confidential transactions in authorized systems. AI can prepare source-linked observations and questions; accountable humans interpret the framework and approve evaluations.

## Ordered workflow

| Step | Action and accountable owner | Output and exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Fix the evaluation boundary | Management identifies objectives, entities, processes, time period and governing assessment criteria. | Approved scope, source versions and intended reporting audience; unresolved boundaries have owners. |
| 2. Map objectives and risks | Process owners connect objectives to risk scenarios and approved control responses. | Complete scoped register, including shared services and dependencies; unmapped objectives remain visible. |
| 3. Walk through design | Owner and reviewer trace a process from initiation through information, action, escalation and oversight. | Documented trigger, performer, frequency, evidence, reviewer and exception route; policy existence alone is insufficient. |
| 4. Establish evidence populations | Owners identify all expected operations in the period and reconcile evidence sources to that population. | Population totals, provenance, changes and missing records; reviewer approves any test selection and its limits. |
| 5. Test operation | Authorized reviewers compare expected execution to dated evidence and investigate contradictions. | Workpapers separating design observations, actual operation, evidence gaps and period coverage; AI uses standard runbook results, not an effectiveness opinion. |
| 6. Assess interactions and deficiencies | Management and independent reviewers consider shared dependencies, linked failures and the governing evaluation criteria. | Human-documented aggregation and severity rationale; individual successful tests do not establish overall effectiveness. |
| 7. Remediate and retest | Owners implement authorized changes; reviewers test the corrected design and subsequent operation. | Retest evidence plus remaining historical-period limitations; a new control does not rewrite earlier failures. |
| 8. Approve and renew | Management and oversight review unresolved issues, monitoring and the proposed representation. | Named decision, supported scope and limitations, next review triggers and accountable handoff. |

## Failure branches and decisions

- **No reliable population:** record what cannot be reconciled and request source-owner correction. Do not call an available subset the full period or score missing records as successful operations.
- **Documented control never performed:** separate design documentation from operation evidence and route the execution gap to the owner.
- **Reviewer approved their own work:** record the actual role conflict and ask the independent reviewer to evaluate the intended control design; do not invent independence from an approval timestamp.
- **Several controls rely on one failed report:** trace the common report's completeness, accuracy and affected uses; retain linked observations for human aggregation instead of independently closing each item.
- **Control changed mid-period:** retain both versions and their effective dates; test the relevant periods separately and state the resulting limits.
- **Severity or applicability disputed:** preserve both rationales with sources and route to the accountable reviewer. Neither majority vote nor AI confidence resolves the framework judgment.

### 1. Objectives, scope, and accountability record

- **Request and owner:** Approved objectives, reporting/process scope, entity and system boundaries, responsibility map, risk inputs, policy/version register, and governance approvals from management, process, finance, risk, and control owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected objective to its process boundary, accountable owner, approved control intent in original language, and dated governance decision. This supports an accountable design record; it cannot establish control design adequacy or effective operation.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize approved inventories and identify unowned or inconsistent metadata. Human management approves objectives, scope, and risk decisions. Refresh after an objective, reporting process, system, ownership, policy, or material risk change.

### 2. Control-operation and change-evidence trace

- **Request and owner:** Human-approved process/control descriptions, evidence-of-operation index, access/change records where applicable, reconciliation or review outputs, exception records, and remediation/retest evidence from process and system owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Independently follow a selected control activity through the defined period to source evidence, reviewer, exception, and corrective action while recording sampling limits and contradictory evidence. This can support a bounded traceability review; it cannot prove population-wide effectiveness or prevent misstatement/fraud.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may create a read-only evidence index and flag missing dates, owners, or links; it cannot perform a control, alter records, judge effectiveness, or close remediation. Humans decide exceptions and remediation. Refresh after a process/system change, control failure, significant deficiency, or period rollover.

### 3. Monitoring, deficiency, and governance-decision record

- **Request and owner:** Monitoring results, internal-audit or independent-review workpapers, deficiency register, management responses, risk-acceptance approvals, and oversight minutes from management and independent reviewers.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected deficiency from detection through severity rationale, accountable decision, remediation target, retest, and governance oversight. This supports transparent issue governance; it cannot replace internal audit judgment or issue an assurance opinion.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag overdue actions and assemble a source-linked challenge packet. Humans approve deficiency treatment, risk acceptance, external representations, and closure. Review during management monitoring and at least annually.


## Cadence and renewal

Set monitoring and testing frequency from approved risk, control frequency and reporting commitments. Annual review is a planning convention, not a universal COSO deadline. Reopen affected work for acquisitions, system migrations, outsourced processes, objective changes, fraud indicators, staff turnover or material deficiencies. Preserve period-specific evidence and decisions through the approved retention process.

## Completion and handoff

Provide the scope and source register, objective/risk/control inventory, population reconciliation, workpapers, dependency analysis, deficiency decisions and remediation evidence. Distinguish prepared, executed, reviewed and approved work. Name the next action, owner and evidence needed without requiring earlier chat. A complete index or passing sample cannot by itself support an effectiveness conclusion; record who may issue that conclusion and its exact scope.
