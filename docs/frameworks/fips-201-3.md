# FIPS 201-3 personal identity verification engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not FIPS content, identity-proofing instructions, credential issuance authority, an authorization decision, or a compliance claim. Confirm current requirements and updates through [FIPS 201-3](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/201-3/final) and the organization’s approved identity, security, legal, privacy, personnel, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Operate a governed lifecycle for in-scope PIV identities, credentials, authenticators, relying systems, credential issuers, and physical or logical access uses. Maintain traceability from identity sponsorship and proofing through registration, issuance, activation, use, access integration, lifecycle events, revocation, and records retention. An identity record, credential status, successful login, or issuer statement is evidence to assess; it does not prove an identity was correctly proofed, access is appropriate, an authenticator is valid, or a system is compliant.

## Source and applicability

The [NIST publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/201-3/final), read 2026-09-04, identifies the January 2022 standard covering federal employee/contractor PIV systems, proofing, interoperability and issuer processes. Its March 2022 potential-update note explicitly does not establish official changes. Obtain the full standard, applicable companion publications and agency policy before assessing requirements. Those details remain unverified here. Credential authentication and permission to enter a facility or use an application are distinct decisions.

## Roles

Sponsors/personnel owners establish authorized lifecycle events. Proofing and issuing authorities make identity and credential decisions. Identity, facilities and relying-system owners manage their approved integrations. Privacy and records owners govern sensitive evidence. Independent reviewers challenge traceability. AI organizes authorized metadata; it cannot proof identities, issue or revoke credentials, grant access, make personnel decisions or attest to compliance.

## Before starting

Record agency, workforce scope, issuer, credential types, physical/logical relying systems, lifecycle processes and authoritative owners. Identify approved evidence access and retention restrictions. Use the [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md) with synthetic QA identities and authorized test credentials handled by qualified operators; do not collect biometrics, PINs or private keys in this repository.

## Ordered workflow

1. **Reconcile the lifecycle population.** Compare sponsor/personnel, identity-account and credential inventories across the approved scope. Output a population register with missing and conflicting identifiers. A card inventory alone does not establish active sponsorship or complete workforce coverage.
2. **Trace proofing and issuance evidence.** Link each scoped process to authorized roles, required decision references and distinct registration, issuance and activation records. Output a process/evidence map. Do not infer completed proofing from a later successful login.
3. **Map relying systems.** Record each physical and logical access integration, authentication mechanism, trust/status dependencies and separate authorization policy. Output an integration matrix. One working application does not prove every door reader or other application enforces the same rules.
4. **Evaluate approved QA scenarios.** Define expected results for valid, unauthorized, expired/status-invalid and unavailable-dependency cases under the applicable policy. Observe authentication and access decisions separately. Output scenario-specific records with untested branches; do not use real employee credentials for convenience.
5. **Trace changes, loss and departure.** Link approved lifecycle events to issuer actions and relying-system updates, including downstream dependencies. Record event, action and observation times. Output reconciliation gaps; a revocation request or badge return is not proof that all relevant access ended.
6. **Check exceptions and recovery.** Identify temporary access, replacement and fallback routes with authority, scope, conditions and expiry. Observe approved QA handling without granting access. Output exception results; a fallback must not silently bypass the defined authorization requirement.
7. **Retest and review.** Assign failures an owner and closure criterion, preserve original evidence and verify corrections across affected integrations. Output a draft review packet, not an assertion that an identity is genuine or an issuer is compliant.

## Evidence and test plan

### Sponsorship, proofing and credential records

Sponsor, personnel, identity and issuer owners provide the complete scoped population, non-sensitive identity references, sponsorship decisions and separate proofing/registration/issuance/activation records. Reconcile identifiers across sources and preserve duplicate, missing and contradictory associations. An issued credential does not retroactively prove that every prerequisite was met.

Record the responsible authority, decision date and permitted evidence reference for each lifecycle step. Review metadata within approved privacy boundaries rather than copying identity documents or biometrics into workpapers. If a protected record cannot be inspected, preserve the access limitation and the authorized review owner instead of marking its content verified.

### Relying systems and access decisions

Identity, application and facilities owners provide the complete physical/logical integration inventory, authentication mechanisms, trust/status dependencies and authorization policies. Link each integration to its actual configuration and observation period. Keep exceptions and offline or cached behavior visible with the applicable policy; do not assume every system performs the same online status check.

Under approved QA authority, qualified operators provide results for each defined access scenario. Record the authenticated identity/credential reference, status evidence, authorization decision and actual access outcome separately. A successful credential read is not proof of authentication or permission. Preserve untested facilities, applications and failure branches instead of extrapolating one successful login across the population.

### Lifecycle propagation, recovery and review

Issuer, personnel, facilities and incident owners provide loss, departure, replacement, suspension/revocation and exception references as applicable to the agency process. Trace each authorized event to actual issuer action and every affected relying-system obligation. Separate event time, request time, processing time and observed enforcement; a job completion receipt does not establish downstream denial.

For replacement or temporary access, verify approved scope, responsible owner, expiry and removal evidence. Reconcile outstanding old credentials, accounts and access paths without assuming a new credential automatically closes them. Preserve failures and assign retests to qualified owners. Distinguish an identity-data correction from access-policy approval, and do not infer a personnel decision from a technical status change.

## Failure branches and decisions

Unavailable lifecycle evidence is `not_tested`; conflicting sponsorship or credential records are `inconclusive`. A relying system permitting access after the policy-required termination point is `not_supported` even if the issuer action succeeded. Record the failed integration separately from credential status.

Fictional desk case: an authorized QA departure event disables the logical-access account, but a test door system continues accepting the credential after the defined deadline. Logical-access removal is `supported`; the physical-access removal criterion is `not_supported`. The agent records both outcomes and escalates to the facilities owner without changing a real credential or door configuration.

## Cadence and renewal

Use agency and issuer requirements for lifecycle deadlines and scheduled reviews; do not invent quarterly checks or annual FIPS renewal. Reassess after workforce, issuer, credential, integration, trust or source changes and consequential failures. Name owners for unresolved lifecycle propagation and evidence gaps.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver the complete population and identifier reconciliation, lifecycle decision references, relying-system matrix, QA observations, propagation timeline and open exceptions. Identify missing proofing evidence, untested integrations, unclear policy requirements and incomplete downstream actions. Assign each gap an owner and next step while preserving privacy restrictions.

Require independent source, skeptical and rights review before publication. Authorized officials retain proofing, issuance, credential lifecycle, personnel and access decisions. This draft has not established any person's identity, changed a real credential or demonstrated agency-wide PIV compliance.
