# FIPS 201-3 personal identity verification engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not FIPS content, identity-proofing instructions, credential issuance authority, an authorization decision, or a compliance claim. Confirm current requirements and updates through [FIPS 201-3](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/201-3/final) and the organization’s approved identity, security, legal, privacy, personnel, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Operate a governed lifecycle for in-scope PIV identities, credentials, authenticators, relying systems, credential issuers, and physical or logical access uses. Maintain traceability from identity sponsorship and proofing through registration, issuance, activation, use, access integration, lifecycle events, revocation, and records retention. An identity record, credential status, successful login, or issuer statement is evidence to assess; it does not prove an identity was correctly proofed, access is appropriate, an authenticator is valid, or a system is compliant.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, identity and access management, security, human resources or sponsor function, facilities where relevant, privacy, legal, credential issuer liaison, relying-system owners, procurement, supplier-management, incident-response, and records-management roles. Operators maintain in-scope population and sponsor records, proofing and registration evidence, credential lifecycle records, issuer and relying-system approvals, access-integration inventory, authentication logs and anomalies, revocation and recovery records, exceptions, and supplier assurances. Reconcile active identities, credentials, and relying-system connections quarterly; review lifecycle events, privileged access, disabled or expired credentials, exceptions, issuer changes, and unresolved findings at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material workforce, system, credential, or policy changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples identity-to-credential-to-access traceability; auditors test evidence without proofing an identity, issuing or revoking credentials, granting access, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied lifecycle, access, issuer, and review evidence, identify missing ownership or stale reviews, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot proof an identity, issue, activate, use, suspend, or revoke a credential, grant access, make personnel decisions, approve an exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.
