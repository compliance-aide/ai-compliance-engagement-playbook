# Texas Cybersecurity Framework engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not Texas framework content, an assessment, a maturity rating, or a compliance claim. Confirm current scope and program context through the [Texas Department of Information Resources framework page](https://dir.texas.gov/information-security/security-policy-and-planning/texas-cybersecurity-framework).

## Engagement focus

Maintain a cybersecurity engagement record that connects organization scope, accountable leaders, asset and service inventories, risk decisions, operating evidence, incident and recovery evidence, training records, supplier dependencies, remediation, and maturity-review inputs. Preserve distinctions among voluntary framework use, agency-specific obligations, independent assessment, and an organization’s own risk decisions.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Executive sponsors and information-security owners set the engagement scope; technology and operations teams preserve evidence; risk and legal owners determine obligations; independent reviewers challenge traceability and assessment readiness. AI may organize approved records, identify evidence gaps, and draft questions, but cannot conduct or score an assessment, determine maturity, approve risk, make a government submission, direct production changes, or claim compliance. Review at least annually and after material system, service, supplier, incident, or organizational changes.
