# IEC 62443-2-1:2024 IACS asset-owner security — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not IEC text, an operational authorization or an industrial-security conclusion.

## Source and applicability

The [IEC publication record](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/62883), checked 2026-09-04, describes security-program policies and procedures for asset owners, including operators, of industrial automation and control systems in operation. It recognizes legacy technical limitations and the possibility of compensating measures. It also identifies a revised structure and maturity model in the 2024 edition. These points do not establish conformity of a particular system or authorize an exception.

Full licensed requirements, maturity evaluation and applicable sector obligations remain unverified. Obtain authorized material and confirm the contracted edition before detailed assessment. Do not place licensed IEC text in AI prompts without explicit permission. A publisher stability date is not an asset-owner recertification deadline.

## Engagement focus

Build an evidence-backed security program for the approved operating boundary. Connect policies to actual owners, operating procedures, maintenance constraints, risk decisions and tested outcomes. Distinguish program maturity from component capability and from any separately assessed system security level.

## Roles

The asset owner sponsors the program and owns risk decisions. Operations and safety leaders approve permitted operational activity and safe conditions. Security, engineering, maintenance and supplier owners supply evidence and implement authorized actions. An independent reviewer challenges completeness and effectiveness. AI may organize approved records and draft questions; it cannot operate industrial systems, initiate scans, change configurations, approve compensating measures, accept risk or declare safe operation.

## Before starting

Record sites, processes, assets, connections, support arrangements, critical services and accountable owners. Identify operating modes, maintenance windows, safety dependencies and permitted evidence sources. Default to existing approved records. Even apparently read-only discovery can affect fragile equipment; no live probing follows merely from this guide. Any later technical activity requires the authorized owner, exact method, environment, stop criteria and recovery plan.

## Ordered workflow

1. **Define the operating boundary.** Reconcile all scoped assets, networks, remote access paths, vendors and interfaces with operations. Record exclusions and unknown devices explicitly. An incomplete inventory is a coverage gap, not proof that an unlisted connection is absent.
2. **Assign program ownership.** Link each applicable licensed requirement to its policy, procedure, responsible role and evidence source within the authorized workspace. Record the applicable version and assessment method. Do not infer 2024 maturity from an old-edition checklist.
3. **Assess operational constraints and risk.** Document threats, consequences, legacy limitations and current controls with qualified safety/security input. Separate inability to patch from a decision about acceptable risk. Record proposed compensating measures, their assumptions, approval owner and review triggers.
4. **Plan controlled implementation.** Prioritize approved gaps, assign owners and define success evidence. Route changes through the operating site's controls, including maintenance coordination and recovery preparation. A security recommendation does not authorize a plant change.
5. **Verify operation.** Inspect approved access, maintenance, configuration, monitoring and supplier records across the full scoped population. Compare actual practice with the approved procedure. Use isolated, representative test environments only when authorized; document differences from operating equipment and do not extrapolate untested behavior.
6. **Exercise response and recovery safely.** Trace detection, escalation, operator coordination and recovery responsibilities using approved scenarios. Distinguish a tabletop discussion, a backup job and a demonstrated restore. Record untested dependencies and failed communication paths. Operations/safety authorities control actual incident actions.
7. **Review and improve.** Preserve findings, compensating-measure decisions, overdue actions and retest evidence. Reassess changed assets, new connections, support expiry and incident lessons. The authorized owner decides residual risk and any external representation; a closed work ticket does not prove program effectiveness.

## Evidence and test plan

Maintain complete scoped accounting for assets, connections, procedures, exceptions, changes, suppliers and findings. Preserve all evidence supplied for assessment without silent trimming or sampling. Document actual testing coverage separately from inventory completeness.

### 1. IACS asset boundary and ownership package

- **Request and owner:** Asset, operations, safety, and security owners provide the approved IACS inventory, zone/conduit or equivalent boundary decisions where used, criticality, asset/accountable owner, connection map references, and stated exclusions.
- **Validate and limit:** Account for all scoped assets and connections and trace each to its operational owner, security owner, boundary record, and approved scope. This tests whether the engagement boundary is reviewable; it cannot establish a safe configuration or complete discovery.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may correlate approved inventories and identify missing ownership or stale maps. Humans approve scope and safety/operational decisions. Refresh before a new connection, ownership change, major modernization, or boundary change.

### 2. Security-program and risk-treatment package

- **Request and owner:** Program and risk owners provide approved policies/procedures, risk and threat records, compensating-measure rationale, exception records, assigned treatment actions, and authorized residual-risk decisions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace each scoped material risk and exception from owner and evidence through mitigation, decision authority, and follow-up. This supports accountable documentation; it cannot validate a compensating measure, accept risk, or assess safe operation.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index authorized workpapers and flag expired exceptions. It must not access control systems, choose safeguards, approve a measure, or accept risk. Reopen after a vulnerability, incident, audit observation, or change in operational conditions.

### 3. Operations, incident, and improvement package

- **Request and owner:** Operations, incident-response, supplier, and maintenance owners provide access/change records, maintenance windows, incident and recovery records, supplier interfaces, exercise or review results, corrective actions, and closure decisions.
- **Validate and limit:** Account for all scoped changes, incidents and corrective actions and inspect each relevant record for owner, operational authorization, evidence trail, safety consideration, and follow-up. This tests the recorded coverage; it cannot prove continuous monitoring, system resilience, or recovery success.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a cross-record timeline and surface overdue actions without operating IACS. Humans authorize operational changes, incident actions, closures, and communications. Review at approved risk-based intervals and after a material incident, supplier change, or failed exercise.


## Failure branches and decisions

- Unknown asset or remote connection: obtain owner and boundary clarification before dependent conclusions or technical activity.
- Unsupported equipment cannot implement a recommended feature: preserve the limitation and seek a qualified compensating-measure decision; do not automatically mark the requirement satisfied or irrelevant.
- Safety and security actions conflict: stop the proposed change and escalate to both accountable authorities; AI cannot choose the operational tradeoff.
- Supplier assurance lacks site-specific evidence: record the missing evidence and customer-side duties; a vendor certificate does not establish the operating site's program.
- Recovery test fails: retain the failure and affected dependencies, assign corrective action and retest under approval.

Use [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md) statuses for factual assertions: supported, not_supported, inconclusive, not_applicable and not_tested. They are not maturity ratings, security levels or risk-acceptance decisions.

## Cadence and renewal

Set reviews according to operational risk, governing requirements and approved procedures. Trigger review after incidents, changes, supplier transitions, expired exceptions and new vulnerability information. Do not invent an annual certification obligation or postpone an urgent operational concern until a calendar review.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver the reconciled boundary, program/evidence references, legacy limitations, authorized risk decisions, test coverage, unresolved findings and next actions with owners and dates. Full source, independent skeptical and rights reviews remain pending for this draft. No live industrial testing or conformity conclusion is implied.

Fictional author desk case: an obsolete controller cannot be patched, and an approved isolation measure is claimed to compensate. The connection inventory reveals an unreviewed vendor remote-access route. The isolation claim is unsupported until its real boundary and effectiveness are resolved; the controller must not be probed or altered by the agent. No actual industrial action occurred.
