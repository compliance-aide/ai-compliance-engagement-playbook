# NIST SP 800-226 differential-privacy evaluation engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST differential-privacy content, a mathematical proof, a privacy guarantee, an authorization to release data, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-226](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/226/final) and the organization's approved privacy, security, legal, research, statistical, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round engagement for evaluating privacy claims associated with in-scope differentially private data products, analytics, models, and releases. Connect each use case to the data population, purpose, collection and transformation boundaries, implementation assumptions, parameters, threat considerations, utility expectations, validation evidence, release channel, recipients, and lifecycle decisions. Keep the mathematical privacy model connected to the real system and operating context in which data is processed or disclosed.

Treat a privacy parameter, proof, notebook, code review, test result, model card, dataset description, or vendor statement as evidence to assess—not by itself a complete representation of privacy risk or a permission to share information. Record assumptions, data lineage, implementation limits, composition and reuse considerations, utility tradeoffs, changes, incidents, complaints, and release decisions. Separate technical analysis from the human legal, policy, ethics, and risk choices that govern whether a use or release is acceptable.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Privacy, data governance, security, legal, research, statistics, product, engineering, business, records, and executive owners establish scope and make accountable decisions. Operators maintain data and use-case inventories, approved purposes, model and implementation records, parameter and assumption documentation, validation evidence, access and release records, incidents, exceptions, and retirement evidence. Reconcile active use cases, datasets, implementations, owners, recipients, and evidence currency at least quarterly; review material changes before release or reuse; and conduct management review annually and after significant privacy, data, threat, or regulatory changes.

Independent reviewers sample a path from purpose and data context through implementation evidence, validation, and release governance. Auditors assess supplied evidence without accessing restricted data, selecting privacy parameters, changing systems, approving releases, accepting risk, making legal conclusions, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize evidence, identify missing assumptions or stale validation, draft workpapers, compare supplied records with approved decisions, and prepare review questions. AI cannot prove privacy guarantees, choose parameters, access or release protected data, approve use cases, accept risk, make legal or ethical conclusions, or attest to conformance.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [NIST SP 800-226 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/226/final), retain the retrieved version and applicable use terms, and record any material update before relying on it; checked 2026-07-31. This is original evidence-planning language, not reproduced NIST material, a mathematical proof, a privacy guarantee, release authorization, or legal conclusion.

### 1. Population, purpose, and disclosure-context package

- **Request and owner:** Data, privacy, product, research, and release owners provide the approved use-case record, population and data-lineage description, intended recipients and release channel, purpose limitation, sensitivity context, and accountable owner list.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one proposed or operating release from its stated purpose and data context to the identified population, recipient, release path, and approved decision record. This evaluates traceability only; it cannot determine lawful processing, adequacy of consent, or permission to disclose data.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize non-sensitive metadata and flag missing ownership or lineage links. Humans decide purpose, scope, data use, and release. Refresh after a data-source, population, recipient, purpose, or release-channel change.

### 2. Privacy-model, implementation, and utility-evidence package

- **Request and owner:** Research, statistics, engineering, privacy, and security owners provide the approved privacy-model and assumption record, parameter-governance reference, non-secret implementation/version traceability, validation and utility-study references, known limitations, and change history.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample an implementation from the approved model and assumptions through its versioned implementation reference and supplied validation/utility evidence. This can identify evidence gaps; it cannot verify a proof, calculate a privacy guarantee, choose parameters, or establish fitness for any release.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare supplied metadata, identify absent assumptions or stale validation, and draft questions. Humans evaluate mathematics, implementation, utility tradeoffs, and acceptance decisions. Recollect after material implementation, parameter-governance, data, utility, or threat-model changes.

### 3. Composition, release governance, and lifecycle package

- **Request and owner:** Privacy, product, operations, legal, records, and risk owners provide release approvals, reuse/composition assessment references, access and release logs, exception and incident records, recipient commitments where applicable, corrective actions, and retention/retirement decisions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one release or reuse event from its accountable approval and composition/reuse record through release evidence, monitoring or incident reference, and next-review decision. This supports a governance observation; it cannot approve sharing, assess legal obligations, or establish privacy protection over time.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag repeated use without a linked review, stale decisions, or unresolved corrective actions. Humans approve release, exceptions, remediation, retention, and risk acceptance. Review quarterly and after release, reuse, incident, complaint, material threat change, or source update.
