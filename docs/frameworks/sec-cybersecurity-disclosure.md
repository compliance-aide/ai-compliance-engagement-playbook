# SEC cybersecurity disclosure readiness — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not legal advice or a disclosure decision. See the [SEC final-rule resource](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2023/07/s7-09-22).

## Engagement focus

Confirm filing applicability with authorized counsel, then maintain a board-owned disclosure operating model. Preserve incident decision timelines, materiality-analysis inputs, governance evidence, draft-review approvals, and annual refresh decisions. Test escalation timing and consistency between operations and drafts.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Management and counsel own materiality and filing decisions. Independent reviewers test traceability, timing, and controls over disclosure preparation. AI can reconcile records and flag inconsistent drafts; it cannot decide materiality, provide legal advice, sign or submit filings, or communicate external conclusions.


## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [SEC final-rule resource](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2023/07/s7-09-22), checked 2026-07-31, and confirm the issuer's filing status, current rule materials, and any related staff guidance with securities counsel. This is original preparation guidance, not legal advice, a materiality determination, or filing text.

### 1. Issuer, governance, and disclosure-boundary record

- **Request and owner:** Obtain the issuer/corporate structure, reporting-calendar owner, board and management cybersecurity-governance materials, approved disclosure controls, reporting-line record, and counsel-led applicability questions from the corporate secretary, legal, security, finance, and investor-relations owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected governance statement or disclosure responsibility to an approved source record, accountable owner, date, and review decision. This supports a preparation boundary; it cannot establish filing obligations, interpret a rule, or determine whether disclosed information is complete.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved records and flag stale ownership or calendar dates. Counsel and authorized issuer leadership approve legal positions and external language. Refresh for a governance change, fiscal-year planning cycle, source update, or proposed disclosure change.

### 2. Incident-to-decision traceability package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain time-stamped incident intake, escalation, investigation, relevant business-impact inputs, decision logs, counsel workstream references, draft versions, and access-controlled communications from incident, legal, finance, and disclosure-control owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected incident from intake through escalation, authorized decision points, evidence references, and approved communications; distinguish facts, open questions, and privileged material. This supports process traceability, not a materiality conclusion, timeliness conclusion, or disclosure decision.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may create a read-only chronology and flag missing handoffs; it may not decide materiality, waive escalation, draft final filing language without review, or submit anything. Trigger on a reportable-security event, changed facts, missed handoff, or disclosure rehearsal.

### 3. Annual readiness, exception, and filing-support package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain disclosure-control testing references, tabletop outcomes, exceptions, remediation owners, management review minutes, evidence-retention confirmation, and counsel-approved filing-preparation milestones from the disclosure committee or equivalent owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample an exception or exercise finding to confirm its owner, approved remediation or risk decision, due date, and retest or closure rationale. This supports readiness governance; it cannot certify controls, approve a filing, or provide an auditor’s conclusion.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile action dates and identify missing approvals. Humans approve exceptions, risk acceptance, filings, and regulator communications. Review annually and after a significant incident, control failure, source change, or filing-cycle change.
