# NIST SP 800-184 cybersecurity event-recovery engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST guidance, an incident-command instruction, a recovery authorization, a legal interpretation, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-184](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/184/final) and the organization’s approved recovery, legal, customer, insurance, and authority direction.

## Engagement focus

Maintain a cyber-event recovery capability that prioritizes essential services, translates recovery objectives into tested playbooks, records recovery metrics and dependencies, and turns exercises and events into controlled improvement work. Connect recovery plans to accountable owners, business impact, resource assumptions, communications, restoration acceptance, incident handoffs, and corrective actions. Keep recovery planning distinct from real-time incident command, service-restoration acceptance, and legal, contractual, notification, or payment decisions.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, business-continuity, technology, security, platform, application, communications, legal, insurance, supplier-management, service-owner, and incident-command roles. Operators maintain critical-service priorities, recovery objectives, dependency maps, playbooks, exercise designs and results, recovery metrics, incident-to-recovery handoffs, communications records, acceptance records, supplier evidence, and improvement status. Review service priorities, recovery assumptions, and open corrective actions quarterly; exercise representative cyber-event recovery scenarios at least annually and after material service, architecture, supplier, or threat changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples recovery plans, exercises, and corrective actions; auditors test the evidence trail without directing recovery, accepting restored services, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied recovery evidence, trace playbooks to critical services, flag stale exercises or unresolved improvements, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot activate recovery, direct incident response, approve restoration, decide notification or payment, determine legal obligations, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.
