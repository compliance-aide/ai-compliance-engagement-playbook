# ISO 28000:2022 — supply-chain security engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not ISO content or certification advice. Check the [ISO record](https://www.iso.org/standard/79612.html).

## Engagement focus

Maintain facilities, partners, routes, logistics technology, dependencies, threats, disruptions, onboarding, exceptions, corrective actions, and leadership-review evidence.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Operators maintain partner evidence; independent reviewers validate scope and provenance. AI detects stale attestations and drafts questions, but cannot accept risk or certify. Review quarterly and annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [ISO 28000:2022 record](https://www.iso.org/standard/79612.html) and an organization-authorized copy for interpretation; checked 2026-07-31. ISO material is licensed and protected. This original plan does not reproduce supply-chain security requirements, assessment questions, or certification methods. Supply-chain leadership confirms applicability, boundaries, and risk decisions.

### 1. Supply-chain boundary, partner, and route package

- **Request and owner:** Supply-chain, logistics, procurement, and security owners provide approved facility/route/service boundary, partner and subcontractor inventory, custody/hand-off points, transport/technology dependencies, geographic or disruption assumptions, and accountable-owner records.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected route, partner, or hand-off to its defined boundary, responsible owner, dependency record, and current approval. This can support a visible supply-chain scope; it cannot prove all parties or routes are included, validate partner behavior, or establish security performance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare approved partner, route, and ownership records and flag stale attestations. Humans approve boundary, onboarding, and applicability decisions. Refresh after new partners, routes, facilities, jurisdictions, technologies, or ownership changes.

### 2. Threat, disruption, and treatment package

- **Request and owner:** Security, logistics, and partner-management owners provide threat/disruption records, risk decisions, safeguards or operational-treatment references, incident/escalation records, deviations, corrective actions, and relevant testing or follow-up evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect a selected threat or disruption case from affected boundary through source evidence, accountable decision, treatment, partner communication, and follow-up. This can support decision traceability for one event; it cannot predict disruption, accept risk, prove route resilience, or validate every partner control.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize supplied records and flag missing decision authority, stale actions, or unlinked partners. Humans approve treatment, partner escalation, exceptions, and closure. Recollect after an incident, threat change, control failure, partner issue, or exception expiry.

### 3. Monitoring, assurance, and leadership-improvement package

- **Request and owner:** Operations, procurement, security, and leadership owners provide monitoring measures, supplier performance/assurance records, internal-review samples, material-change records, management-review minutes, resource decisions, and action/retest evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected monitoring result or supplier issue to the relevant population, period, source/provenance, human review, decision, and improvement follow-up. This can support accountable oversight; it cannot certify a supplier, establish system-wide effectiveness, or substitute for independent certification audit.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare read-only evidence maps and draft challenge questions. Humans approve risk acceptance, resources, supplier decisions, external statements, and final closure. Review quarterly, annually, and after a material source, supplier, or operating change.
