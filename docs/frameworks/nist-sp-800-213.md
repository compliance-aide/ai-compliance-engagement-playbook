# NIST SP 800-213 IoT-device acquisition engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST IoT-device cybersecurity content, a procurement requirement, a device approval, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-213](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/213/final), any active revision process, and the organization's approved security, procurement, privacy, legal, safety, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round engagement for bringing in-scope connected devices into an organization's environment. Establish the device population, intended missions, deployment locations, system connections, data flows, owners, manufacturers, service providers, support periods, and retirement conditions. Translate those facts into approved organization-specific needs for device behavior, supporting services, documentation, monitoring, patching, incident response, supplier accountability, and end-of-life treatment.

Keep a traceable record from mission need and risk analysis through acquisition, integration, acceptance, operation, change, and disposal. A questionnaire response, product sheet, contract clause, laboratory result, deployment inventory, or supplier assertion is evidence to examine—not proof that a device is secure, fits its environment, or meets every approved need. Capture material gaps, compensating measures, changes in supplier support, and decisions about continued use.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Mission, security, acquisition, engineering, system, asset, privacy, legal, safety, supplier, incident-response, and records owners set scope and make accountable decisions. Operators maintain the device and dependency inventory, approved use cases, network and data-flow records, required-capability traceability, onboarding and configuration evidence, support and vulnerability records, supplier communications, exceptions, incidents, and retirement evidence. Reconcile deployed devices, ownership, support status, suppliers, and material connections at least quarterly; review vulnerabilities, updates, exceptions, and supplier changes on the same rhythm; and perform management review annually and after material mission, threat, device, supplier, or guidance changes.

Independent reviewers sample the path from mission need and risk record through acquisition evidence, deployment, and operating records. Auditors evaluate supplied evidence without selecting products, accepting risk, approving contracts, authorizing deployment, changing device settings, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize inventories and evidence, identify incomplete traceability, draft workpapers, compare supplied records with approved requirements, and prepare review questions. AI cannot set procurement requirements, select or approve products, sign contracts, authorize deployment, alter devices, accept risk, make safety or legal conclusions, or attest to conformance.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** At engagement start, retain the retrieved version and applicable use terms for the official [NIST SP 800-213 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/213/final), any active revision material, and approved security, procurement, privacy, legal, safety, and operational direction. These are original evidence-planning packages, not reproduced NIST content, a procurement requirement, device approval, or safety conclusion.

### 1. Mission, device, and requirement-traceability package

- **Request and owner:** Mission, system, asset, engineering, security, privacy, safety, acquisition, and service owners provide intended-use and deployment-context record, device/dependency inventory, connection and data-flow map, approved organization-specific needs, risk record, and accountable ownership.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one device class or acquisition from stated mission and environment through identified dependencies, data/network connections, approved needs, and assigned owners. This can assess traceability of supplied decisions; it cannot establish that a device is suitable, safe, or secure in every deployment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize supplied inventories and flag missing owners, requirements, or dependency links. Humans set requirements, decide scope, and approve safety/privacy/security boundaries. Refresh after mission, location, device, data-flow, integration, or threat change.

### 2. Supplier, acquisition, and onboarding-evidence package

- **Request and owner:** Acquisition, supplier, engineering, security, legal, privacy, safety, and system owners provide supplier documentation, support-period and vulnerability-contact records, contract/requirement evidence, product evaluation records, acceptance criteria, onboarding/configuration records, and unresolved-gap register.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample one acquired device or service from supplier assertions and approved requirements through evaluation/acceptance evidence, onboarding record, and documented gap or exception. This can test the evidence chain; it cannot select a product, approve a contract, authorize deployment, or validate every supplier claim.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare supplied documents to approved needs and draft questions about missing support or evidence links. Humans select products, approve contracts, accept devices, and authorize deployment. Recollect after supplier change, support notice, material vulnerability, failed acceptance check, or contract update.

### 3. Operating support, change, and retirement package

- **Request and owner:** Asset, system, security operations, engineering, supplier, incident-response, records, privacy, safety, risk, and independent-review owners provide deployed-device inventory, update/vulnerability records, monitoring and incident evidence, change approvals, exception records, end-of-support decisions, disposal/retirement evidence, corrective actions, and management review records.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected operating device from ownership and support status through update/change or incident handling, documented exception where applicable, and retirement/continuity decision. This can support an operating-evidence observation; it cannot alter a device, accept risk, determine safety/legal duty, or establish device security over its whole life.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag stale support dates, missing change links, and overdue corrective actions. Humans approve changes, exceptions, risk, continued use, incident response, and retirement. Review quarterly and after a vulnerability, incident, support change, device failure, or material deployment change.
