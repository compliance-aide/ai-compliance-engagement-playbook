# COBIT 2019 — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not ISACA content or a governance assessment.
> Consult authorized [COBIT materials](https://www.isaca.org/store2/product/CB19FGM)
> and applicable licensing terms.

## Engagement focus

Establish decision rights, stakeholder outcomes, risk appetite, material I&T
services, and governance calendar. Document decision and evidence pathways;
compile performance signals, overdue actions, and management statements;
periodically review whether I&T produces intended value within risk and resource
constraints; and refresh the governance design each year.

## Roles

Management owns transparent decision records and approved actions. Independent
reviewers test decision traceability and escalation effectiveness. AI identifies
missing owners, aged commitments, and conflicting metrics and drafts agenda
questions; it cannot determine strategy or an assurance conclusion.

## Source and applicability

Use the [official COBIT resources](https://www.isaca.org/resources/cobit) to identify authorized publications and the engagement's adopted edition. ISACA describes 40 governance and management objectives. Its [design guidance](https://www.isaca.org/resources/news-and-trends/industry-news/2019/designing-your-organizations-custom-cobit) emphasizes tailoring to organizational context before implementation, prioritization under limited resources, and revisiting design when context changes. The Design and Implementation Guides are complementary; a generic checklist is not a completed governance design.

Confirm licensed access before using detailed objectives, mappings, toolkit calculations or rating methods. Do not reconstruct proprietary tables from memory or publish licensed content here. If access is unavailable, continue original stakeholder/evidence planning and mark source-dependent design or assessment decisions unresolved.

## Before starting

Follow the [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md). Name the governance sponsor, management action owners and independent reviewer. Record enterprise boundary, intended decisions and authorized source versions. Keep the current-state evidence, target design and improvement plan separate. Use source-linked workpapers; the existence of a committee or policy does not demonstrate effective governance.

## Ordered workflow

| Step | Action and accountable owner | Output and exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Define the problem | Sponsor and business owners specify stakeholder needs, affected I&T services, value expectations and constraints. | Agreed outcome and decision boundary with an accountable sponsor. |
| 2. Establish context | Owners provide strategy, risk, regulatory, sourcing, operating and technology context for the authorized design method. | Dated design inputs with provenance; unknown inputs are visible rather than defaulted to convenient values. |
| 3. Review objective coverage | Agent indexes the full adopted core model; governance owners record priorities, dependencies and disposition for every objective. | Complete design register without silently omitted objectives; priority is not a compliance exemption. |
| 4. Assess present operation | Owners provide decision records, actual performance data, escalations and evidence of follow-through; reviewers evaluate supported observations. | Evidence-based baseline with scope, period and limitations. Any formal capability rating uses the authorized method and human judgment. |
| 5. Approve target design | Governance authority chooses targets and decision rights; management proposes actions and resource needs. | Approved rationale, owners, dependencies and measures; a toolkit output remains an input to judgment. |
| 6. Implement improvements | Management executes approved process, responsibility and information-flow changes. | Operating records show what changed, where and when; completed training or a rewritten charter alone does not establish the intended outcome. |
| 7. Verify outcomes | Reviewers trace decisions through implementation, measured effect and escalation where targets are missed. | Comparable evidence against the baseline, unresolved findings and corrective actions. |
| 8. Sustain and redesign | Governance leadership reviews benefits, risks, resources and changed context. | Recorded decisions, next review dates and assigned follow-up; redesign is triggered when assumptions no longer hold. |

## Failure branches and decisions

- **Metrics disagree:** preserve their definitions, populations, dates and sources; reconcile before presenting a single outcome. Missing data is not zero risk.
- **No decision owner:** assign the authority question to the sponsor; do not invent approval from attendance at a meeting.
- **Tool recommends a target without reliable inputs:** label it provisional and resolve assumptions before human approval.
- **Action closed but benefit absent:** retain implementation completion separately from outcome verification and continue investigation.
- **Rating requested without method access:** provide observed facts and source gaps; do not fabricate a capability or maturity score.

## Evidence and test plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use authorized current COBIT 2019 materials from the [ISACA COBIT store record](https://www.isaca.org/store2/product/CB19FGM); prior snapshot dated 2026-07-31; verify edition and permitted use before engagement. COBIT content is licensed. This original plan does not reproduce objectives, models, mappings, or assessment methods; a named governance owner confirms authorized access and permitted use before any engagement.

### 1. Governance boundary and decision-rights record

- **Request and owner:** Approved enterprise objectives, I&T service boundary, stakeholder and decision-rights map, committee charter/calendar, risk appetite inputs, and named business, technology, risk, and governance owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected material I&T decision from stated outcome and accountable authority to the dated approval and escalation path. This supports transparent governance traceability; it cannot establish that governance is effective, complete, or aligned to every COBIT concept.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize approved metadata and flag missing owners, decisions, or review dates. Human governance authorities approve scope, priorities, and risk acceptance. Refresh after a material strategy, service, ownership, authority, or risk-appetite change.

### 2. I&T value, risk, and resource operating record

- **Request and owner:** Approved outcome measures, service performance records, material risk/action register, investment or capacity decisions, dependency/supplier records, and management-review inputs from service, finance, risk, and technology owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect a selected measure or overdue action back to its source record, accountable owner, decision, and follow-up. This can support a bounded account of management oversight; it cannot prove value realization, risk reduction, or resource sufficiency.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved measures and flag stale actions or conflicting metadata; it cannot set targets, allocate resources, alter services, or close risks. Humans approve decisions and remediation. Refresh after a missed material measure, major investment, supplier change, incident, or service change.

### 3. Governance review and challenge workpaper

- **Request and owner:** Meeting packs/minutes, exception and escalation records, corrective-action status, independent challenge notes, and annual design-refresh decisions from governance leadership and independent reviewers.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected escalation or exception to its decision authority, rationale, expiry or follow-up, and independent challenge. This supports accountable review; it cannot issue an assurance conclusion or substitute for a licensed assessment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare source-linked, non-authoritative workpapers and flag unlinked decisions. Humans approve external statements, residual-risk decisions, and closure. Review quarterly and complete a human-approved annual governance refresh.


## Cadence and renewal

Agree review frequencies with governance leadership. Quarterly monitoring and annual design review are engagement conventions, not universal COBIT deadlines. Revisit priorities after strategy, risk appetite, regulation, sourcing, service, incident or leadership changes. Preserve historical metric definitions and scope so a changed denominator cannot masquerade as improved performance.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver the context record, full objective disposition register, evidence baseline, approved design, implementation plan, measured outcomes and unresolved decisions. Name the next action, owner and evidence needed without chat history. Distinguish observed improvement from a formal assurance conclusion; the latter requires its own authorized method and independent review.
