# NIST SP 800-218 — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not a release approval or a secure-development
> attestation. Track the [NIST SSDF publication status](https://csrc.nist.gov/Projects/ssdf/publications).

## Engagement focus

Map the actual delivery lifecycle across developed, acquired, configured, and
open-source software. Define practical engineering expectations and exception
handling; review representative releases from intent through delivery; inspect
vulnerability learning loops; include suppliers and components; and report
coverage, meaningful exceptions, and improvement commitments.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Product, engineering, security, and release teams own lifecycle records and
release decisions. Independent reviewers sample for repeatability. AI connects
approved work items, code changes, builds, releases, and findings; it cannot
approve a release, generate attestations, or send sensitive artifacts to
an unapproved model. Improve quarterly and review annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [NIST SSDF publications page](https://csrc.nist.gov/Projects/ssdf/publications), including SP 800-218, and confirm the applicable version before each cycle; checked 2026-07-31. This plan is original evidence-planning language, not reproduced SSDF content, a secure-development attestation, or release authorization.

### 1. Software scope, secure-development governance, and supplier record

- **Request and owner:** Product/service inventory, supported release and deployment inventory, development/acquisition/open-source component records, security roles, supplier commitments, and approved exception register from product, engineering, procurement, and security owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected released component to its owning team, delivery route, supplier/component record, and documented security responsibility. This can reveal unowned or unscoped work; it cannot prove every component is known or that a supplier performs securely.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may correlate authorized inventories and flag ownership gaps. Humans approve scope, supplier decisions, and exceptions. Refresh after acquisition, architecture, supplier, component, or ownership change.

### 2. Representative change-to-release evidence

- **Request and owner:** Approved work item, source-control change references, peer-review record, build/test provenance, artifact/version reference, deployment/release approval, and release exception evidence from engineering and release owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Independently trace a human-selected release from intent through change, build, test, artifact, and approval, preserving failed or omitted checks. This can support a sampled lifecycle trail; it cannot prove code is free of defects, authorize deployment, or establish operating effectiveness for all releases.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize links and identify missing records; it may not write code, merge changes, override gates, approve a release, or generate an attestation. Recollect for critical release failures, tooling changes, or release-period rollover.

### 3. Vulnerability learning, remediation, and management review record

- **Request and owner:** Vulnerability intake/triage records, remediation and retest evidence, incident/defect learning outputs, dependency update decisions, metrics definitions, and quarterly or annual management-review decisions from security and engineering leaders.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected vulnerability or incident lesson to severity rationale, accountable owner, remediation evidence, retest, and accepted exception if any. This can support visible improvement tracking; it cannot independently prioritize risk, close a vulnerability, or attest to secure development.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag overdue remediation and draft source-linked workpapers. Humans decide severity, risk treatment, closure, and external disclosures. Review quarterly and after a material vulnerability, incident, or supplier advisory.
