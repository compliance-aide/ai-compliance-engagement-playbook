# Israel Cyber Defense Methodology engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not Israeli methodology text, a maturity assessment, a regulatory determination, or a compliance claim. Confirm the methodology’s context through the [NIST description of the Israeli National Cyber Directorate methodology](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework/success-story-israel-national-cyber-directorate-version-20) and applicable Israeli authority guidance.

## Engagement focus

Maintain a cybersecurity operating record that connects organizational scope, accountable leadership, assets and services, risk and defense activities, suppliers, incident evidence, recovery decisions, assessment inputs, and improvement work. Preserve the relationship to any applicable Israeli sectoral requirements as a human legal and regulatory decision, not a framework mapping exercise.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Executive and security leaders define scope and approve risk decisions; technical owners retain operating evidence; legal and sectoral owners determine obligations; independent reviewers challenge evidence traceability. AI may organize approved evidence, compare documented change history, identify incomplete ownership, and draft questions, but cannot determine applicability, perform or score an assessment, direct production changes, report an incident, accept risk, or claim compliance. Review annually and at material asset, service, supplier, threat, incident, or regulatory changes.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [NIST description of the Israeli National Cyber Directorate methodology](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework/success-story-israel-national-cyber-directorate-version-20) as contextual material and obtain current Israeli-authority and sectoral guidance through accountable legal/regulatory owners. Record current/historical status, source date, retrieval date, and use rights before relying on a source. Link to methodology materials; do not copy or reconstruct them. This is original operational planning, not a methodology assessment or regulatory determination.

### 1. Organizational defense scope and governance decision record

- **Request and owner:** Request the approved organization/service boundary, asset and critical-service inventory, leadership/security governance charter, sectoral applicability inputs, risk-acceptance authority map, and named executive, security, technical, legal, and regulatory owners. Cover the defined enterprise or service boundary and engagement period.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected critical service or asset grouping to its accountable owner, dependency record, governance/risk decision reference, and applicable-sector question. Inspect approved summaries rather than sensitive operational details. This can support evidence of accountable scope; it cannot decide sectoral obligations, determine criticality, accept risk, or establish cybersecurity effectiveness.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved asset/dependency and ownership metadata and flag missing governance links. Leaders and legal/regulatory owners decide scope and risk authority. Trigger on material service, asset, ownership, sectoral, or business change and at annual planning.

### 2. Defense activity, supplier, and change traceability

- **Request and owner:** Request approved defense operating procedures, selected monitoring/detection review references, vulnerability/change records, supplier/shared-service accountability records, architecture/dependency changes, and remediation ownership from security, engineering, operations, and supplier-management owners. Define the applicable systems and sample window.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected material change, vulnerability, or supplier dependency from scope through documented triage, responsible owner, approved action, validation/review reference, and disposition. This can support operating-trace evidence; it cannot prove detection coverage, secure configuration, supplier performance, or that all risks were identified.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index sanitized records, identify unresolved ownership, and propose human review questions; it cannot change controls, score maturity, direct production remediation, or close a finding. Humans approve actions and exception handling. Trigger on a material change, supplier event, vulnerability, control failure, or remediation deadline.

### 3. Incident, recovery, and improvement learning loop

- **Request and owner:** Request incident intake/escalation references, response and recovery decision logs, communication approvals, exercise/post-incident review records, lessons/action register, retest evidence, and management-review minutes from incident, continuity, security, legal, communications, and executive owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected incident or exercise from detection/intake through escalation, human decisions, recovery/communications references, lessons, action owner, and follow-up validation. This can support lifecycle accountability; it cannot determine reportability, prove recovery effectiveness, prove an incident was absent, or make a regulatory conclusion.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may summarize approved, sanitized event metadata and flag overdue actions. Humans decide incident classification, external reporting, communications, recovery acceptance, and risk treatment. Trigger on an incident, exercise, material threat change, failed recovery test, or annual governance review.
