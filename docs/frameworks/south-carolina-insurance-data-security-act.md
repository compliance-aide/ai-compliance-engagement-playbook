# South Carolina Insurance Data Security Act engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not South Carolina law or an insurance regulatory conclusion. Confirm current applicability through the [South Carolina Insurance Data Security Act](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t38c099.php).

## Engagement focus

Maintain insurer evidence for information-security governance, risk decisions, system and data context, provider oversight, incident preparation and response, reporting decisions, and remediation.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Licensed-entity leadership owns program decisions; security and privacy teams preserve evidence; legal counsel directs notifications; independent reviewers test the record. AI organizes approved artifacts and flags missing dates, owners, or change reviews, but cannot determine legal duties, approve notification, or report to regulators. Review program evidence periodically and preserve human decisions for events and external communications.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the live [South Carolina Insurance Data Security Act](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t38c099.php), checked 2026-07-31, with qualified insurance-regulatory and legal review. This original plan does not reproduce the law or determine whether an entity, information system, event, notice, report, or deadline is within scope.

### 1. Licensee governance, risk, and information-system package

- **Request and owner:** Legal-entity and accountable-officer roster; human-approved security-program governance records; risk-assessment inputs and decisions; information-system and nonpublic-information inventory; data-flow and asset-owner records; policy lifecycle; and management review from insurance leadership, security, privacy, and technology owners. Identify the licensed-entity boundary and evidence period.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected system and risk item to its owner, inventory/data-flow context, risk input, human decision, control evidence reference, exception, and review date. This supports a traceable program record; it cannot determine licensee status, legal sufficiency, risk acceptability, or operating effectiveness across the population.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved records and flag missing ownership, dates, or linked evidence. Human leadership, security, and legal owners approve scope, risk decisions, and exceptions. Refresh after entity, product, system, data, ownership, or material-risk change.

### 2. Third-party service-provider oversight package

- **Request and owner:** Provider inventory; due-diligence and security-evaluation records; contract/instruction governance references; subprovider/change notices; service and security assurance artifacts; exception/escalation records; and periodic review evidence from procurement, vendor risk, security, privacy, and business owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one provider relationship from onboarding through recorded evaluation, approved authority, contractual/instruction evidence, service change, review, and escalation path. This supports accountable oversight evidence; it cannot establish contractual adequacy, provider compliance, or that every provider event is known.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved provider and system inventories and flag stale reviews; it cannot approve providers, interpret contractual duties, or waive exceptions. Human procurement, legal, security, and business owners approve onboarding, terms, risk treatment, and escalation. Trigger on new or changed providers, service scope, assurance expiration, or material incident.

### 3. Cybersecurity-event, reporting-decision, and recovery package

- **Request and owner:** Event intake, investigation timeline, preserved technical-evidence references, human escalation and legal-decision records, reporting/communication approval records where applicable, containment/recovery validation, corrective actions, and post-event review from incident response, legal, executive, communications, and security owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Reperform a tabletop or trace a closed, de-identified event through intake, evidence preservation, scope updates, accountable decisions, communication approval, recovery, and retest. This supports incident readiness and decision traceability; it cannot classify a cybersecurity event legally, conclude a report is due, approve a regulator communication, or establish completed obligations.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize authorized timelines and flag missing evidence links. Counsel and authorized insurance leaders determine legal conclusions, reports, communications, and closure; AI cannot submit or communicate externally. Trigger on an event, contact-path change, exercise finding, remediation completion, or source update.
