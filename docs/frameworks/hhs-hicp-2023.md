# HHS HICP 2023 — healthcare cybersecurity engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not HHS content or patient-safety advice. Confirm current material through the [HHS Cyber Gateway HICP resource](https://hhscyber.hhs.gov/cornerstone-hicp.html).

## Engagement focus

Maintain healthcare service scope, patient-safety dependencies, asset inventory, workforce and device evidence, incident readiness, vendor dependencies, risk decisions, improvement actions, and exercise records.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Healthcare, clinical, security, and risk leaders retain patient-safety and operational decisions; independent reviewers test evidence. AI organizes approved records and highlights gaps, but cannot diagnose, operate medical devices, or direct incident response. Review quarterly and annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [HHS Cyber Gateway HICP resource](https://hhscyber.hhs.gov/cornerstone-hicp.html), applicable healthcare obligations, and authorized organizational records; checked 2026-07-31. This original plan is an engagement aid, not HHS material, clinical advice, a patient-safety determination, or a cybersecurity assurance conclusion.

### 1. Care-delivery dependency and risk-context package

- **Request and owner:** Clinical operations, biomedical engineering, facilities, IT, security, and risk owners provide care-delivery service maps, critical-device and service inventories, downtime dependencies, patient-safety escalation routes, risk decisions, and accountable approvals.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected clinical-support dependency to its service owner, supporting asset/service, stated continuity assumption, escalation route, and latest review. This maps a bounded dependency; it cannot establish safety, clinical priority, or complete operational resilience.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize approved asset/dependency metadata and flag missing owners or stale reviews. Clinical and risk leaders decide patient-safety impacts, priorities, and risk acceptance. Refresh after service redesign, technology replacement, safety event, or business-continuity exercise.

### 2. Protective operations and incident-readiness package

- **Request and owner:** Security, IT operations, biomedical engineering, facilities, and incident-command owners provide access/change records, device/service maintenance evidence, detection/escalation workflows, incident exercises, vendor support arrangements, and corrective-action tracking.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample a selected exercise, maintenance record, or incident handoff to its source evidence, responsible owner, timing, escalation decision, limitation, and follow-up. This can show an operating trail; it cannot direct response or validate effectiveness across every clinical system.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may surface incomplete handoffs, overdue exercises, or unresolved corrective actions from approved records. Humans direct incident response, device operation, communications, and remediation. Recollect after an incident, material vendor change, system downtime, or failed exercise.

### 3. Governance, workforce, and improvement packet

- **Request and owner:** Security leadership, privacy/legal, clinical leadership, HR/training, and independent-review owners provide governance minutes, workforce training evidence, risk register entries, exceptions, assessment observations, management decisions, and retest/closure evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected finding or exception to its authority, affected service, owner, due date, decision record, and retest result. It cannot approve a clinical workflow or make a regulator-facing assertion.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare factual evidence inventories and challenge questions, while humans approve budgets, risk decisions, policy, closure, and external communications. Review quarterly and after significant operational, threat, or regulatory change.
