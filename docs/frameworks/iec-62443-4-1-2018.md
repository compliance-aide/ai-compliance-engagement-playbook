# IEC 62443-4-1:2018 IACS product lifecycle — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not IEC content or a product-security certification claim. Check the [IEC record](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/33615); do not place IEC text in AI prompts.

## Engagement focus

Maintain product scope, lifecycle decisions, security requirements, design and implementation evidence, verification, defect and patch handling, support commitments, end-of-life decisions, and release approvals.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Product, engineering, and security owners make lifecycle and release decisions; independent reviewers sample evidence. AI connects approved artifacts and flags missing traceability, but cannot change code, approve releases, or certify. Review each release and annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

### 1. Product scope and security-requirements package

- **Request and owner:** Product, architecture, security, and product-management owners provide the defined product/release scope, intended deployment context, security requirements record, assumptions/dependencies, accountable owners, and approved change history.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected release to its scope, owner, stated assumptions, requirements record, and approved decision points. This tests the visible record chain; it cannot prove requirements are complete, correct, or suitable for every deployment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved artifacts and flag missing traceability links. Humans approve product scope, requirements, and release intent. Refresh for a material design, deployment, interface, supplier, or threat-context change.

### 2. Secure-development and verification package

- **Request and owner:** Engineering, security, and quality owners provide design/implementation review records, authorized verification results, defect records, test limitations, exception rationale, remediation owners, and release-readiness evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect a selected requirement or defect through implementation evidence, relevant verification, limitation, remediation/exception, and authorized release decision. This supports sample traceability; it cannot prove absence of defects, completeness of testing, or effectiveness in the field.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may correlate approved evidence and surface unlinked defects or stale results. It must not change code, run unauthorized tests, waive findings, or approve a release. Re-evaluate after a significant defect, test failure, or material code change.

### 3. Vulnerability response, support, and end-of-life package

- **Request and owner:** Product-security, support, supplier, and lifecycle owners provide vulnerability intake/triage records, patch/advisory decisions, customer-support commitments, supplier dependencies, end-of-life plans, and authorized communications.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected vulnerability or lifecycle event to intake, owner, decision authority, customer/support interface, and recorded follow-up. This tests a documented response path; it cannot determine severity, prove patch deployment, or guarantee downstream customer action.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize authorized issue records and identify overdue owners without accessing production or external systems. Humans decide triage, release, communications, support commitments, and end-of-life actions. Review each material issue and annually.
