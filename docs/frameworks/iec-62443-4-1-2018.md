# IEC 62443-4-1:2018 IACS product lifecycle — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not IEC text or a product-security certification claim.

## Source and applicability

The [IEC publication record](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/33615), checked 2026-09-04, describes secure development and maintenance processes covering requirements, design, implementation, verification, defects, patches and retirement. It applies to product developers and maintainers, rather than integrators or users in those roles. Full licensed requirements and applicable assessment rules remain unverified for this draft.

Confirm product scope, organizational role, contracted edition and source permissions. Do not put licensed IEC text into AI prompts without permission. Process conformity does not prove that a particular product has no vulnerabilities or meets every deployment's technical requirements.

## Engagement focus

Connect each supported product and release to the security decisions and evidence used to build, verify, maintain and retire it. Preserve the distinction between a merged fix, a tested release artifact, a distributed update and customer installation.

## Roles

Product leadership owns lifecycle and support commitments. Engineering, architecture, security and quality owners approve requirements, design and verification. Product-security response owners handle vulnerability triage and advisories. Named authorities approve releases, exceptions and communications. An independent reviewer challenges traceability and claims. AI may organize authorized evidence and draft questions; it cannot approve releases, waive defects, accept risk, certify products or change code under this review-only engagement.

## Before starting

Record products, hardware/software/firmware variants, supported branches, build identities, intended environments, dependencies and responsible teams. Establish evidence permissions, source licenses, review authority and vulnerability intake. Define allowed test environments. This workflow does not authorize code execution, production testing, deployment or customer communication.

## Ordered workflow

1. **Define lifecycle coverage.** Reconcile all scoped products, variants, branches, dependencies and support states. Record exclusions and accountable owners. Do not assume a fix on the newest branch covers older supported versions.
2. **Approve requirements and design context.** Link security requirements to intended use, trust boundaries, assumptions and threats. Have qualified owners review design choices and deployment prerequisites. Changes in interfaces or dependencies trigger review of affected assumptions.
3. **Trace implementation.** Associate approved requirements and design decisions with exact source/build artifacts, review evidence and implementation findings. Preserve third-party component provenance and unresolved constraints. A review checkbox does not establish the correctness of code it did not examine.
4. **Verify the release candidate.** Record authorized test methods, actual configurations, results, limitations and artifact identity. Include relevant misuse and failure cases as approved by qualified owners. Retain all failures and untested requirements. Tests against a different build cannot establish readiness of the candidate without justified reconciliation.
5. **Resolve findings and gate release.** Assign defects and affected variants, record remediation or authorized exception decisions, and retest the corrected artifact. Assemble release evidence, known limitations and deployment guidance for named approval. Approval, publication and customer adoption are separate states.
6. **Maintain vulnerability and update response.** Trace each report through applicability, affected versions, qualified severity decisions, correction, verification and approved advisory/update handling. Record unsupported branches and customer prerequisites explicitly. A patch file or merged commit does not prove distribution or installation.
7. **Manage support and retirement.** Reconcile commitments, dependency support, end-of-life plans and approved communications. Record remaining obligations and handoff ownership. Review incident and defect lessons for changes to development practices, preserving evidence of effectiveness rather than only revised policy text.

## Evidence and test plan

Maintain complete scoped accounting for products, releases, requirements, defects, dependencies and lifecycle actions. Preserve all evidence supplied for assessment without silent trimming or sampling. Record actual test coverage and exclusions separately from inventory completeness.

### 1. Product scope and security-requirements package

- **Request and owner:** Product, architecture, security, and product-management owners provide the defined product/release scope, intended deployment context, security requirements record, assumptions/dependencies, accountable owners, and approved change history.
- **Validate and limit:** Reconcile all scoped releases and trace each to its scope, owner, stated assumptions, requirements record, and approved decision points. This tests the visible record chain; it cannot prove requirements are complete, correct, or suitable for every deployment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved artifacts and flag missing traceability links. Humans approve product scope, requirements, and release intent. Refresh for a material design, deployment, interface, supplier, or threat-context change.

### 2. Secure-development and verification package

- **Request and owner:** Engineering, security, and quality owners provide design/implementation review records, authorized verification results, defect records, test limitations, exception rationale, remediation owners, and release-readiness evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace every scoped requirement and defect through implementation evidence, relevant verification, limitation, remediation/exception, and authorized release decision. This supports documented traceability; it cannot prove absence of defects, completeness of testing, or effectiveness in the field.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may correlate approved evidence and surface unlinked defects or stale results. It must not change code, run unauthorized tests, waive findings, or approve a release. Re-evaluate after a significant defect, test failure, or material code change.

### 3. Vulnerability response, support, and end-of-life package

- **Request and owner:** Product-security, support, supplier, and lifecycle owners provide vulnerability intake/triage records, patch/advisory decisions, customer-support commitments, supplier dependencies, end-of-life plans, and authorized communications.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace every scoped vulnerability and lifecycle event to intake, owner, decision authority, customer/support interface, and recorded follow-up. This tests a documented response path; it cannot determine severity, prove patch deployment, or guarantee downstream customer action.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize authorized issue records and identify overdue owners without accessing production or external systems. Humans decide triage, release, communications, support commitments, and end-of-life actions. Review each material issue and at approved intervals.


## Failure branches and decisions

- Fix covers one supported branch only: keep other affected branches open until corrected or explicitly dispositioned by authority.
- Test artifact differs from release artifact: reconcile identities and rerun affected verification before claiming release readiness.
- Dependency support ends: escalate the product support impact and approved treatment; do not silently remove it from inventory.
- Exception lacks authority or expiry/review trigger: return it for accountable decision; it does not waive the observed defect.
- Update is available but customer deployment is unknown: report availability only, with installation status unverified.

Use [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md) assertions: supported, not_supported, inconclusive, not_applicable and not_tested. Keep implementation, verification, release authorization and field deployment separate.

## Cadence and renewal

Review each release and material vulnerability or lifecycle change, with additional intervals set by approved procedures and obligations. Reopen evidence after significant code, design, dependency or intended-use changes. Do not invent universal annual certification duties.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver the product/release inventory, requirement-to-artifact traceability, verification coverage, defect decisions, support commitments and open actions with owners and dates. Full source, independent skeptical and rights reviews remain pending. No actual product test, release or certification occurred in preparing this draft.

Fictional author desk case: a vulnerability fix passes tests on branch B, while supported branch A still contains the affected implementation. Branch B verification may be supported; a claim that all supported products are fixed is not_supported. Keep branch A's owner and remediation decision visible.
