# CMMC — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not a DoD publication or a certification claim.
> Before each milestone, check the active solicitation, contract, and current
> [DoD CMMC materials](https://dodcio.defense.gov/CMMC/Resources-Documentation/).

## Engagement focus

Start with the actual contractual requirement, data type, required level, and
current program status. Preserve scope decisions and evidence provenance.
Separate readiness support from the independent assessment role, sustain
readiness throughout contract performance, and re-check official rules before
any assertion, submission, or assessor engagement.

## Roles

The organization owns its scope, implementation, and representations. An
authorized assessor performs the independent work when required. AI coordinates
evidence, detects inconsistencies, and prepares drafts; it cannot represent
the organization to DoD, certify a result, make submissions, or approve scope or remediation decisions.

## Cadence and renewal

Revalidate contract and program status, CUI flows, scope, and evidence; test
operation; track remediation; and trigger early review after award changes,
significant system changes, or program-rule updates.

## Source and applicability

Use this guide to route an engagement, then follow the relevant level guide. The [official overview](https://dodcio.defense.gov/CMMC/about/), checked September 4, 2026, states that Phase II implementation is suspended and Phase I self-assessment requirements remain. The pause does not remove information-safeguarding duties. Verify current contract direction rather than assuming either the old rollout schedule or a blanket exemption.

| Confirmed engagement need | Workflow | Decision to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Level 1 requirement | [Level 1 program](cmmc-level-1-program.md) | FCI scope, annual assessment and affirmation, evidence supporting the actual status. |
| Level 2 requirement | [Level 2 program](cmmc-level-2-program.md) | Required assessment route, CUI scope, applicable baseline and separate assessment/affirmation dates. |
| Expressly applicable Level 3 requirement or preparation | [Level 3 program](cmmc-level-3-program.md) | Current government route and prerequisite scope/status verification before a formal request. |
| Level, information type or route unresolved | Continue authorized intake here | Contracts/data owner resolves the ambiguity; no inferred status or certification claim. |

These routes are not interchangeable and this table does not determine contractual applicability. Keep a source register containing the contract, applicable rule and guide versions, dated program instructions and accountable interpretation. A downloaded guide does not prove an assessment route is currently required or available.

## Before starting

Follow the [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md). Identify the requested outcome: preparation, assessment support, remediation, renewal or submission preparation. Name the contracts owner, evidence custodians, human assessment lead and representation authority. Confirm approved tools and evidence locations; keep FCI, CUI, credentials and restricted assessment material out of unapproved repositories and model inputs.

Create a decision record for each contract/system combination. Separate facts, interpretations and unresolved questions. Reuse approved evidence only after checking its scope, period, version and continuing relevance. Do not average incompatible scopes or merge unrelated status records into an enterprise-wide claim.

## Ordered workflow

| Step | Action and accountable owner | Output and exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Capture the actual ask | Engagement lead identifies intended deliverable, contract context and decision deadline. | Bounded work item and accountable owners; no assumed government submission authorization. |
| 2. Resolve applicability | Contracts and data owners verify clauses, information types, required level and current program direction. | Source-linked decision or explicit unresolved question. |
| 3. Reconcile scope | Security owners map data flows, systems, suppliers and inherited services to the obligation. | Approved scope with responsibility allocation and unresolved coverage visible. |
| 4. Select the level workflow | Agent routes to the linked guide using the approved decision and adopted sources. | Required prerequisites, objectives, evidence plan and human authorities recorded. |
| 5. Execute complete preparation | Owners supply evidence; authorized testers collect observations against every applicable objective. | Full coverage register with dated evidence and limitations, not a sample presented as full compliance. |
| 6. Correct and review | Operational owners remediate approved changes; appropriate reviewers evaluate retests and formal finding rules. | Traceable determinations and unresolved actions; readiness work never silently becomes certification. |
| 7. Prepare representations | Agent assembles exact draft statements, scope identifiers, dates and supporting references. | Human review and explicit authorization for binding affirmation or government submission; verify the resulting official record before reporting completion. |
| 8. Sustain and hand off | Program owner tracks changes and separate assessment, affirmation and remediation dates. | Current calendar, receipts, next actions and named owners; affected conclusions reopen when their supporting facts change. |

## Failure branches and decisions

- **No controlling contract evidence:** continue safe inventory and evidence organization while the contracts owner resolves the obligation. Do not select a level from marketing language.
- **CUI classification dispute:** retain source references and route to the responsible authority. Do not downgrade information to make a workflow easier.
- **Different scope on a status record:** record the difference and obtain reconciliation before using the status for the current system.
- **Draft report says certified:** replace unsupported wording with the actual preparation or reviewed-assessment state; retain the unresolved official-status question.
- **Submission uncertain:** preserve exact error or missing receipt. Verify existing state before retrying a potentially duplicate action.

## Evidence and test plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the active solicitation/contract, current [DoD CMMC materials](https://dodcio.defense.gov/CMMC/Resources-Documentation/), the applicable 32 CFR Part 170 program rule, and any controlling 48 CFR/DFARS contract clause; prior snapshot dated 2026-07-31; revalidate current direction before use. This plan uses original readiness language and does not create a DoD representation, assessment result, SPRS entry, or certification claim. Contract, legal, and accountable security owners confirm the actual requirement and program status.

### 1. Contract, information type, and CUI scope record

- **Request and owner:** Solicitation/award clauses, required level, CUI or other controlled-information flow map, in-scope asset/service inventory, external service/provider boundary, and responsibility record from contracts, program, data, and security owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected information flow to contract input, system/service owner, and scope decision; record uncertainties and inherited services. This can support a readiness boundary. It cannot determine contract interpretation, required level, CUI status, or assessment scope.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize authorized metadata and flag conflicts. Humans approve scope and representations. Revisit after award/modification, information-flow, system, provider, or rule change.

### 2. Implementation and operating-evidence record

- **Request and owner:** Approved implementation narratives, governance/operating records, technical configuration or log evidence, training, incident/recovery, supplier, exception, and remediation records from accountable owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace an approved selection from the scoped inventory through implementation narrative to time-bounded operation and exception handling. This can support an evidence-backed readiness discussion; it cannot prove practice effectiveness, satisfy an assessor, or make a DoD assertion.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved evidence and draft questions but may not alter systems, close gaps, or choose a remediation outcome. Refresh after material changes, incidents, assessment feedback, or evidence-period rollover.

### 3. Assessment-readiness and representation boundary

- **Request and owner:** Readiness workpapers, open POA/remediation items, approved human statements, assessor-engagement inputs, and source-change log from the engagement lead and accountable organization officials.
- **Validate and limit:** Independently trace a selected readiness statement to current source evidence and preserve unresolved items. This can support human preparation; it cannot submit to DoD, make SPRS or certification representations, or substitute for an authorized assessment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a read-only packet and flag unsupported statements. Only authorized humans make submissions, representations, risk decisions, or closure. Revisit before any assertion or assessor engagement and at annual contract review.


## Completion and handoff

Deliver the routing decision, scope, level-guide workpapers, safe evidence index, reviewed results, open issues, representation drafts and renewal calendar. Distinguish prepared, reviewed, authorized, submitted and officially recorded outcomes. State the next action, owner and required evidence without relying on prior chat history. A complete preparation package can still contain gaps that prevent an official compliance statement.
