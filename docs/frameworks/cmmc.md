# CMMC — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not a DoD publication or a certification claim.
> Before each milestone, check the active solicitation, contract, and current
> [DoD CMMC materials](https://dodcio.defense.gov/CMMC/Resources-Documentation/).

## Engagement focus

Start with the actual contractual requirement, data type, required level, and
current program status. Preserve scope decisions and evidence provenance.
Separate readiness support from the independent assessment role, sustain
readiness throughout contract performance, and re-check official rules before
any assertion, submission, or assessor engagement.

## Roles

The organization owns its scope, implementation, and representations. An
authorized assessor performs the independent work when required. AI coordinates
evidence, detects inconsistencies, and prepares drafts; it cannot represent
the organization to DoD, certify a result, make submissions, or approve scope or remediation decisions.

## Annual rhythm

Revalidate contract and program status, CUI flows, scope, and evidence; test
operation; track remediation; and trigger early review after award changes,
significant system changes, or program-rule updates.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the active solicitation/contract, current [DoD CMMC materials](https://dodcio.defense.gov/CMMC/Resources-Documentation/), the applicable 32 CFR Part 170 program rule, and any controlling 48 CFR/DFARS contract clause; checked 2026-07-31. This plan uses original readiness language and does not create a DoD representation, assessment result, SPRS entry, or certification claim. Contract, legal, and accountable security owners confirm the actual requirement and program status.

### 1. Contract, information type, and CUI scope record

- **Request and owner:** Solicitation/award clauses, required level, CUI or other controlled-information flow map, in-scope asset/service inventory, external service/provider boundary, and responsibility record from contracts, program, data, and security owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected information flow to contract input, system/service owner, and scope decision; record uncertainties and inherited services. This can support a readiness boundary. It cannot determine contract interpretation, required level, CUI status, or assessment scope.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize authorized metadata and flag conflicts. Humans approve scope and representations. Revisit after award/modification, information-flow, system, provider, or rule change.

### 2. Implementation and operating-evidence record

- **Request and owner:** Approved implementation narratives, governance/operating records, technical configuration or log evidence, training, incident/recovery, supplier, exception, and remediation records from accountable owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace an approved selection from the scoped inventory through implementation narrative to time-bounded operation and exception handling. This can support an evidence-backed readiness discussion; it cannot prove practice effectiveness, satisfy an assessor, or make a DoD assertion.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved evidence and draft questions but may not alter systems, close gaps, or choose a remediation outcome. Refresh after material changes, incidents, assessment feedback, or evidence-period rollover.

### 3. Assessment-readiness and representation boundary

- **Request and owner:** Readiness workpapers, open POA/remediation items, approved human statements, assessor-engagement inputs, and source-change log from the engagement lead and accountable organization officials.
- **Validate and limit:** Independently trace a selected readiness statement to current source evidence and preserve unresolved items. This can support human preparation; it cannot submit to DoD, make SPRS or certification representations, or substitute for an authorized assessment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a read-only packet and flag unsupported statements. Only authorized humans make submissions, representations, risk decisions, or closure. Revisit before any assertion or assessor engagement and at annual contract review.
