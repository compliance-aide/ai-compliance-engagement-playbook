# NIST SP 800-221 ICT-risk portfolio engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST ICT-risk content, an enterprise risk decision, a risk appetite statement, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-221](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/221/final) and the organization's approved governance, finance, security, privacy, legal, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round engagement that connects information and communications technology risk to the enterprise risk portfolio without losing the operational detail of individual systems, services, data, suppliers, and initiatives. Maintain a reliable path from local risks, assumptions, impacts, owners, treatments, dependencies, and evidence through aggregation, escalation, management reporting, and enterprise decisions. Make clear what is being rolled up, what remains local, and how changes in one setting alter the wider portfolio.

Treat a risk-register entry, dashboard, score, scenario, assurance report, or management presentation as decision-support evidence rather than a conclusion about risk acceptability or performance. Preserve why risks were grouped, normalized, escalated, deferred, accepted, transferred, or re-evaluated. Retain disagreements, uncertainty, data-quality limits, and the human decisions that determine materiality, appetite, tolerance, and prioritization.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Enterprise risk, executive, board or governing body, finance, security, privacy, technology, supply-chain, business-unit, legal, audit, and records owners establish accountability and make decisions. Operators maintain local and enterprise risk records, ownership and dependency links, aggregation logic, evidence references, treatment status, reporting history, exceptions, decisions, and review records. Reconcile material ICT risks, portfolio mappings, owners, evidence freshness, and treatment status at least quarterly; update after material incidents, acquisitions, service changes, threat shifts, or business decisions; and conduct an annual governance review.

Independent reviewers sample risks from operational evidence through aggregation and management action, testing consistency rather than substituting their judgment for accountable leadership. Auditors assess supplied evidence without changing risk records, selecting priorities, setting appetite or tolerance, accepting risk, approving investments, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize and compare risk records, identify missing ownership or stale evidence, draft workpapers and reports, and prepare review questions. AI cannot determine materiality, set appetite or tolerance, select treatment, accept or transfer risk, make financial or legal conclusions, or attest to conformance.


## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** At engagement start, retain the retrieved version and applicable use terms for the official [NIST SP 800-221 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/221/final), along with approved enterprise-risk, governance, finance, security, privacy, legal, and operational decisions. This is original evidence-planning guidance, not reproduced NIST content, a risk appetite statement, or an enterprise risk decision.

### 1. Local ICT-risk, ownership, and dependency package

- **Request and owner:** Business-unit, technology, security, privacy, supplier, finance, and risk owners provide selected local risk records, scope and system/service context, accountable ownership, impact/assumption statements, dependency links, supporting evidence references, treatment status, and current decision history.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one material ICT risk from operational context and evidence references through stated owner, assumptions, dependencies, and local treatment or escalation state. This can evaluate record traceability; it cannot determine materiality, quantify loss, or judge whether the treatment is sufficient.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize supplied records and flag missing owners, disconnected dependencies, or stale evidence dates. Humans define risk criteria, decide materiality, and approve local treatment. Refresh after a material incident, system/service change, supplier change, acquisition, or significant threat development.

### 2. Aggregation, normalization, and management-reporting package

- **Request and owner:** Enterprise risk, security, privacy, finance, business-unit, technology, board-support, and records owners provide approved aggregation method, portfolio mappings, normalization assumptions, roll-up inputs, dashboard/report history, escalation criteria, data-quality limitations, and meeting records.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample one local ICT risk through its portfolio mapping, documented aggregation or normalization logic, management report, stated limitation, and escalation or review outcome. This can test the supplied decision-support trail; it cannot set appetite, select priorities, or prove the portfolio is complete or accurately predictive.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare supplied roll-up records and identify inconsistent mappings or undocumented assumptions. Humans approve aggregation methods, management reporting, escalation, and priority decisions. Recollect after method changes, reporting anomalies, strategic shifts, or a material change in portfolio composition.

### 3. Treatment, challenge, and governance-follow-through package

- **Request and owner:** Risk, executive, finance, technology, security, privacy, legal, audit, and independent-review owners provide treatment plans, accepted/transfer/escalation decisions, investment or action records, exception register, follow-up evidence, independent challenge workpapers, and governance minutes.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected portfolio action, exception, or escalated risk to accountable human authority, rationale and limitation, due date, progress/review evidence, and present disposition. This can assess decision follow-through; it cannot accept risk, approve investments, make financial/legal conclusions, or attest to enterprise risk management.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag overdue treatments, inconsistent statuses, or absent review records and draft challenge questions. Humans approve risk acceptance, transfer, investment, closure, and external statements. Review quarterly and after a material incident, board decision, acquisition, strategy change, or portfolio-method update.
