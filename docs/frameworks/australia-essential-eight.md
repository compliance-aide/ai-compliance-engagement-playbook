# Australia Essential Eight — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not an ASD assessment or maturity result. Check
> the [current Essential Eight source](https://www.cyber.gov.au/business-government/asds-cyber-security-frameworks/essential-eight/essential-eight-maturity-model)
> at engagement start.

## Engagement focus

Leadership selects a target resilience posture based on mission, threat, and
impact. Record a consistent boundary and deviation approach; have strategy
owners document safeguards and compensating measures; validate operational
evidence; review exceptions on a schedule; and test incident/recovery
assumptions.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Leadership owns targets and exceptions. Independent reviewers assess evidence under their
defined mandate. AI may maintain the goal, exception, and evidence ledger and
flag uneven maturity or unsupported conclusions; it cannot determine a formal
result, accept risk, approve an exception, alter systems, or make a maturity claim. Recalibrate target, investment, and risk acceptance annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the current [ASD Essential Eight maturity model](https://www.cyber.gov.au/business-government/asds-cyber-security-frameworks/essential-eight/essential-eight-maturity-model) and organization-approved scope; checked 2026-07-31. This is original engagement language, not ASD material or a maturity assessment. It does not reproduce model content, select a target level, certify implementation, or make a maturity claim; authorized leadership and assessment authorities retain those decisions.

### 1. Target posture, scope, and exception package

- **Request and owner:** Approved mission/threat/impact inputs, in-scope environment and user/device inventory, target-posture decision, safeguard-boundary statement, exceptions, compensating-measure records, and ownership assignments from leadership, security, and technology owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected target or exception to its approved rationale, scope, owner, expiry, and compensating measure. This supports a consistent decision record; it cannot determine the appropriate target or a formal maturity result.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved scope metadata and flag expired exceptions. Humans approve targets and risk decisions. Refresh after material threat, technology, mission, or boundary changes.

### 2. Safeguard-operation and recovery package

- **Request and owner:** Human-approved safeguard implementation descriptions, configuration or administrative evidence, time-bounded operational records, backup/recovery evidence, incident exercise results, and remediation/retest records from security, endpoint, identity, application, and operations owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a sampled declared safeguard through its scoped asset/user population to contemporaneous evidence and an exception or test result. This can support a factual evidence observation; it cannot prove universal deployment, resilience, or maturity.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index authorized evidence and surface gaps in dates or ownership; it may not change configurations or close findings. Recollect after technology changes, recovery tests, incidents, or significant control findings.

### 3. Assessment-readiness and leadership-review package

- **Request and owner:** Assessment workpapers, evidence index, unresolved deviations, management-review inputs, investment decisions, independent-review observations, and source-change log from security and leadership owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Reperform a selected evidence trace from stated target through evidence and open deviation, documenting limitations. This supports readiness for a human-led assessment; it cannot replace an ASD-authorized assessment or approve risk acceptance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a read-only evidence packet and draft questions. Humans approve resource decisions, exceptions, public statements, and closure. Revisit annually and before any maturity representation.
