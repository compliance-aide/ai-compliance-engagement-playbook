# NIST SP 800-234 high-performance-computing overlay engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST overlay content, a control selection, a system authorization, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-234](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/234/final) and the organization's approved mission, security, architecture, privacy, legal, operational, and risk decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round engagement for applying and maintaining a high-performance-computing security overlay in the actual mission and system context. Connect the in-scope HPC environment, performance requirements, workloads, data, architecture, inherited services, applicable security expectations, local tailoring rationale, implementation evidence, assessment approach, exceptions, and improvement work. Treat an overlay as a structured tailoring aid, not as a substitute for accountable scoping, control decisions, evidence, or authorization.

Keep the rationale behind tailoring visible, particularly where performance, specialized hardware, research activity, user environments, or operational constraints influence the decision. A control matrix, architecture diagram, configuration record, assessment result, system plan, waiver, or performance test is evidence to evaluate in context—not automatic proof that a requirement applies, has been satisfied, or is acceptable for a mission. Preserve decisions, assumptions, unresolved gaps, compensating measures, and reassessment triggers.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Mission, HPC operations, security, architecture, risk, system, data, privacy, legal, export-control, performance, supplier, assessment, and records owners establish scope and make accountable decisions. Operators maintain overlay applicability and tailoring records, system and workload inventories, inherited-service evidence, implementation and assessment artifacts, performance-impact records, exceptions, remediation plans, and change history. Reconcile scope, workloads, architecture, inherited dependencies, tailoring decisions, and evidence freshness at least quarterly; reassess before major platform or mission changes; and conduct an annual management review.

Independent reviewers sample a tailored security expectation from scope and rationale through implementation, evidence, and corrective action. Auditors assess supplied records without choosing controls, tailoring requirements, changing cluster settings, accepting risk, authorizing the system, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize evidence and tailoring records, identify missing rationale or stale assessments, draft workpapers, compare supplied records with approved decisions, and prepare review questions. AI cannot select or tailor binding controls, approve compensating measures, alter HPC systems, accept risk, authorize operations, make legal conclusions, or attest to conformance.


## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [NIST SP 800-234 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/234/final) and [NIST copyright and reuse information](https://www.nist.gov/disclaimer) at engagement start; checked 2026-07-31. This is original engagement guidance, not reproduced overlay text or an authorization package. Security, mission, and legal owners decide external source use.

### 1. Mission, HPC boundary, and overlay-applicability package

- **Request and owner:** Mission, system, HPC operations, and security owners provide the approved mission statement, system/workload and data inventory, cluster and inherited-service boundary, performance constraints, applicable security expectations, overlay applicability record, and accountable-owner map.
- **Validate and limit:** Independently trace one representative workload and inherited service to its boundary, mission constraint, applicable expectation, and accountable owner. This validates a bounded decision trail; it does not determine applicability for every workload or authorize the HPC environment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize supplied inventories and identify absent owners, stale scope dates, or inconsistent boundary references. Named human mission and security owners approve scope and applicability. Refresh before a material mission, data, workload, provider, or platform change.

### 2. Tailoring, implementation, and performance-impact package

- **Request and owner:** Security, architecture, and HPC engineering provide approved tailoring rationale, implementation references, compensating-measure decisions, performance test plans/results, configuration/change records, and documented limitations.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected tailored expectation from rationale through approved implementation, relevant performance evidence, limitations, and exception or compensating-measure decision. This does not prove all implementations operate effectively, validate specialized hardware, or approve a tradeoff.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may assemble read-only trace matrices and flag missing rationale or contradictory version references. Humans select and tailor requirements, approve design/change decisions, and accept any residual risk. Refresh after a design, hardware, performance, or control-impact change.

### 3. Assessment, exception, and improvement package

- **Request and owner:** Assessment, risk, and operations owners provide assessment scope/results, finding and remediation registers, waivers, retest evidence, supplier issues, reassessment triggers, and management-review records.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample one finding or exception from discovery through owner, decision authority, planned corrective action, evidence of retest, and current status. This confirms a recorded lifecycle, not assessment completeness, closure validity, or system authorization.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may sort findings, flag overdue actions, and draft reviewer questions without changing records or outcomes. Independent reviewers challenge evidence; named human authorities approve exceptions and closure. Refresh after a failed assessment, significant exception, incident, or annual review.
