# FIPS 140-3 cryptographic-module engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not the FIPS publication, CMVP instructions, a validation certificate, an authorization decision, or a compliance claim. Confirm current requirements and validation status through [FIPS 140-3](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/140-3/final) and the organization’s approved security, legal, procurement, engineering, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage the organizational use, procurement, integration, and lifecycle oversight of cryptographic modules where FIPS 140-3 is applicable. Maintain a governed inventory that ties each in-scope product, module boundary, version, operating mode, deployment, dependency, approved use, validation-status evidence, and change history to the relevant system and risk decision. Treat a product claim, a certificate reference, or a successful technical check as evidence to verify—not proof that a deployed configuration is within a validated boundary, suitable for its use, or compliant.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, security, cryptography or platform engineering, architecture, procurement, product or service owner, system owner, change-management, supplier-management, legal, privacy where relevant, and records-management roles. Operators maintain an in-scope cryptographic-module inventory; approved product, version, and configuration baselines; validation-status and security-policy evidence; deployment and integration records; exception decisions; upgrade, patch, and end-of-support tracking; supplier assurances; and system-to-module traceability. Reconcile deployed modules and versions quarterly; review validation status, material configuration changes, supplier notices, exceptions, and deprecations at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material system, cryptographic, provider, or regulatory changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples inventory-to-deployment traceability and exception handling; auditors test evidence without selecting cryptography, changing system configuration, approving a boundary, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied inventory, deployment, supplier, and validation-status evidence, identify missing traceability or stale reviews, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot determine applicability, validate a module, interpret a certificate for a deployment, configure cryptography, approve an exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.
