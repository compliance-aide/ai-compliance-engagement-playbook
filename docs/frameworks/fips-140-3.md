# FIPS 140-3 cryptographic-module engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not the FIPS publication, CMVP instructions, a validation certificate, an authorization decision, or a compliance claim. Confirm current requirements and validation status through [FIPS 140-3](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/140-3/final) and the organization’s approved security, legal, procurement, engineering, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage the organizational use, procurement, integration, and lifecycle oversight of cryptographic modules where FIPS 140-3 is applicable. Maintain a governed inventory that ties each in-scope product, module boundary, version, operating mode, deployment, dependency, approved use, validation-status evidence, and change history to the relevant system and risk decision. Treat a product claim, a certificate reference, or a successful technical check as evidence to verify—not proof that a deployed configuration is within a validated boundary, suitable for its use, or compliant.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, security, cryptography or platform engineering, architecture, procurement, product or service owner, system owner, change-management, supplier-management, legal, privacy where relevant, and records-management roles. Operators maintain an in-scope cryptographic-module inventory; approved product, version, and configuration baselines; validation-status and security-policy evidence; deployment and integration records; exception decisions; upgrade, patch, and end-of-support tracking; supplier assurances; and system-to-module traceability. Reconcile deployed modules and versions quarterly; review validation status, material configuration changes, supplier notices, exceptions, and deprecations at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material system, cryptographic, provider, or regulatory changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples inventory-to-deployment traceability and exception handling; auditors test evidence without selecting cryptography, changing system configuration, approving a boundary, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied inventory, deployment, supplier, and validation-status evidence, identify missing traceability or stale reviews, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot determine applicability, validate a module, interpret a certificate for a deployment, configure cryptography, approve an exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the current [FIPS 140-3 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/140-3/final) and the [CMVP program](https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/cryptographic-module-validation-program) as official sources; both are public NIST program material, retrieved 2026-07-31. This guide uses original operational language and does not reproduce FIPS requirements, validation criteria, security policies, or certificate content. Cryptography, procurement, system, and authorization officials determine applicability and approved use.

### 1. Module boundary and approved-use package

- **Request and owner:** Cryptography engineering, architecture, and system owners provide the in-scope product/module inventory, version and operating-mode baseline, intended deployment boundary, approved-use record, and system-to-module association for the review period.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected inventory item through its stated boundary, version, environment, accountable owner, and approval record. This can support traceability of an organization’s inventory; it cannot establish that a module is validated, that a deployment remains inside a validated boundary, or that a cryptographic design is appropriate.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare supplied inventory fields and flag absent owners or version drift. Named human authorities decide boundary and use. Refresh after a module, version, operating-mode, hosting, or system change.

### 2. Validation-status and supplier package

- **Request and owner:** Procurement, supplier management, security, and product owners provide vendor attestations or official status references, acquisition records, security-policy references where licitly held, supplier notices, end-of-support status, and review timestamps.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect a selected product/version reference against the organization’s recorded supplier source, acquisition scope, and deployment entry, preserving licensed materials outside this repository. This can support a documented due-diligence trail; it cannot interpret a certificate, confirm a supplier claim, or make a procurement approval.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize references and flag stale supplier evidence. Procurement and cryptography owners decide suitability and escalation. Refresh after supplier notice, validation-status change, renewal, or acquisition.

### 3. Deployment-change and exception package

- **Request and owner:** Platform operations, change management, security, and risk owners provide controlled deployment records, configuration/baseline references, change approvals, exception register entries, incident links, and periodic reconciliation results.
- **Validate and limit:** Reperform a limited inventory-to-deployment comparison for a selected system and inspect a change or exception through named approval and review date. This can show a controlled evidence trail, not cryptographic correctness, operational effectiveness, risk acceptance validity, or authorization.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may identify unmatched records and overdue reviews; it must not access keys or alter settings. Authorized humans decide exceptions and risk. Refresh after a material change, finding, incident, or annual renewal.
