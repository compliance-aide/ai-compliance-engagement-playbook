# ISO/IEC 27018:2025 — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance for public-cloud PII processing, not legal advice. Use authorized
> material and check the [ISO record](https://www.iso.org/standard/27018).

## Engagement focus

Maintain processing/service inventory, instructions, regions, subprocessors,
data lifecycle, deletion/return expectations, and rights/incident escalation.
Reconcile service changes and operations with instructions and contracts; retain
approved service/data-flow records, decisions, handoffs, and available attestations.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Operators manage service operations and contract execution. Independent reviewers
test claims against scoped evidence and contracts. AI detects incomplete
instructions and stale artifacts and drafts non-legal questions; it cannot decide
lawful processing or unnecessarily process sensitive data. Close with annual
governance review and supplier reassessment.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Confirm the edition through the [ISO/IEC 27018 record](https://www.iso.org/standard/27018) and use authorized material only as permitted; checked 2026-07-31. ISO content is licensed. This original plan does not reproduce ISO/IEC text, provide legal advice, determine lawful processing, or make a certification claim.

### 1. Processing instruction and cloud-PII boundary package

- **Request and owner:** Privacy, service, and cloud owners provide the service/processing inventory, documented customer or controller instructions, data categories, regions, processing purposes, responsible contacts, and approved data-flow references.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected processing activity to an accountable owner, stated instruction, service boundary, and data-flow evidence. This supports a reviewable operational boundary; it cannot determine legal basis, jurisdictional applicability, or completeness of processing records.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize authorized metadata and flag missing ownership or stale instructions. Humans determine privacy obligations and approve processing changes. Refresh after a new purpose, region, data category, service, or instruction change.

### 2. Lifecycle, subprocessor, and rights-handling package

- **Request and owner:** Service, privacy, and supplier owners provide retention/deletion or return procedures, subprocessor records, approved changes, rights-request handoffs, and available execution evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect a selected lifecycle or request handoff from instruction through accountable action, evidence, exception, and follow-up. This supports traceability of the selected activity; it cannot prove deletion, legal sufficiency, or every subprocessor obligation.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may identify missing handoff fields and index evidence; it may not process sensitive data beyond authorization, decide a request, or approve a supplier. Recollect after a request failure, new subprocessor, data-transfer change, or deletion exception.

### 3. Incident, assurance, and governance package

- **Request and owner:** Incident, privacy, service, and executive owners provide escalation paths, incident/notification decision records, supplier-assurance materials, unresolved issues, and annual review decisions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected incident or assurance concern to its source evidence, human decision authority, communications status, mitigation, and follow-up. This supports accountable governance; it cannot decide reportability, make a legal conclusion, or substitute for external assurance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a source-linked briefing and flag overdue follow-up. Humans approve notification, risk acceptance, supplier decisions, and public statements. Review annually and after a material incident or supplier change.
