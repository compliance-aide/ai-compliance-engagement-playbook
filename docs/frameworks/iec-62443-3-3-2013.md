# IEC 62443-3-3:2013 IACS system security engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not IEC text, a control selection, a security-level determination, a safety decision, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through the [IEC 62443-3-3:2013 publication record](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/7033).

## Engagement focus

Maintain a system-security evidence record that connects the industrial system boundary, accountable operations and engineering owners, safety and process constraints, risk-informed design inputs, authorized technical evidence, exceptions, remediation, and independent review. Treat the record as a way to prepare a defensible engagement without converting an AI summary into an engineering or assurance conclusion.

## Roles and annual rhythm

OT engineering, operations, safety, maintenance, security, risk, and supplier authorities retain accountable decisions; system and service owners preserve authorized evidence; independent reviewers test traceability, evidence quality, and safety-aware readiness. AI may organize authorized records, identify unresolved evidence questions, draft workpapers, and prepare non-authoritative status views, but cannot select or approve safeguards, determine security levels, decide a safety or engineering outcome, accept risk, alter OT or production configuration, initiate scans or changes, or claim compliance. Review the evidence set quarterly, reassess it annually, and reopen it before material system, process, supplier, safety, incident, or design changes.


## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [IEC 62443-3-3 publication record](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/7033) and an authorized copy; checked 2026-07-31. IEC text is licensed. This plan does not reproduce requirements, choose safeguards, determine security levels, or make safety/engineering/compliance conclusions.

### 1. IACS boundary, connectivity, and safety constraint record

- **Request and owner:** Industrial asset/system inventory, approved boundary and connectivity representation, operations/engineering ownership, safety/process constraints, supplier or integrator responsibilities, and risk-design inputs.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected asset or connection to the approved boundary, owner, maintenance/safety constraint, and supplier responsibility. This can support a safety-aware engagement population; it cannot determine a security level, validate an engineering design, or prove full asset coverage.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize authorized metadata and flag undocumented dependencies. OT, safety, engineering, and risk authorities approve scope and decisions. Revisit before material process, asset, supplier, architecture, or safety change.

### 2. Authorized OT security-operation evidence

- **Request and owner:** Read-only configuration or maintenance records, remote-access/vendor-maintenance approvals, patch/vulnerability disposition, change-window records, monitoring/incident evidence, and backup/recovery exercise records from OT operations and engineering.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect authorized records and trace an approved selection to asset, owner, change window, exception, and follow-up without active scanning or modification. This can support evidence quality and operating traceability; it cannot establish effectiveness under all conditions or justify disruptive testing.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index records and identify gaps; it may not scan, change, or access OT systems beyond approved boundaries. Recollect after an incident, maintenance/change window, vulnerability disposition, or supplier change.

### 3. Exception, remediation, and independent-review record

- **Request and owner:** Risk/compensating-measure decisions, remediation plan, safety review inputs, retest criteria/results, and independent-review workpapers, from accountable authorities.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected exception to its safety/risk authority, expiry, operational constraint, evidence, and retest. This can support governed follow-through; it cannot accept risk, decide safety, or replace an independent engineering assessment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may create a read-only review packet and flag expired decisions. Humans approve all risk, safety, remediation, and external actions. Review quarterly, annually, and before material design change.
