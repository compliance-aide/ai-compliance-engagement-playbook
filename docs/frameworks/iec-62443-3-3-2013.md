# IEC 62443-3-3:2013 IACS system security engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not IEC text, a security-level determination or an industrial safety decision.

## Source and applicability

The [IEC publication record](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/7033), checked 2026-09-04, describes technical control-system requirements and capability security levels used with the defined system, zones and conduits. It states that the April 2014 corrigendum is included. Confirm the exact licensed edition and corrections used by the engagement. Full requirements, enhancements, applicability and evaluation rules remain unverified for this draft.

Do not reproduce protected IEC requirements here. Obtain authorized source access before detailed evaluation. Distinguish the risk-derived target, a claimed capability and the behavior demonstrated by the actual integrated configuration; none alone proves the others.

## Engagement focus

Trace each approved system-security requirement to its implementation, dependencies, test objective, evidence and remaining limitation. A component datasheet or certificate is an input, not automatic proof for the whole deployed system.

## Roles

Asset owners approve scope and risk decisions. Engineering, operations, safety, maintenance and security authorities select and approve technical solutions and test conditions. Suppliers explain implementation constraints. An independent reviewer challenges evidence and conclusion boundaries. AI may organize authorized records and draft workpapers; it cannot determine security levels, approve safeguards, scan IACS, alter configurations, accept risk or declare safe operation.

## Before starting

Record system version, operating modes, assets, zones, conduits, interfaces, owners and approved target decisions. Obtain licensed requirement references and scope decisions. Define evidence permissions and confidentiality. Default to existing records; documentation access does not authorize network discovery or login. Any technical testing needs named operational authorization, safe environment, stop conditions and recovery arrangements.

## Ordered workflow

1. **Reconcile scope and targets.** Compare the approved system boundary with assets, connections and supplier responsibilities. Preserve unknown interfaces and changed configurations. Resolve missing target rationale with the risk/design owner before evaluating dependent claims.
2. **Build requirement traceability.** Within the authorized workspace, record every applicable requirement and enhancement, its source/version, rationale, responsible owner and intended implementation. Have qualified owners approve applicability; do not drop difficult requirements silently.
3. **Establish implementation evidence.** Link each requirement to actual configuration, design and operating records. Record shared services and customer/provider responsibilities. Separate available product capability from enabled and correctly integrated functionality.
4. **Approve verification objectives.** Define expected outcomes, relevant modes, representative configuration, negative cases and limits. Review operational and safety interactions before any proposed test. Isolated lab results must state differences from the operational system; they are not live-system proof.
5. **Evaluate permitted evidence.** Compare observations with the approved objective and preserve raw results, failures and missing coverage. Test execution without a usable result is not a pass. Record the exact version and conditions for each supported claim; no live test is authorized by this guide.
6. **Resolve gaps and retest.** Assign defects, legacy limitations and compensating-measure proposals to accountable owners. Obtain change and risk approvals before implementation. Retest affected requirements and dependencies; an exception approval does not change a failed technical observation into a pass.
7. **Report and maintain the baseline.** Deliver requirement-level findings and claim limits to the authorized reviewer. Keep target, capability and demonstrated outcomes distinct. Reopen affected evidence after configuration, supplier, topology, threat or operating-mode changes.

## Evidence and test plan

Account for the full scoped population of assets, interfaces, requirements, enhancements, tests and exceptions. Preserve all evidence supplied for assessment without silent trimming or sampling. Complete accounting does not establish that all technical combinations were tested; disclose actual coverage.

### 1. IACS boundary, connectivity, and safety constraint record

- **Request and owner:** Industrial asset/system inventory, approved zones/conduits and connectivity representation, operations/engineering ownership, safety/process constraints, supplier or integrator responsibilities, risk-design inputs, and a human-approved target-security-level decision record.
- **Validate and limit:** Reconcile all scoped assets and connections and trace each to the approved zone/conduit, owner, maintenance/safety constraint, supplier responsibility, and target-level decision authority. This can support a safety-aware engagement population; it cannot determine a security level, validate an engineering design, or prove full asset coverage.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize authorized metadata and flag undocumented dependencies. OT, safety, engineering, and risk authorities approve scope and decisions. Revisit before material process, asset, supplier, architecture, or safety change.

### 2. Authorized OT security-operation evidence

- **Request and owner:** Read-only configuration or maintenance records, remote-access/vendor-maintenance approvals, patch/vulnerability disposition, change-window records, monitoring/incident evidence, and backup/recovery exercise records from OT operations and engineering.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect authorized records and trace each scoped record to asset, owner, change window, exception, and follow-up without active scanning or modification. This can support evidence quality and operating traceability; it cannot establish effectiveness under all conditions or justify disruptive testing.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index records and identify gaps; it may not scan, change, or access OT systems beyond approved boundaries. Recollect after an incident, maintenance/change window, vulnerability disposition, or supplier change.

### 3. Exception, remediation, and independent-review record

- **Request and owner:** Risk/compensating-measure decisions, remediation plan, safety review inputs, retest criteria/results, and independent-review workpapers, from accountable authorities.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace every scoped exception to its safety/risk authority, expiry, operational constraint, evidence, and retest. This can support governed follow-through; it cannot accept risk, decide safety, or replace an independent engineering assessment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may create a read-only review packet and flag expired decisions. Humans approve all risk, safety, remediation, and external actions. Review at approved intervals and before material design change.

## Failure branches and decisions

- Supplier certificate covers a different version or configuration: retain it as bounded background evidence and obtain applicable system evidence.
- One shared service supports several requirements: identify every dependent result affected by its failure rather than closing only one ticket.
- A test could disrupt the process: stop the proposed activity and obtain qualified operational/safety review.
- Target is mistaken for achieved behavior: correct the claim and retain the verification gap.
- Compensating measure is approved but untested: keep authorization and effectiveness as separate assertions.

Use [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md) statuses: supported, not_supported, inconclusive, not_applicable and not_tested. They describe evidence assertions, not security levels or risk acceptance.

## Cadence and renewal

Use risk-based review intervals and governing requirements approved for the engagement. Trigger reassessment on relevant changes, incidents, expired evidence or failed controls. Do not invent universal quarterly checks or annual certification duties.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver the versioned boundary, target references, requirement traceability, configuration evidence, verification coverage, exceptions and open actions with owners and dates.
