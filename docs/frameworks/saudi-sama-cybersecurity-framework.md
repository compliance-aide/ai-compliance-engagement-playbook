# Saudi SAMA Cyber Security Framework engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not SAMA framework text, a maturity assessment, a waiver request, or a compliance claim. Confirm the applicable scope and current rulebook position through the [SAMA Cyber Security Framework](https://rulebook.sama.gov.sa/en/cyber-security-framework-2).

## Engagement focus

Maintain an evidence-backed cybersecurity operating record for the regulated financial organization: scope and legal-entity determination, accountable leadership, information assets and services, risk and governance decisions, operational safeguards, suppliers, incidents, maturity evidence, remediation, and periodic review. Preserve the boundaries between internal self-assessment, independent assurance, management decisions, and SAMA’s regulatory authority.

## Roles and annual rhythm

The board and senior management retain accountable oversight; cybersecurity and risk leaders maintain program evidence; technology and operations owners retain system records; procurement maintains supplier evidence; internal audit and independent reviewers challenge evidence; qualified Saudi regulatory counsel handles regulatory interpretation. AI may organize approved records, locate gaps, and draft questions, but cannot conduct or score a maturity assessment, accept risk, authorize compensating measures, request a waiver, report to SAMA, or claim compliance. Review continuously through material changes and perform a documented annual engagement review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Retain the official [SAMA Cyber Security Framework rulebook page](https://rulebook.sama.gov.sa/en/cyber-security-framework-2), retrieved 2026-07-31, and have authorized financial-organization leadership and regulatory counsel confirm the applicable framework version, scope, status, and permitted use. This is original engagement-planning language, not SAMA framework text, a maturity score, a waiver request, a regulatory submission, or a compliance claim. Preserve regulated and customer information in authorized repositories.

### 1. Regulated-entity boundary, governance, and risk-accountability package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain the approved legal-entity and service boundary, regulated activities/context, board and senior-management accountability record, cybersecurity/risk role roster, information/service inventory, risk-governance decisions, and counsel-led applicability questions from leadership, risk, cybersecurity, legal, finance, and operations owners. Define population, reporting period, and excluded entities before review.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected service, risk decision, or governance responsibility to a named accountable owner, approved boundary, dated management/board record where applicable, and open question or authorized decision. This supports a reviewable governance boundary; it cannot determine regulatory scope, set risk appetite, approve a waiver, or establish complete coverage.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved metadata and flag missing owners, committee dates, or risk-decision links. Board/senior management and counsel decide scope, risk, and regulatory escalation. Trigger on organizational or product change, acquisition, material risk event, regulatory update, or annual planning.

### 2. Financial-service operations, access, and resilience-evidence package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain approved policy/procedure references, access and change/release approvals, operations/monitoring or test references, vulnerability and incident records, continuity/recovery exercise references, and approved exceptions from technology, cybersecurity, operations, resilience, and service owners. Keep sensitive system and customer outputs in controlled repositories.
- **Validate and limit:** For a selected critical service, access event, change, or exercise, inspect the evidence trail from accountable approval through time-bounded operating or test reference, exception/incident record, corrective action, and retest link. Verify provenance and reviewer authorization. This supports traceability of stated operation; it cannot prove effectiveness, uninterrupted service, security, or framework conformity.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved metadata and identify missing approvals, dates, or action links; it cannot operate financial systems, change access, score maturity, or modify evidence. Named technology and security leaders decide changes and response. Trigger on material release, access change, incident, failed exercise/test, or annual evidence refresh.

### 3. Supplier, assurance, and regulatory-readiness package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain supplier responsibility and assurance references, internal-audit/independent-review observations, risk or exception approvals, remediation plans, retest evidence, management-review records, and counsel-approved regulatory-communication or waiver-preparation boundaries from procurement, cybersecurity, risk, internal audit, legal, and leadership.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected supplier dependency, finding, or exception to the service boundary, accountable owner, approved decision, evidence source, corrective action, and retest or documented closure rationale. Record unresolved issues and external-communication limits. This supports accountable assurance readiness; it cannot determine supplier compliance, accept risk, approve a waiver, or authorize a SAMA communication.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a source-linked remediation queue and flag overdue owners or expiring decisions. Internal audit and independent reviewers challenge traceability; named humans approve risk, closure, and external communication. Trigger on supplier change, audit finding, material incident, source update, or annual readiness cycle.
