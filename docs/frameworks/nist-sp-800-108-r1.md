# NIST SP 800-108 Revision 1 key-derivation engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST key-derivation content, cryptographic implementation instructions, a validation claim, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-108 Revision 1](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/108/r1/upd1/final) and the organization’s approved security, cryptography, engineering, procurement, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Operate a governed lifecycle for in-scope cryptographic key-derivation uses across applications, modules, protocols, services, and suppliers. Maintain traceability from business purpose and protected flow through source-key custody reference, derivation-use inventory, approved implementation and version baseline, test and validation evidence, deployment, change history, exceptions, supplier dependencies, and lifecycle review. A product setting, test result, or vendor statement is evidence to assess; it does not prove correct cryptographic design, source-key protection, key separation, validation, or compliance.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, security, cryptography or platform engineering, architecture, application and product owners, procurement, supplier-management, change-management, incident-response, and records-management roles. Operators maintain an in-scope derivation-use inventory, approved purpose and owner records, source-key lifecycle references, module or provider versions, test and validation-status evidence, deployment and change records, exception decisions, supplier assurance, and system-to-use traceability. Reconcile uses, owners, dependencies, and deployed versions quarterly; review technical guidance status, source-key or implementation changes, test outcomes, supplier notices, exceptions, and unresolved risks at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material system, protocol, provider, cryptographic, or threat changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples use-to-deployment-to-test traceability; auditors test the evidence trail without deriving or accessing keys, changing production cryptography, approving exceptions, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied inventory, lifecycle, test, supplier, and review evidence, identify missing ownership or stale standards-status records, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot design or implement key derivation, derive or access keys, change production configuration, determine validation, approve an exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.
