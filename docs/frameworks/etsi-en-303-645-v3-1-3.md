# ETSI EN 303 645 V3.1.3 consumer-IoT engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not ETSI standard text, a certification method, or a conformity determination.

## Source and applicability

Confirm the edition in the [ETSI publication record](https://portal.etsi.org/webapp/workprogram/Report_WorkItem.asp?WKI_ID=69970), which identifies V3.1.3, September 2024. Record the intended market and any separate regulatory or certification basis; the work item is not designated a harmonised standard.

[EN 303 645 V3.1.3](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_en/303600_303699/303645/03.01.03_60/en_303645v030103p.pdf), clause 4, establishes a baseline rather than a testing or certification method and points to TS 103 701 for a conformance-assessment method. Obtain the applicable assessment method before claiming test coverage.

Clause 5.0 requires justification for recommendations considered inapplicable or unfulfilled. Annex B distinguishes mandatory, recommended, conditional and feature-dependent provisions. Preserve those distinctions and their conditions; a missing feature and an unimplemented requirement are different cases. Annex B permits reuse of its pro forma for its intended purpose; this guide links to it rather than reproducing it. Its Y/N/N/A entries are implementation statements, separate from the runbook's evidence-result labels.

## Engagement focus

Build a traceable product record across hardware revisions, firmware, companion applications, associated services, interfaces, suppliers, support windows, vulnerability handling and retirement. Identify every version/cohort in scope before choosing any test case. A successful test on one device does not establish fleet-wide behavior.

## Roles

Product owner approves scope and support commitments; engineering owns implementation and device/version records; service operations owns cloud evidence; security owns vulnerability triage; privacy and legal owners decide data and market obligations. Qualified testers perform authorized tests. AI can reconcile records, draft test procedures and execute explicitly authorized bounded tooling; capabilities do not confer authority to change devices or release software. Independent reviewers challenge results; a named human approves external claims.

## Before starting

Follow the [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md). Obtain the selected source and complete provision inventory, product boundary, owners, evidence permissions and test authorization. Identify device safety constraints, recovery procedure, test account, laboratory network and approved QA devices. Never use customer devices or accounts for destructive tests. A text-only agent prepares exact requests and expected observations; a tool-capable agent records commands, versions, outputs and failure states within its authorization.

## Ordered workflow

| Step | Action and owner | Output and exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Freeze scope | Product owner identifies models, hardware/firmware variants, services, markets and support periods. | Approved boundary and complete cohort inventory, including offline and retired devices where relevant. |
| 2. Map provisions | Reviewer reconciles each source provision with features, conditions and evidence owners. | Complete disposition ledger; exclusions need rationale and approval, not absent evidence. |
| 3. Plan evidence | Engineering and service owners link design claims to build, configuration and operational records. | One work item per assertion with source locator, version, period and observable acceptance criterion. |
| 4. Prepare tests | Tester selects the governing assessment method and safe procedures. | Authorized QA plan, prerequisites, expected observations, recovery and stop conditions. |
| 5. Collect and execute | Custodians supply records; authorized tester runs the agreed checks. | Original evidence and exact coverage retained; collector errors logged separately from product failures. |
| 6. Reconcile | Reviewer compares assertions, implementation statements and observed behavior. | Evidence result per assertion, disagreement log and explicit untested populations. |
| 7. Remediate and retest | Engineering corrects confirmed defects through approved release controls. | Fresh version-specific test evidence and distribution evidence; a release ticket is not installation proof. |
| 8. Handoff | Product owner reviews support, residual gaps and public representations. | Approved bounded report or open-action package with owners and due dates. |

## Failure branches and decisions

- Firmware on the device differs from the report: bind findings to each version and collect current evidence; do not silently transfer results.
- Update service returns success but the device remains on the old build: preserve both observations and investigate delivery versus installation. Classify the installation assertion from device evidence.
- Offline devices lack telemetry: retain their count and unknown state; do not remove them from the denominator or count them as updated.
- A recommendation is unfulfilled: record rationale and review it; do not recast the recommendation as mandatory or erase it from coverage.
- Feature applicability is disputed: retain architecture and observed behavior, with an owner to resolve it. Missing documentation alone is not an exclusion.
- Test tooling fails before observing the product: record `not_tested`, the error and a recovery owner. Do not report a product pass or defect from that error alone.
- A test could cause unsafe operation or loss of recoverability: stop that test and obtain a revised authorized method; preserve the coverage gap.
- A vulnerability or support deadline changes: reopen affected model/service claims and route notices to the authorized decision maker.

## Worked handoff example

Fictional QA device A is on firmware 1.0. An approved update job reports successful delivery of 1.1, but the device's authenticated version readback still shows 1.0 after the agreed completion window. The assertion “A installed 1.1” is `not_supported`; delivery success alone cannot close it. Engineering investigates installation logs and retests after a correction. Device B is offline with no current readback: its installation state is `inconclusive`. Keep both devices in the population. Neither result establishes conformity or permits a customer notice.

## Evidence and test plan


**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [ETSI EN 303 645 work-item record](https://portal.etsi.org/webapp/workprogram/Report_WorkItem.asp?WKI_ID=69970) and an authorized edition; prior snapshot 2026-07-31; recheck at engagement start. ETSI materials have applicable rights conditions. This plan is original operational guidance, does not reproduce the standard, and does not create a consumer-IoT security or conformity claim.

### 1. Product, service, interface, and support-window record

- **Request and owner:** Product/model and firmware inventory, companion service/interface inventory, intended-use record, support/update-period decisions, dependency/supplier list, and named product and operations owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected consumer product/version to its service interfaces, owner, support decision, and dependency records. This can surface unowned service or version drift; it cannot prove complete asset discovery, secure design, or that a stated support period will be met.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile supplied inventories and flag inconsistent support dates. Humans approve intended use, support, and product decisions. Refresh after model, firmware, interface, supplier, or service change.

### 2. Secure update, configuration, and operational evidence

- **Request and owner:** Update/release records, configuration/default-decision references, test/quality evidence, deployment telemetry summaries, service operational records, and approved exceptions from engineering and operations owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect a selected update path from approved version through test reference, release decision, deployment evidence, and exception. This can support a bounded lifecycle trace; it cannot test a product, prove update delivery to all devices, or authorize a release.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index version records and flag missing approval or test references; it may not modify devices, services, or release systems. Humans approve release and operational exceptions. Recollect after release failures, configuration changes, or service incidents.

### 3. Vulnerability disclosure, incident, and end-of-life workpaper

- **Request and owner:** Vulnerability intake/contact records, triage/remediation and notification decisions, incident records, supplier advisories, customer communication approvals, end-of-life notices, and closure/retest evidence from security, support, and product owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected report or incident to affected product/version, owner, response decision, customer communication authority, remediation evidence, and known limitations. This can support transparent response governance; it cannot independently assess severity, decide disclosure duties, or conclude product conformance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a redacted case index and flag overdue actions. Humans approve severity, disclosure, customer notices, and closure. Review quarterly and after a vulnerability, incident, supplier, or end-of-life event.


## Cadence and renewal

Use the agreed review schedule and reopen affected work after firmware, hardware, service, supplier, support-period, vulnerability, incident or source changes. Track which prior evidence remains valid and which needs recollection. Periodic review does not replace event-triggered response.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver the source/version manifest, full provision-disposition ledger, product/cohort inventory, evidence index, test records, unresolved discrepancies, remediation/retest records and next owners. Keep implementation statement, evidence confidence and certification decision separate. Independent source, skeptical and rights review plus named human publication approval remain required. No successful case or structural validator proves that every product version conforms.
