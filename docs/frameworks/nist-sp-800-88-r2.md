# NIST SP 800-88 Revision 2 media-sanitization engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST sanitization content, a command to erase or destroy media, a disposal authorization, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-88 Revision 2](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/88/r2/final) and the organization’s approved security, legal, privacy, records, environmental, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Operate a governed media-sanitization program for in-scope storage, devices, removable media, cloud or supplier media responsibilities, and disposition channels. Maintain traceability from asset and data sensitivity through custody, approved disposition decision, authorized technique, execution evidence, verification, certificate or record of completion, exceptions, supplier handoff, and retention. An asset ticket, erase log, destruction certificate, or vendor statement is evidence to assess; it does not prove the target data is unrecoverable, every copy was in scope, lawful retention obligations were met, or compliance.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, security, asset management, data or system owners, privacy, legal, records-management, facilities or environmental management where relevant, procurement, supplier-management, IT operations, incident-response, and internal audit or independent review roles. Operators maintain media and asset inventories, data-sensitivity and retention references, custody records, approved sanitization and disposition decisions, execution and verification evidence, certificates, supplier-chain evidence, exceptions, incident records, and disposal metrics. Reconcile retiring assets, media, and supplier responsibilities quarterly; review incomplete dispositions, exceptions, verification failures, legal holds, supplier performance, and unresolved risks at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material technology, supplier, data-classification, retention, or regulatory changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples assets from custody through approved disposition and verification evidence; auditors test the evidence trail without erasing or destroying media, releasing legal holds, approving disposal, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied asset, custody, disposition, verification, and supplier evidence, identify missing ownership or overdue disposition, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot erase, cryptographically erase, destroy, release, ship, or dispose of media, lift a legal hold, approve an exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [NIST SP 800-88 Revision 2 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/88/r2/final) and approved security, legal, privacy, records, environmental, and disposal direction at engagement start; record the version retrieved and applicable use terms. This is original evidence planning, not reproduced NIST sanitization content, a disposal authorization, or a claim that data is unrecoverable.

### 1. Media population, sensitivity, and disposition-authority package

- **Request and owner:** Asset management, data/system, security, privacy, legal, records, procurement, facilities, and supplier owners provide media/asset inventories, ownership and custody records, data-sensitivity references, retention/hold status, disposition queue, approved decision authority, and supplier responsibility map.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected asset or media item from inventory and custody through owner, data/retention reference, hold check, authorized disposition decision, and planned handoff. This can assess traceability; it cannot release a legal hold, determine retention law, or prove every copy is in scope.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile supplied inventory, ownership, and status records and flag unowned or overdue items. Humans decide scope, holds, disposition authority, and supplier actions. Refresh after technology, ownership, classification, hold, retention, or supplier change.

### 2. Authorized execution and verification package

- **Request and owner:** IT operations, authorized sanitization/disposal personnel, security, asset-management, and supplier owners provide approved work orders, method selection rationale, execution logs, chain-of-custody transfers, verifier records, failed-verification or incident records, and certificates where applicable.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample an item from approved decision through custody, recorded execution, verifier evidence, exception/failure record, and completion record. This can establish that stated artifacts exist and connect; it cannot operate media, authenticate every vendor statement, or prove unrecoverability.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index authorized records and flag missing custody, verification, or owner fields. It cannot erase, destroy, ship, release, or modify media. Authorized humans execute, verify, and escalate failures. Recollect after verification failure, lost custody, method change, or supplier exception.

### 3. Supplier, exception, and assurance package

- **Request and owner:** Procurement, supplier-management, security, legal, records, risk, and independent-review owners provide supplier obligations, handoff/return records, service performance evidence, exceptions, corrective actions, management decisions, sampled-review workpapers, and remediation/retest evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected supplier handoff or exception to accountable parties, custody evidence, documented limitation, human decision, corrective action, and follow-up date. This cannot interpret a contract, accept residual risk, approve disposal, or certify a supplier process.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag missing supplier evidence or expiring exception records and prepare review packets. Humans approve exceptions, contract decisions, risk, closure, and external representations. Review quarterly and after supplier, legal/hold, verification, or material disposal-process change.
