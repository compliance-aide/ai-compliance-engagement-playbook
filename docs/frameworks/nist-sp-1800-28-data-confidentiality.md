# NIST SP 1800-28 data-confidentiality engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST practice-guide content, a security architecture, a breach conclusion, a compliance claim, or professional advice. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 1800-28](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/1800/28/final) and the organization's approved security, privacy, legal, risk, financial, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round engagement for identifying and protecting important data and supporting assets against confidentiality attacks. Maintain traceability among data types, locations, systems, identities, interfaces, business uses, owners, suppliers, attack scenarios, protections, monitoring, recovery dependencies, and evidence. Prioritize based on approved business and risk decisions; do not treat a data inventory, tool report, architecture diagram, or vendor statement as standalone proof that data is protected.

Record the protection strategy and its limits for material data paths: discovery and classification evidence, access and privilege decisions, authentication and administrative safeguards, encryption and key-management dependencies, endpoint and cloud controls, logging and alerting, supplier responsibilities, exceptions, incidents, testing, and improvement work. Keep assumptions and unknowns visible so prioritization does not imply certainty about every data location or exposure path.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Business, data, security, privacy, technology, identity, cloud, endpoint, supplier, legal, risk, incident-response, records, and finance owners establish scope and make accountable decisions. Operators maintain inventories, ownership and data-flow links, approved protection requirements, configuration and access evidence, monitoring records, supplier materials, exceptions, incident records, test results, remediation plans, and review history. Reconcile critical-data scope, ownership, access evidence, supplier dependencies, and open actions at least quarterly; review material changes and incidents promptly; and conduct an annual protection-strategy review.

Independent reviewers sample a critical data path from inventory and approved requirements through technical evidence, exception handling, and leadership review, testing traceability and currency. Auditors evaluate supplied evidence without identifying protected data categories, choosing safeguards, operating systems, approving access, accepting risk, making legal or financial conclusions, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize supplied inventories and evidence, flag missing owners or stale reviews, summarize data-path records, draft workpapers, and prepare review questions. AI cannot decide data classification, determine adequate protection, grant access, select or approve safeguards, accept risk, make legal or financial conclusions, or attest to conformance.
