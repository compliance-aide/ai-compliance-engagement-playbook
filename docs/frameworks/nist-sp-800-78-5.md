# NIST SP 800-78-5 PIV cryptography engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST cryptographic specifications, PIV configuration instructions, credential issuance authority, a cryptographic approval, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-78-5](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/78/5/final) and the organization’s approved identity, security, cryptography, engineering, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Operate a governed inventory and lifecycle process for cryptographic capabilities supporting in-scope PIV credentials, cards, client interfaces, infrastructure, and relying systems. Maintain traceability among credential purpose, owner, approved cryptographic baseline, module or provider version, key and certificate lifecycle reference, deployment and test evidence, changes, exceptions, supplier dependencies, and retirement decisions. A product configuration, successful test, or vendor statement is evidence to assess; it does not prove correct cryptographic implementation, credential validity, protected key custody, approved access, or compliance.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, identity and access management, security, cryptography or platform engineering, PIV issuer liaison, application and relying-system owners, endpoint or device management, procurement, supplier-management, change-management, incident-response, and records-management roles. Operators maintain an in-scope cryptographic-use inventory, approved baseline and version evidence, credential and key lifecycle references, deployment and interoperability tests, module or provider status, change approvals, exception decisions, supplier assurance, and system-to-credential traceability. Reconcile deployed versions, owners, and dependencies quarterly; review baseline changes, certificate or key lifecycle events, test results, supplier notices, exceptions, and unresolved risks at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material credential, system, provider, cryptographic, or policy changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples baseline-to-deployment-to-lifecycle traceability; auditors test evidence without selecting cryptography, accessing private keys, changing PIV configuration, issuing credentials, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied inventory, deployment, lifecycle, supplier, and review evidence, identify missing ownership or stale baselines, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot design or implement cryptography, select parameters, access private keys, configure PIV systems, issue a credential, approve an exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.
