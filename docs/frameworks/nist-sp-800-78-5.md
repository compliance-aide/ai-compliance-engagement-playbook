# NIST SP 800-78-5 PIV cryptography engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST cryptographic specifications, PIV configuration instructions, credential issuance authority, a cryptographic approval, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-78-5](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/78/5/final) and the organization’s approved identity, security, cryptography, engineering, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Operate a governed inventory and lifecycle process for cryptographic capabilities supporting in-scope PIV credentials, cards, client interfaces, infrastructure, and relying systems. Maintain traceability among credential purpose, owner, approved cryptographic baseline, module or provider version, key and certificate lifecycle reference, deployment and test evidence, changes, exceptions, supplier dependencies, and retirement decisions. A product configuration, successful test, or vendor statement is evidence to assess; it does not prove correct cryptographic implementation, credential validity, protected key custody, approved access, or compliance.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, identity and access management, security, cryptography or platform engineering, PIV issuer liaison, application and relying-system owners, endpoint or device management, procurement, supplier-management, change-management, incident-response, and records-management roles. Operators maintain an in-scope cryptographic-use inventory, approved baseline and version evidence, credential and key lifecycle references, deployment and interoperability tests, module or provider status, change approvals, exception decisions, supplier assurance, and system-to-credential traceability. Reconcile deployed versions, owners, and dependencies quarterly; review baseline changes, certificate or key lifecycle events, test results, supplier notices, exceptions, and unresolved risks at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material credential, system, provider, cryptographic, or policy changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples baseline-to-deployment-to-lifecycle traceability; auditors test evidence without selecting cryptography, accessing private keys, changing PIV configuration, issuing credentials, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied inventory, deployment, lifecycle, supplier, and review evidence, identify missing ownership or stale baselines, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot design or implement cryptography, select parameters, access private keys, configure PIV systems, issue a credential, approve an exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.


## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [NIST SP 800-78-5 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/78/5/final) and [NIST copyright and reuse information](https://www.nist.gov/disclaimer) at engagement start; checked 2026-07-31. This plan is original operational guidance, not a cryptographic specification, credential profile, or key-management instruction. Identity, cryptography, and legal owners control source use and any external representation.

### 1. PIV cryptographic-use and dependency package

- **Request and owner:** Identity, cryptography, PIV issuer liaison, and relying-system owners provide the approved credential-use inventory, relying-system map, cryptographic baseline reference, module/provider and card/interface versions, certificate/key lifecycle references, and supplier/dependency owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Independently trace one credential use from business purpose through relying system, approved baseline reference, deployed dependency/version, and accountable owner. This is a sample of recorded traceability; it does not prove credential validity, algorithm correctness, or protected key custody.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index supplied inventories and flag missing owners, obsolete versions, or inconsistent dependency references. Named human identity and cryptography owners approve scope and baseline use. Refresh after a credential, relying-system, provider, module, or policy change.

### 2. Deployment, lifecycle, and interoperability package

- **Request and owner:** PIV operations, endpoint/device, infrastructure, and application owners provide controlled deployment references, issuance/lifecycle process evidence, certificate status references, approved interoperability test plans/results, change records, and stated test limitations.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one selected relying-system integration from approved deployment through lifecycle reference, test result, limitation, and change or rollback record. This cannot issue or validate a credential, access private keys, prove every integration interoperates, or approve a cryptographic configuration.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize read-only evidence references and flag missing lifecycle or test linkage. Humans operate PIV systems, manage keys/credentials, and approve changes. Refresh after deployment, certificate/key lifecycle event, interoperability failure, or material system change.

### 3. Exception, vulnerability, and review package

- **Request and owner:** Security, supplier-management, risk, and incident-response owners provide exception records, supplier notices, vulnerability/incident records, compensating-measure decisions, remediation/retest evidence, and management review decisions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one exception or vulnerability from detection through affected dependency, accountable decision authority, remediation plan, retest, and current status. This demonstrates a decision trail; it does not accept risk, establish cryptographic strength, or attest to compliance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prioritize supplied exceptions and identify overdue actions, but cannot access secrets, approve an exception, accept risk, or close remediation. Named human authorities make decisions and an independent reviewer challenges the evidence. Refresh on supplier notice, vulnerability, incident, exception, or annual review.
