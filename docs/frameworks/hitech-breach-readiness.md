# HITECH breach readiness — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance for a statutory overlay, not a breach determination. Use [HHS OCR breach resources](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/breach-notification/index.html).

## Engagement focus

Maintain scope and business-associate inventory, risk decisions, incident-to-breach decision records, notification rehearsal, vendor reporting paths, and corrective actions.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Authorized privacy and legal owners decide breach status and notices. Independent reviewers sample risk decisions, timelines, notices, and evidence lineage. AI can flag missing approvals and timing but cannot determine breach status, process PHI in unapproved systems, or submit reports.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [HHS OCR breach resources](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/breach-notification/index.html), together with counsel’s current-law record. Retrieve the governing resource and effective guidance for each exercise or event. This plan is original operational guidance, not legal text, a breach determination, or a substitute for counsel.

### 1. Regulated-data, entity, and associate event boundary

- **Request and owner:** Request the approved data/service inventory, covered-entity and business-associate relationship references, incident intake taxonomy, affected-system/service boundary, and named privacy, security, legal, and business-associate owners. For an event, record the established reporting period and approved facts without placing PHI in the repository.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected incident record to its affected service/data classification inputs, accountable entities, vendor/associate notification path, preservation decision, and fact-collection owner. Inspect references rather than sensitive records. This can support an accountable event boundary; it cannot determine whether data were compromised, whether an entity is regulated, or whether a notice duty exists.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize redacted intake metadata, flag missing ownership, and prepare an evidence-request list in an approved environment. Legal and privacy leaders determine applicability and data-handling limits. Trigger on a suspected event, new associate, material data/service change, or annual inventory review.

### 2. Risk-analysis and notification-decision trail

- **Request and owner:** Request the counsel-approved decision record, fact timeline, risk-analysis inputs, review/approval metadata, notification drafting/approval references, recipient/category calculation support, and documented rationale from privacy, legal, incident response, and communications owners. Use the particular event or rehearsal population.
- **Validate and limit:** Reperform the chronology for a selected event or tabletop from intake through fact freeze, authorized decision makers, approved notice path, and retained rationale; check that decision dates and source records are attributable. This can support traceability of the decision process; it cannot decide breach status, prove statutory timing, validate every affected individual count, or approve a notification.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may calculate nonbinding date reminders from human-entered milestones and identify missing approvals, but cannot determine risk, draft final legal notices without human review, or submit a report. Counsel and authorized privacy officials decide and approve actions. Trigger on a material incident, revised facts, delayed response, or rehearsal.

### 3. Associate readiness, exercise, and corrective-action evidence

- **Request and owner:** Request business-associate contact/escalation records, contract/assurance references, incident reporting exercise artifacts, communications runbook approvals, corrective-action register, retest evidence, and governance review minutes from vendor-management, privacy, security, and legal owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected associate or drill scenario to current contact ownership, reporting path, decision handoff, corrective action, retest/rehearsal outcome, and management review. This can support preparedness and follow-up evidence; it cannot prove third-party performance, legal adequacy, readiness in an actual event, or notification compliance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may track missing exercise artifacts and action aging using approved metadata. Humans decide supplier escalation, exercise acceptance, remediation closure, and legal communications. Trigger on contract/associate change, failed drill, incident, corrective-action due date, and annual readiness review.
