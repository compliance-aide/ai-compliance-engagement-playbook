# FIPS 200 minimum-security requirements — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not FIPS text or a federal authorization decision. Confirm current status at the [NIST publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/200/final).

## Engagement focus

Maintain boundary and categorization dependencies, implementation evidence, inherited-service records, exceptions, assessment results, remediation decisions, risk acceptance, and authorization-package traceability.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Accountable federal authorities make security and authorization decisions; independent assessors form conclusions separately. AI indexes evidence and highlights gaps, but cannot select requirements, accept risk, or authorize operation. Monitor continuously and review annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the current [FIPS 200 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/200/final) and applicable official federal program material; public NIST source retrieved 2026-07-31. This is original engagement-planning language, not FIPS text, an authorization package, or an authorization decision. Designated federal officials determine applicability, authorization scope, implementation decisions, and risk acceptance.

### 1. System boundary and categorization-dependency package

- **Request and owner:** System owner, security officer, enterprise architecture, data owner, and authorization-support staff provide the system boundary description, inventory references, information-type/categorization dependency records, interconnection and inherited-service records, and accountable-owner assignments.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected component or service through boundary, data/context record, inherited-service relationship, and owner approval. This can support documented scope and dependency traceability; it cannot determine categorization, authorize a boundary, or establish that every dependency is complete.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize records and flag contradictory owners or outdated links. Authorized federal humans decide scope and categorization. Refresh after a system, data, interface, cloud, or inherited-service change.

### 2. Implementation and assessment-evidence package

- **Request and owner:** Security engineering, operations, control owners, assessment support, and service providers provide approved implementation references, evidence locator records, assessment plans/results where appropriately shareable, remediation links, and inheritance or provider evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect a selected implementation claim to its evidence locator, period, responsible owner, access restriction, and related assessment or remediation record. This can show evidence governance and traceability; it cannot select safeguards, independently assess technical operation, or establish effectiveness.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index authorized evidence and identify expired, missing, or conflicting metadata. Assessors and accountable officials decide test conclusions and corrective action. Refresh after assessment activity, material finding, provider change, or control implementation change.

### 3. Authorization, continuous monitoring, and risk package

- **Request and owner:** Authorizing officials, risk executives, system/security owners, continuous-monitoring teams, and records owners provide authorization artifacts or decision references, monitoring schedules, change/risk registers, POA&M or remediation tracking, exceptions, and management-review records.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected monitoring finding or change through detection, owner assignment, decision authority, remediation status, and review evidence. This can support an accountable monitoring trail; it cannot accept risk, grant authorization, close a finding, or assert compliance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may surface aging items and missing decision records. Only designated federal authorities decide authorization, exceptions, and risk. Refresh after a significant change, monitoring finding, authorization event, or annual review.
