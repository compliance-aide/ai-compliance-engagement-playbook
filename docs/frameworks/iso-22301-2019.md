# ISO 22301:2019 — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance for continuity, not an ISO publication or recovery signoff. Use
> an authorized source and check the [ISO record](https://www.iso.org/standard/75106.html)
> and current amendment/revision status.

## Engagement focus

Set services, dependencies, disruption assumptions, recovery objectives,
authorities, and stakeholder commitments. Refresh dependencies and recovery risks,
exercise realistic scenarios, retain plans and evidence, assign corrective actions,
and close with management resourcing and risk decisions.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Operators own recovery decisions, exercises, and fixes. Independent reviewers
test traceability between claims, evidence, decisions, and exercises. AI may index authorized
evidence, flag stale dependencies, and draft scenario questions; it cannot
declare recovery capability, sign off, approve recovery decisions, or alter operating services. Review quarterly and close annually.


## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use an authorized ISO 22301:2019 copy, its Amd 1:2024, and the [ISO record](https://www.iso.org/standard/75106.html); checked 2026-07-31. Check the record for the pending revision before each cycle. The source is licensed; this plan does not reproduce requirements or make a continuity certification or recovery-capability claim. Continuity leadership approves service priorities, recovery objectives, and risk decisions.

### 1. Service priority, dependency, and disruption record

- **Request and owner:** Business-impact inputs, prioritized service/process inventory, dependencies, disruption scenarios, recovery objectives, stakeholder commitments, and accountable owner records from business and continuity leaders.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected priority or dependency to its business owner, scenario assumption, and approved recovery decision. This can support a transparent planning boundary; it cannot prove an objective is achievable or that every dependency is captured.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile dependency records and flag unowned assumptions. Humans approve priorities and objectives. Refresh after a material service, supplier, facility, technology, or business change.

### 2. Continuity plan and exercise evidence

- **Request and owner:** Current plan/version records, role/contact records, exercise designs and results, recovery/communications evidence, issue log, and corrective actions from continuity, service, and communications owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect a human-approved exercise selection from scenario through result, evidence, exception, and corrective action. This can support performance under the tested conditions; it cannot establish recovery capability under untested scenarios or authorize live changes.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index plans and exercise records and flag stale dependencies; it may not run recovery actions or sign off outcomes. Recollect after a failed exercise, actual disruption, or plan/change trigger.

### 3. Management decision and annual readiness record

- **Request and owner:** Management-review inputs, resourcing/risk decisions, unresolved exceptions, remediation/retest results, and annual readiness review from executive and continuity owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected exception to decision authority, expiry, mitigations, and retest. This can support accountable governance; it cannot accept risk or replace independent review.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a source-linked review packet. Humans approve investment, residual risk, external statements, and closure. Review quarterly and close annually.
