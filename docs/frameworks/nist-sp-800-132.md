# NIST SP 800-132 password-based key-derivation engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST key-derivation content, password-storage instructions, a cryptographic approval, or a compliance claim. Confirm current status and revision activity through [NIST SP 800-132](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/132/final) and the organization’s approved security, cryptography, identity, privacy, legal, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Operate a governed lifecycle for in-scope password-based key-derivation uses that protect stored data or data-protection keys. Maintain traceability from protected use and data context through owner, credential or secret-handling boundary, approved implementation and version baseline, deployment evidence, migration and standards-status review, changes, exceptions, supplier dependencies, and lifecycle evidence. A vendor assertion, configuration record, or test result is evidence to assess; it does not prove password protection, key custody, appropriate parameter selection, secure implementation, or compliance.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, security, cryptography or platform engineering, identity, architecture, application and product owners, privacy and legal where relevant, procurement, supplier-management, change-management, incident-response, and records-management roles. Operators maintain an in-scope use inventory, approved purpose and ownership records, secret-handling and key-lifecycle references, module or provider versions, deployment and test evidence, standards-status and migration records, exception decisions, supplier assurance, and system-to-use traceability. Reconcile uses, owners, dependencies, and deployed versions quarterly; review technical guidance status, implementation or secret-handling changes, test outcomes, supplier notices, exceptions, and unresolved risks at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material system, provider, cryptographic, credential, or threat changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples use-to-deployment-to-review traceability; auditors test the evidence trail without accessing passwords or keys, changing production configuration, approving exceptions, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied inventory, lifecycle, test, supplier, and review evidence, identify missing ownership or stale standards-status records, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot design or implement password-based derivation, access passwords or keys, change production configuration, determine validation, approve an exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.
