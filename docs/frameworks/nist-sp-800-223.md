# NIST SP 800-223 high-performance-computing security engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST high-performance-computing content, a security architecture, an authorization to operate, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-223](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/223/final) and the organization's approved security, research, privacy, export-control, legal, safety, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round engagement for in-scope high-performance-computing environments and their distinctive architectures, workloads, users, data, software stacks, orchestration services, facilities, and external dependencies. Maintain an understandable system view that captures compute, storage, network, identity, management, scheduling, remote-access, monitoring, and recovery components alongside mission-specific threats and constraints. Tailor the engagement to the actual environment rather than forcing a conventional enterprise pattern onto specialized research or mission workloads.

Treat an architecture diagram, cluster inventory, job-scheduler record, configuration export, test output, threat analysis, supplier statement, or incident record as evidence to evaluate in context. Preserve the chain from scoped mission and data requirements through architecture choices, threat analysis, protective measures, changes, observed operation, exceptions, and improvement work. Do not interpret platform complexity, automated telemetry, or a technical assessment as proof of acceptable security posture.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Research or mission, HPC operations, security, architecture, network, storage, identity, facilities, data, privacy, export-control, supplier, incident-response, continuity, and records owners establish scope and make accountable decisions. Operators maintain system and workload inventories, architecture and dependency records, approved configurations, threat and risk records, monitoring and test evidence, user and administrator access records, exceptions, incidents, recovery evidence, and retirement plans. Reconcile major components, software stacks, workloads, owners, data uses, and suppliers at least quarterly; reassess after material workload, architecture, vulnerability, or threat changes; and conduct annual management review.

Independent reviewers sample a path from mission and architecture through protective evidence, operations, and corrective actions. Auditors examine supplied evidence without running workloads, accessing research data, altering cluster settings, approving architecture, accepting risk, authorizing operations, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize inventories and evidence, identify stale dependencies or missing ownership, draft workpapers, compare supplied records with approved decisions, and prepare review questions. AI cannot design or approve architecture, change cluster configurations, schedule jobs, access protected data, grant access, accept risk, make export-control or legal conclusions, or attest to conformance.


## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** At engagement start, retain the retrieved version and applicable use terms for the official [NIST SP 800-223 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/223/final), with approved research/mission, security, privacy, export-control, legal, safety, supplier, and operational decisions. This is original evidence-planning guidance, not reproduced NIST content, an authorization to operate, or a conclusion about cluster security.

### 1. HPC mission, workload, data, and architecture package

- **Request and owner:** Research or mission, HPC operations, architecture, compute, storage, network, identity, data, privacy, export-control, and security owners provide in-scope cluster and service inventory, workload/use record, data classifications and handling boundaries, architecture/dependency diagrams, user/administrator roles, and accountable ownership.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one material workload or platform service from mission/use and data boundary through compute, storage, network, identity, scheduling, and named owner dependencies. This can assess the supplied system view; it cannot access protected data, determine export-control duties, or prove all cluster components are secure.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize supplied inventories and diagrams and flag missing owners, dependencies, or data-boundary records. Humans approve scope, architecture, workload authorization, and privacy/export-control decisions. Refresh after a material workload, data use, cluster component, identity integration, or facility/architecture change.

### 2. Administrative-path, protective-operation, and change package

- **Request and owner:** HPC operations, security, identity, network, storage, facilities, platform, and change owners provide approved configuration and access records, administrative-path evidence, job-scheduler and management-plane records, vulnerability/update records, monitoring and incident samples, change approvals, and exception documentation.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample one administrative or platform-management path through approved access/configuration evidence, relevant change record, operational observation, and associated exception or corrective action where applicable. This can support a bounded evidence observation; it cannot grant access, run workloads, change a cluster, or establish continuous protection.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may identify unlinked changes, stale dependencies, or incomplete access and monitoring evidence. Humans administer infrastructure, authorize access, investigate incidents, and approve changes/exceptions. Recollect after a material vulnerability, management-plane change, failed monitoring check, access event, or operating incident.

### 3. Specialized-risk, recovery, and corrective-action package

- **Request and owner:** Research or mission, HPC operations, security, safety, privacy, export-control, supplier, continuity, incident-response, risk, records, and independent-review owners provide threat/risk records, supplier/dependency assumptions, recovery and exercise evidence, incident follow-up, exception approvals, remediation register, retest evidence, and management decisions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected specialized threat, disruption, exception, or corrective action to human authority, relevant operating limitation, target date, follow-up/retest evidence, and current disposition. This can assess governance of supplied work; it cannot accept risk, declare a system safe, make legal/export conclusions, or attest to security.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag overdue action, stale recovery evidence, or undocumented dependencies and prepare source-linked review questions. Humans approve risk treatment, continuity decisions, exception renewal, closure, and external statements. Review quarterly and after a material threat, incident, exercise, supplier, workload, or recovery-capability change.
