# Vermont Security Breach Notice Act engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not Vermont statutory text, legal advice, a breach determination, notice advice, or a compliance claim. Confirm current law through [Vermont 9 V.S.A. section 2435](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/09/062/02435) and qualified counsel.

## Engagement focus

Maintain an incident-readiness record that connects data systems and owners, event intake, investigation scope, evidence preservation, consumer and regulator decision paths, authorized communications, recovery actions, exceptions, and review dates. Record facts, sources, timing, uncertainty, and accountable decisions in a durable timeline. Use the record to make an incident reviewable; it cannot by itself decide whether an event triggers duties, whether an exception applies, or when a notice should be sent.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Legal, privacy, security, incident response, technology, records, communications, business, and executive owners retain accountable decisions. Incident leaders coordinate fact collection; system owners preserve technical evidence; counsel directs legal interpretation and notification decisions; authorized officials make submissions; and auditors evaluate evidence in their independent role. Independent reviewers exercise the workflow and sample incidents from intake through investigation, evidence preservation, decision records, communications, recovery, lessons learned, and retained support, challenging gaps in authority, timeline, scope, contact paths, and closure evidence.

AI may organize authorized metadata, identify missing source or owner fields, draft factual questions, compare retained evidence with an approved workflow, and prepare non-authoritative workpapers. AI cannot determine whether an event is a security breach, decide notification or reporting duties, conclude that a deadline has been met, submit a notice, give legal advice, transmit personal information, alter evidence, or claim compliance. Reassess before material data, system, provider, incident, contact, or legal changes; exercise incident readiness periodically and conduct an annual independent readiness review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the live [Vermont 9 V.S.A. section 2435](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/09/062/02435), checked 2026-07-31, with qualified Vermont counsel. This original plan does not reproduce statutory text or decide a security breach, whether an exception applies, notice/reporting content, timing, recipients, or legal compliance.

### 1. Covered-data, system, and service-provider readiness package

- **Request and owner:** Data and system inventory; data-flow and ownership records; security-control references; access/logging and evidence-retention design; provider inventory and contact/escalation records; lifecycle/disposal references; and scope/change reviews from privacy, security, technology, records, and procurement owners. Define the business, environment, provider, and review-period boundary.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected information flow from collection or receipt to storage, access, provider handoff, log/evidence-retention reference, and lifecycle/disposal record; inspect source dates, owners, and sensitive-evidence access controls. This supports incident-readiness context; it cannot classify information legally, establish completeness of the inventory, or determine whether a statutory exception applies.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved metadata and flag unowned systems, stale contacts, or missing evidence links. Human privacy, security, legal, and procurement owners decide scope, classification, provider treatment, and exceptions. Refresh after material data, system, provider, logging, contact, or ownership changes.

### 2. Incident fact collection, scope, and preservation package

- **Request and owner:** Event intake/triage records; de-identified incident timeline; original evidence references and chain-of-custody record; system-owner facts; investigation assignments; scope hypotheses and updates; containment/recovery records; and counsel-directed escalation log from incident response, security, records, and legal owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Reperform a tabletop or trace a closed, de-identified event from intake through preservation, fact-source tracking, scope updates, containment, recovery, and lessons learned. This supports a reviewable investigation process; it cannot determine that an event is a security breach, prove investigation completeness, establish a statutory exception, or decide any legal obligation.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize authorized timeline metadata and identify missing sources or contradictory dates. Humans direct investigation, preserve evidence, and decide escalations; AI cannot alter evidence, interpret legal duties, or communicate externally. Trigger on an event, exercise finding, material forensic/logging change, or remediation issue.

### 3. Notice-decision, communication, and corrective-action package

- **Request and owner:** Counsel-approved decision record; authorized communication/notification roster; contact-validation exercise evidence; notice-template governance references; regulator/consumer communication approvals where applicable; recovery validation; corrective actions; retest; and management review from legal, communications, executive, and security owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect a sampled exercise or event file for the factual record, legal-review authority, decision date, communication approval, recovery evidence, corrective-action owner, and retest. This supports accountable decision and follow-through evidence; it cannot approve notice, prove its legal sufficiency or timing, choose recipients, or certify completion.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare factual checklists and flag missing approvals. Named counsel and authorized officials decide notices, reports, communications, risk treatment, and closure; AI cannot submit or send them. Refresh after an incident, contact change, legal-source update, corrective action, or annual independent review.
