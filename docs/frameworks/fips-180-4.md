# FIPS 180-4 secure-hash engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not FIPS secure-hash content, an algorithm-selection decision, an approval, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [FIPS 180-4](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/180-4/upd1/final) and the organization’s approved cryptography, security, architecture, legal, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Operate a governed lifecycle for in-scope secure-hash uses. Connect each use case to an approved purpose and scope, protected data and system context, implementation and configuration decisions, protocol and signature dependencies where relevant, interfaces, access, logging, validation, supplier dependencies, changes, incidents, transition planning, and retirement. A configuration extract, implementation record, supplier statement, test result, or audit log is evidence to assess; none alone proves suitable hash use, correct implementation, protected data, approved use, secure operation, or compliance.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, security, cryptography or platform engineering, architecture, application and data owners, identity or key-management operations where relevant, supplier-management, legal and privacy where relevant, change-management, incident-response, and records-management roles. Operators maintain scoped use inventories, approved design and configuration decisions, dependency and interface records, implementation and validation baselines, access and audit records, supplier materials, exception and incident records, transition decisions, and retirement evidence. Reconcile uses, owners, dependencies, interfaces, and suppliers quarterly; review integrity failures, implementation changes, exceptions, incidents, transition needs, and unresolved risks at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material cryptographic, product, provider, threat, or publication changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples purpose-to-implementation-to-operation-to-review traceability; auditors test supplied evidence without selecting hash algorithms or parameters, changing configuration, approving exceptions, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied inventory, design, implementation, test, supplier, and review evidence, identify missing owners or stale decisions, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot select hash algorithms or parameters, change cryptographic configuration, approve an exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the current [FIPS 180-4 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/180-4/upd1/final), public NIST material retrieved 2026-07-31. This is original evidence-planning language, not the secure-hash specification or a cryptographic implementation instruction. Cryptography and system authorities select approved designs and decide scope.

### 1. Hash-use and data-flow package

- **Request and owner:** Application, platform, data, and cryptography owners provide a use inventory identifying purpose, application/service, message or data-flow class, implementation reference, dependency, environment, and accountable owner.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected use from its declared purpose to a design or configuration reference and deployed-service record, while excluding secrets and sensitive payloads. This can support a stated-use inventory; it cannot prove complete discovery, correct algorithm implementation, or suitable protection for the data.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may normalize inventories and flag owner or dependency gaps. Human cryptography and architecture owners decide design choices. Refresh after a new use, interface, data-flow, supplier, or material design change.

### 2. Implementation and release package

- **Request and owner:** Engineering, release management, and quality owners provide approved implementation baseline references, build/release provenance, code-review or test records, deployment records, and defects or remediation links for the sampled period.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect one release trail from approved baseline to deployment and test observation without reproducing source code or executing cryptographic changes. This can support that a release process retained evidence; it cannot certify implementation correctness, resistance to attack, or production effectiveness.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may correlate release metadata and highlight missing provenance. Engineering owners decide remediation and deployment. Refresh after a release, test failure, vulnerability notice, or material dependency update.

### 3. Lifecycle, exception, and review package

- **Request and owner:** Security, risk, supplier management, and records owners provide standards-status reviews, exception decisions, dependency notices, incident references, transition plans, and annual review minutes.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected exception or transition item to its reason, accountable authority, review date, and follow-up action. This can show governance of a recorded decision; it cannot select an algorithm, accept risk, or conclude compliance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag aging exceptions and stale standards checks. Authorized humans decide exceptions and transition timing. Refresh after a publication update, supplier notice, incident, or annual review.
