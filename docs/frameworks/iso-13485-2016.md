# ISO 13485:2016 medical-device quality management — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not ISO text, a safety conclusion, market authorization or certification advice.

## Source and applicability

Confirm the standard through the [ISO record](https://www.iso.org/standard/59752.html) and authorized source review. Do not input protected ISO material into AI tools without permission. The full licensed standard and jurisdiction-specific obligations remain unverified in this draft.

For US scope, [FDA QMSR guidance](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/postmarket-requirements-devices/quality-management-system-regulation-qmsr), checked through official indexed content on 2026-09-04, states that the revised regulation became effective February 2, 2026 and incorporates ISO 13485:2016. Treat it as effective rather than a future transition. Regulatory owners must verify all applicable FDA additions and other obligations; a certificate is not proof of market authorization or complete regulatory compliance. Other markets require their own current-source decisions.

## Engagement focus

Connect the exact device and quality-system scope to controlled lifecycle records, supplier/process evidence, release decisions, feedback and effective corrective action. Preserve historical versions and decisions. A revised procedure or closed corrective-action ticket alone does not prove the affected device or process is acceptable.

## Roles

Quality owns the QMS and corrective-action decisions. Regulatory owners determine markets, device status and reporting duties. Product, engineering, manufacturing, service and supplier owners maintain technical evidence. Qualified independent reviewers challenge traceability and conclusions. AI may organize approved organization-owned records and flag gaps, but cannot make clinical, safety, regulatory, release, risk-acceptance, CAPA-closure or certification decisions, initiate device tests, or send regulatory communications.

## Before starting

Record legal entities, sites, devices/families, intended uses, markets, lifecycle activities, outsourced processes and period. Obtain approved private access to scope decisions, specifications, risk records, design and process evidence, supplier records, production/service history and feedback. Do not put patient data, restricted design information or licensed standards in public workpapers.

## Ordered workflow

1. **Approve scope and source decisions.** Map each device, activity and market to its accountable quality/regulatory owner, applicable source and justified exclusions. Preserve unresolved questions. Reconcile the complete known population against product, site and supplier inventories; never silently sample, trim or cap assessment evidence.
2. **Establish lifecycle traceability.** Link approved requirements, risk decisions, design outputs, reviews and verification/validation references to exact versions and configurations. Preserve distinction between planned evidence and executed results. Qualified engineers decide adequacy; AI checks whether the references and decisions exist and agree.
3. **Review suppliers and production/service controls.** Map purchased inputs and outsourced processes to approved specifications, supplier decisions and acceptance evidence. Record process qualification/validation decisions, equipment status, personnel authority and handling requirements where applicable. A supplier certificate does not replace evidence for the actual input or process.
4. **Reconcile release and change records.** Link each scoped release or change to its approved configuration, required evidence, unresolved deviations and authorized disposition. Identify affected inventory and distributed units through approved traceability records. Approval of one version does not automatically cover a changed supplier, process, software or intended use.
5. **Connect feedback to timely decisions.** Reconcile complaints, service observations, nonconformities and other relevant signals. Preserve original receipt times and known facts. Escalate potential safety or reportability issues promptly to qualified owners; do not wait for a completed root-cause analysis before seeking a time-sensitive regulatory decision. AI does not determine reportability or contact authorities.
6. **Verify corrective action effectiveness.** Separate immediate correction, containment, cause investigation, systemic action and effectiveness evidence. Assign each action an owner, scope and verification criterion before closure. Inspect affected processes and versions, including possible adverse effects of the change. Keep unresolved findings visible; training attendance or document publication alone is not proof of effectiveness.
7. **Prepare management and independent review.** Reconcile the full scoped evidence index with open findings, audit results, trends, resource decisions and follow-up. Preserve disagreements and evidence limitations. Named quality/regulatory officials approve conclusions and external statements. Hand off remaining work with responsible owners and due dates.

## Evidence and test plan

The three packages preserve and expand the earlier tailored plan.

| Package | Minimum records | Reviewer challenge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Device/QMS scope and lifecycle governance | Devices, sites, markets, activities, sources, exclusions, responsibilities and changes | Does the stated boundary match actual operations? |
| Design, production and feedback traceability | Versioned specifications, risk decisions, verification/validation references, suppliers, release and feedback records | Does evidence apply to the exact device and process configuration? |
| Issues, corrective action and oversight | Findings, containment, cause analysis, action approvals, effectiveness evidence, audits and management decisions | Was the cause addressed and the result verified within the claimed scope? |

Use the [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md) statuses `supported`, `not_supported`, `inconclusive`, `not_applicable` and `not_tested` for individual assertions. Separate evidence completeness from technical adequacy and authorized release. Complete inventory accounting does not mean every device configuration was tested.

Fictional author desk case: a supplier changes a material, but a release packet cites validation for the previous material only. The old result cannot establish acceptability of the changed configuration. Preserve the gap, identify affected scope and route the change for qualified review; AI neither releases nor rejects real devices.

## Failure branches and decisions

Conflicting versions require owner reconciliation. Missing validation cannot become a pass through a risk exception. A potentially urgent safety signal follows the approved quality/regulatory escalation process. Unsupported supplier evidence requires an assigned action. An inaccessible record remains a limitation; do not reconstruct results or backdate approvals to fill it.

## Cadence and renewal

Use verified regulatory obligations, QMS plans and risk to set review frequencies. Reopen after device, intended-use, market, supplier, process or source changes and material feedback. Do not invent blanket quarterly or annual ISO mandates; time-sensitive reporting and release decisions remain separate from management-review schedules.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver the scope/source register, private evidence index, lifecycle links, qualified decisions and open actions. Independent source, skeptical and rights review remain pending before publication. Author desk checks are not device validation, independent audit or cross-model usability trials.
