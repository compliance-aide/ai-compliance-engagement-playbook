# Washington My Health My Data Act engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not legal advice, statutory text, a legal applicability determination, or a compliance claim. Confirm current requirements through the [Washington My Health My Data Act, Chapter 19.373 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373) and the organization’s approved legal, privacy, security, product, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Maintain a defensible, Washington-specific operating record for consumer-health-data activities. Connect data categories, collection points, purposes, notices, consent or authorization decisions, disclosures, consumer requests, processors, security measures, retention, geofencing features, incidents, and exceptions to accountable owners and evidence. Treat a data map, notice publication, consent record, or technical test as evidence to evaluate—not proof of legal applicability, lawful processing, valid authorization, or complete coverage.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, privacy, legal, security, product, data-governance, marketing, analytics, engineering, customer-support, procurement, supplier-management, incident-response, and records-management roles. Operators maintain the scoped processing inventory; notice, consent, and authorization artifacts; request and appeal records; processor due-diligence and contractual evidence; access and security reviews; retention and deletion records; geofencing feature assessments; incident records; and approved exception decisions. Reconcile in-scope data flows and suppliers quarterly; review notices, consumer requests, permissions, access, processors, geofencing, and unresolved risks at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material product, data, vendor, marketing, security, or legal changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples data flows through notice, purpose, decision, processor, and request evidence; auditors test the evidence trail without making legal determinations, changing product collection, sending communications, approving a disclosure, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied processing, request, supplier, and review evidence, identify missing owners or stale artifacts, flag changes for legal review, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot determine applicability, give legal advice, infer consent or authorization, change collection or sharing, respond to a consumer, approve a disclosure or exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.
