# Washington My Health My Data Act engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not legal advice, statutory text, a legal applicability determination, or a compliance claim. Confirm current requirements through the [Washington My Health My Data Act, Chapter 19.373 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373) and the organization’s approved legal, privacy, security, product, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Maintain a defensible, Washington-specific operating record for consumer-health-data activities. Connect data categories, collection points, purposes, notices, consent or authorization decisions, disclosures, consumer requests, processors, security measures, retention, geofencing features, incidents, and exceptions to accountable owners and evidence. Treat a data map, notice publication, consent record, or technical test as evidence to evaluate—not proof of legal applicability, lawful processing, valid authorization, or complete coverage.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, privacy, legal, security, product, data-governance, marketing, analytics, engineering, customer-support, procurement, supplier-management, incident-response, and records-management roles. Operators maintain the scoped processing inventory; notice, consent, and authorization artifacts; request and appeal records; processor due-diligence and contractual evidence; access and security reviews; retention and deletion records; geofencing feature assessments; incident records; and approved exception decisions. Reconcile in-scope data flows and suppliers quarterly; review notices, consumer requests, permissions, access, processors, geofencing, and unresolved risks at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material product, data, vendor, marketing, security, or legal changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples data flows through notice, purpose, decision, processor, and request evidence; auditors test the evidence trail without making legal determinations, changing product collection, sending communications, approving a disclosure, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied processing, request, supplier, and review evidence, identify missing owners or stale artifacts, flag changes for legal review, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot determine applicability, give legal advice, infer consent or authorization, change collection or sharing, respond to a consumer, approve a disclosure or exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [Washington My Health My Data Act, Chapter 19.373 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373) and qualified Washington privacy review; checked 2026-07-31. This is original operational planning, not statutory text or a decision on consumer-health-data scope, consent, valid authorization, disclosure, geofencing, or consumer outcome.

### 1. Consumer-health-data inventory and notice package

- **Request and owner:** Data inventory, collection points, health-data classification rationale, purpose/retention record, public-facing notice versions, system/data-flow diagrams, and change approvals from privacy, product, marketing, analytics, and engineering owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected collection flow to its interface, system, data category, stated purpose, notice version, owner, and retention record. This supports evidence-backed human review; it cannot decide whether data is covered or whether a notice is legally sufficient.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare approved metadata and identify a changed flow without an owner or notice reference. Privacy/legal humans approve classifications and notices. Refresh for a new collection point, health-data use, purpose, campaign, or product change.

### 2. Consent, authorization, disclosure, and request package

- **Request and owner:** Human-approved consent/authorization design and version records where applicable, disclosure and recipient/processor register, request intake/identity procedure and log, access controls, and response-quality samples from privacy, support, product, and supplier owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a redacted sample to the relevant interface/configuration, recorded decision source, recipient entry, accountable human, and request outcome record. This can show a controlled workflow; it cannot infer consent, validate authorization, authenticate a consumer, or decide whether a disclosure is permitted.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may create a de-identified evidence index and flag missing approvals. Humans decide consent/authorization, request outcomes, disclosures, and communications. Recheck after a request, supplier change, disclosure change, or control failure.

### 3. Geofencing, security, incident, and governance package

- **Request and owner:** Feature assessments for location/geofencing behavior, technical configuration/test evidence, security and access-review references, incident/exercise records, supplier oversight, exceptions, remediation/retest, and management-review decisions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected feature or incident item to scope, evidence, human authority, limitation, action owner, and retest. This supports review of operated safeguards; it cannot establish absence of prohibited behavior, determine notification duty, accept risk, or attest compliance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag stale reviews and assemble a redacted management packet. Authorized humans approve risk, exceptions, public statements, and closure. Review quarterly and after material feature, vendor, incident, or legal changes.
