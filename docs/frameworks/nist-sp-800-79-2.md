# NIST SP 800-79-2 PIV issuer-authorization engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST issuer controls, an authorization package, a reliability determination, credential issuance authority, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-79-2](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/79/2/final) and the organization’s approved identity, security, privacy, legal, procurement, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Operate a governed assessment and oversight cycle for in-scope PIV card and derived-PIV-credential issuers. Maintain traceability from issuer scope and services through accountable leadership, policies, personnel, physical and technical safeguards, issuance and lifecycle operations, assessment evidence, findings, corrective actions, independent review, and management decisions. A supplier certificate, completed questionnaire, credential record, or assessment artifact is evidence to assess; it does not establish issuer reliability, authorize operations, prove control effectiveness, or constitute a compliance conclusion.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, issuer operations, identity and access management, security, privacy, legal, procurement, supplier-management, personnel or sponsor function, physical security where relevant, internal audit or independent review, incident-response, change-management, and records-management roles. Operators maintain issuer-scope records, policy and procedure approvals, service and credential inventories, personnel and facility evidence, technical safeguard records, assessment workpapers, findings and corrective-action evidence, exceptions, supplier evidence, management decisions, and authorization status records. Reconcile issuer scope, services, suppliers, and accountable owners quarterly; review findings, credential lifecycle anomalies, changes, exceptions, incidents, and corrective actions at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material issuer, service, system, personnel, supplier, or policy changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples issuer evidence and corrective-action closure; auditors test the evidence trail without issuing credentials, directing issuer operations, making an authorization decision, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied issuer, assessment, finding, and review evidence, identify missing ownership or overdue corrective actions, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot determine issuer reliability, authorize an issuer, issue or revoke credentials, direct issuer operations, approve exceptions, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.


## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [NIST SP 800-79-2 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/79/2/final) and [NIST copyright and reuse information](https://www.nist.gov/disclaimer) at engagement start; checked 2026-07-31. This plan is original operational guidance, not an issuer authorization method, reliability decision, or reproduction of NIST material. Identity, legal, procurement, and security owners determine approved source use and external disclosures.

### 1. Issuer scope, service, and accountability package

- **Request and owner:** Issuer operations, identity, procurement, privacy, and security owners provide the approved issuer scope, card/derived-credential service inventory, organizational and supplier map, accountable-role assignments, data/physical-facility boundary references, and approved policies/procedures.
- **Validate and limit:** Independently select an issuer service and trace it to scope, accountable operator, applicable policy reference, relevant supplier/facility boundary, and dated approval. This checks a bounded accountability trail; it does not determine issuer reliability or authorize operations.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index supplied scope and ownership evidence and flag conflicting service or owner records. Named human authorities approve scope and policies. Refresh after issuer, service, facility, personnel, supplier, or policy change.

### 2. Safeguard operation and credential-lifecycle package

- **Request and owner:** Issuer, physical-security, IAM, personnel/sponsor, and security owners provide relevant procedure records, training/role authorization evidence, access and facility-control references, credential lifecycle records, technical-safeguard references, change records, and operating-test outputs.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one selected lifecycle event or safeguard operation from authorized role through procedure, time-bounded operational evidence, limitation, and exception/change record. This does not validate a person’s identity, issue/revoke a credential, prove all safeguards effective, or direct issuer operations.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize redacted evidence indexes and flag missing approvals or stale procedure versions; it may not access sensitive credential data beyond authorized materials, change issuer systems, or execute lifecycle actions. Humans operate the issuer and approve changes. Refresh after a process, personnel, facility, or system change.

### 3. Assessment, finding, and authorization-decision package

- **Request and owner:** Assessment, audit, risk, and management owners provide assessment plans/workpapers, findings, corrective-action plans, exception decisions, retest evidence, independent review notes, and records identifying the authority and date for each decision.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample one finding from assessment through responsible owner, corrective action, retest or open status, reviewer challenge, and management decision. This supports transparent oversight; it cannot establish reliability, accept risk, authorize an issuer, or provide an attestation.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize workpapers, identify overdue corrective actions, and draft questions for an independent reviewer. Only named humans make reliability, authorization, risk, or external representation decisions. Refresh after an assessment, incident, material finding, authorization decision, or annual review.
