# NIST SP 1308 cybersecurity-risk-and-workforce engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST CSF content, a workforce plan, a risk decision, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 1308](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/1308/final) and the organization's approved governance, human-resources, security, privacy, legal, finance, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round engagement that connects cybersecurity risk reality to enterprise risk communication and workforce planning. Relate material risks, planned responses, required capabilities, accountable roles, capacity constraints, training and development needs, suppliers, succession, evidence, and management decisions. Use the engagement to make staffing and skill decisions explainable in terms of real risk and mission need, rather than treating workforce lists or training completions as proof of operational readiness.

Treat an organization chart, job description, skills matrix, training record, risk register, staffing plan, supplier statement, metric, or dashboard as evidence to evaluate in context. Preserve assumptions about capacity, role coverage, separation of duties, reliance on external parties, and the decisions that prioritize hiring, training, retention, automation, or accepted exposure. Keep personnel information minimal, appropriately protected, and distinct from performance or legal conclusions.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Executive, enterprise-risk, security, workforce, human-resources, business, finance, privacy, legal, supplier, audit, and records owners establish scope and make accountable decisions. Operators maintain role and capability records, risk-to-workforce traceability, capacity and dependency evidence, training and development records, supplier responsibilities, exceptions, improvement actions, and review history. Reconcile material risk changes, role coverage, capacity assumptions, and key supplier dependencies at least quarterly; review staffing and capability impacts after material incidents or strategy changes; and conduct an annual workforce-and-risk governance review.

Independent reviewers sample a risk response from business need through assigned capability, operating evidence, and follow-up. Auditors evaluate supplied records without selecting personnel, making employment decisions, setting compensation, accepting risk, approving budgets, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize role and evidence records, identify coverage gaps, draft workpapers and nonbinding plans, compare supplied records with approved decisions, and prepare review questions. AI cannot make hiring, performance, compensation, or employment decisions; set workforce policy; accept risk; approve budgets; make legal conclusions; or attest to conformance.
