# CISA Secure by Design Pledge engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not CISA pledge text, a pledge determination, a public representation, or a compliance claim. Confirm current scope and participation conditions through the [CISA Secure by Design Pledge](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/2024-05/CISA%20Secure%20by%20Design%20Pledge_508c.pdf).

## Engagement focus

Maintain a product-security progress record that connects the organization’s chosen in-scope products and services, accountable leaders, approved goals, baseline evidence, product roadmaps, engineering and operational changes, customer-impact evidence, public-representation decisions, limitations, and review dates. Separate a planned action, measured change, management assertion, and externally published statement. Treat voluntary participation as a transparent progress process, not as certification or a substitute for applicable law, contracts, or independent assurance.

## Roles

Executive leadership, product, engineering, security, privacy, support, legal, communications, and customer-success owners retain accountable decisions. Product owners maintain scope and roadmap evidence; security owners coordinate measurement; legal and communications approve public representations; and auditors evaluate evidence in their independent role. Independent reviewers sample declared progress from baseline through engineering records, measurement method, customer-impact evidence, management approval, and proposed publication, challenging selective scope, ambiguous metrics, unsupported claims, and omissions that could mislead users.

AI may organize authorized program metadata, identify missing evidence or review fields, compare approved statements with retained records, draft questions, and prepare non-authoritative workpapers. AI cannot decide participation, define a product scope, determine measurable progress, publish a representation, make a pledge, certify a product, accept risk, alter evidence, or claim compliance. Reassess before material product, customer, goal, metric, vulnerability, incident, or public-commitment changes; review progress routinely and conduct an annual independent readiness review.

## Source and applicability

The official pledge describes voluntary, nonbinding commitments for enterprise software and services, with seven goals and a one-year period from signing. Its stated scope excludes consumer and physical products while welcoming voluntary progress there. Confirm current participation instructions and the actual signing date. Distinguish core goal criteria from illustrative approaches. Detailed current goal review remains pending; do not invent a certification or use examples as mandatory requirements.

## Before starting

Apply the [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md). Obtain participation evidence, approved product population, source edition, seven-goal register, baseline dates, metric definitions and publication authority. Preserve the difference between proposed participation and an actual signed commitment. Reuse valid evidence access; signing and public statements remain authorized executive actions.

## Ordered workflow

| Step | Action and owner | Output and exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Establish commitment | Executive/legal owners verify participation, date, scope and current terms. | Source-linked commitment record or clearly labeled proposal; no presumed signature. |
| 2. Define coverage | Product/security owners map all goals to products, owners and evidence needs. | Complete goal register with scope limitations and roadmap for deferred products. Selective initial coverage cannot be presented as portfolio-wide results. |
| 3. Establish baseline | Measurement owners record source populations, dates, definitions and known gaps. | Reproducible starting measure; missing historical data remains a limitation rather than a reconstructed fact. |
| 4. Plan change | Engineering/product owners choose source-aligned outcomes and dependencies. | Owned roadmap with acceptance criteria, resources and authorized change path. |
| 5. Implement and observe | Operators execute approved changes and collect resulting product/customer observations. | Release and operating evidence. Shipping a feature is distinct from customer adoption or use. |
| 6. Compare consistently | Reviewer reconciles baseline and current populations, definitions and periods. | Supported change with method and limitations; denominator drift is explained before crediting improvement. |
| 7. Draft transparent update | Program owner prepares goal-by-goal progress and challenges, with evidence citations. | Review-ready statement separates achieved, planned and unmeasured work. No progress is not disguised as success. |
| 8. Review and publish if authorized | Independent reviewer challenges claims; named approvers authorize external representation. | Actual published readback if publication occurs, otherwise a clearly labeled draft and next action. |

## Evidence and test plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [CISA Secure by Design Pledge](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/2024-05/CISA%20Secure%20by%20Design%20Pledge_508c.pdf), its current participation instructions, and organization-approved legal and communications direction; prior locator snapshot 2026-07-31; current detailed-goal review pending. This is original progress-evidence planning, not pledge text, a participation determination, CISA endorsement, or an authorized public representation. Human owners confirm source currency, permitted use, pledge scope, and publication authority.

### 1. Pledge scope, product population, and baseline package

- **Request and owner:** Executive, product, security, and legal owners provide the proposed in-scope product/service population, accountable leaders, eligibility/scope rationale, baseline date, metric definitions, source-system references, exclusions, and approved limitations.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected in-scope product and baseline measure to its version/service boundary, source record, owner, calculation method, and documented exclusion. This can test whether claimed progress starts from a defined record; it cannot decide participation, validate metric completeness, or determine whether a pledge expectation is met.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile authorized inventory and metric metadata and flag missing baselines or scope drift. Humans approve participation, scope, metric definitions, and exclusions. Refresh before a material product, portfolio, ownership, or metric-method change.

### 2. Secure-development, remediation, and progress-measurement package

- **Request and owner:** Engineering, security, release, vulnerability-management, support, and product owners provide approved roadmap items, development/review references, release and remediation records, measurement inputs, trend calculations, exceptions, corrective actions, and review history.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample a reported improvement from baseline through product/version evidence, measurement input, engineering or remediation record, human review, and stated remaining limitation. This can assess traceability of one reported change; it cannot prove product security, absence of vulnerabilities, or progress across every product.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved artifacts, compare periods, and identify stale calculations or missing approvals. Humans interpret results, prioritize remediation, accept exceptions, and approve releases. Refresh after a material vulnerability, failed measure, release, incident, or roadmap change.

### 3. Public-statement, customer-impact, and challenge package

- **Request and owner:** Legal, communications, product, customer-success, security, and independent-review owners provide proposed statements, supporting evidence citations, customer-impact analysis, approval trail, limitations, corrections, and skeptical-review workpapers.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected statement to its approved scope, evidence period, metric method, named approvers, limitations, and correction route. This can identify unsupported or outdated language; it cannot authorize publication, make a pledge, determine customer obligations, or attest for management.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare supplied draft language to approved evidence and flag absent qualifiers. Humans approve external statements, customer communications, corrections, and risk decisions. Recheck before publication and after a material product, metric, vulnerability, incident, or public-commitment change.


## Failure branches and decisions

If signing evidence is absent, retain proposed status. If metrics change, preserve both definitions and comparable results or explain why comparison is unavailable. Failed acceptance keeps the roadmap item open. Missing customer-use evidence prevents adoption claims. Preserve full authorized scorer-bound evidence without trimming, sampling or capping; approved inspection selections must state their limits. Unsupported public language goes back to its owner for correction before publication. On interruption, save versions, measures, pending decisions and next safe action.

## Cadence and renewal

Track the actual signing anniversary and pledge reporting expectations; an annual internal review alone does not establish a public progress update. Review changes and evidence routinely within that window. Reopen affected claims after product, metric, vulnerability, incident or commitment changes. Do not automatically renew a pledge or imply perpetual achievement.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver participation/scope records, complete goal register, baseline/current measurements, engineering and adoption evidence, limitations, roadmap, approvals and actual publication receipts. Independent source and skeptical review and named human approval remain necessary before final representations; participation does not certify product security.
