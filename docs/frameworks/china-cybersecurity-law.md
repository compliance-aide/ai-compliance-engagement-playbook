# China Cybersecurity Law engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not statutory text, Chinese legal advice, a regulatory filing, or a compliance claim. Confirm the current law and implementing measures through the [National Laws and Regulations Database record](https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?fileId=&id=021e7d7684474107b8f3febbb1c4f8b5&title=%E4%B8%AD%E5%8D%8E%E4%BA%BA%E6%B0%91%E5%85%B1%E5%92%8C%E5%9B%BD%E7%BD%91%E7%BB%9C%E5%AE%89%E5%85%A8%E6%B3%95&type=) and qualified local counsel.

## Engagement focus

Maintain a jurisdiction-aware cybersecurity operating record for in-scope networks, systems, services, operators, suppliers, and data flows. Preserve scope determinations, accountable roles, asset and service inventories, security-operating evidence, incident records, change decisions, supplier dependencies, and escalation history. Treat interactions with other China data, privacy, sectoral, and implementing rules as a legal and operational coordination problem, not as an automated mapping exercise.

## Roles and annual rhythm

China-qualified legal leadership determines scope and regulatory obligations; system and service owners maintain operation evidence; security teams retain monitoring, incident, and change records; procurement manages supplier commitments; independent reviewers test evidence traceability. AI may organize approved records, identify missing ownership and stale evidence, and draft review questions, but cannot determine legal applicability, classify criticality as a legal conclusion, direct technical operation, report an incident, make a filing, or claim compliance. Reassess annually and before material network, service, supplier, deployment-location, data-flow, or incident changes.

### 1. China network-service boundary and accountability packet

- **Request and owner:** Approved jurisdiction and service-boundary inputs; network, system, service, connectivity, hosting-location, user/population, and supplier inventory; data-flow context; accountable operator, business, security, privacy, and legal owners; and a record of assumptions and unresolved questions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected in-scope service to its system owner, deployment/location record, network/dependency context, supplier, change history, and counsel-reviewed applicability question. This supports an accountable fact packet; it cannot determine operator status, legal scope, criticality, localization, or regulatory jurisdiction.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved service and asset metadata and flag missing owners or changed routes. China-qualified legal and designated business/security humans decide scope. Refresh before a new China-facing service, hosting/location change, material network connection, acquisition, or annual scope review.

### 2. Security-operation, incident, and change trace

- **Request and owner:** Human-approved security-operation procedures; access and administration review references; monitoring and alert case metadata; vulnerability/patch/change records; incident exercise and selected incident timelines; recovery/retest records; and material-release approval evidence from security, engineering, operations, and service owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Follow a selected material change, alert, or incident through affected service, assigned owner, decision record, action, escalation, recovery or retest, and retained limitation. Use sanitized metadata and referenced evidence rather than customer or sensitive security data. This can support traceability of the stated operating process; it cannot prove continuous security, timeliness, incident classification, reportability, or effectiveness.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize approved metadata and identify stale reviews or broken evidence links; it cannot operate controls, classify an incident, direct containment, notify an authority, or close a finding. Named humans authorize technical action, incident decisions, communications, and closure. Trigger on an incident, exploited vulnerability, material control failure, or architecture/release change.

### 3. Supplier, resilience, and regulatory-change governance

- **Request and owner:** Supplier/service dependency register; due-diligence and responsibility records; approved security/service-change notices; business-continuity or recovery exercise references; exception and remediation log; management review evidence; and a legal/regulatory-change watch owned by procurement, service, security, legal, and leadership.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected supplier or resilience issue to the impacted service boundary, owner, human decision, remediation or contingency action, due date, and follow-up review. This supports supplier and resilience governance traceability; it cannot establish contract sufficiency, supplier performance, legal notification duty, or accept risk.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare redacted review packets and flag expiring supplier reviews, unresolved remediation, or material source changes. Human procurement, legal, security, and leadership owners approve contracts, risk acceptance, external communication, filings, and closure. Review annually and upon a supplier, resilience, regulatory, or material service change.
