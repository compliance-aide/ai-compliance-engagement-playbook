# IEC 62443-2-4:2023 IACS service-provider security — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not IEC text or a service-provider conformity conclusion.

## Source and applicability

The [IEC publication record](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/67631), read 2026-09-04, describes security-related integration and maintenance processes offered by IACS service providers. It permits profiles for particular environments and distinguishes provider processes from asset-owner responsibilities. This does not permit arbitrary exclusions or establish the adequacy of a particular provider.

Confirm the applicable edition, licensed requirements, profile, contract and assessment method before evaluation. Full licensed standard, profile rules and conformity methodology remain unverified for this draft. Do not put IEC text into AI prompts without permission. Do not confuse a provider program assessment with certification of a customer's industrial system.

## Engagement focus

Trace promised service capabilities to the specific customer's approved scope and delivered work. Preserve the boundary between provider competence, customer authorization, execution and verified operational outcome.

## Roles

Provider leadership owns its security program and personnel competence. Service delivery owns work records and subcontractor interfaces. Customer asset owners and operations/safety leaders approve site access, operating conditions and changes. An independent reviewer challenges evidence and claims. AI may correlate permitted records and draft questions; it cannot connect to IACS, approve access, perform industrial changes, accept risk or claim conformity.

## Before starting

Record customer, sites, service type, integration versus maintenance scope, supported assets, approved contacts, responsibilities, profile and exclusions. Reconcile contract commitments with actual work orders. Identify confidential evidence, permitted workspace, access expiry, incident contacts and handback requirements. Documentation review does not authorize discovery traffic, remote login or production testing.

## Ordered workflow

1. **Confirm the service boundary.** Inventory every scoped engagement, supported system, remote route and subcontractor. Record who owns each obligation. Obtain approval for profile exclusions and unresolved contract interpretations before dependent conclusions.
2. **Establish readiness.** Check personnel competence, role authorization, approved procedures and required customer prerequisites for each service. Generic staff training does not prove authorization for a particular customer or task.
3. **Authorize the work.** Link the request to its exact assets, performer, scope, time window and customer approval. Include safety coordination, stop criteria and rollback or recovery arrangements. A support contract does not authorize every session or change.
4. **Verify execution records.** Compare actual access/session and change records with the approved job. Preserve deviations, errors and emergency authorization paths. No live IACS action is performed under this guide; obtain permitted records from accountable owners.
5. **Verify handback.** Record customer acceptance or unresolved concerns, configuration/baseline updates, test outcomes and access termination. Distinguish a completed ticket from a verified change. Check temporary accounts, credentials, tunnels and subcontractor access through authorized evidence.
6. **Handle incidents and supplier failures.** Preserve timelines, affected customers and escalation records. Identify provider/customer decision authority and approved communications. Track containment, recovery and corrective actions without assuming that supplier closure proves customer recovery.
7. **Review capability and improve.** Reconcile all in-scope work and findings, retest corrections and update procedures or training. Report unsupported service claims and open customer dependencies. Obtain authorized decisions for residual risk and external assurance wording.

## Evidence and test plan

Maintain complete scoped accounting for customers, services, personnel, sessions, changes, suppliers and incidents. Preserve all evidence supplied for assessment without silent trimming or sampling. Record actual test coverage and unverified outcomes separately from inventory completeness.

### 1. Customer-service boundary package

- **Request and owner:** Service delivery, customer, contract, and security owners provide the approved service description, customer/provider responsibility decisions, supported assets/environments, remote-support boundaries, authorized contacts, and stated exclusions.
- **Validate and limit:** Reconcile all scoped service engagements and trace each to an accountable provider owner, customer owner, authorization boundary, and contract/service reference. This tests clarity of the documented boundary; it cannot establish contract performance or responsibility for unrecorded services.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile authorized service records and flag unclear ownership. Humans approve engagement and responsibility decisions. Refresh upon customer onboarding, scope change, remote-access change, or contract amendment.

### 2. Personnel, remote work, and change-control package

- **Request and owner:** Service management and operations owners provide personnel authorization/competence records, approved remote-support sessions or access-review evidence, maintenance/change tickets, customer approvals where required, and exception records.
- **Validate and limit:** Account for all scoped changes and remote-support events and inspect each applicable record for authorized requester, accountable performer, recorded approvals, evidence trail, and exception handling. This supports documented traceability; it cannot prove every session was authorized or every change was safe.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may link approved tickets, access evidence, and customer approvals, and flag stale access. It must not connect to IACS, approve access, or perform changes. Recollect after an access anomaly, failed review, material change, or personnel role change.

### 3. Incident, supplier, and service-review package

- **Request and owner:** Incident, supplier, service, and customer-relations owners provide incident/escalation records, supplier dependencies, corrective actions, customer communications approved for release, performance/review records, and open-risk decisions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace each scoped incident and material service issue to escalation owner, customer interface, supplier dependency, corrective action, and authorized closure or risk escalation. This tests accountability across the recorded coverage; it cannot determine root cause, guarantee service continuity, or accept residual risk.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may assemble an authorized timeline and surface overdue dependencies. Humans decide incident communications, supplier action, closure, and risk acceptance. Review at approved intervals and after an incident, significant supplier issue, or service-boundary change.


## Failure branches and decisions

- Customer prerequisite missing: stop the dependent job and assign the customer/provider handoff; do not record successful service delivery.
- Session or change exceeds authorization: preserve the discrepancy and escalate; later ticket closure does not retroactively prove authorization.
- Legacy system cannot support a planned control: obtain qualified risk and compensating-measure decisions with the asset owner before dependent work.
- Subcontractor activity is undocumented: retain the provider accountability gap rather than treating outsourcing as an exclusion.
- Access persists after handback: record failed offboarding and request authorized remediation and readback.

Use [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md) assertions: supported, not_supported, inconclusive, not_applicable and not_tested. Keep provider capability, job authorization, execution and effectiveness separate.

## Cadence and renewal

Set service and program reviews according to risk, contracts and verified requirements. Trigger review after personnel changes, incidents, scope changes, new remote access or supplier changes. Do not invent universal quarterly reviews or annual certification duties.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver the service boundary, responsibility decisions, approved profile references, job/evidence register, test coverage, customer handbacks and open actions with owners and due dates. Full source, independent skeptical and rights reviews remain pending. No actual industrial operation or conformity assessment occurred.

Fictional author desk case: a maintenance ticket is closed and customer handback is recorded, but the temporary support tunnel remains enabled. Ticket closure is supported; access termination is not_supported. Escalate the remaining route for authorized correction rather than declaring the engagement complete.
