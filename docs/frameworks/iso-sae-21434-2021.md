# ISO/SAE 21434:2021 automotive cybersecurity engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not ISO/SAE standard text, an automotive cybersecurity assessment, or a conformity claim. Confirm status and edition through the [ISO/SAE 21434:2021 publication record](https://www.iso.org/standard/70918.html).

## Engagement focus

Maintain a lifecycle record for road-vehicle electrical and electronic systems: product intent, cybersecurity governance, assets and interfaces, supplier responsibilities, risk decisions, engineering work products, verification evidence, production and service changes, vulnerability reports, field events, and retirement decisions. Preserve the links between vehicle, component, software version, responsible organization, and customer or regulatory commitments.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Vehicle and product leaders own product intent and lifecycle decisions; engineering maintains development and change evidence; cybersecurity leads coordinate risk and vulnerability work; suppliers supply approved evidence; independent reviewers challenge traceability across the supply chain. AI may organize approved evidence, compare versions, identify missing provenance, and draft review questions, but cannot perform engineering verification, accept residual risk, authorize a vehicle or software release, contact regulators, or claim compliance. Reassess at material design, supplier, production, field, vulnerability, or retirement changes, with accountable human approval for every release and external statement.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [ISO/SAE 21434:2021 publication record](https://www.iso.org/standard/70918.html) and an organization-authorized copy for interpretation; checked 2026-07-31. ISO material is licensed. This original plan does not reproduce the standard, replace vehicle engineering work products, or make a cybersecurity-management or type-approval claim.

### 1. Item, asset, interface, and responsibility record

- **Request and owner:** Vehicle/item and version inventory, cybersecurity-relevant asset/interface records, architecture references, intended-use assumptions, supplier allocation, and named product/cybersecurity owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected item or interface to its version, responsible organization, source record, and supplier boundary. This can show missing ownership or version drift; it cannot prove asset completeness, safety suitability, or technical security performance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare authorized metadata and flag inconsistent versioning. Humans approve item scope, architecture decisions, and supplier allocations. Refresh after design, interface, supplier, or intended-use change.

### 2. Cybersecurity risk, engineering, and verification workpaper

- **Request and owner:** Approved threat/risk decision records, engineering work-product references, security test/verification results, change records, deviations, and residual-risk approvals from cybersecurity, engineering, and product owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected risk decision to its item/version, evidence, decision authority, verification reference, exception, and retest path. This can support decision traceability; it cannot perform threat analysis, validate engineering results, accept residual risk, or authorize a vehicle/software release.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize references and draft discrepancy questions; it may not alter designs or test products. Accountable humans approve risk and release decisions. Refresh after a material design, test failure, or release change.

### 3. Production, field, supplier, and vulnerability-response record

- **Request and owner:** Production configuration/version release records, supplier change notices, vulnerability intake and handling records, field-event/incident records, remediation/retest evidence, and retirement decisions from operations, suppliers, and cybersecurity owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect a selected field or vulnerability event from intake through affected version/supplier, decision, remediation, and closure evidence. This can support accountable response tracking; it cannot determine regulatory reporting, prove fleet-wide remediation, or replace independent assessment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index notices and flag affected-version gaps. Humans approve customer/regulator communication, release, remediation closure, and retirement. Review quarterly and upon supplier, production, field, or vulnerability event.
