# FAR 52.204-27 — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not legal advice or a contract-compliance conclusion. Confirm current clause use at the [Acquisition.gov clause guidance](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/4.2203?searchTerms=52.204-27).

## Source and applicability

Use the [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md). Source check, 2026-09-04: read the
complete [official June 2023 clause](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/52.204-27),
displayed under FAC 2026-01. Its prohibition covers presence or use on specified
Government and contractor IT, including employee-provided equipment in scope;
the IT definition excludes equipment incidental to a contract. An exception
requires Contracting Officer written notification. Paragraph (c) addresses
subcontract flowdown, including commercial products/services. Actual award,
deviations, OMB guidance and current successor-application interpretation remain
to be verified; do not infer a blanket ban on every employee's personal device.


## Engagement focus

Maintain covered-contract inventory, endpoint and managed-device records, application governance, authorized exceptions, communications, incident and remediation evidence, and contract-file traceability.

## Before starting

Define contract identifiers, review period, complete approved device/service
population, evidence permissions, decision owners and reviewer roles. Use
[work items](../../templates/work-item.md) with separate criteria for application
presence, use, prevention and removal. Preserve actual device identifiers and
personal records in controlled evidence locations, not this public repository.

Have the owner approve observation freshness and collection scope before drawing
absence conclusions. Document which profiles, installations or service paths the
method can observe. A tool's default view is not automatically the complete
population. Use only QA-named environments and synthetic data for demonstrations.

## Ordered workflow


1. Obtain the incorporated clause/version, contract modifications and written
   agency instructions. Record contract/legal ownership of applicability and any
   interpretive questions. An online clause revision does not itself amend an award.
2. Reconcile Government-owned/managed, contractor-provided and employee-provided
   IT used for the scoped contract. Link each item to its use and approved scope
   decision. A managed-device inventory alone may omit authorized personal devices;
   a payroll roster alone does not establish which devices are used under contract.
3. Define the authorized evidence method for each device/service category and the
   exact application identifiers or service-use observations to examine. Do not
   assume an application name substring captures every covered service or successor;
   legal/security owners resolve identity and scope from current sources.
4. Compare observed presence and use to the approved criterion. Keep software
   inventory, service access and prevention-policy results separate. A configured
   network block does not prove removal of an installed application; an empty
   software inventory does not prove the service was never used.
5. Record any asserted exception with the actual written Contracting Officer
   notification, affected contract, equipment/use, conditions and period. A local
   manager's approval or pending request is not evidence of the required exception.
6. Route gaps to authorized owners for remediation. Prepare the affected-device
   list and proposed action, then obtain any needed endpoint/privacy authorization.
   Do not inspect personal content or uninstall software from real devices during
   guide development. Use synthetic records in QA-named workspaces for examples.
7. After authorized correction, retrieve a fresh observation for every affected
   item. Record command acceptance, execution and verification separately. Devices
   offline at the observation point remain unresolved; a queued uninstall cannot
   support a completed-removal claim.
8. Reconcile required flowdown to executed subcontracts and responsible owners.
   Keep contract-language evidence distinct from subcontractor operating evidence.
   Preserve all scoped records, failed checks and access gaps without reducing
   evidence supplied to an assessment scorer.

## Failure branches and decisions

 unavailable device evidence is `not_tested`; overall
coverage remains `inconclusive`. An observed installation against an approved
absence criterion is `not_supported`. A disputed successor-app classification
requires an owned decision rather than a confident name-based match. No test
result grants an exception or authorizes a contract representation.

**Fictional desk case:** removal is queued to ten QA device records. Nine return
fresh observations showing absence; the tenth is offline. The nine observations
support their bounded assertions. The tenth verification is `not_tested`, and
“all ten verified clear” is not established. Delivery of the command cannot
substitute for the missing observation. No real employee device was accessed.

## Roles

Authorized contract, legal, security, and IT owners determine scope and compliance actions; independent reviewers test evidence. AI identifies inventory gaps and drafts questions, but cannot remove software, grant exceptions, or make contract representations. Review at the approved award and change triggers below.


## Evidence and test plan

Retain the three PR #340 packages below with full in-scope coverage. An earlier
review does not approve the revised workflow.

### 1. Covered-contract and device-scope package

- **Request and owner:** Contracts, program, endpoint-management, and application-governance owners provide covered award/solicitation records, approved scope decision, managed-device and relevant application inventory, ownership, boundary assumptions, and review calendar.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace each in-scope device or application population entry to its contract context, inventory source, owner, scope rationale, and update date. This confirms documented traceability; it cannot decide whether a contract or technology is covered.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved contract and inventory metadata and flag unowned or stale entries. Humans decide coverage and scope. Refresh after award/modification, inventory discovery, endpoint change, or annual review.

### 2. Software-governance and remediation package

- **Request and owner:** IT, endpoint, software-asset, security, and change-management owners provide approved software governance records, deployment/configuration observations, detection/investigation records, remediation/change tickets, test outcomes, and documented limitations.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace each in-scope remediation or governance assertion to a dated artifact, affected bounded population, accountable owner, validation result, unresolved limitation, and follow-up. This does not establish complete detection or authorize removal of software.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index supplied evidence, flag missing validation or closure dates, and draft remediation questions. Humans validate results and authorize technical changes. Refresh after detection, failed test, material endpoint change, or remediation milestone.

### 3. Exception, representation, and escalation package

- **Request and owner:** Contracts, legal, security, procurement, and executive owners provide exception/risk records, representation-preparation materials, authorized contact list, escalation scenarios, decision approvals, communication log, and management-review evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace each in-scope exception or escalation scenario to its source, named authority, timing, limitation, approval, and follow-up. This supports governance review; it cannot grant an exception, make a representation, or communicate externally.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag stale authorities, unmet follow-up dates, and incomplete decision trails. Authorized humans decide exceptions and communications; independent reviewers challenge the record. Refresh after exception request, contract event, technology discovery, or source change.

### Observation completeness and freshness

Record collection time separately from the device's last observation time. A
newly exported management report may contain old device data. Compare each record
to the owner's approved freshness criterion; do not silently count stale records
as present-day evidence of absence.

Reconcile devices and relevant service paths to the contract-use inventory using
stable identifiers. Preserve offline, unmanaged, duplicate and retired records
with their disposition. Removal from a management console does not by itself
establish that a device ceased contract use. Request the owner's supporting
transition record and current use decision.

For a synthetic QA test, compare the approved evidence method with a known test
state and confirm that it observes the intended profile/scope. Preserve collection
errors and blind spots. A successful query returning no rows is useful only when
the query reached the intended scope and completed successfully.

After an authorized remediation, match the fresh observation to the exact device
and approved criterion. Retest any relevant prevention check separately. Absence
at one time does not prove reinstallation is prevented, and policy assignment
does not prove the endpoint applied that policy.

## Cadence and renewal

Recheck after awards, amendments, device onboarding/offboarding, new contract-use
arrangements, application changes or exception changes. Use a documented internal
review cadence; do not invent a universal annual requirement in this clause.
Reconcile stale/offline records before relying on an older review for a new
representation. Escalate known gaps independently of scheduled reviews.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver the contract/scope ledger, full device/service population, application
identity decisions, dated observations, evidence-method limitations, written
exception references, subcontract records and correction/retest results. Keep
stale and inaccessible items visible with owners and next actions.

State precisely whether an artifact establishes policy configuration, observed
absence, observed use or verified remediation. No single management dashboard
establishes all four. Final contract conclusions and communications remain with
named authorized humans. Independent source, skeptical, rights, publication and
cross-model reviews remain pending; structural checks cannot establish them.
