# FAR 52.204-27 — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not legal advice or a contract-compliance conclusion. Confirm current clause use at the [Acquisition.gov clause guidance](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/4.2203?searchTerms=52.204-27).

## Engagement focus

Maintain covered-contract inventory, endpoint and managed-device records, application governance, authorized exceptions, communications, incident and remediation evidence, and contract-file traceability.

## Device and application verification sequence

Use the [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md). Source check, 2026-09-04: read the
complete [official June 2023 clause](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/52.204-27),
displayed under FAC 2026-01. Its prohibition covers presence or use on specified
Government and contractor IT, including employee-provided equipment in scope;
the IT definition excludes equipment incidental to a contract. An exception
requires Contracting Officer written notification. Paragraph (c) addresses
subcontract flowdown, including commercial products/services. Actual award,
deviations, OMB guidance and current successor-application interpretation remain
to be verified; do not infer a blanket ban on every employee's personal device.

1. Obtain the incorporated clause/version, contract modifications and written
   agency instructions. Record contract/legal ownership of applicability and any
   interpretive questions. An online clause revision does not itself amend an award.
2. Reconcile Government-owned/managed, contractor-provided and employee-provided
   IT used for the scoped contract. Link each item to its use and approved scope
   decision. A managed-device inventory alone may omit authorized personal devices;
   a payroll roster alone does not establish which devices are used under contract.
3. Define the authorized evidence method for each device/service category and the
   exact application identifiers or service-use observations to examine. Do not
   assume an application name substring captures every covered service or successor;
   legal/security owners resolve identity and scope from current sources.
4. Compare observed presence and use to the approved criterion. Keep software
   inventory, service access and prevention-policy results separate. A configured
   network block does not prove removal of an installed application; an empty
   software inventory does not prove the service was never used.
5. Record any asserted exception with the actual written Contracting Officer
   notification, affected contract, equipment/use, conditions and period. A local
   manager's approval or pending request is not evidence of the required exception.
6. Route gaps to authorized owners for remediation. Prepare the affected-device
   list and proposed action, then obtain any needed endpoint/privacy authorization.
   Do not inspect personal content or uninstall software from real devices during
   guide development. Use synthetic records in QA-named workspaces for examples.
7. After authorized correction, retrieve a fresh observation for every affected
   item. Record command acceptance, execution and verification separately. Devices
   offline at the observation point remain unresolved; a queued uninstall cannot
   support a completed-removal claim.
8. Reconcile required flowdown to executed subcontracts and responsible owners.
   Keep contract-language evidence distinct from subcontractor operating evidence.
   Preserve all scoped records, failed checks and access gaps without reducing
   evidence supplied to an assessment scorer.

**Failure branches:** unavailable device evidence is `not_tested`; overall
coverage remains `inconclusive`. An observed installation against an approved
absence criterion is `not_supported`. A disputed successor-app classification
requires an owned decision rather than a confident name-based match. No test
result grants an exception or authorizes a contract representation.

**Fictional desk case:** removal is queued to ten QA device records. Nine return
fresh observations showing absence; the tenth is offline. The nine observations
support their bounded assertions. The tenth verification is `not_tested`, and
“all ten verified clear” is not established. Delivery of the command cannot
substitute for the missing observation. No real employee device was accessed.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Authorized contract, legal, security, and IT owners determine scope and compliance actions; independent reviewers test evidence. AI identifies inventory gaps and drafts questions, but cannot remove software, grant exceptions, or make contract representations. Review at award, change, and annually.
