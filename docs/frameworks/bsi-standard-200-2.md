# BSI Standard 200-2 IT-Grundschutz methodology engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not BSI methodology or a certification result. Confirm the current source and its applicability through the [BSI Standard 200-2 publication](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Grundschutz/International/bsi-standard-2002_en_pdf.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=2).

## Engagement focus

Maintain a living protection-methodology record: organizational scope, service and asset structure, selected approach, security objectives, implemented work, exceptions, risk decisions, evidence of operation, and improvement actions.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Management establishes the desired protection level and accepts risk; security leadership coordinates the program; service owners maintain current evidence; independent reviewers test that claims have support. AI creates evidence inventories, follows change impacts, and drafts targeted review questions, but cannot select a protection approach, judge sufficiency, or issue certification claims. Refresh the engagement plan with material organizational or technical change, conduct periodic evidence reviews, and record human approvals.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [BSI Standard 200-2 publication](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Grundschutz/International/bsi-standard-2002_en_pdf.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=2); checked 2026-07-31. The methodology is protected source material. This guide supplies original evidence-planning language only and does not reproduce modules, requirements, mappings, or certification methods. Management and security leadership confirm the selected approach and applicability.

### 1. Method-selection, scope, and structure package

- **Request and owner:** Security leadership, architecture, and business owners provide approved methodology/approach decision, organizational and service boundary, structure/asset/dependency records, protection needs or objectives, accountable owners, and changes affecting scope.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected service or asset grouping from stated scope to structure record, protection decision, named owner, and source date. This can support a transparent application boundary; it cannot determine the correct approach, prove complete modeling, or establish sufficiency.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare supplied inventories and identify unassigned components or stale change records. Humans select the approach, approve scope, and decide applicability. Refresh after material organizational, service, architecture, supplier, or technology change.

### 2. Implementation, gap, and risk-decision package

- **Request and owner:** Service, security, and risk owners provide implementation references, documented gap or deviation records, risk analysis/decision references, treatment actions, target dates, owners, and completion or retest evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect a selected gap from discovery through its affected boundary, evidence, human decision, treatment, and validation record. This can support visible handling of one identified gap; it cannot prove all gaps are known, authorize exceptions, or make a conformance conclusion.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize evidence links and flag missing decision authority or overdue actions. Humans approve deviations, risk treatment, resource allocation, and closure. Reassess after a failed validation, material risk change, exception expiry, or incident.

### 3. Review, maintenance, and renewal package

- **Request and owner:** Program and service owners provide review schedules, change-impact assessments, operating evidence samples, internal-review results, improvement actions, and management review decisions for the period.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected material change through impact review, owner decision, updated evidence, independent challenge, and follow-up action. This can support disciplined maintenance; it cannot establish ongoing effectiveness or replace an external assessment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare read-only change and evidence summaries and draft review questions. Humans approve updates, risk decisions, and external statements. Review periodically and renew with annual management review or a source-version change.
