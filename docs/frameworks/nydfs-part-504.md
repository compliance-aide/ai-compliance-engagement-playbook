# NYDFS Part 504 — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not AML/sanctions judgment or certification. Consult the [NYDFS transaction-monitoring resource](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry_guidance/transaction_monitoring).

## Engagement focus

Maintain scope, filing ownership, program change, data quality, tuning, governance, testing, alert performance, training, independent testing, remediation, and certification-support evidence.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Operators own procedures and alerts; independent reviewers evaluate filing support without operating scenarios. AI triages evidence gaps, but cannot disposition alerts, override judgments, sign/submit certification, or conceal defects. Review quarterly and annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Plan status:** Draft; independent source and skeptical review required before reliance.

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the current [NYDFS transaction-monitoring resource](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry_guidance/transaction_monitoring) and [23 NYCRR Part 504 text](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2020/07/revpart504text_1.pdf); checked 2026-07-31. Confirm covered-institution scope, current rule status, and certification obligations with counsel and designated compliance leadership. This plan uses original artifact categories and does not direct alert disposition or make AML, sanctions, or certification judgments.

### 1. Coverage, governance, and program-design record

- **Request and owner:** Covered-entity determination, accountable senior-management/program roles, approved transaction-monitoring and filtering program documentation, data lineage, and change-governance records from compliance, technology, and risk owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected system/data feed or material program change to ownership, approval, validation rationale, and effective date. This supports that a governed program record exists; it cannot judge model adequacy, legal applicability, or regulatory sufficiency.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved metadata and identify missing owners/dates. Humans set program policy and approve change. Refresh for a new product, system/feed, merger, material model/tuning change, or rule change.

### 2. Data quality, scenario/filter, and independent-testing record

- **Request and owner:** Data-quality controls and issue logs; approved scenario/filter tuning and change records; validation/testing plans and results; and independent-testing reports and response tracking from technology, compliance, model-risk, and independent-test owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Reconcile a selected data-quality issue or change record to approval, test evidence, defect handling, and closure decision. This can support traceability of declared controls; it cannot validate every alerting outcome, certify model performance, or replace independent testing.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag missing test periods or unresolved findings, but cannot tune a live program or disposition transactions. Authorized humans approve testing conclusions and changes. Recollect after a material defect, adverse test, data change, or annual review.

### 3. Alert governance, training, and certification-support record

- **Request and owner:** Procedures, role-based training completion, alert/escalation quality-review evidence, issue/remediation register, and human-prepared certification-support pack from compliance and operations owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample a completed, approved alert-quality or training record and trace it to date, responsible role, exception, and corrective action without retaining customer/transaction content. This shows an evidence trail, not correct suspicious-activity decisions or filing readiness.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare redacted evidence indexes and gaps reports; authorized personnel make alert, escalation, and certification decisions. Refresh before an annual certification cycle and after significant findings.
