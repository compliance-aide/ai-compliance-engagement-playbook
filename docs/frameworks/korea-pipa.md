# Korea Personal Information Protection Act — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not Korean legal advice or a regulatory conclusion. Confirm the current law and implementing materials through the [Korean Personal Information Protection Commission](https://www.pipc.go.kr/eng/user/lgp/law/lawsRegulations.do).

## Engagement focus

Maintain processing inventory, purpose and authority records, data flows, individual-rights handling, processor oversight, incident evidence, overseas-transfer decisions, retention records, privacy-impact reviews, and remediation history.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Privacy, security, product, and legal owners determine applicability and responses; independent reviewers test evidence separately. AI inventories approved records and drafts questions, but cannot interpret law, decide rights requests, notify authorities, or make representations. Review quarterly and annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [Korean Personal Information Protection Commission](https://www.pipc.go.kr/eng/user/lgp/law/lawsRegulations.do) law and implementing-material hub; checked 2026-07-31. This original plan does not decide applicability, valid collection or use, overseas-transfer conditions, notification duty, or an individual's request outcome.

### 1. Processing purpose, notice, and lifecycle register

- **Request and owner:** Processing inventory, data-flow diagram, collection/notice records, purpose and retention rationale inputs, sensitive or child-data escalation records, and disposal evidence from privacy, product, records, and system owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one processing route from collection interface to stated purpose, owner, system, retention event, and deletion/disposition record. This verifies a factual trail; it cannot determine a lawful basis, notice sufficiency, or retention legality.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare approved inventories and flag unexplained systems or review dates. Legal/privacy humans determine classifications and actions. Refresh for a new purpose, data category, interface, or material system change.

### 2. Requests, processors, and cross-border decision evidence

- **Request and owner:** Individual-request workflow/logs, identity-check procedure, processor contracts and oversight, recipient/service register, overseas-transfer review inputs, and escalation decisions from privacy, legal, procurement, and customer-operations owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a redacted request or processor change to facts, owner, decision authority, communication record, and follow-up. This supports workflow traceability; it cannot decide a request or validate a transfer.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may create an authorized evidence index and deadline prompts; it may not contact individuals, approve a processor, or send regulatory communications. Recollect after a recipient, processor, transfer, or request-process change.

### 3. Security incident and accountability review

- **Request and owner:** Safeguard-operation records, access and monitoring evidence, incident exercise/event timeline, privacy-impact review inputs, exceptions, remediation, and management review from security, privacy, and incident owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample an incident exercise or remediation item through detection, escalation, authority, corrective action, and retest. This does not prove control effectiveness or determine reporting duty.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize redacted evidence and identify stale remediation. Named humans decide escalation, notification, risk, and closure. Review quarterly, annually, and after an incident or source change.
