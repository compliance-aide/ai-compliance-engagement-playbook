# NIST SP 800-73-5 PIV interfaces engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST interface specifications, smart-card or client-API implementation instructions, credential issuance authority, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-73-5 Part 1](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/73/pt1/5/final), Parts 2 and 3, and the organization’s approved identity, security, engineering, privacy, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Operate a governed lifecycle for in-scope PIV card applications, data handling, interfaces, client integrations, devices, middleware, and relying systems. Maintain traceability from use case and system owner through approved interface and version baseline, test and interoperability evidence, deployment, access controls, changes, incident handling, exceptions, supplier dependencies, and lifecycle retirement. A successful interface test, card read, client log, or vendor statement is evidence to assess; it does not prove secure configuration, credential validity, approved access, interoperability in every environment, or compliance.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, identity and access management, security, endpoint or device management, platform engineering, application and relying-system owners, privacy, legal, procurement, supplier-management, change-management, incident-response, and records-management roles. Operators maintain an in-scope interface inventory, approved version and configuration baselines, device and middleware records, integration test results, deployment and change evidence, access and anomaly records, interoperability defects, exceptions, supplier assurances, and decommissioning records. Reconcile deployed interface versions, devices, middleware, and relying systems quarterly; review changes, failed authentication or interface events, supplier notices, exceptions, and unresolved risks at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material credential, device, application, provider, or policy changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples integration-to-deployment-to-change traceability; auditors test the evidence trail without issuing credentials, changing card or client configuration, granting access, approving exceptions, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied inventory, test, deployment, change, and review evidence, identify missing ownership or stale versions, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot implement or change a card interface or client API, issue or use a credential, grant access, access private keys, approve an exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [NIST SP 800-73-5 Part 1 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/73/pt1/5/final), applicable Parts 2 and 3, and approved identity, security, engineering, privacy, and operational decisions; checked 2026-07-31. This is original evidence-planning language, not a reproduction of PIV interface specifications, an implementation recipe, credential issuance authority, or a conformance claim.

### 1. PIV application, interface, and relying-system inventory package

- **Request and owner:** Identity, endpoint, platform, application, and relying-system owners provide the in-scope card-application and interface inventory, client/middleware and device versions, approved use cases, system and endpoint boundaries, interface/dependency references, accountable owners, and lifecycle status.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one interface use from its approved use case to the application/card-service boundary, client or middleware baseline, device class, relying system, owner, and review date. This cannot read a card, validate an APDU or API exchange, establish interoperability, credential validity, access authorization, or compliance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile authorized inventory metadata and flag unowned or stale interface/version entries. Humans approve scope, supported configurations, use cases, and changes; AI cannot connect to a card, client API, or relying system. Refresh after a device, middleware, application, interface, or ownership change.

### 2. Integration, deployment, and access-control package

- **Request and owner:** Engineering, endpoint management, security, and application owners provide approved integration designs, non-sensitive test references, deployment/change records, endpoint/device configuration baselines, administrative-access records, release approvals, defect records, and rollback plans.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample one integration or release from approved design through test reference, controlled deployment, device/client baseline, access boundary, owner, and follow-up. This cannot execute an interface transaction, test every environment, reveal protected configuration, grant access, or establish secure deployment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index deployment evidence and flag missing change, baseline, or owner links. Humans approve releases, configuration changes, access, exceptions, and remediation. Refresh after a material integration, deployment, configuration, defect, or access-control change.

### 3. Interoperability defects, supplier, and retirement package

- **Request and owner:** Operations, supplier-management, security, incident-response, and records owners provide interoperability test/defect summaries, vendor support and compatibility notices, incident and escalation records, exception decisions, replacement/retirement plans, evidence-retention references, and annual review outputs.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected defect, supplier notice, or retirement event to its source record, affected boundary, accountable decision, corrective action, compatibility/retest reference where available, and closure or next-review date. This cannot certify interoperability, determine root cause, accept residual risk, or attest for management.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may identify recurring defect categories, stale supplier notices, and overdue retirement actions from supplied records. Humans decide escalation, procurement, remediation, exception/risk acceptance, and retirement. Review quarterly and after a supplier notice, incident, material failure, or lifecycle change.
