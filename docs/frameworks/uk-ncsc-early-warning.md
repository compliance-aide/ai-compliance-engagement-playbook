# UK NCSC Early Warning engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NCSC service instructions, an enrollment decision, an incident determination, a command to remediate, a certification, or a compliance claim. Confirm current eligibility and service operation through [NCSC Early Warning](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/section/active-cyber-defence/early-warning) and the organization’s approved security, legal, privacy, incident-response, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Operate a reliable intake-to-closure process for potential-threat notifications associated with approved organizational domains and public IP addresses. Establish documented asset scope, authorized contacts, alert routing, triage criteria, investigation records, escalation paths, remediation decisions, exception handling, communications, and closure validation. A notification is a prompt to investigate; it does not establish compromise, severity, root cause, legal-reporting obligations, or that every exposure is covered.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, security-operations, network, domain, vulnerability-management, incident-response, service-owner, privacy, legal, communications, supplier-management, and records-management roles. Operators maintain authorized account and contact records, registered-asset reconciliation, alert-routing rules, triage and escalation procedures, investigation artifacts, ticket links, remediation and exception evidence, and metrics for timeliness and aging. Reconcile registered assets and contact paths quarterly; review unresolved and recurring alerts, escalation effectiveness, and supplier dependencies at least quarterly; and perform an annual management review after material asset, contact, service, network, or threat changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples alerts from receipt through closure and verifies that scope, decisions, and evidence are traceable; auditors test the evidence trail without registering assets, directing incident response, deciding whether an alert is an incident, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied alert and case evidence, correlate approved asset inventories with documented registration, identify missing ownership or overdue follow-up, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot enroll assets, monitor or access networks, determine compromise, direct remediation, make notification decisions, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.
