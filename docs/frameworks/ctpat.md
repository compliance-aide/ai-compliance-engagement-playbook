# CTPAT — supply-chain program engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not CBP criteria or a program submission. Consult [CBP CTPAT](https://www.cbp.gov/border-security/ports-entry/cargo-security/CTPAT).

## Engagement focus

Confirm entity category and participation status; maintain international map, partner verification, contract, annual profile-review, reassessment, validation-interview, and walkthrough evidence.

## Roles

Operators maintain evidence; independent reviewers test claimed operation. AI flags partner and route gaps, but cannot fabricate submissions, certify, or submit to CBP. Review annually and on material events.

## Source and applicability

Start with the [CBP CTPAT program](https://www.cbp.gov/border-security/ports-entry/cargo-security/CTPAT), current Minimum Security Criteria for the entity category and authorized member guidance. CBP describes eligibility review, application and a security profile explaining actual implementation; risk assessment precedes that profile. Certification and subsequent CBP validation are different milestones. Do not interpret a completed internal review or portal receipt as either milestone.

Record entity category, legal entity, facilities, international supply-chain roles and participation status. Retrieve the complete applicable criteria and current portal instructions before defining obligations or deadlines. Do not apply importer criteria indiscriminately to a carrier, broker or other participant category. Keep CTPAT security-program work distinct from separate trade-compliance commitments.

## Before starting

Follow the [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md). Assign trade compliance, logistics, facility security, procurement, IT security and management owners. Use authorized business records and safe walkthroughs; do not expose routes, access arrangements or security weaknesses in public artifacts. Create work items per applicable criterion, facility/route and accountable party. AI prepares factual records and questions; authorized humans approve profile answers, program representations and government submissions.

## Ordered workflow

| Step | Action and accountable owner | Output and exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Confirm program boundary | Trade compliance checks entity category, criteria version and actual participation records. | Approved scope and source register; unknown eligibility or status stays unresolved. |
| 2. Map the supply chain | Logistics and procurement reconcile facilities, routes, handoffs, carriers and other partners. | Complete scoped map with ownership and evidence gaps; subcontracted legs remain visible. |
| 3. Assess risks | Management and security owners evaluate documented threats, vulnerabilities and existing practices. | Approved risk assessment connected to actual routes and facilities, before drafting implementation claims. |
| 4. Allocate criteria | Owners review every applicable criterion against internal and partner responsibilities. | Full disposition register, evidence requests and justified exceptions; no blanket outsourcing assumption. |
| 5. Verify operation | Reviewers trace approved inspections, access events, cargo handoffs, training and incident records. | Dated workpapers with expected/observed operation, site/route coverage and contradictions. |
| 6. Correct and retest | Owners resolve approved failures and verify affected partners, sites and processes. | Corrective-action evidence and remaining risks; a revised procedure alone does not prove changed practice. |
| 7. Reconcile the profile | Trade compliance compares each proposed statement with current evidence and prior CBP findings. | Human-approved factual profile draft; unresolved gaps and supporting records retained for authorized review. |
| 8. Sustain and hand off | Management prepares for authorized CBP engagement and tracks profile review, changes and findings. | Separate preparation/submission/certification/validation records with actual evidence and next owners. |

## Failure branches and decisions

- **Partner status is asserted but unverified:** request authorized current verification and record its date and scope; a logo cannot prove participation or performance.
- **Cargo handoff lacks inspection or seal evidence:** identify the affected shipment and responsible parties, preserve records and invoke the approved escalation procedure. AI does not decide reporting duties.
- **New subcontractor bypasses review:** reopen route and partner risk assessment and assign the unreviewed handoff; do not inherit the prime partner's assurance automatically.
- **Profile promises a practice absent at the site:** retain the discrepancy, correct the practice through authorized action and revise the draft claim as appropriate before approval.
- **Prior CBP finding is marked closed internally:** request the actual required closure evidence and distinguish internal remediation from CBP acceptance.
- **Unexpected security incident during review:** follow the site's response authority and preserve evidence; do not continue a walkthrough that interferes with operations or investigation.

## Evidence and test plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [CBP CTPAT program page](https://www.cbp.gov/border-security/ports-entry/cargo-security/CTPAT) and the organization’s authorized program materials; prior snapshot 2026-07-31; verify current category-specific criteria. Program criteria and validation materials may have use conditions. This is original evidence-planning guidance, not reproduced CBP criteria, a validation, a program application, or a participation claim. Accountable human leaders determine entity category, scope, and any communication with CBP.

### 1. Supply-chain scope and business-partner record

- **Request and owner:** Legal-entity and facility scope, international route and conveyance map, business-partner inventory, partner onboarding/verification records, contracts, and owner assignments from trade compliance, logistics, procurement, and legal owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a human-selected route or partner from current scope through verification evidence, contractual context, owner, and review date. This supports evidence traceability for the selected record; it cannot determine CTPAT eligibility, establish complete supply-chain coverage, or prove program conformity.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile authorized inventory metadata and flag unassigned, expired, or inconsistent partner reviews. Humans determine program scope, approve partners and contracts, and make CBP representations. Refresh after a new country, facility, route, carrier, supplier, acquisition, or material logistics change.

### 2. Site and conveyance security-operation workpaper

- **Request and owner:** Site-access, visitor, seal/conveyance, cargo-handling, incident, training, inspection, and corrective-action records from facility security, logistics, operations, and security owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected operational event, inspection, or exception to its contemporaneous record, accountable response, corrective action, and retest or follow-up. This can demonstrate a limited evidence trail; it cannot validate every operating practice, certify physical security, or decide whether an event must be reported.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved records and flag missing dates, owners, or closure links; it may not access restricted areas, alter a security process, classify an event, or authorize a corrective action. Humans decide investigations, notifications, remediation, and closure. Recollect after a security incident, failed inspection, facility/conveyance change, or control-process revision.

### 3. Profile-review and independent-challenge packet

- **Request and owner:** Annual profile-review inputs, prior validation/interview and walkthrough records where authorized, exception register, management decisions, remediation evidence, and evidence-retention record from trade compliance, leadership, and independent-review owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected open issue or profile change to the supporting evidence, accountable decision, due date, and closure/retest. This supports readiness transparency; it cannot replace a CBP validation, accept residual risk, or authorize a profile update or submission.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a non-authoritative evidence index and factual challenge questions. Humans approve profile changes, risk acceptance, closure, and any external submission. Review annually and after a validation contact, material exception, program change, or significant supply-chain event.


## Cadence and renewal

Maintain annual profile-review planning and event-driven reassessment. Verify exact current requirements and member-specific dates from applicable criteria, portal records and authorized CBP correspondence. Reopen affected work after new routes, facilities, partners, acquisitions, security incidents or criteria changes. Historical program timelines do not establish the next validation date for a particular member.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver the source/category decision, complete criteria dispositions, supply-chain map, risk assessment, operation workpapers, corrective actions and approved profile draft. Record actual external milestones only when supported by CBP records. Name the next action, owner and evidence needed without earlier chat. Readiness does not establish program certification, validation or unrestricted supply-chain security.
