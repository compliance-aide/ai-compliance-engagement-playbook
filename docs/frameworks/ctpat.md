# CTPAT — supply-chain program engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not CBP criteria or a program submission. Consult [CBP CTPAT](https://www.cbp.gov/border-security/ports-entry/cargo-security/CTPAT).

## Engagement focus

Confirm entity category and participation status; maintain international map, partner verification, contract, annual profile-review, reassessment, validation-interview, and walkthrough evidence.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Operators maintain evidence; independent reviewers test claimed operation. AI flags partner and route gaps, but cannot fabricate submissions, certify, or submit to CBP. Review annually and on material events.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [CBP CTPAT program page](https://www.cbp.gov/border-security/ports-entry/cargo-security/CTPAT) and the organization’s authorized program materials; checked 2026-07-31. Program criteria and validation materials may have use conditions. This is original evidence-planning guidance, not reproduced CBP criteria, a validation, a program application, or a participation claim. Accountable human leaders determine entity category, scope, and any communication with CBP.

### 1. Supply-chain scope and business-partner record

- **Request and owner:** Legal-entity and facility scope, international route and conveyance map, business-partner inventory, partner onboarding/verification records, contracts, and owner assignments from trade compliance, logistics, procurement, and legal owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a human-selected route or partner from current scope through verification evidence, contractual context, owner, and review date. This supports evidence traceability for the selected record; it cannot determine CTPAT eligibility, establish complete supply-chain coverage, or prove program conformity.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile authorized inventory metadata and flag unassigned, expired, or inconsistent partner reviews. Humans determine program scope, approve partners and contracts, and make CBP representations. Refresh after a new country, facility, route, carrier, supplier, acquisition, or material logistics change.

### 2. Site and conveyance security-operation workpaper

- **Request and owner:** Site-access, visitor, seal/conveyance, cargo-handling, incident, training, inspection, and corrective-action records from facility security, logistics, operations, and security owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected operational event, inspection, or exception to its contemporaneous record, accountable response, corrective action, and retest or follow-up. This can demonstrate a limited evidence trail; it cannot validate every operating practice, certify physical security, or decide whether an event must be reported.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved records and flag missing dates, owners, or closure links; it may not access restricted areas, alter a security process, classify an event, or authorize a corrective action. Humans decide investigations, notifications, remediation, and closure. Recollect after a security incident, failed inspection, facility/conveyance change, or control-process revision.

### 3. Profile-review and independent-challenge packet

- **Request and owner:** Annual profile-review inputs, prior validation/interview and walkthrough records where authorized, exception register, management decisions, remediation evidence, and evidence-retention record from trade compliance, leadership, and independent-review owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected open issue or profile change to the supporting evidence, accountable decision, due date, and closure/retest. This supports readiness transparency; it cannot replace a CBP validation, accept residual risk, or authorize a profile update or submission.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a non-authoritative evidence index and factual challenge questions. Humans approve profile changes, risk acceptance, closure, and any external submission. Review annually and after a validation contact, material exception, program change, or significant supply-chain event.
