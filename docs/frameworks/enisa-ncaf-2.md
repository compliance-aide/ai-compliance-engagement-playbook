# ENISA National Capabilities Assessment Framework 2.0 engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not ENISA framework content or a national maturity determination. Confirm the current framework through the [ENISA NCAF 2.0 page](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/national-cyber-security-strategies/ncaf).

## Engagement focus

Maintain a policy-level evidence record for cybersecurity strategy execution: accountable authorities, capability objectives, program decisions, stakeholder inputs, measures, maturity evidence, gaps, priorities, and improvement actions.

## Roles

National authorities own strategic direction; program leads maintain evidence; stakeholders provide operational inputs; independent reviewers test the assessment narrative. AI structures approved records, compares stated goals to evidence, and drafts review questions, but cannot assess national maturity, set policy, or make public claims. Review the record on a planned cycle and after material strategy or threat changes, with human approval for published results.

## Source and applicability

Use the [NCAF 2.0 report](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/national-capabilities-assessment-framework-20) and matching tool, published April 22, 2026. ENISA describes a national-authority self-assessment of cybersecurity-strategy implementation at strategic and operational levels. This is not an enterprise certification checklist or proof that an individual organization meets NIS2.

Record the selected methodology and tool versions, national strategy, assessment period and authorized sponsor. Reconcile earlier assessments before comparing results: the updated framework changes maturity descriptions, objective groupings and questions. Retrieve the complete instrument before claiming coverage; do not invent scoring rules or substitute a small set of easily available indicators.

## Before starting

Follow the [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md). Assign national sponsor, coordination lead, capability owners, agency evidence custodians and independent reviewers. Agree protected-information handling, publication scope and stakeholder participation. Keep classified, operationally sensitive and personal records outside public workpapers. Create one work item per assessment question/objective with source, authority, evidence owner and period.

## Ordered workflow

| Step | Action and accountable owner | Output and exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Establish mandate | Sponsor approves the strategy boundary, period, participants and intended use. | Written authority and scope; partial institutional coverage is explicitly identified. |
| 2. Reconcile the instrument | Coordination lead inventories all selected-version objectives and questions. | Complete coverage ledger with old/new-version differences and approved disposition of every item. |
| 3. Allocate evidence | Capability owners identify agencies, programs and sources needed for each claim. | Request register distinguishing policy adoption, implementation and demonstrated outcomes. |
| 4. Collect and reconcile | Custodians supply authorized evidence; reviewers track missing, stale and conflicting inputs. | Source-linked records with period and national coverage limits; one agency's example is not national proof. |
| 5. Evaluate the narrative | Qualified reviewers compare evidence against the official method and challenge assumptions. | Human-reviewed response rationale and unresolved disagreements; AI does not assign national maturity. |
| 6. Prioritize improvements | Sponsor and capability owners assess dependencies, resources and strategic consequences. | Approved priorities with accountable owners, milestones and measurable intended outcomes. |
| 7. Verify progress | Reviewers trace completed actions to implementation and outcome evidence. | Retest/follow-up records; funding approval or policy issuance alone does not prove delivered capability. |
| 8. Approve reporting | National authority reviews confidentiality, limitations and representations. | Approved report or restricted handoff, with publication decision and next assessment triggers. |

## Failure branches and decisions

- **Single-agency evidence presented as national coverage:** identify the missing agencies/sectors and collect support or narrow the assertion explicitly.
- **Strategy exists but execution evidence is absent:** retain policy evidence and the implementation gap separately; do not infer operational maturity.
- **Two agencies disagree:** preserve both sources, scope and period; route reconciliation to the coordinator rather than averaging narratives.
- **Metric rises because its denominator changed:** reconcile population and method before claiming improvement.
- **Restricted evidence cannot be shared:** retain an authorized custodian/reference and disclose the review limitation; do not fabricate a public substitute.
- **New framework score compared with old score:** verify methodological comparability before describing a trend; display version differences and unresolved effects.

## Worked handoff example

A fictional capability owner supplies an approved national incident plan but no exercise or operating records. The assertion that the plan was approved may be `supported`; the separate assertion that coordination works nationally remains `inconclusive`. Request authorized exercise/operation evidence from the coordinator and participating agencies. Neither result assigns a maturity level, and neither permits publishing the national capability as proven.

## Evidence and test plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Consult the official [ENISA NCAF 2.0 page](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/national-cyber-security-strategies/ncaf); prior snapshot 2026-07-31; verify current methodology and terms. This is original policy-level engagement guidance, not a reproduction of NCAF content, a national assessment, or a maturity determination. A named national authority or authorized program sponsor confirms source terms, scope, and any publication permissions.

### 1. National capability boundary and authority record

- **Request and owner:** Approved strategy/capability boundary, responsible authority and stakeholder map, program mandate, planning assumptions, priority outcomes, legal/policy dependencies, and dated sponsor decisions from national authorities and program leads.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected capability objective to its mandated owner, approved strategic context, stakeholder input, and decision record. This can support transparent program framing; it cannot determine national maturity, policy adequacy, or statutory compliance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize authorized metadata and flag missing authority, evidence, or review-date fields. Human authorities approve strategy, scope, priority, and publication decisions. Refresh after a strategy, mandate, responsible-authority, material threat, or capability-boundary change.

### 2. Capability implementation and measurement evidence

- **Request and owner:** Approved program plans, initiative portfolios, responsible-agency inputs, measures and data-source descriptions, milestone records, exercise/incident learning where authorized, and known gaps from capability and program owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect a selected initiative or measure from stated objective through source record, accountable owner, status rationale, and documented limitation. This supports a bounded evidence narrative; it cannot verify national coverage, measure effectiveness, or validate classified/operational information.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile supplied, authorized records and flag stale measures or unlinked milestones; it cannot collect protected data, assign maturity, set targets, or alter a program. Humans approve measurement methods and actions. Refresh after a material initiative, measurement-method, incident-learning, or stakeholder change.

### 3. Gap, improvement, and independent challenge record

- **Request and owner:** Gap/prioritization records, investment or remediation decisions, implementation follow-up, independent challenge notes, management review, and proposed public-report approvals from program leadership and reviewers.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected gap to evidence, accountable authority, priority rationale, action owner, review date, and independent challenge. This can support accountable improvement governance; it cannot establish that gaps are resolved or support a public maturity claim.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare non-authoritative review packets and flag overdue actions. Humans approve priorities, resources, risk treatment, external statements, and closure. Review to the approved program cycle and following a material strategic or threat change.


## Cadence and renewal

Use the sponsor-approved strategy and assessment cycle. Reopen affected records after mandate, strategy, capability ownership, threat context or methodology changes. Preserve historical evidence and definitions so progress is assessed consistently. Public reporting intervals and legal duties require separate authoritative decisions.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver mandate/source records, full question coverage, agency evidence inventory, reviewed rationales, disagreements and improvement decisions. State the institutional and temporal limits of the assessment. Name the next owner, action and evidence without earlier chat. A complete response set or dashboard does not independently prove national effectiveness or authorize publication.
