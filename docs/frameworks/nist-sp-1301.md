# NIST SP 1301 organizational-profile engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST CSF content, an organizational-profile determination, a compliance claim, or professional advice. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 1301](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/1301/final) and the organization's approved mission, security, privacy, legal, financial, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage organizational profiles as living decision-support records that connect business objectives, stakeholder expectations, threats, requirements, cybersecurity outcomes, current conditions, target conditions, gaps, priorities, and investment choices. Make clear whether each profile is enterprise-wide, service-specific, product-specific, or scoped to another defined boundary. Preserve the assumptions, sources, owners, and approval history behind each selected outcome so profile updates do not become untraceable spreadsheet edits.

Use a current profile to describe evidence-backed conditions and a target profile to express approved future intent. Link each meaningful difference to accountable work, dependencies, treatment rationale, evidence expectations, review timing, and escalation conditions. Distinguish an incomplete record from an unmet target; neither should be automatically treated as a failure without responsible human assessment.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Leadership establishes mission priorities and approves material targets. Business, security, technology, privacy, legal, finance, risk, product, supplier, records, and service owners contribute within their authority. Operators maintain scope statements, profile versions, outcome-to-evidence links, gap records, work plans, assumptions, dependencies, decisions, exceptions, and review history. Independent reviewers examine traceability and evidence; auditors evaluate supplied records without selecting outcomes, setting targets, accepting risk, approving plans, or attesting.

Review profiles at least quarterly for evidence freshness, ownership, target progress, and material changes. Revisit them after an incident, service or architecture change, supplier change, acquisition, regulatory development, strategic shift, or material new risk. Conduct an annual profile review that confirms scope, stakeholder needs, current and target conditions, remaining gaps, resourcing decisions, and next-year priorities.

Independent reviewers sample one profile outcome from scope and rationale through evidence, prioritization, planned work, and leadership review, testing whether changes are explainable. Auditors evaluate supplied evidence without defining organizational objectives, choosing outcomes, setting targets, accepting risk, approving investment, making legal or financial conclusions, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize profile records, compare approved versions, identify missing owners or stale evidence, draft workpapers and review questions, and summarize supplied information. AI cannot set scope, select outcomes, determine adequacy, prioritize investments, accept risk, approve plans, make legal or financial conclusions, or attest to conformance.
