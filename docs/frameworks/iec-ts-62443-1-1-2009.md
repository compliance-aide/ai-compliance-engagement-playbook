# IEC TS 62443-1-1:2009 IACS security concepts engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not IEC definitions, a binding interpretation or a security-level decision.

## Source and applicability

The [IEC publication record](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/7029), checked 2026-09-04, identifies terminology, concepts and models supporting the 62443 series. Full licensed definitions and their relationships to later part-specific editions remain unverified. Obtain authorized sources and record exact editions before resolving technical meanings; do not reproduce protected definitions or diagrams here.

This guide maintains an engagement's working vocabulary and model. Agreement on labels does not establish architecture correctness, safety or conformity. Use the [series routing guide](iec-62443.md) to identify related workstreams for qualified applicability review.

## Engagement focus

Make each important term resolve to the same object, boundary and decision context for operators, engineers, reviewers and AI agents. Preserve ambiguity instead of silently turning similar words into equivalent requirements.

## Roles

Engineering and operations own system boundaries and model references. Safety, security and risk owners approve interpretations affecting their decisions. Suppliers clarify their own terminology. An independent reviewer challenges ambiguous labels and unsupported equivalence. AI may organize permitted vocabulary and identify conflicts; it cannot issue authoritative definitions, set security levels, accept risk, alter OT or approve safety/architecture decisions.

## Before starting

Record engagement scope, systems, stakeholders, source editions and existing glossaries. Collect approved diagrams, inventories and decision records. Define permissions for restricted source material. Use identifiers as well as human-readable names to avoid merging distinct assets with similar labels. No industrial access or scanning is authorized by this work.

## Ordered workflow

1. **Collect the working vocabulary.** Inventory all scoped terms, abbreviations, diagram labels and named objects used in decisions. Record source document, context, owner and version. Keep conflicting meanings visible.
2. **Identify authoritative references.** Qualified owners locate permitted definitions and part-specific context. Record references rather than copying restricted text. Distinguish an organization's local label from a standards term.
3. **Reconcile the model.** Link each asset, interface and responsibility to its approved boundary and identifier. Flag missing objects and inconsistent relationships. Do not infer isolation, ownership or security from a name alone.
4. **Resolve consequential ambiguity.** Route disputes to the relevant engineering, safety or risk authority. Preserve proposed meanings, evidence, decision and limits. If unresolved, stop dependent conclusions while continuing unrelated evidence organization.
5. **Apply approved terminology.** Update authorized working records with reviewed labels and traceable references. Preserve original source wording and change history so old decisions remain reconstructable. Renaming an object must not silently change its scope.
6. **Challenge the handoff.** Have the reviewer trace terms from a requirement or decision through the diagram, inventory and evidence. Record where another reader cannot identify the same object or distinguish a target from an observed result.
7. **Maintain versioned meaning.** Reopen affected records after source, architecture, supplier or organizational changes. Assign owners and next actions for unresolved interpretations. Approval of the glossary does not approve the underlying industrial system.

## Evidence and test plan

Maintain complete scoped accounting of terms, objects, references and disputes. Preserve all evidence supplied for assessment without silent trimming or sampling. Record what was checked separately from claims of model completeness.

### 1. Working-boundary and vocabulary package

- **Request and owner:** Human-maintained system/asset boundary record, approved working glossary, interface labels, accountable owners, and a log of undefined or disputed terms, from OT engineering and governance owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace every scoped term and boundary label to its owner, context, date, and unresolved ambiguity. This supports shared evidence handling; it cannot establish an authoritative interpretation or complete architecture.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may identify inconsistent labels and draft questions. Humans resolve terms and scope. Refresh before material architecture, ownership, or governance changes.

### 2. Context and decision-trace package

- **Request and owner:** Process/safety dependency context, approved diagrams or inventories, decision records, assumptions, and references to authorized evidence, from operations, safety, and system owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace each scoped decision term to its stated context, human authority, and evidence reference. This supports traceability; it cannot decide safety impact, engineering outcome, or risk.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may map authorized references into a read-only workpaper; it cannot change records or infer approvals. Reopen after an incident, process change, or material supplier change.

### 3. Ambiguity escalation and independent challenge package

- **Request and owner:** Ambiguity/escalation register, human resolutions or deferrals, review dates, affected evidence links, and independent reviewer challenge notes.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace every recorded ambiguity to a responsible authority, documented disposition, limitation, and revisit date. This supports disciplined escalation; it cannot turn a draft resolution into a binding framework interpretation.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag unowned or overdue entries. Independent reviewers test traceability and boundary clarity. Review at approved intervals and whenever a material interpretation question emerges.

## Failure branches and decisions

- Same name identifies different objects: retain separate identifiers and obtain naming clarification before combining evidence.
- Different names appear to identify one object: require owner confirmation rather than automatic deduplication.
- Later source uses a term differently: record edition-specific treatment and qualified resolution; do not silently rewrite historical decisions.
- A security-level label lacks context: obtain the source and decision type before interpreting it.
- Supplier terminology conflicts with the engagement: preserve both and resolve the interface explicitly.

Use [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md) assertions: supported, not_supported, inconclusive, not_applicable and not_tested. A supported terminology reference is not evidence that the described control operates.

## Cadence and renewal

Review when relevant terminology, editions, architecture, roles or suppliers change and at approved governance intervals. Do not invent annual certification duties or a universal glossary expiry.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver the versioned working glossary, object/boundary links, authoritative references, approved resolutions and open disputes with owners and dates.
