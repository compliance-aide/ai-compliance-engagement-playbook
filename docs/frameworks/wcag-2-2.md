# WCAG 2.2 — accessibility engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not a W3C conformance claim. Consult the [WCAG 2.2 Recommendation](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/).

## Engagement focus

Maintain product/content inventory, target level, acceptance, automated and human evaluation, findings, user feedback, remediation, retest, and unresolved-barrier evidence.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Humans validate with assistive technology and approve claims; independent reviewers sample results. AI triages issues and drafts suggestions, but cannot make conformance claims. Test continuously, review quarterly, and annually.


## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [WCAG 2.2 Recommendation](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/); checked 2026-07-31. This is original evaluation-planning language, not a copied criterion list or conformance claim. A named product/accessibility owner approves target level, evaluation scope, and any public statement.

### 1. Product/content inventory and target record

- **Request and owner:** Complete-process and relevant page-set inventory, product/template/content/platform and third-party boundaries, declared target level, accessibility-supported technology assumptions, acceptance criteria, release owner, and known exclusions from product and accessibility owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected user journey or page to the complete process/page set, target, supported technology, owner, and release boundary. This can support a stated test population; it cannot prove the population is complete or establish conformance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize inventories and flag changed surfaces. Humans approve target, scope, exceptions, and public claims. Refresh after a material release, template/component, content, or third-party change.

### 2. Automated and human evaluation evidence

- **Request and owner:** Tool outputs, manual keyboard/focus and assistive-technology test records, test environment/version, evaluator notes, user-feedback inputs where authorized, and defect records from accessibility and product teams.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace an approved selection to the actual product version, test method, evaluator, result, limitation, and defect follow-up. This can support test evidence for the stated sample; automation alone cannot establish conformance or usability for all people and contexts.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may triage findings and draft remediation suggestions but cannot run an unapproved claim process or decide a result. Re-evaluate after material fixes/releases and during quarterly review.

### 3. Barrier remediation and claim-governance record

- **Request and owner:** Prioritized issue backlog, remediation/retest evidence, unresolved-barrier and third-party records, exception decisions, and human review of any statement from product, legal, and accessibility owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected issue from discovery to owner, remediation, retest, and any remaining limitation. This can support accountable improvement; it cannot convert unresolved barriers into a conformance determination.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag overdue retests and assemble evidence. Humans approve disposition, exceptions, and external statements. Review continuously, quarterly, and annually.
