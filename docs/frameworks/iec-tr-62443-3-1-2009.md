# IEC TR 62443-3-1:2009 IACS technology-assessment engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not IEC text, product selection or an industrial safety decision.

## Source and applicability

The [IEC publication record](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/7031), checked 2026-09-04, describes an assessment of cybersecurity technology categories and their advantages, limitations and use in IACS. The report dates to 2009. Its publication status does not establish that historical technology advice is suitable for a current product or threat environment. Verify current supplier evidence, support, vulnerabilities and governing requirements before decisions.

Full licensed report and current detailed applicability remain unverified. Obtain authorized source access; do not reproduce protected descriptions here. This workflow organizes evaluation evidence rather than prescribing a technology or establishing conformity.

## Engagement focus

Connect the operational problem to approved evaluation criteria, current candidate evidence, integration constraints and an accountable decision. Separate marketed capability, observed performance and suitability for the intended industrial environment.

## Roles

Engineering, operations, safety and security owners approve technical criteria and interpret suitability. Procurement owns commercial evidence and purchase authority. Suppliers explain capabilities and limitations. An independent reviewer challenges comparison fairness and unresolved assumptions. AI may organize permitted records and draft questions; it cannot select products, approve architecture, accept risk, initiate scans, change OT or make safety decisions.

## Before starting

Record the problem, affected processes, boundaries, interfaces, operating modes and approved security objectives. Identify existing controls, safety constraints, owner roles and permitted evidence. Define candidate population and exclusion rationale. No product installation, purchase, remote access or active industrial testing is authorized by this documentation review.

## Ordered workflow

1. **Define the decision.** State the required outcome and operational constraints. Distinguish a missing security capability from a configuration or ownership problem before considering new technology.
2. **Approve comparison criteria.** Qualified owners establish required behavior, compatibility, performance, failure handling, maintenance and support expectations. Separate mandatory conditions from preferences. Record criteria before inspecting favorable results.
3. **Reconcile candidate evidence.** Inventory every scoped candidate, exact version, supplier claim, dated source and uncertainty. Check current support and vulnerability information. Do not treat a 2009 category description as a present-day product endorsement.
4. **Review integration assumptions.** Trace dependencies, data/control paths, administrative access and operating modes. Examine how failure, loss of connectivity or maintenance affects the process. Supplier compatibility statements require confirmation for the actual intended configuration.
5. **Evaluate permitted observations.** Use approved lab reports or separately authorized isolated evaluations. Preserve conditions, raw outcomes, failures and coverage limits. A test that demonstrates detection does not necessarily demonstrate prevention, response or safe operational behavior.
6. **Resolve conflicts and decide.** Present evidence against approved criteria, retaining disagreements and untested conditions. Named authorities decide suitability, procurement and residual risk separately. A favorable comparison does not authorize a purchase or deployment.
7. **Hand off and revisit.** Record selected configuration, prerequisites, operating owner, verification plan and unresolved actions. Reopen after product, supplier, vulnerability, architecture or process changes. Verify implemented outcomes through separately authorized work before claiming protection.

## Evidence and test plan

Account for all scoped questions, candidates, observations and decisions. Preserve all evidence supplied for assessment without silent trimming or sampling. Complete accounting does not establish exhaustive product testing or threat coverage.

### 1. Assessment purpose and industrial-context package

- **Request and owner:** Human-approved evaluation purpose, system boundary, operational and safety constraints, interfaces/dependencies, stakeholder roles, and permitted evidence sources, from OT engineering and operations.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace every scoped evaluation question to a named owner, industrial context, constraint, and source boundary. This supports a bounded work program; it cannot establish complete scope or authorize design work.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize supplied metadata and surface missing ownership. Humans approve scope. Revisit before material architecture, process, supplier, or safety change.

### 2. Authorized technology-evaluation package

- **Request and owner:** Approved comparison criteria, supplier representations, test/lab observations, integration assumptions, lifecycle/support information, and identified conflicts or unknowns, from procurement and technical owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace each recorded observation to its source, date, assumption, and accountable reviewer, while retaining conflicting evidence. This supports transparent evaluation records; it cannot determine suitability, performance, or secure operation.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may group evidence and draft unanswered questions; it cannot rank, recommend, or select technology. Refresh when evidence changes or a vulnerability/supplier notice occurs.

### 3. Decision boundary and independent challenge package

- **Request and owner:** Human decision record, risk/safety review inputs, exception or deferred-question tracker, remediation/next-step owner, and independent reviewer workpapers.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace each decision and deferral to authority, rationale source, expiry/review date, and unresolved issue. This supports decision governance; it cannot approve procurement, accept risk, or substitute for engineering assessment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may create read-only packets and flag stale assumptions. Named human authorities approve decisions and external actions. Review at approved intervals and after material incident or lifecycle change.

## Failure branches and decisions

- Candidate lacks evidence for a mandatory condition: retain the gap; do not substitute marketing language for verification.
- Comparisons use different traffic, configurations or time periods: record the mismatch and obtain comparable evidence before a comparative conclusion.
- Security feature disrupts required operations: escalate to safety and engineering owners rather than trading off consequences autonomously.
- Supplier refuses needed details: document unavailable evidence and the owner decision; missing information is not a pass.
- Product changes after evaluation: assess the impact on conclusions instead of automatically inheriting old results.

Use [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md) assertions: supported, not_supported, inconclusive, not_applicable and not_tested. Keep observed capability, suitability, procurement approval and deployment verification distinct.

## Cadence and renewal

Set review timing by approved risk and lifecycle obligations. Trigger review on changed assumptions, incidents, support expiry and material product or architecture changes. Do not invent annual certification requirements.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver approved criteria, full candidate/evidence register, comparison limitations, authorized decisions and next actions with owners and dates.
