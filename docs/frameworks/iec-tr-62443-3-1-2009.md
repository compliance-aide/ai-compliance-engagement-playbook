# IEC TR 62443-3-1:2009 IACS technology-assessment engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not IEC text, a technology recommendation, a safety decision, a product selection, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through the [IEC TR 62443-3-1:2009 publication record](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/7031).

## Engagement focus

Maintain a technology-assessment record that connects industrial system context, accountable owners, operational and safety constraints, candidate technology categories, approved evaluation evidence, supplier representations, deployment assumptions, exception decisions, remediation, and independent review. The engagement record should support careful analysis of industrial cybersecurity technology without letting an AI-generated comparison become a procurement, engineering, or assurance conclusion.

## Roles and annual rhythm

OT engineering, operations, safety, maintenance, security, procurement, risk, and supplier authorities retain accountable decisions; system and evidence owners preserve authorized evaluation records; independent reviewers test traceability, conflict handling, and safety-aware escalation. AI may organize authorized evidence, identify unresolved evaluation questions, draft comparison criteria, and prepare non-authoritative workpapers, but cannot recommend or select a product, determine suitability, approve an architecture, decide safety or operational impacts, accept risk, alter OT or production configuration, initiate scans or changes, or claim compliance. Revisit technology assumptions annually and before material system, supplier, vulnerability, safety, incident, lifecycle, or architecture changes.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [IEC TR 62443-3-1:2009 publication record](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/7031) and an authorized copy; checked 2026-07-31. IEC text is licensed. This original plan does not reproduce requirements, recommend a technology, select a product, or make a safety, architecture, risk, or compliance conclusion.

### 1. Assessment purpose and industrial-context package

- **Request and owner:** Human-approved evaluation purpose, system boundary, operational and safety constraints, interfaces/dependencies, stakeholder roles, and permitted evidence sources, from OT engineering and operations.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected evaluation question to a named owner, industrial context, constraint, and source boundary. This supports a bounded work program; it cannot establish complete scope or authorize design work.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize supplied metadata and surface missing ownership. Humans approve scope. Revisit before material architecture, process, supplier, or safety change.

### 2. Authorized technology-evaluation package

- **Request and owner:** Approved comparison criteria, supplier representations, test/lab observations, integration assumptions, lifecycle/support information, and identified conflicts or unknowns, from procurement and technical owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected observation to its source, date, assumption, and accountable reviewer, while retaining conflicting evidence. This supports transparent evaluation records; it cannot determine suitability, performance, or secure operation.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may group evidence and draft unanswered questions; it cannot rank, recommend, or select technology. Refresh when evidence changes or a vulnerability/supplier notice occurs.

### 3. Decision boundary and independent challenge package

- **Request and owner:** Human decision record, risk/safety review inputs, exception or deferred-question tracker, remediation/next-step owner, and independent reviewer workpapers.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected decision or deferral to authority, rationale source, expiry/review date, and unresolved issue. This supports decision governance; it cannot approve procurement, accept risk, or substitute for engineering assessment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may create read-only packets and flag stale assumptions. Named human authorities approve decisions and external actions. Review annually and after material incident or lifecycle change.
