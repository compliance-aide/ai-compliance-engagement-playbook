# NIST SP 800-216 vulnerability-disclosure engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST vulnerability-disclosure content, a disclosure policy, a safe-harbor decision, authorization to test, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-216](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/216/final) and the organization's approved legal, security, privacy, communications, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Run a governed, year-round process for receiving and managing good-faith reports of suspected vulnerabilities in in-scope systems, hardware, software, and services. Define and preserve the boundaries among published intake information, ownership, scope, authorized handling, protected communications, technical assessment, tracking, remediation coordination, disclosure timing, and program oversight. Keep vulnerability disclosure separate from emergency incident response, active testing, and any authority to access or alter systems.

Every report, researcher message, scan result, reproduction note, ticket, remediation record, or disclosure draft is evidence to assess in context. Do not assume that receipt proves a condition, that a severity label decides priority, or that a public-facing statement authorizes testing or disclosure. Maintain the decision path from intake through triage, technical validation, affected-owner engagement, remediation, communication, closure, and lessons learned, including disagreements and unresolved risks.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Program, security, technical-owner, legal, privacy, communications, incident-response, vulnerability-management, records, and executive owners establish scope and make accountable decisions. Operators maintain intake channels, report records, communication history, ownership and routing data, triage evidence, remediation tracking, disclosure decisions, metrics, exceptions, and retention records. Review intake routing, open reports, aging, remediation coordination, and communication obligations regularly; reconcile scope, contacts, channels, and overdue actions at least quarterly; and conduct management review annually and after material policy, legal, threat, or service changes.

Independent reviewers sample reports from intake through disposition and validate that evidence, communications, and decision records remain consistent. Auditors examine supplied records without testing systems, contacting reporters as the organization, changing services, approving safe harbor, accepting risk, deciding disclosure timing, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize reports and evidence, remove routine duplication, identify aging or missing ownership, draft nonbinding workpapers, and prepare review questions. AI cannot authorize testing or access, make legal or disclosure decisions, send binding communications, change systems, accept risk, or attest to conformance.
