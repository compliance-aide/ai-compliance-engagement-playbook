# ISO/IEC 27001:2022 — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not an ISO publication or a certification claim.
> Use an authorized copy and check the [ISO record](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001)
> and applicable amendments before each milestone.

## Engagement focus

Define the information-security management boundary; refresh organizational
context and information-risk decisions; maintain applicability and rationale
records without recreating the standard; govern changes and suppliers; exercise
incident and continuity capability; review performance and corrective action.

## Roles

The organization runs its management system. An independent reviewer evaluates
the work under the applicable engagement rules. AI maintains traceability,
evidence schedules, and open-question logs; it cannot certify a management
system or determine certification readiness.

## Annual rhythm

Set objectives and risk priorities, then perform periodic operational and
evidence reviews. Trigger scope and risk review after significant business,
technology, supplier, incident, or legal changes. End each cycle with internal
review, leadership review, corrective-action follow-through, and improvement
planning.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use an authorized ISO/IEC 27001:2022 copy and the [ISO record](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001); checked 2026-07-31. ISO text is protected: this plan links out and requests original evidence categories only. The ISMS owner and certification-contact owner confirm edition, amendments, applicability, and any certification route.

### 1. ISMS context, boundary, and risk-treatment record

- **Request and owner:** ISMS scope/context statement, interested-party and supplier dependencies, information-risk assessment/treatment records, applicability rationale, objectives, and approved exclusions, from ISMS, risk, and business owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected material service or risk to its boundary, treatment decision, accountable owner, and source evidence. This can support that the management-system cycle has a documented basis; it cannot establish clause conformity, acceptable risk, or certification readiness.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize traceability and flag stale decisions. Humans approve scope, treatment, and applicability. Revisit after business, technology, supplier, incident, legal, or objective change.

### 2. Management-system operation and performance evidence

- **Request and owner:** Competence/training, policy/procedure versioning, operational measure, supplier/change, incident/continuity exercise, internal-review, and corrective-action records from their accountable owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect provenance and trace a human-approved selection from a stated objective or risk to operation, measurement, exception, and follow-up. This can support observable operation; it cannot prove continuous effectiveness or replace a certification audit.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may produce read-only evidence indexes and gap questions, never certify, alter systems, or close corrective actions. Refresh after failed measures/exercises, an expired action, or a material operating change.

### 3. Leadership review and improvement record

- **Request and owner:** Management-review agenda/minutes, performance input, audit/exception summary, resource and improvement decisions, action owners, and verification of closure from ISMS leadership.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected leadership decision to its input evidence, named authority, due date, and retest or follow-up. This can support accountable improvement governance; it cannot substitute for leadership judgment or a registrar decision.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may draft a source-linked review packet. Humans approve decisions, risk acceptance, public statements, and certification communications. Refresh at the planned annual review and after a material source or scope change.
