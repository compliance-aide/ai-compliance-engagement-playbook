# APRA CPS 230 operational-risk-management engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not APRA text, a prudential determination, legal advice, or a compliance claim. Confirm current applicability through [APRA CPS 230](https://www.apra.gov.au/standards/cps-230) and qualified regulatory counsel.

## Engagement focus

Maintain an operational-resilience record that connects critical operations, risk and control ownership, service-provider dependencies, disruption tolerances approved by accountable people, continuity and response evidence, scenario exercises, incidents, remediation, governance decisions, and review dates. Record the source, scope, assumptions, and limitations of every material conclusion. Keep provider arrangements and disruption evidence traceable to the operation they support, rather than treating policy documents as proof of resilience.

## Roles and annual rhythm

The board, senior management, risk, operations, continuity, procurement, technology, security, legal, and service-provider owners retain accountable decisions. Operations owners maintain critical-operation evidence; provider owners maintain relationship and performance records; management decides remediation and resources; and auditors evaluate the program in their independent role. Independent reviewers trace sampled operations through dependencies, scenarios, observations, decision records, remediation, and retesting, challenging unowned services, untested assumptions, and closure without evidence.

AI may organize authorized operational-risk metadata, identify missing owners or review dates, compare approved plans with evidence records, draft questions, and prepare non-authoritative workpapers. AI cannot identify an operation as critical, set a tolerance, approve an arrangement, determine resilience, authorize remediation, accept risk, or claim compliance. Reassess before material operation, dependency, supplier, disruption, regulatory, or risk changes; exercise preparedness periodically and conduct an annual independent readiness review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the current [APRA CPS 230 page](https://www.apra.gov.au/standards/cps-230) and applicable organization records; checked 2026-07-31. APRA is the authoritative source for current prudential material, but this plan is original operational guidance and does not reproduce the standard or decide applicability, a prudential obligation, or compliance. Legal, risk, and accountable management confirm those matters.

### 1. Critical-operation and tolerance decision record

- **Request and owner:** Service/process inventory, critical-operation analysis inputs, disruption-tolerance proposals, dependency map, accountable-owner assignments, and approval records from operations, risk, and accountable management.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected operation from business rationale through dependency and tolerance inputs to the human decision record. This supports evidence of a governed decision path; it cannot determine criticality, adequacy of a tolerance, or resilience.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved inventory metadata and flag missing ownership. Humans decide criticality and tolerances. Refresh after a material service, dependency, business-model, or disruption change.

### 2. Provider, continuity, and disruption-evidence package

- **Request and owner:** Material-provider/service records, due-diligence and performance records, continuity plans, exercise designs/results, incident timelines, communications, and remediation records from procurement, provider, continuity, and operations owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a sampled critical operation through its provider/dependency record to a relevant exercise or disruption record, then to issue ownership and retest. This can show retained evidence for the sampled path; it cannot establish provider performance or preparedness in an untested event.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved records and identify stale dates; it may not approve an arrangement or direct a response. Recollect after supplier changes, failed exercises, disruptions, or material service changes.

### 3. Governance, exception, and annual-review package

- **Request and owner:** Board/management reporting inputs, exceptions, risk and resourcing decisions, remediation/retest evidence, independent-review workpapers, and source-change log from risk and management owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected exception to authority, rationale, expiry, mitigation, and retest status. This supports accountable oversight; it cannot accept risk, close remediation, or make a prudential conclusion.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a read-only, source-linked review packet. Humans approve resourcing, residual risk, and closure. Revisit at annual review and after a material regulatory or risk change.
