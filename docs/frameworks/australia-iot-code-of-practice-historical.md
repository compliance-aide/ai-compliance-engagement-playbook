# Australia IoT Code of Practice historical engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not Australian government code text, a product-safety determination, a legal interpretation, or a compliance claim. Confirm the historical [IoT Code of Practice manufacturer guidance](https://www.cyber.gov.au/business-government/secure-design/secure-by-design/iot-secure-by-design-guidance-for-manufacturers) and current Australian smart-device requirements with applicable authority and counsel.

## Engagement focus

Use this guide when a product record, customer request, or prior program explicitly references Australia’s voluntary IoT Code of Practice or related manufacturer guidance. Maintain a product-lifecycle record covering devices, supported software, interfaces, service dependencies, suppliers, data handling, vulnerability intake, updates, end-of-support decisions, customer information, security testing, and material changes. Keep historical voluntary-code evidence separate from any current statutory, contractual, certification, or market-access obligation; identify the governing source and owner for each claim.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable product, engineering, security, privacy, support, supplier-management, legal, and release owners. Operators retain device and software inventories, dependency and supplier records, design and test evidence, vulnerability and update records, end-of-support communications, incident evidence, and customer-facing documentation. Review supported products, vulnerability and update status, supplier changes, end-of-support dates, and external representations quarterly. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples product evidence and version labels to verify that voluntary historical-code claims are not being presented as current legal conclusions. Auditors test records and operating practices without deciding product safety, legal applicability, or management representations.

AI may organize supplied product evidence, reconcile inventories and support dates, flag missing ownership or conflicting records, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot determine product security or safety, decide legal applicability, authorize release or end-of-support decisions, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.
