# Australia IoT Code of Practice historical engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not Australian government code text, a product-safety determination, a legal interpretation, or a compliance claim. Confirm the historical [IoT Code of Practice manufacturer guidance](https://www.cyber.gov.au/business-government/secure-design/secure-by-design/iot-secure-by-design-guidance-for-manufacturers) and current Australian smart-device requirements with applicable authority and counsel.

## Engagement focus

Use this guide when a product record, customer request, or prior program explicitly references Australia’s voluntary IoT Code of Practice or related manufacturer guidance. Maintain a product-lifecycle record covering devices, supported software, interfaces, service dependencies, suppliers, data handling, vulnerability intake, updates, end-of-support decisions, customer information, security testing, and material changes. Keep historical voluntary-code evidence separate from any current statutory, contractual, certification, or market-access obligation; identify the governing source and owner for each claim.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable product, engineering, security, privacy, support, supplier-management, legal, and release owners. Operators retain device and software inventories, dependency and supplier records, design and test evidence, vulnerability and update records, end-of-support communications, incident evidence, and customer-facing documentation. Review supported products, vulnerability and update status, supplier changes, end-of-support dates, and external representations quarterly. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples product evidence and version labels to verify that voluntary historical-code claims are not being presented as current legal conclusions. Auditors test records and operating practices without deciding product safety, legal applicability, or management representations.

AI may organize supplied product evidence, reconcile inventories and support dates, flag missing ownership or conflicting records, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot determine product security or safety, decide legal applicability, authorize release or end-of-support decisions, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the historical [IoT Code of Practice manufacturer guidance](https://www.cyber.gov.au/business-government/secure-design/secure-by-design/iot-secure-by-design-guidance-for-manufacturers), checked 2026-07-31, only to understand the referenced voluntary context. This original plan neither reproduces the historical guidance nor treats it as a current statutory duty. Product, legal, security, and release owners decide applicability and any customer or market statement.

### 1. Historical-reference and product-lifecycle register

- **Request and owner:** Product, legal, and security owners provide the specific historical reference, product/model and version catalogue, interfaces and service dependencies, target-market context, ownership assignments, and dated decision distinguishing voluntary historical work from current requirements.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected device version to its historical-reference notation, accountable owner, dependency record, and current support status. This supports honest provenance; it cannot determine legal applicability or establish product security.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile catalogue, support, and dependency metadata and flag conflicting dates. Humans approve scope, historical characterizations, and external wording. Refresh upon a product, market, supplier, service, or support-policy change.

### 2. Secure-development, update, and vulnerability evidence

- **Request and owner:** Engineering, security, support, and supplier owners provide approved design/review records, release and update history, vulnerability intake and disposition records, security-test references, supplier commitments, and end-of-support communications.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected vulnerability or update from intake through accountable assessment, customer-impact decision, release/update artifact, and support communication. This supports a bounded lifecycle trail; it cannot prove absence of defects, compel an update, or establish compliance with any current rule.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may create a read-only gap list from supplied records. Humans approve design changes, releases, vulnerability disclosures, and support commitments. Recollect after material vulnerabilities, updates, supplier changes, or end-of-support decisions.

### 3. Historical-claim review and improvement workpaper

- **Request and owner:** Product, legal, communications, and assurance owners provide approved customer materials, historical-program claims, exception rationale, review findings, remediation actions, and independent challenge notes.
- **Validate and limit:** Compare a selected historical claim to its approved source reference, product/version scope, owner approval, and supporting evidence. This supports claim discipline; it cannot certify a product, make a legal conclusion, or replace independent review.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag claims lacking a version, owner, or dated support record. Humans approve exceptions, remediation closure, and external statements. Review before reusing historical claims, at annual catalogue review, and after legal or regulatory change.
