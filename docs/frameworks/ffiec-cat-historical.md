# FFIEC Cybersecurity Assessment Tool — historical migration guide

> Original operational guidance for legacy records only. The [FFIEC sunset notice](https://www.ffiec.gov/cyberassessmenttool.htm) confirms the voluntary tool was removed in 2025; do not use it as a current framework.

## Engagement focus

Inventory legacy artifacts, preserve retention-required records, identify lingering references, and move active governance and evidence to a currently authorized approach. Make migration decisions explicit, dated, owned, and independently challengeable.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Record owners retain and classify material; compliance owners select the current approach. Independent reviewers verify migration claims against operating practice. AI can catalog evidence and identify unresolved references; it cannot score an institution, predict regulator views, represent the tool as active, or dispose of records without approval.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [FFIEC sunset notice](https://www.ffiec.gov/cyberassessmenttool.htm), the organization’s retention schedule, and the currently approved successor governance sources; checked 2026-07-31. The CAT is historical material, not a current assessment target. This original plan does not reproduce the tool, determine regulatory expectations, authorize destruction, or assert a migration is complete.

### 1. Legacy-record inventory and retention package

- **Request and owner:** Records, security, risk, and compliance owners provide the legacy CAT artifact inventory, locations, custodians, retention/legal-hold decisions, access restrictions, and preservation exceptions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a human-selected legacy artifact to its repository, owner, retention basis, access boundary, and review date. This establishes a bounded custody trail; it cannot determine all records are complete or authorize deletion.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved metadata and flag missing custodians, unreachable locations, or overdue reviews. Human records and legal owners decide retention, holds, and disposal. Recollect after a repository move, hold notice, retention-rule change, or material audit request.

### 2. Active-reference discovery and remediation package

- **Request and owner:** Security governance, policy, vendor-management, and business owners provide policy/procedure references, contracts, questionnaires, dashboards, reports, training material, open findings, and remediation tickets that still cite the retired tool.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample one reference to its business use, owner, retirement decision, replacement or removal action, due date, and evidence of change. This can show a controlled cleanup path; it cannot prove every reference has been found or endorse a successor framework.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may search authorized repositories for named references and produce a review queue; humans decide whether a reference is material and approve replacement language. Refresh after a policy release, third-party assessment, merger, or new evidence request.

### 3. Successor-governance and independent-challenge packet

- **Request and owner:** Compliance leadership, risk, audit, and approved framework owners provide the successor approach decision, applicability rationale, leadership approval, transition milestones, exception register, independent-review observations, and closure/retest evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected migration claim to a named authority, current framework source, transition decision, accountable owner, and independent challenge result. It cannot make a regulator-facing representation or accept residual risk.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize factual status and flag milestones with no evidence. Humans approve successor selection, risk acceptance, external statements, and closure. Review at least annually and after changes to supervisory expectations or material operating scope.
