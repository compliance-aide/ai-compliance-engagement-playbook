# New Zealand Information Security Manual — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not New Zealand Government content or an assurance conclusion. Confirm current applicability through the [NZISM page](https://www.ncsc.govt.nz/what-we-do/regulations-and-standards/nzism/).

## Engagement focus

Maintain agency and service scope, information and system inventory, risk decisions, identity and access evidence, logging, incident records, suppliers, vulnerabilities, exceptions, assurance results, and improvement plans.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Agency security and system owners make implementation and residual-risk decisions; reviewers assess evidence independently. AI indexes approved evidence and highlights drift, but cannot determine applicability or accept risk. Review quarterly and perform an annual engagement assessment.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [New Zealand Information Security Manual page](https://www.ncsc.govt.nz/what-we-do/regulations-and-standards/nzism/), current agency direction, and authorized security/risk interpretation; checked 2026-07-31. This original plan does not reproduce NZISM content, determine agency applicability, accept risk, or make an assurance conclusion.

### 1. Agency scope, assets, and risk-governance package

- **Request and owner:** Agency security, system, information, risk, and service owners provide agency/service boundaries, information and system inventories, accountable-owner records, approved classification or handling references, risk register entries, governance decisions, and review calendar.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one selected service or system from inventory through accountable owner, information boundary, documented risk/governance record, and current review date. This cannot determine completeness, classification, or residual-risk acceptability.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare supplied inventory and ownership records and flag missing dates or unmapped dependencies. Authorized humans decide scope, classification, and risk posture. Refresh after material service, system, information, owner, or mission change.

### 2. Operating security, supplier, and incident package

- **Request and owner:** Identity, operations, infrastructure, application, supplier, incident, and vulnerability owners provide approved operating records, access reviews, security-event/log references, incident handoffs, vulnerability treatment, supplier/service evidence, change approvals, and deviations.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample one documented service or incident from ownership through operating/review evidence, supplier or dependency record, exception/issue, and treatment status. This cannot configure systems, test all security outcomes, or approve a production change.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index authorized evidence and flag stale reviews, missing handoffs, or overdue treatments. Humans assess incidents, authorize changes, approve suppliers, and decide remediation. Refresh after material access, supplier, architecture, incident, vulnerability, or change event.

### 3. Assurance, exception, and annual-improvement package

- **Request and owner:** Security governance, internal assurance, risk, system, and independent-review owners provide assessment workpapers, issue/risk records, exception approvals, management actions, evidence limitations, assurance reports, and annual improvement follow-up.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one assurance observation, exception, or improvement action to source evidence, accountable decision-maker, stated limitation, treatment owner, and follow-up date. This cannot accept risk, issue an assurance opinion, or certify an agency.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare an evidence index and surface missing provenance or overdue actions. Authorized humans approve risk treatment, reports, and closure; independent reviewers retain challenge authority. Recheck before reporting and during the annual engagement assessment.
