# NARA CUI Program engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not CUI policy text, a marking or category determination, a dissemination authorization, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through the [NARA CUI policy and guidance page](https://www.archives.gov/cui/registry/policy-guidance), the CUI Registry, and applicable agency or contract authorities.

## Engagement focus

Maintain a controlled-information lifecycle record that connects authorized category and handling determinations, accountable owners, sources of authority, systems and locations, approved recipients, access and dissemination boundaries, markings or notices as determined by authorized personnel, incident records, decontrol or disposition decisions, training evidence, exceptions, and review dates. Treat the Registry and governing authority as authoritative; do not let an AI model infer a category, marking, or dissemination permission from a document’s contents.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Agency or contract program officials, information owners, security, privacy, legal, records, systems, procurement, training, and incident-response owners retain accountable decisions. Authorized CUI program personnel determine category and handling matters; information owners maintain lifecycle context; system owners preserve operating evidence; and incident authorities decide escalation and reporting. Independent reviewers sample information paths from authority and determination through access, transfer, storage, training, incident handling, decontrol, and disposition, escalating uncertainty to the authorized program authority.

AI may organize authorized, sanitized metadata; identify missing ownership or review fields; draft questions; and prepare non-authoritative workpapers. AI cannot access restricted information, determine CUI status, apply or remove markings, authorize dissemination, transmit CUI, modify systems, interpret agreements, report an incident, accept risk, or claim compliance. Use AI only with permitted, minimized metadata. Reassess before material authority, category, system, recipient, contract, incident, or lifecycle changes; conduct periodic self-inspection and an annual independent readiness review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [NARA CUI policy and guidance page](https://www.archives.gov/cui/registry/policy-guidance), the current CUI Registry, and applicable agency, contract, and security authorities; checked 2026-07-31. This original planning aid does not make a category, marking, dissemination, safeguarding, or incident-reporting determination.

### 1. Authority, category, and information-flow boundary

- **Request and owner:** Request the contract or agency authority, authorized category determination records, information-owner register, system/location inventory, approved recipient and transfer paths, and designated CUI-program contacts from the program manager, information owner, security, legal, and contract owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected approved metadata record from governing authority to an authorized determination, system/location record, recipient boundary, and responsible owner. This supports a traceable scope record; it cannot infer CUI status or authorize a marking or transfer.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize only permitted minimized metadata and flag missing fields. Authorized CUI personnel decide category, marking, and dissemination. Refresh before a new contract, authority, system, location, recipient, category, or transfer path enters scope.

### 2. Handling, access, training, and supplier operation

- **Request and owner:** Request approved access rosters, authorization/recertification evidence, handling-procedure records, training completion records, service-provider or subcontractor responsibilities, and approved exceptions from system, training, procurement, and CUI-program owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Select an authorized user or approved service path and trace it to the responsible owner, dated access or training record, handling boundary, and exception/remediation history. This evidences the existence of declared records; it cannot prove authorized access in every circumstance or determine contractual sufficiency.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may detect expired reviews in sanitized indexes but may not access CUI, grant access, apply markings, or approve an exception. Recollect after personnel, system, supplier, training, authorization, or handling-process change.

### 3. Incident, decontrol, disposition, and self-inspection record

- **Request and owner:** Request incident and escalation metadata, authorized reporting decisions, decontrol/disposition records, periodic self-inspection results, corrective-action log, and source-change watch from incident, records, CUI-program, and leadership owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected event or lifecycle decision to its facts, authorized decision-maker, required follow-up, and closure evidence. This supports accountability; it cannot report an incident, decontrol information, accept risk, or make a compliance claim.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may assemble a sanitized workpaper and flag overdue actions. Humans decide escalation, reporting, decontrol, disposition, and closure. Revisit annually and after an incident, inspection finding, authority update, or unresolved exception.
