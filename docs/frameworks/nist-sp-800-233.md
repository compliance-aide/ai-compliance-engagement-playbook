# NIST SP 800-233 service-mesh-proxy engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST service-mesh-proxy content, a cloud-native architecture, a configuration authorization, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-233](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/233/final) and the organization's approved security, architecture, privacy, legal, operational, and change decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round engagement for service-mesh proxy models used by in-scope cloud-native applications. Connect each application and service to its proxy model, traffic paths, identities, policy-enforcement points, control-plane and data-plane dependencies, configuration sources, workload boundaries, observability, supplier components, deployment topology, changes, incidents, and retirement. Use the engagement to understand the effects of proxy placement and responsibility, rather than assuming that a service mesh automatically provides adequate protection.

Treat architecture diagrams, manifests, proxy configurations, policy extracts, telemetry, test outputs, deployment records, or supplier assertions as evidence to evaluate in context. Preserve the chain from approved application and security design through runtime configuration, observed operation, exception handling, incident response, and corrective work. Distinguish configuration intent from active runtime behavior and from business authorization for service-to-service access.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Application, platform, security, architecture, network, identity, operations, privacy, supplier, change, incident-response, and records owners establish scope and make accountable decisions. Operators maintain service and proxy inventories, approved topology and policy records, configuration and deployment evidence, identity and traffic-path traceability, monitoring and test results, supplier dependencies, exceptions, incidents, and decommissioning records. Reconcile applications, proxies, owners, policies, dependencies, and exposed paths at least quarterly; review material policy, deployment, vulnerability, and incident changes as they occur; and conduct management review annually.

Independent reviewers sample services from approved design through proxy configuration, deployment, observed telemetry, and remediation. Auditors assess supplied evidence without changing policies or workloads, granting access, deploying code, approving architecture, accepting risk, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize topology and evidence, identify stale configurations or missing ownership, compare supplied records with approved decisions, draft workpapers, and prepare review questions. AI cannot design or approve service-mesh architecture, modify proxy settings, grant access, deploy workloads, accept risk, make legal or privacy conclusions, or attest to conformance.


## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [NIST SP 800-233 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/233/final) and [NIST copyright and reuse information](https://www.nist.gov/disclaimer) at engagement start; checked 2026-07-31. This plan uses original operational language and links rather than reproducing publication content. Security and legal owners confirm any external use before publishing.

### 1. Mesh scope, topology, and responsibility package

- **Request and owner:** Application, platform, architecture, and service owners provide the approved service inventory, mesh and non-mesh boundary, current topology and traffic-path diagrams, workload identities, proxy/control-plane versions, dependency register, and named enforcement-point owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Independently select a material service path and trace it from the approved application boundary through identities, proxy placement, policy owner, and dependency record. This tests traceability of a sample; it does not prove every path is discovered, reachable only as intended, or securely configured.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare supplied inventories and diagrams, flag orphaned services or version conflicts, and prepare read-only trace workpapers. Named human architecture and security owners approve boundaries and responsibility. Refresh on material topology, workload, identity, proxy, or supplier change.

### 2. Policy, configuration, and deployment package

- **Request and owner:** Platform and application operators provide approved traffic and identity policies, versioned configuration references, change approvals, deployment records, rollback evidence, exception decisions, and time-bounded validation outputs.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one selected policy from approval through the deployed version, relevant workload/proxy, validation result, and rollback or exception record. This can expose an unsupported change or missing linkage; it cannot establish policy effectiveness across all services or approve production settings.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index configurations and correlate approved identifiers with supplied deployment records; it cannot change policy, deploy a workload, grant service access, or close an exception. Human change authorities approve changes and exceptions. Refresh after policy, deployment, failed validation, or incident change.

### 3. Runtime observation, incident, and remediation package

- **Request and owner:** Operations, security monitoring, and incident-response owners provide retained telemetry references, alert/runbook records, test scope and limitations, incident tickets, root-cause records, corrective actions, and management review decisions.
- **Validate and limit:** For one selected alert, failure, or incident, trace detection to affected service path, triage, accountable decision, corrective action, and retest or open status. This verifies an evidence trail, not continuous monitoring coverage, root-cause correctness, or risk acceptance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize observability references, detect missing closure fields, and draft questions from supplied records. Only designated humans direct response, accept risk, and approve remediation closure. Refresh after a security event, material telemetry change, failed exercise, or annual review.
