# Australia CIRMP — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance for a Critical Infrastructure Risk Management
> Program (CIRMP), not legal advice or an approved statutory report.

## Source and applicability

Use the [current CIRMP Rules](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2023L00112/latest)
and the linked Security of Critical Infrastructure Act. Checked 2026-09-04: the
register identifies compilation F2026C00562, dated 10 June 2026. The text separates
baseline and enhanced requirements, with asset-class and transitional provisions
in sections 4, 4A and 12. Counsel must resolve which provisions apply to each asset;
a previous declaration does not automatically settle enhanced applicability.

## Engagement focus

Create an asset-specific program linking hazards, dependencies, approved treatment,
operating evidence and responsible decisions. Include personnel, supply-chain,
physical/natural and cyber contexts; a security scan alone is not an all-hazards
program. Preserve uncertainty about assets and treatment effectiveness.

## Roles

Asset owners provide operations and dependencies. Risk and security owners maintain
hazard/treatment evidence. Counsel and the responsible entity decide legal scope;
authorised governance bodies approve program/report decisions. Independent
reviewers challenge evidence. AI may reconcile records and draft workpapers; it
cannot classify an asset legally, approve treatment, assess worker suitability,
accept risk or sign/submit a report.

## Before starting

Obtain asset/entity inventory, declarations and scope decisions, current source
versions, program owner, hazard register, treatment and exercise records, reporting
calendar and evidence permissions. If enhanced applicability is undecided, prepare
its factual inputs and list affected steps as pending. Use the
[agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md).

## Ordered workflow

| Step | Action and owner | Output and exit check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Resolve each asset's scope | Counsel/responsible entity compare asset class, ownership, declarations and transition provisions. | Asset-by-asset applicability record with source/date; no company-wide inference from one asset. |
| 2. Map dependencies and hazards | Asset/risk owners connect critical components, services, people and suppliers to hazard scenarios. AI reconciles IDs and missing owners. | All-hazards map with unresolved dependencies explicitly listed. |
| 3. Plan treatments and evidence | Owners document approved treatment, responsible operator, verification method and relevant baseline/enhanced obligations. | Treatment/evidence plan shows what observable result would support each claim. |
| 4. Collect operating records | AI indexes permitted configuration, personnel-process metadata, supplier and physical/continuity records. | Evidence has provenance, period and covered asset; sensitive worker/security information stays in approved storage. |
| 5. Exercise scenarios | Authorised operators run reviewer-approved QA/tabletop scenarios and retain actual observations. | Results match predeclared expectations; simulation limitations and failed paths remain visible. |
| 6. Correct and retest | Owners address adverse findings through approved changes; reviewer verifies the same claim again. | Finding-to-change-to-retest trail, including unresolved treatment gaps. |
| 7. Prepare governance and reporting | Risk/counsel owners assemble the program-review packet and verify statutory report requirements and approvers. | Reviewed draft, open issues and exact submission authority; delivery is separate from preparation. |

This is an original evidence-work sequence. The Act, Rules and approved legal
register supply the binding duties, not this table.

## Evidence and test plan

| Request and custodian | Check | Expected observation | Failure or limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Asset/applicability record from counsel and asset owner | Trace asset IDs to relevant source provisions and decisions. | Baseline/enhanced status and transition rationale are explicit. | An old declaration alone cannot prove current enhanced scope. |
| Hazard/treatment map from risk owner | Follow one scenario across dependencies, treatment and operating evidence. | Treatment actually addresses the scoped scenario with an owner. | A cyber-only review leaves other hazards untested. |
| Exercise/results from operations | Compare observed behavior with the approved method and expected result. | Assumptions, adverse observations and coverage are retained. | A discussion cannot demonstrate live operational resilience. |
| Governance/report packet from responsible entity | Trace each material report assertion to evidence and authorised review. | Assertions match the asset and period; unresolved matters are visible. | An AI draft cannot establish statutory compliance or submission. |

## Failure branches and decisions

Unclear ownership or asset class blocks the affected scope conclusion while factual
inventory continues. Conflicting baseline/enhanced interpretations go to counsel
with both sources. Missing worker or supplier evidence is a gap, not permission
to access restricted records. A failed exercise creates corrective work rather
than an edited success narrative. Actual incidents activate their separately
approved response/reporting route immediately; annual program reporting is not a
substitute for event notification.

## Cadence and renewal

Maintain the statutory reporting/calendar obligations from the current Act and
Rules with exact period, due date and approver. Quarterly working reviews are a
planning default. Reassess after ownership, asset-class, declaration, dependency,
hazard, incident or source changes. Do not reuse a prior-year report without
current-period evidence and renewed authority.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver asset applicability, hazard/dependency and treatment records, protected
evidence index, exercise receipts, findings/retests, governance draft and next
queue. Name any unresolved enhanced requirements or untested hazards. Statutory
reporting is complete only after authorised submission and independent receipt.
