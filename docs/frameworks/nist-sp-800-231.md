# NIST SP 800-231 cybersecurity-bug-classification engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST Bug Framework content, a finding determination, a vulnerability score, a remediation approval, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-231](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/231/final) and the organization's approved security, engineering, product, legal, operational, and disclosure decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round engagement for consistently describing, connecting, and learning from cybersecurity weaknesses, faults, and vulnerabilities across in-scope software, firmware, and hardware. Establish a common internal vocabulary that ties reports and observed defects to the affected component, version, weakness chain, causal conditions, impact evidence, ownership, remediation or mitigation, verification, and lessons learned. Use classification to improve analysis and trend discovery, not as a replacement for evidence-based technical and business judgment.

Treat scanner results, bug reports, code analysis, test cases, model outputs, classifications, labels, or datasets as inputs to review—not automatic findings, severity determinations, or proof of exploitation. Preserve the decision path from intake and reproduction through classification, triage, owner assignment, remediation, validation, communication, closure, and recurrence analysis. Keep handling of suspected vulnerabilities distinct from public disclosure, emergency response, and production change authority.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Security, engineering, product, quality, hardware, operations, vulnerability-management, legal, privacy, supplier, incident-response, and records owners establish scope and make accountable decisions. Operators maintain component and version inventories, weakness and vulnerability records, evidence links, classification history, triage decisions, owner assignments, remediation and validation records, exceptions, trend analyses, and retention records. Reconcile open items, ownership, component coverage, recurring patterns, and evidence quality at least quarterly; review urgent findings and material changes as they arise; and conduct an annual management review of patterns and improvement work.

Independent reviewers sample records from source evidence through classification, disposition, remediation, and validation, noting ambiguity and inconsistent treatment. Auditors examine supplied artifacts without accessing systems beyond approval, declaring findings, setting severity, changing code or hardware, accepting risk, approving disclosure, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize reports, identify likely duplicate or incomplete records, trace evidence, draft nonbinding workpapers, and prepare review questions. AI cannot determine vulnerability validity, assign binding severity, approve remediation, change systems, accept risk, make legal or disclosure conclusions, or attest to conformance.
