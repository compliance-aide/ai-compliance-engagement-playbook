# NIST SP 800-231 cybersecurity-bug-classification engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST Bug Framework content, a finding determination, a vulnerability score, a remediation approval, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-231](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/231/final) and the organization's approved security, engineering, product, legal, operational, and disclosure decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round engagement for consistently describing, connecting, and learning from cybersecurity weaknesses, faults, and vulnerabilities across in-scope software, firmware, and hardware. Establish a common internal vocabulary that ties reports and observed defects to the affected component, version, weakness chain, causal conditions, impact evidence, ownership, remediation or mitigation, verification, and lessons learned. Use classification to improve analysis and trend discovery, not as a replacement for evidence-based technical and business judgment.

Treat scanner results, bug reports, code analysis, test cases, model outputs, classifications, labels, or datasets as inputs to review—not automatic findings, severity determinations, or proof of exploitation. Preserve the decision path from intake and reproduction through classification, triage, owner assignment, remediation, validation, communication, closure, and recurrence analysis. Keep handling of suspected vulnerabilities distinct from public disclosure, emergency response, and production change authority.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Security, engineering, product, quality, hardware, operations, vulnerability-management, legal, privacy, supplier, incident-response, and records owners establish scope and make accountable decisions. Operators maintain component and version inventories, weakness and vulnerability records, evidence links, classification history, triage decisions, owner assignments, remediation and validation records, exceptions, trend analyses, and retention records. Reconcile open items, ownership, component coverage, recurring patterns, and evidence quality at least quarterly; review urgent findings and material changes as they arise; and conduct an annual management review of patterns and improvement work.

Independent reviewers sample records from source evidence through classification, disposition, remediation, and validation, noting ambiguity and inconsistent treatment. Auditors examine supplied artifacts without accessing systems beyond approval, declaring findings, setting severity, changing code or hardware, accepting risk, approving disclosure, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize reports, identify likely duplicate or incomplete records, trace evidence, draft nonbinding workpapers, and prepare review questions. AI cannot determine vulnerability validity, assign binding severity, approve remediation, change systems, accept risk, make legal or disclosure conclusions, or attest to conformance.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [NIST SP 800-231 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/231/final), retaining the retrieved version and applicable use terms; checked 2026-07-31. This original plan manages evidence about classifications; it does not reproduce NIST Bug Framework content, declare a defect or vulnerability, assign binding severity, authorize disclosure, or make a conformance claim.

### 1. Intake, component, and evidence-provenance package

- **Request and owner:** Security, engineering, quality, hardware, and product owners provide report-intake records, affected component/version inventory references, source-evidence links, handling classification, reporter/contact restrictions, and accountable owner assignment.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one record from intake through its component/version reference, evidence provenance, handling status, and assigned owner. This assesses record completeness; it cannot reproduce a finding, confirm a defect, or determine exploitability.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize metadata, suggest potential duplicates, and flag missing component or evidence links. Humans control access, decide intake treatment, and determine whether technical investigation proceeds. Refresh when new evidence, an affected version, or handling restrictions change.

### 2. Classification, triage, and disposition package

- **Request and owner:** Vulnerability-management, security, engineering, product, legal, privacy, and incident-response owners provide classification rationale, ambiguity notes, triage/impact evidence references, disposition decision, remediation owner/date, and disclosure or incident-routing references where applicable.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample a record from supplied evidence through its classification rationale, accountable triage decision, disposition, and owner assignment. This can reveal inconsistent decision trails; it cannot make a binding classification, severity decision, legal conclusion, or disclosure determination.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare metadata for inconsistent labels and prepare questions about gaps. Humans resolve ambiguity, set disposition, prioritize work, and approve communication. Reassess after reproduction evidence, impact change, new affected component, incident linkage, or disclosure decision.

### 3. Remediation, verification, and recurrence-learning package

- **Request and owner:** Engineering, quality, security, operations, supplier, records, and management owners provide remediation/change references, test or verification evidence, exception/risk decisions, closure criteria, recurrence/trend analysis, supplier actions, and review minutes.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one closed or deferred record from assigned remediation through supplied verification or exception evidence, closure decision, and recurrence-learning record. This does not validate a fix, change production, accept risk, or establish that the issue cannot recur.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may group recurring patterns, flag overdue owners, and draft nonbinding workpapers. Humans approve changes, exceptions, closure, disclosure, and risk decisions. Review quarterly and after urgent findings, failed verification, repeat patterns, supplier notices, or material release changes.
