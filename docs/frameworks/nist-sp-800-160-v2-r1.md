# NIST SP 800-160 Volume 2 Revision 1 cyber-resiliency engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST text, an engineering approval, a resilience determination, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through the [NIST SP 800-160 Volume 2 Revision 1 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/160/v2/r1/final).

## Engagement focus

Maintain a cyber-resiliency engineering record that connects mission or service outcomes, adverse-condition scenarios, critical dependencies, architecture assumptions, accountable owners, lifecycle decisions, resilience objectives, evidence from design and operation, exercises, incidents, recovery learning, exceptions, and reassessment triggers. Treat demonstrated performance in one scenario as scoped evidence, not a guarantee that the system will withstand every adverse condition.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Mission, business, systems engineering, security, operations, resilience, safety, privacy, risk, supplier, and executive owners retain accountable decisions. System and service owners define mission context; engineering owners preserve design and change evidence; operations leaders govern exercises and recovery; risk authorities decide treatment within their mandate; and executives decide material trade-offs. Independent reviewers sample scenarios from assumptions through architecture, test or exercise evidence, incident learning, exception decisions, and resulting changes, then challenge untested dependencies and unsupported resilience claims.

AI may organize authorized artifacts, identify missing scenario-to-evidence links, summarize exercise records, draft evidence requests, and prepare non-authoritative reviewer workpapers. AI cannot alter a system, select or approve an architecture, initiate a test or recovery action, determine mission acceptability, accept risk, authorize release, or claim compliance. Reassess after material mission, architecture, dependency, threat, incident, supplier, or operating-environment changes; conduct periodic exercises, quarterly reviews, and an annual independent readiness review.
