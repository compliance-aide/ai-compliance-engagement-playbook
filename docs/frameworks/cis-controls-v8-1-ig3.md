# CIS Controls v8.1 Implementation Group 3 engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not CIS Controls text, a safeguard selection, a security decision, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through CIS’s [Implementation Group 3 resource](https://www.cisecurity.org/controls/implementation-groups/ig3).

## Engagement focus

Maintain a high-assurance security engagement record that connects organizational scope, accountable risk and service owners, sensitive or critical systems, specialized assessment evidence, threat-informed priorities, exceptions, remediation, supplier dependencies, executive decisions, and independent review. Use AI to make the evidence set navigable for auditors and operators while reserving high-impact risk and security judgments for accountable people.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Business leadership, IT, security, privacy, legal, risk, procurement, and service owners retain accountable decisions; evidence owners preserve authorized records; independent reviewers challenge high-impact scope, evidence quality, exception treatment, and escalation paths. AI may organize authorized evidence, identify unresolved questions, draft workpapers, and prepare non-authoritative status views, but cannot choose an implementation group, select or approve safeguards, determine sufficiency, accept risk, alter production configuration, initiate scans or changes, or claim compliance. Review operating evidence quarterly, conduct annual independent readiness review, and reopen the plan before material asset, service, threat, incident, business, regulatory, or technology changes.

### 1. Critical-service, threat, and escalation boundary

- **Request and owner:** A critical-service/system inventory, material business-impact and threat inputs, dependency and supplier context, accountable executive/risk/service owners, and documented escalation thresholds from security architecture, risk, and service leadership. Cover the approved high-impact population and additions or changes since the last review.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected critical service to the human-approved criticality rationale, threat/context input, accountable owner, dependency record, and escalation path. This can support a documented high-assurance planning boundary; it cannot prove threat coverage, establish priority correctness, or select an implementation group.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may correlate approved metadata and flag unowned critical services or unresolved escalation records. Humans approve criticality, risk framing, and escalation decisions. Refresh after material threat intelligence, incident, supplier, architecture, regulatory, or business change and annually.

### 2. High-impact operational-assurance trace

- **Request and owner:** Protected references to approved operating evidence for critical services—such as privileged-operation review, technical validation metadata, detection/response follow-up, change/recovery exercise records, and relevant third-party assurance inputs—with declared population, period, custodian, integrity data, and access restrictions.
- **Validate and limit:** Use a human-approved, least-privilege trace from selected critical-service evidence to its source record, reviewer, time window, exception, and follow-up. This can support observable assurance activity in the declared conditions; it cannot prove resilience against all threats, uninterrupted operation, or external conformance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize redacted metadata, flag missing periods, and draft skeptical questions; it cannot access protected evidence outside authorization, run security actions, change systems, or conclude sufficiency. Humans approve test design, access, remediation, and findings. Recollect after a material detection, failed exercise, major release, or quarterly review.

### 3. Executive exception and independent-challenge record

- **Request and owner:** High-impact exception/risk decisions, mitigation and contingency context, named authority, expiry, supplier escalation records, independent challenge notes, management review materials, and retest/renewal results from executives, legal/risk, security, and procurement owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect a selected exception from evidence boundary through independent challenge, decision authority, mitigation, expiry, and retest. This can support accountable governance and challenge; it cannot accept risk, create a legal conclusion, bind a supplier, or substitute for an independent assessment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag approaching expiry and prepare a source-linked, read-only review packet. Humans adjudicate disagreements, accept risk, authorize supplier actions, and approve closure. Trigger on exception expiry, unresolved challenge, material incident/supplier change, or annual independent readiness review.
