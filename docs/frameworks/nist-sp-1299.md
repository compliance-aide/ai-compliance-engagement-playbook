# NIST SP 1299 CSF resource-governance engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST CSF content, a framework interpretation, a compliance claim, or professional advice. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 1299](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/1299/final), the current CSF resources, and the organization's approved security, privacy, legal, financial, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage the use of CSF-related resources as a controlled engagement input rather than a collection of disconnected downloads. Maintain an authoritative register of the resources the organization relies on, including publication or resource identity, version or release date, purpose, owner, applicability, source location, licensing or usage considerations, related profiles or work items, and last review. Record why a resource is relevant and which local decision or evidence it informs.

Use the register to distinguish framework materials, supplemental guidance, implementation references, tools, and locally approved procedures. Check for changes before significant assessment, planning, or reporting events. When an external resource changes, identify affected profiles, evidence requests, training, procedures, contracts, or improvement plans; route material impacts to accountable humans for decision. Do not infer that a new reference automatically changes an organization’s obligations or conformance position.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Security, risk, technology, privacy, legal, procurement, records, business, and framework-program owners determine relevant sources and local use. Operators maintain the resource register, version checks, provenance records, applicability notes, change-impact records, linked work items, and review history. Leadership approves material scope, policy, investment, and risk decisions; auditors evaluate evidence without selecting authoritative materials, interpreting legal obligations, setting scope, approving changes, or attesting.

Review high-use and high-impact resources at least quarterly, validate the source register before annual planning and assessment preparation, and perform an annual governance review of resource ownership, currency, usage, and unresolved impacts. Trigger targeted review when a publisher releases a material change, an engagement scope changes, a new contractual or regulatory requirement appears, or a material incident reveals a gap.

Independent reviewers sample a resource from official source through applicability decision, local use, impacted records, and subsequent review, testing traceability and currency. Auditors evaluate supplied evidence without declaring which resources govern, deciding applicability, making legal conclusions, approving policy, accepting risk, or issuing an attestation. AI may monitor approved source lists, compare versions, organize registers, flag stale reviews, draft change-impact questions, and summarize supplied records. AI cannot determine legal applicability, select authoritative sources, approve scope or policy, accept risk, make legal or financial conclusions, or attest to conformance.
