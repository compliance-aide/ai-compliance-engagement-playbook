# NIST SP 800-210 cloud-access-control engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST cloud access-control content, an access-control policy, a cloud configuration authorization, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-210](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/210/final) and the organization's approved security, architecture, privacy, legal, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Run a governed, year-round engagement for access to in-scope cloud services. Tie each service, tenant, account, identity, administrative path, resource, and data category to its accountable owner, approved purpose, applicable authorization rules, identity and attribute sources, configuration baseline, monitoring, and retirement path. Account for how responsibilities change among infrastructure, platform, and software service arrangements, including the provider's managed components and the customer's remaining duties.

Treat a policy document, configuration export, identity listing, access-review record, log sample, supplier statement, or test result as evidence to evaluate in context—not automatic proof that access is appropriate, enforced, or operating effectively. Preserve the decision trail from business need through policy and architecture to configuration, recurring review, exceptions, incidents, and corrective work.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Security, cloud, architecture, identity, system, data, privacy, legal, supplier, change, incident-response, and records owners define scope, approve policy and risk decisions, and retain decision evidence. Operators keep the service and identity inventory, approved architecture, authorization design, role and attribute traceability, configuration baselines, access-review results, logging and monitoring evidence, supplier dependencies, exceptions, incident records, and retirement evidence current. Reconcile services, accounts, identities, resources, owners, and suppliers at least quarterly; review authorization failures, policy or configuration changes, exceptions, and incidents on the same rhythm; and complete a management review annually and after material provider, architecture, identity, threat, or published-guidance changes.

Independent reviewers sample the path from scope and policy through configuration and observed operation. Auditors test supplied evidence without granting access, changing cloud settings, approving exceptions, accepting risk, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize evidence, identify missing links, draft workpapers, compare approved baselines with supplied records, and prepare review questions. AI cannot define or approve policy, grant or revoke access, alter cloud configurations, approve exceptions, accept risk, make legal conclusions, or attest to conformance.
