# NIST SP 800-201 cloud-forensic-readiness engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST guidance, legal advice, a forensic conclusion, a provider authorization, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-201](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/201/final) and the organization’s approved cloud, legal, privacy, records, security, and incident-response decisions.

## Engagement focus

Maintain cloud forensic readiness that connects material cloud services, shared-responsibility assumptions, data and evidence sources, provider capabilities, access paths, preservation methods, investigation handoffs, and known gaps. Use the reference architecture to structure evidence planning across cloud environments, not to assume that a tenant has access to provider-held evidence or that technical artifacts settle legal, contractual, or investigative questions.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, cloud, security, platform, application, data, privacy, legal, records-management, procurement, supplier-management, incident-command, and service-owner roles. Operators maintain cloud-service inventories, responsibility matrices, evidence-source maps, access and preservation procedures, provider-support records, retention approvals, exercise results, unresolved gaps, and remediation status. Review material cloud services, provider capabilities, evidence assumptions, and access paths quarterly; test representative preservation and provider-handoff scenarios at least annually and after material provider, architecture, data, or contract changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples readiness and exercise artifacts; auditors test the evidence trail without accessing data outside authority, directing an investigation, approving provider arrangements, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied cloud-evidence records, identify missing responsibility or source mappings, flag stale provider artifacts, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot access cloud accounts without authorization, preserve or alter evidence, determine legal discovery duties, make an investigative finding, approve a provider arrangement, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** At engagement start, retain the retrieved version and applicable use terms for the official [NIST SP 800-201 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/201/final), with approved cloud, legal, privacy, records, security, and incident-response direction. This is original evidence planning, not reproduced NIST content, legal discovery advice, or a conclusion that provider-held material is obtainable.

### 1. Cloud scope, responsibility, and evidence-source package

- **Request and owner:** Cloud, platform, application, data, security, privacy, records, procurement, supplier, and service owners provide cloud-service inventory, tenancy/account map, data and service criticality records, shared-responsibility matrix, evidence-source map, and identified access or capability gaps.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one material cloud service from owner and data/service context through its responsibility assignment and stated evidence sources, including provider-held boundaries. This can assess documented readiness assumptions; it cannot prove evidence availability or resolve contractual or legal access rights.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare supplied maps and flag absent owners, unmapped sources, or stale provider assertions. Humans approve scope, responsibility interpretation, supplier arrangements, and risk treatment. Refresh after provider, service, tenancy, architecture, data, or contract change.

### 2. Preservation, access, and provider-handoff package

- **Request and owner:** Cloud, security, incident-command, legal, privacy, records, identity, and supplier owners provide approved preservation/access procedures, authorization paths, provider-support contacts and commitments, retention settings/decisions, exercise records, and access-test outcomes.
- **Validate and limit:** Walk one authorized readiness scenario from trigger through human approval, preservation/access procedure, provider handoff, and recorded result or limitation. This can test a documented handoff path; it cannot preserve evidence, compel a provider, determine discovery duties, or establish chain-of-custody sufficiency.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a read-only readiness packet and identify missing authority or contact links. Humans authorize access, preservation, provider escalation, and legal/records decisions. Re-test after provider process changes, failed exercise, relevant incident, or material access change.

### 3. Gap governance, exercise, and improvement package

- **Request and owner:** Risk, cloud, incident-command, legal, privacy, records, supplier, service, and independent-review owners provide gap register, approved compensating measures, exercise observations, corrective-action records, supplier follow-up, retest evidence, and management decisions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected readiness gap or exercise finding to its accountable owner, human decision, limitation, target date, and follow-up/retest. This can support improvement governance; it cannot accept risk, certify readiness, or make an investigative or legal finding.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag overdue actions and draft challenge questions from supplied records. Humans approve exceptions, risk decisions, remediation closure, and external statements. Review quarterly and after a provider, contract, incident, exercise, retention, or regulatory change.
