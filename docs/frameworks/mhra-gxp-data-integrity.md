# MHRA GxP data-integrity engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not MHRA guidance or an investigation conclusion. Consult the [MHRA data-integrity guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guidance-on-gxp-data-integrity).

## Engagement focus

Maintain GxP data lifecycle, outsourced flows, criticality, lineage, anomalous changes, training, investigations, CAPA, provenance, and closure evidence.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Humans approve investigations and CAPA closure; independent reviewers use frozen sample ledgers. AI finds anomalies and gaps, but cannot accept risk or reach conclusions. Review quarterly and annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [MHRA GxP data integrity guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guidance-on-gxp-data-integrity). Quality and regulatory leadership must confirm current status, product/site applicability, and any inspection implications. Retrieved 2026-07-31. This is original operational planning, not MHRA text, an inspection method, or a GxP conclusion.

### 1. GxP process, critical-data, and lifecycle boundary

- **Request and owner:** Approved process/system inventory, intended-use and data-flow descriptions, critical-data/process rationale, lifecycle/retention boundary, supplier-hosted component record, and named quality, system, process, and data owners. Cover each approved site, product/process, and computerized or hybrid record system in the engagement boundary.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace selected critical data from creation through processing, review, retention, and retrieval to identified accountable owners and the approved lifecycle description. Use sanitized metadata and controlled access. This can support an accountable scope and lineage record; it cannot establish data integrity, GxP applicability, validation adequacy, or inspection readiness.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may catalog approved flow metadata and identify incomplete ownership or lifecycle links. Quality leadership decides criticality, intended use, and scope. Refresh for new/changed systems, outsourced processes, material data-flow change, or annual quality review.

### 2. Record provenance, access, and review trace

- **Request and owner:** Role/access approvals, audit-trail availability and review references, contemporaneous-record review records, controlled procedure/training references, backup/restore or archive evidence, and deviation/exception records from system owners, quality, IT, and process owners. Bound samples to the frozen population and period selected by the human reviewer.
- **Validate and limit:** Reperform a read-only trace for selected records from source entry through attributable review, relevant change history, and retention/retrieval evidence. Do not alter production GxP data or expose regulated/patient/personal data. This supports traceability for the sample; it cannot prove completeness, accuracy, contemporaneity, or effectiveness across all records.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare approved logs and review metadata for gaps, but cannot change records, approve review, or interpret anomalies as misconduct. Human quality owners determine follow-up. Trigger on audit-trail anomaly, access change, restoration, data migration, or periodic review.

### 3. Deviation, investigation, CAPA, and supplier oversight

- **Request and owner:** Sanitized deviation/incident register, investigation and impact-assessment references, CAPA/change-control linkage, effectiveness-check record, supplier quality/oversight evidence, and closure approvals owned by quality, regulatory, system, and supplier-management leaders. Cover all material events in the defined period.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace selected events to detection, preservation, investigation ownership, impact decision, corrective/preventive action, supplier coordination where applicable, and human closure. Preserve privileged and regulated content outside the public repository. This can support a controlled event history; it cannot conclude root cause, product impact, reportability, or CAPA effectiveness.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may cluster sanitized event metadata and flag missing dependencies or overdue steps. Humans decide investigation outcome, impact, regulatory communication, CAPA acceptance, and closure. Trigger on a suspected integrity issue, migration, supplier failure, inspection observation, or annual management review.
