# California workforce privacy — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not a standalone law or employment-law advice.

## Source and applicability

Use the [CPPA FAQs](https://cppa.ca.gov/faq) and linked current law/regulations,
checked 2026-09-04. The FAQs identify California employees and applicants among
residents with CCPA rights; covered-business scope still requires analysis.
Workforce status is not a blanket exemption. CPRA amended CCPA rather than creating
a separate law. The FAQs are explanatory, not binding regulatory guidance.

The [employee-data rulemaking page](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/employee_data.html)
is a source-change watch point, not proof that proposed rules are operative.
Counsel must resolve current requirements and any employment, retention or other
legal obligations. See the [author record](../refresh-reviews/california-workforce-privacy.md).

## Engagement focus

Trace applicant, active and former-worker information through recruiting, employment
and offboarding. Reconcile notices, purposes, providers, rights and retention with
actual systems. Keep payroll, benefits, monitoring and sensitive information in
approved storage; review metadata rather than copying personnel files into AI tools.

## Roles

HR owns workforce processes; privacy coordinates rights and notices; IT/security
and provider owners supply operational evidence. California privacy/employment
counsel decides applicability, exceptions and retention conflicts. Authorised owners
approve responses; independent reviewers challenge evidence. AI may reconcile
metadata and prepare cases, but cannot decide employment outcomes, deny rights,
accept risk or transmit unauthorised personal information.

## Before starting

Obtain entity/applicability decisions, workforce populations, HR/recruiting/provider
map, notice versions, retention/hold rules, approved rights procedures and evidence
permissions. Identify former-worker access routes separately from employee login.
Use fictional QA records and the [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md).

## Ordered workflow

| Step | Action and owner | Output and exit check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Resolve scope | Legal/privacy identify covered entity, workforce relationships and data-specific issues. | Dated applicability record with unresolved questions. |
| 2. Map collection and use | HR/provider owners connect intake, purposes, systems, recipients and retention. AI reconciles metadata. | Population and flow map covering applicant-to-former-worker lifecycle. |
| 3. Reconcile notices and decisions | Privacy/legal compare actual uses with notices, sensitive-data handling and provider instructions. | Approved decision references and visible inconsistencies. |
| 4. Plan rights cases | Privacy defines request-specific identity, search, exception, clock and response routes. | Fictional cases include former workers and retention conflicts. |
| 5. Observe execution | Authorised QA operators run approved searches, corrections/disposals and access checks. | Actual readback matches the decision without exposing other workers. |
| 6. Correct and retest | Owners address failed handoffs and provider gaps through approved work; reviewer repeats the case. | Finding/change/retest chain and remaining issues. |
| 7. Renew governance | HR/privacy review new tools, monitoring, provider and legal changes. | Updated evidence, decisions and next queue. |

## Evidence and test plan

| Evidence and custodian | Check | Expected observation | Failure or limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Workforce flow map, HR/privacy | Trace recruiting and payroll routes to notice, purpose and provider. | Lifecycle stages and owners reconcile. | One HR database cannot establish all workforce processing. |
| Fictional request, privacy | Exercise current and former-worker routes under approved rules. | Request reaches an owner without relying solely on active employee login. | A disabled account must not silently discard a request. |
| Retention/hold case, records/legal | Compare eligible disposal with a legally held fictional record. | Actions follow the specific approved decision and are read back. | A generic deletion command cannot resolve retention duties. |
| Provider/access evidence, IT/vendor owner | Trace permitted action and provider instruction to observed completion. | Scope and receipt match the intended record. | A sent instruction is not proof of provider completion. |

## Failure branches and decisions

Missing inventory stays a gap. Identity conflicts go to privacy without collecting
extra documents on AI initiative. A hold or employment dispute requires specific
legal review, not blanket denial of every request. Potential incidents follow the
separate incident/privacy route promptly. After interruption preserve original
request clocks and check existing actions before repeating disclosure or erasure.

## Cadence and renewal

Configure separate approved timing rules for each right; do not use one timer for
all requests. Quarterly HR/provider reviews and annual governance review are planning
defaults. Reassess before new recruiting or monitoring tools, sensitive-data uses,
providers, retention changes or operative legal changes.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver scope, lifecycle/provider map, notice versions, protected evidence, QA cases,
findings/retests, legal decisions and next queue. State unresolved populations and
provider actions. Independent source, engagement and skeptical reviews and named
human publication approval remain pending. Original prose and links only; no real
personnel evidence or statutory tables reproduced.
