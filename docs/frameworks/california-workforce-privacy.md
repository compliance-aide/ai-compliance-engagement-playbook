# California workforce privacy — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not a standalone legal regime or legal advice. Check the [California Privacy Protection Agency FAQs](https://cppa.ca.gov/faq).

## Engagement focus

Maintain applicability, applicant/workforce data, purpose, recipient, provider, retention, sensitive-data, notice, verified-rights, vendor, disclosure, and exception evidence.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Counsel decides scope; independent reviewers test notices and workflows. AI organizes evidence, but cannot deny or respond to rights requests without human authorization. Review quarterly and annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [California Privacy Protection Agency FAQs](https://cppa.ca.gov/faq) and qualified California privacy/employment counsel; checked 2026-07-31. This original plan does not decide whether a workforce relationship, entity, data set, exemption, sensitive-data activity, notice, request, or response is subject to California requirements.

### 1. Workforce data, purpose, and notice package

- **Request and owner:** Human-approved scope inputs; applicant, employee, contractor, and former-workforce data inventory; collection sources; purposes; systems; recipients/providers; retention; sensitive-data flags; and notice/version records owned by HR, privacy, security, IT, and vendor-management owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected workforce data flow from collection channel to stated purpose, system, recipient/provider, notice version, retention record, and accountable owner. This supports a factual inventory; it cannot determine employment-law treatment, legal scope, a data classification, or whether a notice is sufficient.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare approved HR-system, identity, and vendor metadata and flag unowned changes. Counsel and privacy/HR leaders decide scope and material notice changes. Refresh for an HR platform, recruiting channel, sensitive-data use, recipient, or policy change.

### 2. Rights, verification, and internal-exception operation package

- **Request and owner:** Request-intake and routing procedure; redacted request, verification, response, and escalation records; access and correction/deletion workflow evidence where used; internal exception/review log; employee-support training; and remediation evidence from privacy, HR, legal, IT, and support owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Independently trace a de-identified case from intake through assigned owner, verification step, system search, human decision, response, and closure. This can evidence workflow traceability; it cannot authenticate a requester, decide an exception, determine a deadline, or establish a legally sufficient outcome.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare de-identified samples and flag missing handoffs or aging cases; it cannot decide or transmit a response. Authorized humans handle identity, exceptions, communications, and closure. Recheck after a request pattern, workflow defect, or HR-system change.

### 3. Provider, retention, security, and change-governance package

- **Request and owner:** HR/payroll/recruiting/provider register; contractual/instruction and due-diligence records; role-based access reviews; retention/deletion outputs; incident/exercise records; material-change and management approvals; and remediation/retest evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected provider, access-review, deletion, or incident item to evidence provenance, authority, stated limitation, corrective action, and retest. This supports governance traceability; it cannot prove contract sufficiency, security effectiveness, breach duties, or legal compliance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved evidence and flag expiring reviews. Human HR, legal, privacy, security, and leadership owners approve risk, contracts, notices, external communications, and closure. Refresh after a provider, access, retention, incident, or annual governance change.
