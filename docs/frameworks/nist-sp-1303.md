# NIST SP 1303 CSF enterprise-risk-management engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST CSF content, an enterprise risk decision, a risk appetite statement, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 1303](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/1303/final) and the organization's approved governance, finance, security, privacy, legal, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round engagement that integrates cybersecurity risk information into enterprise risk management without flattening important technical and operational context. Connect material services, systems, data, suppliers, risk scenarios, owners, impacts, assumptions, treatments, escalation paths, enterprise reporting, and management decisions. Make the transition from system-level evidence to enterprise-level discussion traceable so decision-makers can understand what is known, uncertain, changing, or unresolved.

Treat risk registers, profiles, dashboards, metrics, scenarios, assessments, and board materials as decision-support evidence—not automatic proof of risk level, control effectiveness, or acceptable exposure. Preserve the rationale behind aggregation, prioritization, escalation, treatment, acceptance, and reevaluation. Record evidence limitations and disagreements so the enterprise risk view does not create false precision or obscure local obligations.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Executive, governing body, enterprise-risk, finance, security, technology, privacy, business, supplier, legal, audit, and records owners establish scope and make accountable decisions. Operators maintain risk and profile records, ownership and dependency links, evidence references, aggregation and reporting records, treatment status, exceptions, management decisions, and review history. Reconcile material cybersecurity risks, enterprise mappings, owners, evidence freshness, and treatment progress at least quarterly; update after material incidents, strategic changes, acquisitions, supplier shifts, or regulatory developments; and conduct an annual enterprise-risk governance review.

Independent reviewers sample a cybersecurity risk from operational evidence through enterprise reporting and management action, testing traceability and consistency. Auditors evaluate supplied evidence without setting risk appetite, assigning priority, accepting risk, approving investment, changing risk records, making legal or financial conclusions, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize evidence, identify missing ownership or stale risk records, draft workpapers and reports, compare supplied records with approved decisions, and prepare review questions. AI cannot determine materiality, set appetite or tolerance, select treatment, accept risk, approve investments, make legal or financial conclusions, or attest to conformance.
