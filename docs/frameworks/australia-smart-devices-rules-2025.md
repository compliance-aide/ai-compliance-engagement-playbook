# Australia smart-device security rules — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not legislation, a statement of compliance or
> authority to release a product.

## Source and applicability

Use the [Rules register](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2025L00276/latest/versions)
and [Home Affairs guidance](https://www.homeaffairs.gov.au/cyber-security-subsite/Pages/security-standards-for-smart-devices.aspx).
Checked 2026-09-04: the register lists F2025L00276; Home Affairs confirms commencement
on 4 March 2026 and scope tied to manufacture on/from that date, consumer use and
applicable exclusions. Sale date alone is insufficient. Counsel must check the
complete section 8 exclusions and each entity's manufacturer/supplier role.

The guidance identifies password, vulnerability-reporting, security-support and
statement/retention requirements. It does not prescribe one universal way to
supply a product with its statement. Record a product-specific legal decision.
Use the actual Act/Rules for detailed criteria, not this guide as a replacement.
See the [author record](../refresh-reviews/australia-smart-devices-rules-2025.md).

## Engagement focus

Connect each in-scope model and manufacturing batch to implementation evidence,
support commitments and an authorised statement. Keep historical voluntary work
and foreign assurance separate from the Australian statutory decision.

## Roles

Product/supply chain own models, batch dates and channels. Engineering/security
own implementation and tests. Support owns vulnerability intake and update dates.
Legal resolves applicability and statement requirements; authorised signatories
approve statements and release decisions. Independent reviewers challenge evidence.
AI may reconcile metadata and draft packets; it cannot decide market access,
product safety, compliance or legal exceptions.

## Before starting

Obtain the model/batch inventory, manufacture-date evidence, intended-use and market
facts, entity roles, current source, approved obligation register, QA test devices,
support policy, statement versions and retention rules. Unknown manufacture date
blocks the affected scope conclusion. Follow the [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md).

## Ordered workflow

| Step | Action and owner | Output and exit check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Resolve scope | Product supplies facts; legal evaluates date, use, exclusions and entity role. | Model/batch applicability record with source and decision owner. |
| 2. Set verification criteria | Security/legal map applicable duties to approved test methods, evidence and expected observations. | Coverage plan with every scoped duty accounted for. |
| 3. Check actual products | Authorised QA operators run approved credential/configuration tests on identified versions. | Actual observations match product identity; failures remain visible. |
| 4. Check support operation | Support verifies published intake and support information against internal case handling and commitments. | Observed fictional-case route and consistent support dates. |
| 5. Assemble the statement packet | AI reconciles approved statement inputs, product identifiers, evidence and proposed distribution/retention method. | Traceable draft with unresolved fields explicitly marked. |
| 6. Decide and verify delivery | Signatory/legal approve the exact statement and supply method; authorised operator performs permitted distribution. | Approval and readback reference the same artifact; preparation alone is not delivery. |
| 7. Maintain and correct | Owners assess changes/failures, obtain applicable decisions and retest affected claims. | Updated evidence and statements with prior versions retained. |

## Evidence and test plan

| Evidence and custodian | Check | Expected observation | Failure or limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Manufacturing/role register, supply chain | Trace a model/batch to dated production and channel records. | Scope facts are supported. | A sales invoice alone cannot settle manufacture date. |
| Credential QA results, engineering | Compare actual setup and operational states against the approved legal criteria. | Tested hardware/software and expected/observed outcomes are explicit. | Testing one state cannot establish every variant's behavior. |
| Support/intake evidence, support | Reconcile public dates and contact route with an approved fictional internal case. | Commitments agree and a responsible owner handles the case. | A published page cannot prove future update delivery. |
| Statement packet, signatory/records | Match statement, product, approval, supply method and retention schedule. | Same approved artifact is retained and supplied by the authorised route. | A draft or generic QR code is not proof of legal sufficiency. |

## Failure branches and decisions

Missing scope facts require product/legal resolution, not an inferred exclusion.
A failed credential test prevents the affected supported-compliance claim and
creates corrective work. Conflicting support dates go to the commitment owner
before external reuse. Suspected non-compliance follows the approved legal/product
escalation route for applicable remedial duties; AI does not invent a recall.
After interruption, check whether distribution already occurred before retrying.

## Cadence and renewal

Check before release and after model, firmware, manufacturing, channel, support or
legal changes. Use a legally approved retention schedule from the current Rules.
Quarterly active-product review is an operating default; it does not replace
release checks or event-driven action.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver applicability decisions, duty/coverage plan, QA evidence, support records,
statement and supply/retention receipts, findings and retests. Identify unresolved
variants and signatory decisions. This guide's independent source/engagement review
and named human publication approval remain pending. Only original workflow and
links are included; no statutory tables or private product evidence are reproduced.
