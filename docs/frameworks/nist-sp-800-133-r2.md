# NIST SP 800-133 Revision 2 cryptographic key-generation engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST key-generation content, a cryptographic design, an approval, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-133 Revision 2](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/133/r2/final) and the organization’s approved cryptography, security, architecture, legal, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Operate a governed lifecycle for in-scope cryptographic key-generation capabilities, covering the purpose and scope of each key type, approved design decisions, key-generating components, entropy or other input dependencies, generation events, interfaces, storage and handoff, access, monitoring, testing, supplier dependencies, incidents, changes, replacement, and retirement. Connect each key-generation decision to its accountable owner and supporting evidence. A configuration extract, supplier statement, test result, generation record, or audit log is evidence to assess; none alone proves adequate key material, correct implementation, approved use, secure operation, or compliance.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, security, cryptography or platform engineering, architecture, key-management operations, identity, application and data owners, supplier-management, legal and privacy where relevant, change-management, incident-response, and records-management roles. Operators maintain a scoped inventory of key-generation capabilities and dependencies, approved design and use decisions, configuration and component baselines, test and monitoring evidence, access and audit records, supplier materials, exceptions, incident records, replacement decisions, and retirement evidence. Reconcile capabilities, owners, dependencies, integrations, and suppliers quarterly; review generation-related alerts, component or configuration changes, exceptions, incidents, and unresolved risks at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material cryptographic, product, provider, threat, or revision changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples purpose-to-generation-to-lifecycle traceability; auditors test supplied evidence without generating or accessing keys, changing configuration, approving exceptions, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied inventories, design decisions, configuration, test, supplier, and review evidence, identify stale records or missing ownership, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot select or generate key material, access or derive keys, change cryptographic configuration, approve an exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.
