# CISA secure-software attestation readiness — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not an attestation, legal conclusion, or authorization to submit. Consult the [CISA attestation resource](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/secure-software-development-attestation-form) and the applicable solicitation.

## Engagement focus

Maintain the requested-product boundary, responsible parties, development and supply-chain evidence, exception analysis, release records, supporting artifacts, and customer communication history.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Authorized organizational representatives control representations and submissions; independent reviewers challenge the evidence package. AI creates an evidence inventory and drafts gaps, but cannot complete, sign, transmit, or characterize an attestation. Reassess each request and annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [CISA secure-software development attestation resource](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/secure-software-development-attestation-form), the requesting agency's current solicitation, and organization-approved legal direction; checked 2026-07-31. This original plan does not reproduce the form, interpret a solicitation, determine an exception, complete an attestation, or authorize submission. Authorized human representatives confirm source currency, scope, and use conditions.

### 1. Solicitation, offered-software, and signer-authority package

- **Request and owner:** Capture the applicable solicitation/request, offered software and version list, delivery/hosting boundary, organizational role, known exceptions, responsible executives, proposed signer authority, and legal/procurement interpretation record.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected offered product/version to the request, delivery boundary, accountable organization, and documented signer authorization. This can expose mismatched product scope or missing authority; it cannot interpret procurement law, decide eligibility, or authorize a representation.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize authorized request/product metadata and flag missing version, scope, or authority fields. Humans resolve solicitation applicability, exception posture, and signer authority. Refresh for each request, material offer change, or signer/organizational-role change.

### 2. Secure-development and software-supply-chain evidence package

- **Request and owner:** Engineering, security, release, and supplier owners provide development-process references, source/build provenance pointers, release approvals, dependency/supplier records, security-test references, vulnerability/remediation records, and approved deviations relevant to the offered software.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample one offered release from source/build or supplier evidence through security/release record, exception approval if applicable, and retained provenance. This can test a bounded evidence chain; it cannot validate all code, determine conformance, or replace the requested attestation analysis.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may create a read-only evidence index and identify absent provenance, dates, or owners. Humans judge sufficiency, approve deviations, and decide remediation/release actions. Refresh after material code, build, dependency, supplier, vulnerability, or release changes.

### 3. Exception, representation, and submission-readiness package

- **Request and owner:** Legal, procurement, executive, security, and independent-review owners provide exception analysis, supporting citations, unresolved-gap register, management decisions, reviewer challenge notes, proposed representation, and submission approval trail.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected exception or proposed statement to its evidence, human authority, limitation, decision date, and required follow-up. This can show whether readiness governance is documented; it cannot conclude that an exception is permitted, sign, transmit, or bind the organization.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may draft discrepancy questions and flag stale evidence; it cannot complete forms or make representations. Humans approve legal/procurement posture, final language, signature, and transmission. Recheck before every submission and annually for baseline readiness.
