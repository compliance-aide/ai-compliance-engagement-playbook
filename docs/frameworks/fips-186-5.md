# FIPS 186-5 digital-signature engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not FIPS content, digital-signature implementation instructions, a cryptographic approval, a certificate, or a compliance claim. Confirm current requirements and errata through [FIPS 186-5](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/186-5/final) and the organization’s approved security, cryptography, legal, engineering, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage the approved use of digital signatures across in-scope applications, services, documents, software-delivery processes, and suppliers. Maintain traceability between the business purpose, signer identity or service identity, certificate or key reference, application or module, deployment environment, verification behavior, change history, incident handling, and accountable owner. A successful signature verification, product setting, or certificate record is evidence to assess; it does not prove signer authority, correct cryptographic implementation, legal non-repudiation, continued key security, or compliance.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, security, cryptography or platform engineering, architecture, identity, application and service owners, legal, privacy where relevant, procurement, supplier-management, incident-response, change-management, and records-management roles. Operators maintain an in-scope signature-use inventory, approved purposes and signers, approved module and version baselines, key or certificate lifecycle references, deployment and verification evidence, supplier assurance, exception decisions, compromise and revocation procedures, and system-to-signature traceability. Reconcile uses, owners, certificates, and deployed versions quarterly; review expiring or revoked credentials, implementation changes, failed verifications, supplier notices, exceptions, and unresolved risks at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material application, cryptographic, supplier, or regulatory changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples a signature use through authorization, deployment, verification, and lifecycle evidence; auditors test the evidence trail without signing, accessing private keys, configuring cryptography, approving an exception, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied use, lifecycle, and verification evidence, identify missing ownership or overdue review, correlate approved inventories with deployment records, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot select or implement cryptography, sign or verify on behalf of a user, access private keys, determine signer authority, approve an exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.
