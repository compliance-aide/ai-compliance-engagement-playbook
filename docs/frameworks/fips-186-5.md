# FIPS 186-5 digital-signature engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not FIPS content, digital-signature implementation instructions, a cryptographic approval, a certificate, or a compliance claim. Confirm current requirements and errata through [FIPS 186-5](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/186-5/final) and the organization’s approved security, cryptography, legal, engineering, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage the approved use of digital signatures across in-scope applications, services, documents, software-delivery processes, and suppliers. Maintain traceability between the business purpose, signer identity or service identity, certificate or key reference, application or module, deployment environment, verification behavior, change history, incident handling, and accountable owner. A successful signature verification, product setting, or certificate record is evidence to assess; it does not prove signer authority, correct cryptographic implementation, legal non-repudiation, continued key security, or compliance.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, security, cryptography or platform engineering, architecture, identity, application and service owners, legal, privacy where relevant, procurement, supplier-management, incident-response, change-management, and records-management roles. Operators maintain an in-scope signature-use inventory, approved purposes and signers, approved module and version baselines, key or certificate lifecycle references, deployment and verification evidence, supplier assurance, exception decisions, compromise and revocation procedures, and system-to-signature traceability. Reconcile uses, owners, certificates, and deployed versions quarterly; review expiring or revoked credentials, implementation changes, failed verifications, supplier notices, exceptions, and unresolved risks at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material application, cryptographic, supplier, or regulatory changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples a signature use through authorization, deployment, verification, and lifecycle evidence; auditors test the evidence trail without signing, accessing private keys, configuring cryptography, approving an exception, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied use, lifecycle, and verification evidence, identify missing ownership or overdue review, correlate approved inventories with deployment records, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot select or implement cryptography, sign or verify on behalf of a user, access private keys, determine signer authority, approve an exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the current [FIPS 186-5 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/186-5/final), public NIST material retrieved 2026-07-31. This guide is original planning guidance, not digital-signature instructions, a validation artifact, or a legal conclusion. Identity, cryptography, legal, and service owners decide signer authority and approved purpose.

### 1. Signature-purpose and authority package

- **Request and owner:** Business, legal, identity, application, and cryptography owners provide the signature-use inventory, purpose statement, service or signer identity reference, authorization/approval record, relying-party context, and retention boundary.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected use to a named purpose, accountable owner, identity/authorization record, and application or document workflow without exposing private keys or personal data. This can support documented ownership and purpose; it cannot establish signer identity, legal non-repudiation, or authority to sign.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may identify inconsistent owner, system, or expiry metadata. Humans decide authority and legal use. Refresh after a new signing workflow, role change, service integration, or legal requirement change.

### 2. Credential and deployment lifecycle package

- **Request and owner:** Identity, key/certificate operations, platform engineering, and supplier owners provide credential lifecycle references, approved module/version records, deployment associations, expiration/revocation monitoring, and supplier notices.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect a selected signature service’s recorded credential reference, deployment baseline, and lifecycle review timestamp. This can support lifecycle evidence traceability; it cannot verify key custody, issue a credential, or confirm the cryptographic operation.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag expiring references or missing deployment links without accessing protected key material. Authorized operational owners decide renewal, revocation, and configuration. Refresh after issuance, rotation, revocation, supplier change, or release.

### 3. Verification, incident, and exception package

- **Request and owner:** Service operations, security, incident response, change management, and risk owners provide verification-monitoring records, anomaly/incident references, change approvals, exception decisions, and independent-review observations.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected failed verification or exception to detection, triage, named decision authority, remediation, and closure evidence. This can show an accountable response trail; it cannot assess every transaction, determine compromise, or accept risk.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may cluster failures and flag overdue follow-up. Humans decide incident severity, exception approval, and external communication. Refresh after an anomaly, incident, material change, or annual renewal.
