# FIPS 186-5 digital-signature engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not FIPS content, digital-signature implementation instructions, a cryptographic approval, a certificate, or a compliance claim. Confirm current requirements and errata through [FIPS 186-5](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/186-5/final) and the organization’s approved security, cryptography, legal, engineering, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage the approved use of digital signatures across in-scope applications, services, documents, software-delivery processes, and suppliers. Maintain traceability between the business purpose, signer identity or service identity, certificate or key reference, application or module, deployment environment, verification behavior, change history, incident handling, and accountable owner. A successful signature verification, product setting, or certificate record is evidence to assess; it does not prove signer authority, correct cryptographic implementation, legal non-repudiation, continued key security, or compliance.

## Source and applicability

The [NIST publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/186-5/final), read 2026-09-04, identifies the February 2023 edition and a May 2025 note linking potential corrections. Retrieve the full standard, applicable errata and related parameter/transition guidance before accepting an implementation. The publication page alone does not resolve those details. Keep mathematical verification, identity binding, signer authorization and legal effect separate; this guide does not establish any of them for a real transaction.

## Roles

Cryptography owners approve algorithm and parameter choices. Identity and key-management owners provide authorized public-key and lifecycle evidence. Application owners define the signed object and consuming decision. Legal owners determine authority and legal interpretation. Independent reviewers challenge source applicability and evidence. AI organizes approved metadata and drafts findings; it cannot access private keys, sign on a user's behalf, approve cryptography, accept risk or attest to compliance.

## Before starting

Record every in-scope signing and verification use, system, purpose, algorithm/parameter reference, implementation version, public-key identifier, signed-object format and relying party. Define the permitted signer and expected verification decision through the approved owner. Use the [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md) and authorized synthetic QA artifacts; do not put private keys or confidential signed records in this repository.

## Ordered workflow

1. **Map the transaction and authority.** Link the intended business action to the signing service/person and authorized owner decision. Output a use/authority register. A key that verifies a signature is not by itself evidence that the signer was authorized to approve this action.
2. **Define the signed bytes.** Record serialization, canonicalization, fields, attachments and contextual identifiers covered by the signature. Output an input contract. Displayed content and signed content must be reconciled; unsigned fields must not silently inherit authenticity claims.
3. **Identify implementation and trust inputs.** Record actual signing/verifying builds, approved parameter references, public-key source and applicable certificate/trust policy where used. Output a baseline manifest. Do not assume all signature uses employ certificates or the same trust model.
4. **Prepare approved QA cases.** Include the valid synthetic object, altered covered content, wrong key, malformed signature and relevant context mismatch. Define expected outcomes before execution. Output a test matrix with full in-scope coverage and untested cases; vector success is not module validation or proof of legal effect.
5. **Observe the consuming action.** Verify the application distinguishes mathematical verification from trust and authorization decisions, then observes its approved acceptance/rejection policy. Output end-to-end evidence. An error retrieving trust information must not be silently converted into a successful decision.
6. **Review lifecycle and historical use.** Trace key changes, compromise reports, revocation information where applicable, timestamps and retention needs to the use-specific policy. Preserve verification context for historical objects. Output owner decisions; do not invent a universal rule that current expiration proves a historical signature invalid or valid.
7. **Retest and hand over gaps.** Link each correction to the affected format, implementation and consumer. Preserve original adverse observations. Output evidence-backed results and assigned unresolved questions rather than a blanket signature-compliance claim.

## Evidence and test plan

### Signature purpose and authority

Business, legal, identity and application owners provide the complete use inventory, approved purposes, signer/service references, authorization records and relying-party requirements. Reconcile each use to the exact action authorized and the content covered. Preserve unsigned attachments or fields as explicit boundaries; an attractive signature badge must not imply protection of content outside the signed object.

Match authorization to the relevant time and transaction type. A current role roster does not establish historical authority, and possession of a recognized key does not settle business approval. Keep identity, key binding and authority decisions separately attributable to their qualified owners. Do not copy sensitive personal identity records into public workpapers.

### Credential and deployment lifecycle

Identity, platform and supplier owners provide public credential identifiers, approved implementation baselines, deployment associations and lifecycle decisions. Reconcile every in-scope signing and verification endpoint with the appropriate version and trust configuration. Record missing environments instead of inferring fleet-wide coverage from one service.

Where certificates or timestamp services are used, preserve the relevant policy, validation context and evidence references. Distinguish a timestamp asserted by the signed application from separately established time evidence. Retain the basis for historical verification decisions without assuming that today's certificate state alone determines a past transaction. Keep private keys and sensitive signing operations outside this evidence-review workflow.

### Verification, incidents and exceptions

Service, security and risk owners provide the complete scoped verification-result population, failed-operation logs, incident records, changes and approved exceptions. Reconcile rejected, indeterminate and processing-error outcomes separately from successful checks. A parser failure or unavailable trust service must not be counted as a verified signature.

For synthetic QA, compare the exact signed object with the object the user sees and the object the application acts upon. Record expected policy behavior for altered content, wrong context and unauthorized signer cases. Preserve all failures and untested branches. A valid low-level verification must not erase a failed application authorization criterion.

Trace each correction through approval, implementation and retest on the affected baseline. Link repeated verification failures to prior actions, preserving unresolved causes. Have qualified owners evaluate suspected compromise and any external communication duties; a failed signature alone does not establish a stolen key or legal breach.

## Failure branches and decisions

Unavailable trust evidence is `inconclusive` for the affected trust assertion; an unperformed operation check is `not_tested`. A known unauthorized signer accepted against the approved policy is `not_supported` even if mathematical verification succeeds. Keep each assertion separate and route cryptographic and legal judgments to the appropriate owners.

Fictional desk case: a QA document verifies against a recognized public key, but the approved signatory roster excludes that signer for the requested transaction. Mathematical verification is `supported`; acceptance as an authorized approval is `not_supported` if the application proceeds. The agent records both results without signing, changing the roster or asserting a legal outcome.

## Cadence and renewal

Review after implementation, parameter, key/trust, signed-format, source or business-authority changes and after verification incidents. Use applicable policy for scheduled reviews and credential lifecycle actions. Do not invent quarterly reviews or annual FIPS renewal. Assign ownership for historical verification and unresolved transition requirements.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver the complete use/authority register, signed-content contracts, implementation/trust inventory, lifecycle evidence, QA matrix and incident/exception register. Separate supported mathematical checks from identity, authority, trust and legal conclusions. Name missing periods, untested consumers and unresolved source issues with owners and next actions.

Independent source, skeptical and rights review remain required before publication. Human owners retain signing authority, cryptographic choices, key lifecycle actions and legal interpretation. This draft has not validated a real implementation, signed a document or established legal non-repudiation.
