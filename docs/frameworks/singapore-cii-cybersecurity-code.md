# Singapore CII cybersecurity code — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not Singapore legal advice or a regulatory representation. Confirm the current code and notices through the [Cyber Security Agency of Singapore](https://www.csa.gov.sg/legislation/codes-of-practice/).

## Engagement focus

Maintain critical-infrastructure scope, owner and authority records, risk assessments, security operations evidence, incident decision records, audit materials, suppliers, exercises, corrective actions, and regulator-interaction history.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Designated infrastructure owners and legal authorities make compliance and reporting decisions; qualified auditors reach conclusions independently. AI coordinates evidence and flags missing records, but cannot determine CII status, report an incident, or make a representation. Exercise and review annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Retain the official [Cyber Security Agency of Singapore codes-of-practice page](https://www.csa.gov.sg/legislation/codes-of-practice/), retrieved 2026-07-31, and have the designated entity and qualified Singapore counsel confirm the applicable code, current status, scope, and permitted use. This is original operational evidence planning, not code text, legal advice, a CII designation, an audit conclusion, an incident report, or a regulatory representation. Keep infrastructure and incident evidence in authorized repositories.

### 1. Critical-information-infrastructure scope and authority package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain the designated-entity or open-status record, approved critical service/system boundary, accountable executive and operational roles, sector/regulator interaction owner, asset/service dependencies, and counsel-led applicability questions from designated infrastructure owners, operations, legal, cybersecurity, and risk. State the engagement population, period, and access restrictions before collecting evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected system/service to its accountable owner, approved boundary, dependency record, and authorized designation/scope decision or escalation record. Use approved summaries where detailed infrastructure information is restricted. This supports scope provenance; it cannot determine CII status, legal applicability, or complete system coverage.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile authorized inventory metadata and flag missing owners, boundaries, or review dates. Designated owners and counsel decide designation/scope issues and regulator escalation. Trigger on service change, acquisition, material dependency change, source update, or annual review.

### 2. Operations, incident-escalation, and exercise package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain approved security-operation and access/change references, monitoring/event records, incident intake/escalation chronology, exercise/test summaries, continuity/recovery references, and exception records from operations, cybersecurity, engineering, resilience, and incident owners. Establish safe handling and privilege boundaries before evidence inspection.
- **Validate and limit:** For a selected event, change, or exercise, inspect the trail from accountable owner and approved operational reference through time-bounded evidence, escalation/decision record, action, and retest or closure link. This supports traceability and readiness governance; it cannot determine reportability, prove effective operation, establish resilience, or replace an auditor’s conclusion.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare read-only chronologies and identify missing handoffs or action links; it cannot operate systems, decide incident reporting, submit a notice, or alter evidence. Named human incident and infrastructure owners decide response and reporting. Trigger on an incident, material change, failed exercise, missed escalation, or annual refresh.

### 3. Supplier, audit, and accountable-remediation package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain supplier/support responsibility records, audit or independent-review references, findings, risk/exception decisions, corrective-action plans, retest evidence, management-review records, and approved regulator-communication boundaries from procurement, cybersecurity, operations, legal, and accountable leaders.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected supplier dependency, audit observation, or exception to the infrastructure boundary, accountable owner, approved action/risk decision, evidence source, due date, and retest or documented closure rationale. Preserve unresolved regulator and supplier constraints. This supports accountable remediation governance; it cannot prove supplier compliance, accept risk, direct an audit conclusion, or authorize regulator communication.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may maintain a non-authoritative action queue and flag stale actions or missing approvals. Independent reviewers challenge traceability; named humans approve risk, closure, and communications. Trigger on supplier change, audit result, material finding, incident, code change, or annual readiness cycle.
