# NIST SP 1302 CSF-tier engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST CSF content, a maturity rating, an assurance conclusion, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 1302](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/1302/final) and the organization's approved governance, security, privacy, legal, operational, and risk decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round engagement for using organizational cybersecurity risk-governance characteristics to inform improvement planning. Connect the relevant organizational profile, risk context, management practices, evidence, accountable owners, strengths, gaps, dependencies, improvement actions, and review decisions. Use tier-oriented discussion to create shared context about the rigor and integration of cybersecurity risk management, not to assign an unsupported maturity label or compare organizations without understanding their missions and exposures.

Treat self-assessments, profile documents, metrics, policies, meeting records, audit outputs, or improvement plans as evidence to evaluate in context. Preserve who made each characterization, the basis and limits of the evidence, what has changed, and what decision follows. Do not treat a tier label, score, or AI-generated comparison as proof of security effectiveness, compliance, or risk acceptability.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Executive, governing body, enterprise-risk, security, business, technology, privacy, legal, supplier, audit, and records owners establish scope and make accountable decisions. Operators maintain profile and context records, evidence references, governance and management activity records, improvement actions, owner assignments, exceptions, decisions, and review history. Reconcile the profile context, material risks, ownership, evidence freshness, and improvement progress at least quarterly; revisit after material incidents, strategy changes, acquisitions, supplier shifts, or regulatory developments; and conduct an annual governance review.

Independent reviewers sample a stated governance characteristic through supporting evidence, operating practice, and improvement follow-up. Auditors evaluate supplied material without assigning binding tiers, changing profiles, accepting risk, approving investments, making legal conclusions, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize evidence, identify missing ownership or stale context, draft workpapers, compare supplied records with approved decisions, and prepare review questions. AI cannot assign authoritative tiers, set risk appetite, select investments, accept risk, make legal conclusions, or attest to conformance.
