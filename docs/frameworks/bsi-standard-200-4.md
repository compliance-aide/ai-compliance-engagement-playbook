# BSI Standard 200-4 business-continuity engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not BSI emergency-management content or a continuity assurance conclusion. Confirm the current source material through the [BSI Standard 200-4 resources](https://www.bsi.bund.de/DE/Themen/Unternehmen-und-Organisationen/Standards-und-Zertifizierung/BSI-Standards/Standard-200-4/standard-200-4_node.html).

## Engagement focus

Maintain a year-round continuity record for critical services: accountable owners, dependencies, disruption assumptions, response and recovery arrangements, exercise results, communications, lessons, and approved improvement actions.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Business owners prioritize service recovery; continuity leaders maintain plans and exercises; technical and supplier teams provide evidence; independent reviewers assess traceability from plans to exercised results. AI indexes plans and evidence, finds stale dependencies or open corrective actions, and prepares exercise questions, but cannot decide safety actions, declare recoverability, or activate a crisis response. Review the record after exercises and material changes, conduct scheduled readiness reviews, and retain human decisions about priorities and acceptance of residual risk.

Business and continuity leaders approve priorities and residual-risk decisions.

### 1. Critical-service, dependency, and disruption-assumption package

- **Request and owner:** Business and continuity owners provide critical-service/process inventory, prioritized dependencies, disruption assumptions, recovery priorities/objectives, stakeholder commitments, supplier/facility links, and accountable-role approvals.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected critical service to its business owner, dependency evidence, disruption assumption, and approved recovery-priority decision. This can support an explicit planning boundary; it cannot prove every dependency is known or that a recovery objective is achievable.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile dependency records and flag stale ownership or assumptions. Humans approve priorities, objectives, and risk acceptance. Refresh after material service, site, supplier, technology, or business change.

### 2. Response, recovery, and exercise package

- **Request and owner:** Continuity, technical, and communications owners provide current plan/version references, response roles/contact records, exercise design and results, recovery/communications artifacts, issues, exceptions, and corrective actions.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect one human-approved exercise trail from scenario through assigned roles, observed result, exception, corrective action, and retest/follow-up. This can support behavior under tested conditions; it cannot establish crisis readiness, activate a response, or prove recovery in untested conditions.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved exercise material and highlight open actions; it cannot run an exercise or send emergency communications. Humans authorize exercises, live response, and action closure. Recollect after an exercise, disruption, plan failure, or material change.

### 3. Management readiness and improvement package

- **Request and owner:** Executive and continuity owners provide readiness-review inputs, unresolved exercise issues, resource and priority decisions, action registers, test/retest evidence, and annual review records.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected unresolved issue to responsible authority, mitigating decision, due date, retest evidence, and current status. This can support accountable improvement oversight; it cannot accept risk for management or make an assurance statement.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may draft source-linked readiness packets and escalation questions. Humans approve priorities, investments, residual risk, and external communications. Review after major events and at scheduled/annual readiness reviews.
