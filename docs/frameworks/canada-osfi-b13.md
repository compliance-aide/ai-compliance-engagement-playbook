# Canada OSFI Guideline B-13 technology and cyber risk-management engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not OSFI text, a supervisory determination, legal advice, or a compliance claim. Confirm current applicability through [OSFI Guideline B-13](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/guidance/guidance-library/technology-cyber-risk-management) and qualified regulatory counsel.

## Engagement focus

Maintain a technology and cyber-risk record that connects governance, accountable ownership, technology assets and services, critical dependencies, architecture and change evidence, resilience planning, cyber observations, incident records, remediation, third-party context, management decisions, and review dates. Make the rationale, sources, scope, and limitations of material statements visible. Keep the record connected to operating evidence so an assessment can distinguish documented intent from observed practice.

## Roles and annual rhythm

The board, senior management, risk, technology, security, operations, privacy, procurement, data, and service owners retain accountable decisions. Technology owners maintain asset and service context; cyber owners coordinate assurance; management decides resources and treatment; incident authorities make escalation and reporting decisions; and auditors evaluate evidence in their independent role. Independent reviewers sample technology services through governance, dependency, design or change, resilience, security evidence, observations, remediation, and closure, challenging unmanaged dependencies, stale evidence, unsupported capability claims, and untested recovery assumptions.

AI may organize authorized metadata, identify missing owners or review fields, compare approved expectations with retained evidence, draft factual questions, and prepare non-authoritative workpapers. AI cannot determine regulatory applicability, decide materiality, approve a risk treatment, direct reporting, authorize a change, close remediation, accept risk, or claim compliance. Reassess before material technology, service, supplier, threat, incident, business, or regulatory changes; review evidence routinely and conduct an annual independent readiness review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the current [OSFI Guideline B-13 record](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/guidance/guidance-library/technology-cyber-risk-management); checked 2026-07-31. This plan is original operational planning, not OSFI text, legal advice, a supervisory conclusion, or a technology/cyber compliance claim. Accountable directors, management, and legal/regulatory owners determine applicability, materiality, risk treatment, and any regulatory communication.

### 1. Technology governance, asset, and risk-context record

- **Request and owner:** Approved technology/cyber governance records, asset and business-service inventory, critical dependencies, architecture/context diagrams, risk assessments, ownership assignments, and management review records from technology, security, risk, and business-service owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected critical service to asset/service ownership, dependency context, risk source, management decision, and review date. This can support a bounded view of evidence linkage; it cannot prove inventory completeness, determine materiality, or decide whether a risk is acceptable.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved inventories, compare review dates, and identify unowned dependencies. Humans approve scope, architecture decisions, and risk treatments. Refresh after material service, asset, supplier, threat, or business changes.

### 2. Secure-operation, resilience, and incident evidence

- **Request and owner:** Authorized change and configuration records, security-monitoring and test outputs, vulnerability/remediation evidence, backup/recovery or resilience exercises, incident records, and service/provider performance evidence from technology, security, resilience, and service owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Follow a human-selected operational change, test, or incident from source evidence through owner response, decision, corrective action, and retest/review. This can demonstrate traceability for the selected activity; it cannot prove continuous control effectiveness, establish recoverability, or determine reporting duties.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a read-only evidence index and flag missing timestamps, owners, or retests. Humans authorize production changes, declare incidents, direct recovery, and decide escalation. Recollect after a failed test, material vulnerability, incident, recovery exercise, or significant provider change.

### 3. Independent assurance, exception, and management-decision workpaper

- **Request and owner:** Assurance plan/results, open-risk and exception register, remediation plans, management decisions, resource approvals, closure/retest evidence, and independent-review observations from risk, internal-audit, technology, security, and management owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected observation or exception to its evidence, accountable decision-maker, mitigation, due date, and independent retest or challenge. This can support accountable improvement; it cannot close remediation, accept residual risk, or replace independent assurance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may identify stale findings and draft factual challenge questions. Authorized humans approve exceptions, resources, closure, and external statements. Review routinely, conduct an annual readiness challenge, and revisit after a material finding or regulatory change.
