# NIST SP 800-86 forensic-integration engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST guidance, legal advice, a law-enforcement determination, an investigative conclusion, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-86](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/86/final) and the organization’s approved incident-response, legal, privacy, records, and investigative direction.

## Engagement focus

Maintain a forensic-readiness capability that enables appropriate technical investigation of security incidents and operational issues without compromising legal, privacy, or business decision-making. Connect material systems and data sources to preservation procedures, evidence access, handling records, technical collection methods, analysis handoffs, case records, retention decisions, and communication boundaries. Treat technical evidence collection as distinct from legal privilege, law-enforcement activity, personnel action, regulatory reporting, and conclusions about responsibility.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, incident-command, security, platform, application, data, legal, privacy, records-management, human-resources, and service-owner roles. Operators maintain forensic-readiness procedures, source and access inventories, preservation and collection records, evidence-handling records, technical case artifacts, legal or privacy handoffs, retention approvals, exercise results, and remediation status. Review material evidence sources, access paths, and preservation assumptions quarterly; test representative forensic handoffs at least annually and after material system, data, provider, or legal-process changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples evidence-handling and exercise records; auditors test the evidence trail without conducting an investigation, directing legal strategy, reaching a culpability finding, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied case and readiness records, flag missing handling artifacts, trace documented evidence sources to owned systems, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot collect or alter evidence, make an investigative or legal finding, identify a person as responsible, determine disclosure duties, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.
