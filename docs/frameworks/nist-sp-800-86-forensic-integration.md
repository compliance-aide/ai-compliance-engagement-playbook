# NIST SP 800-86 forensic-integration engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST guidance, legal advice, a law-enforcement determination, an investigative conclusion, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-86](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/86/final) and the organization’s approved incident-response, legal, privacy, records, and investigative direction.

## Engagement focus

Maintain a forensic-readiness capability that enables appropriate technical investigation of security incidents and operational issues without compromising legal, privacy, or business decision-making. Connect material systems and data sources to preservation procedures, evidence access, handling records, technical collection methods, analysis handoffs, case records, retention decisions, and communication boundaries. Treat technical evidence collection as distinct from legal privilege, law-enforcement activity, personnel action, regulatory reporting, and conclusions about responsibility.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, incident-command, security, platform, application, data, legal, privacy, records-management, human-resources, and service-owner roles. Operators maintain forensic-readiness procedures, source and access inventories, preservation and collection records, evidence-handling records, technical case artifacts, legal or privacy handoffs, retention approvals, exercise results, and remediation status. Review material evidence sources, access paths, and preservation assumptions quarterly; test representative forensic handoffs at least annually and after material system, data, provider, or legal-process changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples evidence-handling and exercise records; auditors test the evidence trail without conducting an investigation, directing legal strategy, reaching a culpability finding, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied case and readiness records, flag missing handling artifacts, trace documented evidence sources to owned systems, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot collect or alter evidence, make an investigative or legal finding, identify a person as responsible, determine disclosure duties, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [NIST SP 800-86 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/86/final) and approved incident-response, legal, privacy, records, and investigative direction at engagement start; record the version retrieved and applicable use terms. This is original forensic-readiness planning, not reproduced NIST content, legal advice, or an investigative conclusion.

### 1. Evidence-source, authority, and readiness package

- **Request and owner:** Incident-command, security, platform, application, data, legal, privacy, records, and service owners provide material-system/source inventory, collection and preservation procedures, access-role records, ownership map, retention/hold references, approved tool/process boundaries, and readiness exercises.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one material evidence source to its system owner, authorized access path, preservation procedure, handling boundary, and current review date. This can show documented readiness; it cannot authorize collection, establish legal privilege, or prove all sources are available.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize source and ownership metadata and flag missing review or handoff records. Humans decide collection authority, legal/privacy boundaries, and readiness scope. Refresh after system, source, provider, data, access, legal-process, or retention change.

### 2. Preservation, collection, and chain-of-handling package

- **Request and owner:** Authorized incident, technical, legal, privacy, and records personnel provide case authorization or intake reference, preservation notices, evidence identifiers, custody/transfer logs, collection-method record, integrity checks, storage/access records, and exceptions.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample a closed or exercise case record from authorization through preservation, documented collection, custody transfers, integrity evidence, access restriction, and exception handling. This assesses record continuity; it cannot determine evidentiary admissibility, direct collection, or conclude what occurred.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may check supplied records for missing identifiers, dates, or owners and prepare redacted workpapers. It cannot collect, alter, classify, or disclose evidence. Authorized humans perform collection, preservation, access, and disclosure decisions. Recollect after a material process failure or evidence-handling exception.

### 3. Analysis handoff, retention, and improvement package

- **Request and owner:** Technical analysts, incident-command, legal, privacy, records, service, and independent-review owners provide approved analysis handoffs, factual technical findings, retention/disposition decisions, communication approvals, exercise lessons, corrective actions, and retest evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected technical observation to its evidence reference, analyst/handoff record, documented limitation, human decision point, and corrective follow-up. This cannot identify responsibility, determine reporting duties, or make a legal or compliance conclusion.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may assemble source-linked workpapers and flag unsupported transitions or overdue improvements. Humans determine findings, retention, disclosure, risk, and closure. Review quarterly and after incidents, exercises, legal/privacy changes, or material forensic-source changes.
