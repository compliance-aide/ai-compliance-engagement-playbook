# U.S. federal software-security memorandum readiness — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not federal policy text or procurement advice. Track the [OMB software-security update](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/M-23-16-Update-to-M-22-18-Enhancing-Software-Security.pdf) and agency-specific acquisition terms.

## Engagement focus

Maintain covered-offering scope, customer requirements, software lifecycle evidence, supplier and component records, exceptions, representation support, response owners, and date-sensitive change history.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Procurement, legal, security, and engineering owners interpret current customer requirements together; accountable people authorize responses; independent reviewers test traceability and response readiness. AI may track changes and evidence gaps, but cannot interpret terms, make representations, sign or submit, approve a supplier or release, or alter production systems. Review on requirement changes and annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [OMB M-23-16 update](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/M-23-16-Update-to-M-22-18-Enhancing-Software-Security.pdf), current solicitation/agency terms, and approved legal/procurement interpretation; checked 2026-07-31. This original plan is not procurement advice, an attestation, a representation, or a substitute for the request-specific terms.

### 1. Covered-offering and request interpretation package

- **Request and owner:** Procurement, legal, product, security, and engineering owners provide the request or contract reference, offered product/version list, delivery/hosting boundary, organizational role, customer-specific directions, responsible representative, and dated interpretation record.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one response package to the request, product/version boundary, accountable response owner, and documented human interpretation. This cannot interpret legal terms, decide coverage, or authorize a response.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved request and product metadata and flag conflicts in versions, owners, or dates. Humans determine applicability, response posture, and signer authority. Refresh for every request and material offering or term change.

### 2. Development, release, and supply-chain evidence package

- **Request and owner:** Engineering, release, security, and supplier owners provide process references, build/release provenance pointers, dependency/supplier records, security-test references, vulnerability/remediation records, release approvals, and authorized deviations.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample one offered release from source/build or supplier evidence through security/release record, documented deviation if any, and retained provenance. This tests a bounded chain; it cannot inspect all code or determine conformance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may create a read-only evidence inventory and flag missing owner, date, provenance, or exception references. Humans assess evidence, approve deviations, and authorize releases. Refresh after material code, build, dependency, supplier, vulnerability, or release change.

### 3. Representation-readiness and renewal package

- **Request and owner:** Legal, procurement, executive, security, and independent-review owners provide proposed response language, evidence cross-reference, exception analysis, decision/approval trail, unresolved-gap register, and annual reviewer workpapers.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one planned statement or exception to supporting evidence, authority, limitation, decision date, and action owner. This cannot bind the organization, sign, submit, or approve a supplier.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may draft discrepancy questions and flag stale evidence; it cannot make or transmit a representation. Authorized humans approve final language, signature, transmission, and risk decisions. Recheck before every response and annually.
