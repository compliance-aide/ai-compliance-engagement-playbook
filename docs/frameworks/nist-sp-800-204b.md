# NIST SP 800-204B ABAC service-mesh engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST ABAC deployment guidance, an authorization policy, a configuration authorization, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-204B](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/204/b/final) and the organization’s approved security, architecture, engineering, privacy, legal, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Operate a governed engagement for in-scope attribute-based access control in a service-mesh-supported microservices environment. Connect service, identity, attribute, and resource scope to approved authentication and authorization policy decisions, policy-enforcement dependencies, deployment and configuration evidence, access, monitoring, tests, supplier dependencies, changes, incidents, and retirement. A policy extract, architecture diagram, deployment record, test result, or audit log is evidence to assess; none alone proves access is authorized correctly, attributes are accurate, communication is protected, a deployment is safe, or compliance.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, security, architecture, platform and microservices engineering, identity and access-management operations, application and data owners, supplier-management, privacy and legal where relevant, change-management, incident-response, and records-management roles. Operators maintain scoped service, identity, attribute, and policy inventories, approved architecture and authorization decisions, enforcement and interface traceability, deployment and configuration baselines, access and audit evidence, monitoring and test records, supplier materials, exceptions, incidents, and retirement evidence. Reconcile services, identities, attributes, owners, policies, interfaces, and suppliers quarterly; review authorization failures, policy or configuration changes, exceptions, incidents, and unresolved risks at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material architecture, identity, platform, provider, threat, or publication changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples scope-to-policy-to-enforcement-to-review traceability; auditors test supplied evidence without granting access, changing authorization policy, deploying workloads, approving exceptions, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied inventory, architecture, policy, deployment, monitoring, and review evidence, identify missing owners or stale decisions, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot define or change authorization policy, grant or revoke access, deploy workloads, approve an exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.
