# NIST SP 800-215 enterprise-network landscape engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST network guidance, a network architecture, an authorization to implement, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-215](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/215/final) and the organization's approved security, architecture, privacy, legal, operational, and change decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round engagement for the evolving enterprise network rather than treating network security as a perimeter-only exercise. Maintain the relationships among users, devices, applications, data, on-premises and cloud resources, network paths, trust boundaries, protective services, managed providers, administrative planes, monitoring, and recovery dependencies. Document why each material connection exists, who owns it, how it is governed, and how changes are evaluated before use.

Assess architecture and operational evidence across distributed and cloud-connected environments without assuming that a product deployment or diagram proves the intended protections. A topology, configuration export, service-provider report, alert sample, test record, or change ticket is evidence to evaluate in context. Preserve findings and decisions about segmentation, remote access, service integration, protective-service coverage, exceptions, incidents, and corrective work so reviewers can follow the operating story over time.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Security, network, cloud, architecture, application, endpoint, identity, privacy, supplier, change, incident-response, continuity, and records owners establish scope and retain accountable decisions. Operators maintain current inventories and diagrams, connection and trust-boundary records, approved patterns, configuration baselines, monitoring evidence, provider dependencies, test results, exceptions, incident records, and recovery evidence. Reconcile material network paths, services, assets, owners, and suppliers at least quarterly; review significant configuration, threat, alert, and incident changes on the same cadence; and conduct management review annually and after material architecture or provider changes.

Independent reviewers sample a path from approved business need through architecture, configuration, observed operation, and corrective action. Auditors examine supplied evidence without changing network or cloud settings, approving architecture, granting access, accepting risk, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize architecture and evidence records, identify missing ownership or stale baselines, compare approved patterns with supplied records, draft workpapers, and prepare questions. AI cannot design or approve network architecture, change configurations, grant access, authorize releases, select providers, accept risk, make legal conclusions, or attest to conformance.


## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** At engagement start, retain the retrieved version and applicable use terms for the official [NIST SP 800-215 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/215/final), together with approved architecture, security, privacy, supplier, and operational decisions. This is original evidence-planning guidance, not reproduced NIST text, an architecture approval, or a conformance conclusion.

### 1. Enterprise-network landscape and trust-boundary package

- **Request and owner:** Network, cloud, architecture, identity, application, data, security, and service owners provide the current in-scope service and asset inventory, logical and physical path diagrams, trust-boundary record, administrative-plane map, data-flow record, approved patterns, and named ownership for material connections.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one material business flow from its stated purpose through participating services, identity and administration paths, network boundaries, data handling, and accountable owners. This can evaluate whether supplied landscape records connect; it cannot prove every route is known, safe, or correctly configured.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize supplied diagrams and inventories and flag conflicting owners, absent dependencies, or stale records. Humans approve architecture, data boundaries, and scope. Refresh after a new connection, cloud/service-provider change, material application migration, network redesign, or change to administrative access.

### 2. Protective-service, configuration, and operating-observation package

- **Request and owner:** Network, security operations, cloud, identity, endpoint, platform, and service owners provide approved configuration baselines, relevant exports, change records, segmentation or remote-access evidence, protective-service coverage records, monitoring samples, alert investigations, and documented exceptions.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample one path or protective service from approved design and configuration/change record through a relevant operational signal, investigation, and exception or corrective action where applicable. This can support a bounded evidence observation; it cannot change settings, demonstrate continuous effectiveness, or prove that no unauthorized path exists.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare supplied records to approved baselines and identify missing change, alert, or exception links. Humans configure services, investigate events, approve changes, and authorize exceptions. Recollect after a material configuration change, protection failure, alert trend, remote-access change, or significant incident.

### 3. Dependency, resilience, and corrective-action governance package

- **Request and owner:** Architecture, network, cloud, supplier, continuity, incident-response, risk, records, and independent-review owners provide provider/dependency records, recovery and exercise evidence, incident follow-up, exception approvals, remediation register, retest results, and management decisions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected dependency disruption, recovery exercise, exception, or corrective action to the responsible human authority, documented limitation, target date, follow-up evidence, and current disposition. This can assess governance of supplied work; it cannot accept risk, assure resilience, or attest to network security.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag stale supplier assumptions, overdue actions, and unlinked recovery evidence and prepare questions for review. Humans approve risk treatment, recovery decisions, exception renewal, closure, and external statements. Review quarterly and after a supplier, incident, exercise, architecture, or recovery-capability change.
