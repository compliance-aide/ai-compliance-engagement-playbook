# Singapore MAS Technology Risk Management — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not MAS content or a regulatory conclusion. Confirm current guidance through the [MAS Technology Risk Management Guidelines](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/guidelines/technology-risk-management-guidelines).

## Engagement focus

Maintain technology inventory, governance decisions, risk and resilience evidence, service-provider records, cyber operations, change controls, incident decisions, testing results, exceptions, and management reporting.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Financial-institution leaders and accountable risk owners decide technology and regulatory actions; independent reviewers test evidence. AI correlates approved records and highlights gaps, but cannot determine compliance, make notification decisions, or submit returns. Review quarterly and annually.


## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [MAS Technology Risk Management Guidelines](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/guidelines/technology-risk-management-guidelines), checked 2026-07-31. Confirm the current version, institution-specific applicability, and supervisory expectations with accountable financial-institution and legal/regulatory owners. This original plan is not MAS guidance, a regulatory return, or a compliance conclusion.

### 1. Technology estate, governance, and risk-decision package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain the approved technology/service inventory, criticality and dependency records, governance and risk-committee decisions, risk register, ownership assignments, and change calendar from technology, risk, security, and business-service owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected critical service to its business owner, technology dependency, risk decision, review date, and any unresolved issue. This supports a risk-aware engagement boundary; it cannot set risk appetite, classify regulatory significance, or prove inventory completeness.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved inventories and identify gaps in owners, dates, or links. Accountable management decides risk treatment and scope. Trigger on a material service, outsourcing, architecture, or risk-profile change and on periodic governance review.

### 2. Resilience, cyber-operation, and third-party package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain authorized policy/procedure references, access/change/monitoring evidence, resilience and recovery exercise records, incident and problem records, outsourcing/service-provider accountability evidence, exceptions, and remediation ownership from operations, security, resilience, and procurement owners.
- **Validate and limit:** For a selected service or event, inspect the chain from approved operating reference through time-bounded evidence, supplier allocation, exception, and corrective-action follow-up. This supports evidence traceability; it cannot determine adequacy, operating effectiveness, or a MAS notification obligation.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may build a read-only evidence index and flag missing periods or dependencies; it may not modify production records, decide outsourcing risk, or initiate notifications. Human owners decide actions. Trigger on an outage, incident, exercise finding, supplier change, or control failure.

### 3. Management challenge and regulatory-readiness package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain management reports, independent challenge records, findings, corrective-action plans, risk acceptances, retest references, source-change review, and authorized regulatory-communication approvals from risk, assurance, legal, and executive owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected finding to source evidence, challenger, accountable action owner, approval, target date, and retest or closure rationale. This supports governance readiness; it cannot certify compliance, accept risk, or communicate with MAS.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may produce a source-linked action register and flag stale decisions. Independent reviewers challenge evidence; authorized humans approve closure and communications. Review quarterly and after a material supervisory, service, or source change.
