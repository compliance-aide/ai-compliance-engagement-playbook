# NIST SP 800-185 SHA-3-derived-functions engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST function specifications, a cryptographic design, an approval, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-185](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/185/final) and the organization’s approved cryptography, security, architecture, legal, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Operate a governed lifecycle for in-scope uses of SHA-3-derived functions, including documented purpose and scope, approved design and implementation decisions, consuming systems and data flows, keyed-material dependencies where relevant, parameter and interface dependencies, test and validation evidence, access, logging, supplier dependencies, changes, incidents, transition planning, and retirement. Connect each use to accountable ownership and supporting evidence. A configuration extract, implementation record, supplier statement, test result, or audit log is evidence to assess; none alone proves suitable function selection, correct implementation, protected material, approved use, secure operation, or compliance.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, security, cryptography or platform engineering, architecture, application and data owners, key-management operations where relevant, supplier-management, legal and privacy where relevant, change-management, incident-response, and records-management roles. Operators maintain scoped use inventories, approved design and parameter decisions, dependency and interface records, implementation and configuration baselines, validation and monitoring evidence, access and audit records, supplier materials, exception and incident records, transition decisions, and retirement evidence. Reconcile uses, owners, dependencies, integrations, and suppliers quarterly; review implementation alerts, material changes, exceptions, incidents, transition needs, and unresolved risks at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material cryptographic, product, provider, threat, or publication changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples purpose-to-implementation-to-operation-to-review traceability; auditors test supplied evidence without selecting functions or parameters, accessing protected material, changing configuration, approving exceptions, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied inventory, design, implementation, test, supplier, and review evidence, identify missing owners or stale decisions, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot select cryptographic functions or parameters, access protected material, change configuration, approve an exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** At engagement start, retain the retrieved version and applicable use terms for the official [NIST SP 800-185 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/185/final), together with approved cryptographic standards and local architecture decisions. These packages are original planning guidance, not reproduced function specifications, cryptographic design advice, or an approval of a function, parameter, or implementation.

### 1. In-scope use and design-decision package

- **Request and owner:** Cryptography or platform engineering, architecture, application, data, and security owners provide an inventory of in-scope SHA-3-derived-function uses, their stated purpose, consuming services and interfaces, approved design/parameter decision references, data classification, named owners, and exceptions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected use from stated business or security purpose through consuming application and interface, approved decision reference, owner, and exception status. This can assess whether use records are traceable; it cannot select a function or parameter, validate algorithmic correctness, or determine appropriateness for a use case.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare supplied inventories and flag unmatched owners, interfaces, or decision dates. Humans select cryptographic approaches and approve scope and exceptions. Refresh after a new use, material integration, parameter decision, product change, or publication change.

### 2. Implementation, protected-material, and supplier package

- **Request and owner:** Platform, application, security, key-management where relevant, supplier, and change owners provide implementation/version baselines, build and deployment provenance, protected-material handling boundary where applicable, access-role records, supplier component records, interface contracts, and approved change history.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one selected implementation from approved baseline through build/deployment record, consuming interface, access boundary, supplier component where relevant, and approved change. This can test a bounded provenance trail; it cannot expose protected material, prove secure storage, or establish that implementation behavior is correct.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index supplied metadata and flag a missing version, owner, or change reference; it must not access keys, secrets, or protected material. Humans approve configuration and supplier treatment. Recollect after a library, build, hosting, access, supplier, or configuration change.

### 3. Validation, transition, and lifecycle-review package

- **Request and owner:** Cryptography/platform engineering, security, application, operations, incident, records, risk, and management owners provide authorized validation/test records, monitoring and discrepancy records, incident handoffs, transition/retirement decisions, remediation/retest evidence, and annual management review.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected validation outcome or lifecycle issue through named reviewer, discrepancy or limitation, accountable remediation, retest or documented rationale, and transition decision where applicable. This can support a reviewable lifecycle record; it cannot certify conformance, guarantee resistance to attack, or accept residual risk.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize outcomes and flag overdue lifecycle actions. Humans interpret results, approve transition or retirement, close exceptions, and accept risk. Review quarterly and after a validation failure, vulnerability, incident, supplier change, cryptographic transition, or publication change.
