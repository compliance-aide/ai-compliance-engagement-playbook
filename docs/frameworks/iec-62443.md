# IEC 62443 — industrial cybersecurity engagement guide

> Original operational guidance for coordinating an engagement, not IEC/ISA text or a conformity conclusion.

## Source and applicability

Use the individual publisher records linked by the part-specific guides below. The [ISA series page](https://www.isa.org/standards-and-publications/isa-standards/isa-iec-62443-series-of-standards), read 2026-09-04, describes a family of standards with different stakeholder roles. Its displayed notice restricts AI use of ISA intellectual property. Obtain explicit permission before processing restricted materials; viewing or buying a standard does not establish AI-processing rights.

Full licensed requirements, edition equivalence, certification scheme rules and source-use permissions remain unverified for this draft. Record the exact publisher, part, edition, corrections and contractual basis. Do not treat a generic request for “IEC 62443” as an agreed assessment scope.

## Engagement focus

Route each obligation to the right owner, system boundary and lifecycle stage, then reconcile the resulting evidence and decisions. This is a coordination workflow; detailed requirements must come from authorized sources and qualified reviewers.

## Roles

The sponsor approves intended outcomes. Asset owners, operators, integrators, service providers and product developers identify their actual responsibilities. Safety and operations authorities control industrial activity. An independent reviewer challenges gaps and unsupported assurance claims. AI may organize permitted records and draft workpapers; it cannot select binding requirements, set security levels, accept risk, change OT, authorize testing or certify.

## Before starting

Record the business request, parties, sites, products, services, operating modes, assets, connections and desired assurance outcome. Obtain applicable contracts and approved source access. Establish owner contacts, evidence permissions and escalation routes. Default to existing records; no active discovery, remote access or plant change is authorized by this guide.

## Ordered workflow

1. **Clarify the requested outcome.** Separate operational improvement, design review, product evaluation, service-provider assessment and formal certification. Record the exact claim the sponsor wants supported and its intended recipient.
2. **Assign roles and parts.** Use the routing table as a starting point, then have qualified owners verify applicable requirements and editions. One organization may hold several roles; document each separately rather than applying only its preferred part.
3. **Reconcile the full boundary.** Inventory all scoped assets, zones/conduits where defined, interfaces, products, services and suppliers. Assign each dependency to an accountable party. Resolve mismatches between contracts, drawings and delivered systems.
4. **Plan linked workstreams.** Define each part-specific objective, evidence source, owner, method, approval and handoff. Link design assumptions to implementation and operating responsibilities. Record unsupported assumptions and source gaps explicitly.
5. **Evaluate permitted evidence.** Follow the relevant guide and authorized method. Preserve actual configuration, test conditions, failures and limitations. Do not extrapolate supplier certificates to an entire installation or convert a target into achieved behavior.
6. **Resolve cross-party gaps.** Assign findings and dependency failures to named owners, obtain change/risk decisions and retest affected outcomes. Preserve all impacted workstreams when one shared service fails. Approval of an exception does not prove technical effectiveness.
7. **Issue a bounded handoff.** Reconcile every scoped obligation and unresolved item. Obtain authorized review of external wording against actual artifacts and scheme scope. Keep preparation, assessment, certification and deployment as separate states.

### 2. Dependency and evidence register

Maintain complete scoped accounting for parties, assets, interfaces, requirements, evidence and findings. Preserve all material supplied for assessment without silent trimming or sampling. Record actual examination coverage separately from inventory completeness. Link every claimed inherited capability to its exact scope, conditions and remaining customer responsibilities.

### 3. Decision and handoff record

Keep approved methods, safety/operational constraints, risk decisions, changes, retests and recipient acknowledgments. Distinguish a document received from a verified technical outcome. Reconcile unresolved supplier and customer actions before declaring a workstream complete.

## Failure branches and decisions

- Request names only the series: obtain a specific role, part, edition and desired claim before detailed evaluation.
- Licensed input lacks processing permission: stop restricted ingestion and continue permitted metadata organization.
- Two parties each assign a responsibility to the other: escalate the ownership gap; do not mark it inherited.
- Certificate covers only a product or process: restrict the claim to that artifact and separately evaluate system and operational obligations.
- Proposed verification threatens operations: stop and obtain the required safety/operations decision.

Use [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md) assertions: supported, not_supported, inconclusive, not_applicable and not_tested. These are evidence statuses, not security levels, maturity ratings or certification results.

## Cadence and renewal

Set coordinated reviews using approved obligations and risk. Reopen affected work after changes in systems, suppliers, versions, connections, incidents or source requirements. Do not invent a single annual certification cycle for the whole series.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver the role/part/edition register, reconciled boundary, linked evidence, claim limits and open actions with owners and dates. Independent source, skeptical and rights review remain pending. No live industrial activity or conformity assessment occurred in preparing this draft.

Fictional author desk case: a vendor supplies a component certificate, while the buyer asks whether the whole plant is certified. The artifact can support only its stated component scope. Record system integration and asset-owner program coverage as unresolved rather than extending the certificate.
