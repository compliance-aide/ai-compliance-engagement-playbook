# NIST Privacy Framework 1.0 — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance for privacy-risk management, not a legal
> determination or an authorization to process or disclose personal data.

## Source and applicability

Use the [NIST Privacy Framework program page](https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework)
and [Privacy Framework 1.0](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.CSWP.01162020).
Checked 2026-09-04: the program page links 1.0 and labels 1.1 as an Initial
Public Draft. Confirm status at engagement start; do not silently migrate to a
draft. Version 1.0 supports Current and Target Profiles for managing privacy
risk. Using the framework does not settle applicable law or individual rights.

Rights: original instructions and links; no imported tables or mappings. See
[NIST's rights notice](https://www.nist.gov/copyrights-disclaimers) and the
[refresh record](../refresh-reviews/nist-privacy-framework-1.md). Independent
publication review remains pending.

## Engagement focus

Create a traceable picture of data processing and its possible effects on people,
then connect approved privacy objectives to operating evidence and improvement
work. Include risks from authorized processing, not only unauthorized access or
breaches. Keep legal obligations in a separately approved legal register.

## Roles

Business/data owners describe processing and operate approved practices. Privacy
leadership owns risk priorities; counsel resolves legal questions; custodians
control evidence access. Independent reviewers test assertions without approving
their own work. AI may reconcile metadata and draft profiles, workpapers and
questions. It cannot choose a lawful basis, decide an individual's request,
approve a transfer, accept risk or ingest data outside the approved boundary.

## Before starting

Obtain the charter; selected edition; processing/system inventory; purposes and
data categories; affected groups; vendors/recipients; retention decisions;
applicable-law register; risk owner; and reviewer. Draft missing records for
owner review. Do not request raw personal data when metadata or a redacted
reference suffices. Follow the [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md).

## Ordered workflow

| Step | Action and owner | Output and exit check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Define the processing boundary | Business/privacy owners identify products, processes, people affected, organizational roles and legal-review dependencies. | Approved boundary and decision owners; unsettled legal scope is recorded for counsel. |
| 2. Reconcile the data lifecycle | AI traces approved metadata from collection through use, sharing, retention and disposal; owners confirm purpose and dependencies. | Versioned processing map with custodians, recipient roles and unresolved flows. Unknown flows are gaps, not exclusions. |
| 3. Record risks to people | Privacy and domain owners consider possible adverse effects and existing treatments. AI drafts scenarios and evidence requests without determining legal outcomes. | Reviewed risk record tied to processing activities, affected groups and uncertainty. |
| 4. Build Current and Target Profiles | AI assembles current assertions from evidence and separately drafts desired improvements. The privacy risk owner approves priorities and profile scope. | Comparable profiles with source references and decisions; desired practices are not marked operational. |
| 5. Test selected practices | Independent reviewers approve methods and selection. Authorized operators demonstrate workflows with fictional or appropriately protected records. | Workpapers trace the same process, period and assertion; results state coverage limits. |
| 6. Improve and retest | Owners propose and perform approved corrections. Reviewers retest; responsible humans decide unresolved risk and legal questions. | Correction evidence, retest result and authorized disposition; no closure based only on a revised policy. |
| 7. Maintain and renew | Privacy owners review source, purpose, product, supplier and processing changes. AI creates affected-record refresh tasks. | Updated map/profiles, open questions, next checks and retained prior versions. |

The execution order is an original planning aid. Profiles may be developed
iteratively; the owner can establish the target first when appropriate.

## Evidence and test plan

| Request and custodian | Check | Expected observation | Failure or limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Processing/data-flow inventory from business and technology | Trace a reviewer-selected activity across source, use, recipients and retention records. | Records agree on the same activity, owners and data categories. | Unobserved transfers or missing systems leave coverage inconclusive. |
| Notices and approved purpose records from privacy | Compare the approved notice version with the documented activity and change history. | Statements and approved operating records are consistent for the period. | Consistency does not establish legal adequacy. |
| Request/retention workflow records from privacy and operations | Trace a fictional or authorized redacted case through intake, approved decision, execution and readback. | The documented handoff and decision trail can be demonstrated. | AI cannot decide identity, exemptions, legal deadlines or deletion authority. |
| Supplier/event records from supplier and privacy owners | Follow a material change or event to impact review, accountable decision and follow-up. | Affected processing records and actions were updated with evidence. | A contract or questionnaire alone does not prove supplier performance. |

## Failure branches and decisions

If requested evidence contains sensitive records outside the authorization, use
approved references and name the access limit. If actual processing conflicts
with the inventory or notice, preserve both records and open a review item.
Potential reportable events go immediately to the approved incident/privacy
route; do not infer a notification deadline from this voluntary framework.
A security test pass does not resolve all privacy risks. Use the runbook's result
labels and keep unresolved legal decisions out of automated conclusions.

## Cadence and renewal

The owner selects risk-based monitoring; monthly change triage, quarterly
improvement review and annual profile review are planning defaults, not NIST or
statutory deadlines. Reassess before material purpose, recipient, retention,
product or supplier changes and after complaints, incidents or source updates.
Apply separately verified legal deadlines through the responsible authority.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver the processing map, privacy-risk record, Current/Target Profiles, evidence
index, workpapers, decisions, remediation/retest history and change queue. List
unknown flows and untested practices explicitly. No legal or privacy assurance
claim follows merely from completing these records.
