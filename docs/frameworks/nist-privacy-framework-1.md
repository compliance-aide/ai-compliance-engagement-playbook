# NIST Privacy Framework 1.0 — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not legal advice or a privacy conclusion. Check
> the [NIST Privacy Framework](https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework/privacy-framework)
> alongside applicable law and qualified review.

## Engagement focus

Map personal-data categories, purposes, decision points, rights and expectations,
owners, and legal-review boundaries. Identify privacy-risk scenarios as effects
on people; record material processing and vendor changes; review requests,
complaints, supplier evidence, training, and incidents; and maintain a current
improvement portfolio.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Operators maintain accurate processing records and escalate unusual activity.
Independent reviewers check that evidence supports stated decisions and impact consideration.
AI may normalize authorized inventories, find undocumented flow changes, and draft clear
questions; it cannot make legal determinations, answer rights requests alone,
approve data uses, or ingest sensitive data outside approved boundaries. Review monthly changes,
quarterly effectiveness, and annual governance.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [NIST Privacy Framework](https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework/privacy-framework) together with applicable laws and qualified privacy review; reviewed 2026-07-31. This plan organizes evidence for privacy-risk management and does not decide legal applicability, lawful processing, or an individual's request outcome.

**Plan status:** Independently reviewed; see the [review receipt](../evidence-plan-reviews/nist-privacy-framework-1.md).

### 1. Data-processing and privacy-risk map

- **Request and owner:** An approved inventory of personal-data categories, purposes, systems, data flows, recipients, retention points, and affected people, plus privacy-risk scenarios and accountable owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected processing entry to a system/data-flow source and responsible owner, documenting unknowns and access constraints. This can support a current risk-management view; it cannot prove legal accuracy, completeness of all flows, or a lawful purpose.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may normalize authorized metadata and identify inconsistent flow records. Privacy/legal humans approve purpose and risk decisions. Refresh for new data, purpose, vendor, transfer, product feature, or rights channel.

### 2. Communication, rights, and data-management evidence

- **Request and owner:** Notice/version records, rights-request workflow metrics, retention/deletion evidence, data-quality or minimization decisions, training material, and complaint/escalation records from privacy and operational owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Independently trace a selected request or disposal event to source records, timing, responsible party, and escalation while redacting personal data. This can support that a process operates with recorded artifacts; it cannot determine the correct individual disposition or legal deadline.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may create a redacted evidence index and flag stale materials; it may not decide a request, identity proof, exemption, or data-use approval. Refresh for policy, process, complaint, or regulatory change.

### 3. Supplier, incident, and improvement record

- **Request and owner:** Vendor data-handling inventory, responsibility/change records, privacy-event or exercise records, remediation register, and governance-review decisions from vendor, security, and privacy owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected supplier or event to source provenance, decision authority, and corrective action. This can support accountable improvement tracking; it cannot prove a supplier's privacy performance or determine incident-reporting duties.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag missing reviews and prepare traceability. Humans approve suppliers, risk treatment, notifications, and closure. Refresh on a supplier/incident change and at quarterly and annual reviews.
