# CISA Cross-Sector Cybersecurity Performance Goals — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not CISA certification or endorsement. Check
> the current [CISA CPG source](https://www.cisa.gov/cybersecurity-performance-goals)
> and record the version retrieved at engagement start.

## Engagement focus

Define business and operational scope, executive ownership, risk appetite, and
the voluntary-use purpose. Build a risk-ranked improvement backlog from factual
service, recovery, access, and exposure evidence; execute with owners and
dates; and independently validate claims quarterly.

## Roles

Operators describe current reality and retain evidence. Independent reviewers
sample claims and test whether exceptions remain acceptable. AI may keep the source
register, evidence requests, and stale-claim log and draft risk narratives, but
cannot decide risk, accept an exception, alter systems, or make a certification or endorsement claim. Re-rank the roadmap annually and after material change.

## Ordered workflow

| Step | Action and owner | Output and exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Confirm scope and version | Sponsor and security lead approve improvement purpose and source edition. | Scope/source register with current-versus-prior changes and unresolved interpretations. |
| 2. Establish coverage | Owners reconcile services and dependencies to the complete adopted goal set. | Each goal has owner, applicability decision or open question, evidence need and affected services. Unfunded work stays visible. |
| 3. Assess current reality | Operators supply dated configuration and operating records; reviewer checks criteria and provenance. | Supported, failed and unassessed observations kept distinct. A policy alone cannot prove operation. |
| 4. Prioritize dependencies | Sponsor/security lead compare risk reduction, prerequisites, operational impact and resources. | Ordered backlog with measurable outcomes and owners. AI drafts options; accountable people choose tradeoffs. |
| 5. Implement safely | Service owners execute authorized work after approved QA and recovery planning, including OT safety review where relevant. | Actual change receipt and acceptance observations. Failed checks stop expansion. |
| 6. Verify outcome | Reviewer compares resulting operation with agreed criteria and affected population. | Retest evidence and remaining limitations. A backup job alone does not prove service recovery. |
| 7. Resolve gaps | Risk authority reviews deviations, dates and residual risk; owners remediate. | Explicit decisions and owned next actions; expired exceptions do not renew automatically. |
| 8. Renew roadmap | Independent reviewer challenges progress; sponsor reviews priorities. | Handoff with source versions, evidence, open work and next triggers. No CISA certification implied. |

## Evidence and test plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [CISA Cybersecurity Performance Goals page](https://www.cisa.gov/cybersecurity-performance-goals), record the version retrieved, and preserve its applicable terms; prior locator snapshot 2026-07-31; detailed current-goal review pending. This plan is original operational guidance for voluntary improvement, not an endorsement, certification, or reproduction of CISA material.

### 1. Scope, ownership, and priority record

- **Request and owner:** Executive sponsor and security owner provide business/service boundary, mission dependencies, accountable owners, risk criteria, current-state claims, and risk-ranked improvement backlog.
- **Validate and limit:** Independently trace selected priority items to a named service, factual evidence, owner, decision date, and planned outcome. This can establish whether the roadmap is evidence-linked; it cannot decide organizational risk appetite or completeness.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize source versions, evidence requests, and changed-service alerts. Executives and security humans set priorities and accept exceptions. Refresh after a material service, ownership, threat, or recovery dependency change.

### 2. Essential cyber-operation and recovery evidence

- **Request and owner:** IT and security operators provide identity/access records, exposed-service or vulnerability records, backup/recovery test results, logging/alerting evidence, incident records, and remediation tracking relevant to selected goals.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace sampled claims through authoritative records, timing, responsible owner, and a retest or exercise result where applicable. This supports a limited evidence observation; it cannot establish that every system is secure or recoverable.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may identify stale claims and prepare test questions from approved artifacts; it cannot change systems, decide an incident result, or close remediation. Recollect after a material incident, significant exposure, failed exercise, or system change.

### 3. Exception, progress, and independent-challenge record

- **Request and owner:** Program owner supplies deviations, compensating actions, due dates, resource decisions, status reports, independent review notes, and annual roadmap re-ranking evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample exceptions and completed backlog items for human authority, measurable evidence, remaining limitation, due date, and independent challenge. This can show governance discipline; it cannot accept risk or certify progress.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag overdue actions and assemble a redacted review packet. Humans approve exceptions, resource tradeoffs, and external statements. Review quarterly and re-rank annually and after material change.

## Failure branches and decisions

Missing source criteria hold affected conclusions while authorized inventory work continues. Missing evidence stays unassessed. If a claimed improvement reflects scope reduction, reconcile the population before crediting progress. Preserve all scorer-bound evidence without trimming, sampling or capping; any approved inspection selection must state its population and limits. Failed service/safety checks invoke the approved recovery path. On interruption, preserve source versions, completed observations, unresolved decisions and next safe action.

## Cadence and renewal

Quarterly validation and annual prioritization are proposed management rhythms, not universal CISA mandates. Apply adopted goal-specific operating frequencies and binding obligations separately. Reopen affected goals after source, incident, service, recovery, supplier or safety changes.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver source and scope records, complete adopted-goal register, prioritized backlog, implementation/retest evidence, exceptions and next review dates. Keep implementation, observed effectiveness and remaining uncertainty distinct. Independent source and skeptical review and named human approval remain required before final conclusions or external claims.
