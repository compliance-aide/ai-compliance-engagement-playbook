# UK NCSC 10 Steps to Cyber Security engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NCSC guidance, a technical design approval, a risk decision, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through the [NCSC 10 Steps to Cyber Security](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/10-steps) and the organization’s approved security, legal, privacy, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Maintain a coherent cyber-security program that connects risk management, people engagement, asset knowledge, secure architecture, vulnerability management, identity, data security, monitoring, incident management, and supply-chain security to owned services and evidence. Use the 10 Steps as a management framework rather than as a checklist that proves protection. Preserve management’s responsibility for prioritization, technical design, risk acceptance, and incident decisions.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, security, technology, asset, identity, data, platform, application, incident, business-continuity, procurement, supplier-management, legal, privacy, and service-owner roles. Operators maintain risk and asset records, training evidence, architecture and configuration artifacts, vulnerability and access records, data-protection evidence, monitoring records, incident artifacts, supplier evidence, exceptions, and improvement status. Review material risk, asset, access, data, monitoring, incident, and supplier assumptions quarterly; exercise representative scenarios at least annually and after material service, architecture, supplier, data, or threat changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples evidence across the program; auditors test the evidence trail without designing safeguards, accepting risk, directing incidents, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied evidence, identify stale ownership or missing review artifacts, trace program records to owned services, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot approve technical design, alter systems, accept risk, direct incident response, determine legal obligations, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** At engagement start, retain the retrieval date, current-status check, and applicable use terms for the official [NCSC 10 Steps to Cyber Security collection](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/10-steps). Source status is current when that official collection remains available; this guide uses original planning language and does not reproduce NCSC material. A named human confirms applicability and any local obligations.

### 1. Programme ownership, risk, and service-boundary package

- **Request and owner:** Executive, security, risk, service, asset, architecture, privacy, and legal owners provide the approved service inventory, accountable-owner map, risk and exception register, material data/dependency map, security objectives, and management-review record for the selected period.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one material service from ownership and asset facts through a recorded risk decision, planned treatment, and management review; inspect that evidence access is restricted to the engagement. This supports an observation about supplied governance traceability, not proof that all risks, services, or legal duties are identified or managed.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize supplied records and flag stale ownership or missing decision links. Humans define scope, approve design and treatment, and accept risk. Refresh after a material service, supplier, architecture, data, or threat change and at annual renewal.

### 2. Preventive-control and operational-assurance package

- **Request and owner:** Technology, identity, data, platform, application, vulnerability, security-operations, and training owners provide approved standards, configuration/change records, access-review evidence, patch or vulnerability workflow records, data-protection evidence, monitoring coverage records, and workforce-engagement artifacts.
- **Validate and limit:** Select an in-scope service and trace a preventive or detection activity from approved expectation through implementation evidence and a time-bounded operating record, using read-only access or redacted exports where necessary. This can test the supplied evidence chain; it cannot establish continuous effectiveness, safe configuration, or complete technical coverage.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare supplied inventories and identify missing review dates or evidence links. Humans authorize technical changes, resolve vulnerabilities, and decide whether evidence is sufficient. Recollect after a material configuration, identity, data-handling, vulnerability, or monitoring change.

### 3. Incident, recovery, and supplier-resilience package

- **Request and owner:** Incident, continuity, procurement, supplier, communications, legal, privacy, and service owners provide playbooks, exercised scenarios, incident/corrective-action records, recovery dependencies, supplier assurance and escalation evidence, exceptions, and lessons-learned decisions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one exercised scenario, incident, or supplier disruption from detection/escalation through responsible decision, recovery or remediation evidence, and follow-up. This can support a readiness observation; it cannot direct a live incident, validate every supplier assertion, or prove resilience against future events.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may draft timelines and flag unresolved actions. Humans direct incidents, approve communications, invoke recovery, accept exceptions, and decide supplier actions. Review after an incident, exercise, supplier change, or continuity-impacting service change.
