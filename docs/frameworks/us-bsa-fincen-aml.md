# U.S. BSA / FinCEN AML program — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not legal advice or a filing decision. Check the [FinCEN BSA resource](https://www.fincen.gov/resources/statutes-and-regulations/bank-secrecy-act).

## Engagement focus

Maintain entity, product, geography, regulator, risk, program, data, case, filing/record calendar, training, third-party, exception, and remediation evidence.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Humans decide customer disposition, SAR-related judgment, filings, and remediation; independent reviewers test program evidence. AI performs read-only quality checks, but cannot make customer or filing decisions. Review quarterly and annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the current [FinCEN BSA resource](https://www.fincen.gov/resources/statutes-and-regulations/bank-secrecy-act) and institution-specific regulator guidance; checked 2026-07-31. This original plan is not legal advice, a SAR narrative, a filing decision, or a conclusion about program adequacy. Do not place customer-sensitive information, SAR information, or filing details in AI workpapers without authorized controls.

### 1. Covered-activity, risk, and program-governance record

- **Request and owner:** Legal-entity and product/service inventory, jurisdiction and delivery-channel inputs, approved AML risk assessment/method, designated-responsibility records, board or senior-management oversight evidence, and policy governance records from compliance leadership.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected risk factor to its source input, accountable owner, approved program decision, and review date. This can support a bounded program-planning record; it cannot determine coverage obligations, validate risk ratings, or establish effectiveness.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile non-sensitive metadata and flag unowned or stale assessments. Authorized compliance and governance humans approve risk method and program decisions. Refresh after a new product, geography, entity, regulatory change, or material risk event.

### 2. Monitoring, investigation, and record-retention evidence

- **Request and owner:** Approved monitoring configuration/change records, alert/case workflow metrics, investigation quality-control outputs, record-retention calendar, and access-controlled evidence lineage from AML operations, technology, and records owners.
- **Validate and limit:** In an authorized secure environment, trace a human-selected anonymized or access-controlled case sample to source, review steps, escalation, date, and retention record. This can support evidence traceability for selected workflow activity; it cannot decide customer disposition, establish complete detection, or make a SAR/filing judgment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may perform read-only completeness checks on approved metadata and flag late or missing handoffs. Humans approve investigations and all customer or filing actions. Recollect after model/rule changes, material alerts, system changes, or retention-cycle events.

### 3. Independent-testing, training, and remediation record

- **Request and owner:** Independent-test plan/results, issue/remediation log, role-based training completion and content approvals, third-party oversight records, management responses, and retest evidence from compliance, HR, and independent-review owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected finding to independence record, owner, root-cause analysis, corrective action, due date, and retest. This can support accountable program improvement; it cannot replace required independent testing, accept risk, or approve remediation closure.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may assemble a restricted evidence index and identify stale actions. Compliance/governance humans approve training, risk decisions, remediation closure, and external communications. Review quarterly, annually, and after material finding or program change.
