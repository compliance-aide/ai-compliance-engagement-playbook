# NIST SP 800-57 Part 2 Revision 1 key-management-organization engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST key-management content, a key-management policy, an approval, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-57 Part 2 Revision 1](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/57/pt2/r1/final) and the organization’s approved cryptography, security, architecture, legal, procurement, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Operate a governed organizational key-management engagement that connects accountability, policy, planning, specifications, practice statements, key-management services, technical dependencies, supplier arrangements, operations, incidents, change, and evidence retention. Maintain traceability from organization-level decisions through service and system execution. A policy, planning record, architecture diagram, supplier statement, configuration extract, test result, or audit log is evidence to assess; none alone proves effective key management, secure operation, approved use, or compliance.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, security, cryptography or platform engineering, architecture, key-management operations, identity, application and data owners, procurement, supplier-management, legal and privacy where relevant, change-management, incident-response, and records-management roles. Operators maintain approved policy and practice records, scope and service inventories, key-management plans and specifications, ownership and interface traceability, implementation and configuration baselines, access and audit evidence, supplier materials, exceptions, incidents, review decisions, and retirement evidence. Reconcile services, owners, dependencies, interfaces, and suppliers quarterly; review key-management operations, policy or practice changes, exceptions, incidents, and unresolved risks at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material organization, system, provider, cryptographic, threat, or publication changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples policy-to-practice-to-operation-to-review traceability; auditors test supplied evidence without accessing keys, operating services, changing configuration, approving exceptions, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied policy, planning, inventory, implementation, supplier, and review evidence, identify missing owners or stale decisions, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot establish key-management policy, access or derive keys, operate key-management services, change configuration, approve an exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [NIST SP 800-57 Part 2 Revision 1 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/57/pt2/r1/final) and approved organizational cryptography decisions; checked 2026-07-31. This original plan collects organizational key-management governance and operating evidence only. It does not reproduce the publication, establish a policy, prescribe a key-management architecture, validate a service, or make a conformance claim.

### 1. Governance, scope, and service-accountability package

- **Request and owner:** Security and cryptography leaders provide approved scope, policy/practice and planning references, key-management service inventory, accountable-role assignments, service/interface ownership, and references to approved organizational decisions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one in-scope service from its organizational scope and accountable role through the relevant policy/practice reference, service owner, and interface/dependency record. This assesses supplied governance traceability; it cannot establish policy adequacy, service security, or organizational compliance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved records and flag a missing service owner, scope rationale, interface, or review date. Humans establish policy, select service treatment, and approve organizational decisions. Refresh after a material policy, service, ownership, or organizational change.

### 2. Service operation, change, and access-evidence package

- **Request and owner:** Key-management operations, platform, and identity owners provide non-secret configuration/baseline references, privileged-access role records, approved change and release references, service audit/logging availability records, and operational issue summaries.
- **Validate and limit:** Link a sampled change or operating record to the service identity, approved change, designated operator, protected-access record, and available follow-up evidence. This does not access keys or services, test configuration, operate the service, or establish effective access control.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile non-secret metadata and identify missing change, owner, access, or follow-up links. It may not connect to or operate a key-management service, access key material, or alter configuration. Humans approve changes, access, exceptions, and remediation. Refresh after a service, access, release, or incident change.

### 3. Supplier, exception, and governance-review package

- **Request and owner:** Supplier-management, security, and records owners provide provider provenance and notices, exception/incident references, service transition or retirement decisions, management-review minutes, corrective-action references, and retention locations.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample a provider notice, exception, or governance review through source reference, accountable challenge, recorded action, and next-review date. This supports reviewable oversight; it cannot certify a provider, prove service effectiveness, or accept risk.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag stale supplier notices, expired exceptions, and absent review/action links. Humans decide remediation, provider transition, risk acceptance, and external statements. Review quarterly and after material supplier, threat, service, policy, or publication changes.
