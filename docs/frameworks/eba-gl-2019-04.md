# EBA ICT and security risk-management engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not EBA guidance or a supervisory determination. Confirm applicability, amendments, national implementation, and interaction with DORA through the [EBA consolidated EBA/GL/2019/04 publication](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-05/c8fb6168-0aef-42f6-8da9-114a8baf21b9/Consolidated%20version%20of%20EBA%20ameding%20Guidelines%20on%20ICT%20and%20security%20risk%20management.pdf).

## Engagement focus

Operate a year-round record of ICT governance, material services, security and operational-risk decisions, provider oversight, change and incident evidence, customer-impact considerations, remediation, and accountable review.

## Roles

The regulated firm and accountable leaders determine scope and reporting duties; risk, technology, security, and supplier owners maintain evidence; independent audit or review tests the engagement record. AI links approved evidence to owners and dates, highlights conflicting or missing records, and drafts review questions, but cannot decide regulatory applicability, make a prudential judgment, or submit to a supervisor. Reassess applicability after regulatory or business changes, use scheduled management reviews, and retain human sign-off for regulatory communications.

## Source and applicability

Do not treat the original 2019 ICT framework as an unchanged current checklist. The [EBA amendment announcement](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/eba-amends-its-guidelines-ict-and-security-risk-management-measures-context-dora-application) explains scope reduction following DORA. The official amending report identifies retained payment-service-user relationship management and repeal of other parts. Retrieve the consolidated text, amendments and national supervisory position before deciding which provisions apply to the entity.

Create separate records for current EBA duties, DORA/other duties and historical evidence. The broader ICT evidence packages below are reusable supporting records; they do not imply that superseded provisions remain current EBA obligations. Counsel must identify applicable payment-service-provider status, retained requirements and effective dates. Missing consolidated text blocks a complete applicability conclusion, not factual inventory work.

## Before starting

Follow the [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md). Assign regulatory/legal, payment-service product, customer support, security and independent-review owners. Identify entity permissions, payment services, customer channels and current supervisory commitments. Use synthetic customer scenarios and authorized metadata. Keep confidential customer records and supervisory correspondence outside public artifacts.

## Ordered workflow

| Step | Action and accountable owner | Output and exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Resolve amended scope | Regulatory/legal owner reviews consolidated guidance and national application. | Provision-level current/historical/other-regime register, with unresolved scope decisions assigned. |
| 2. Map customer-facing services | Product and operations identify payment services, channels, users and supporting providers. | Complete scoped journey and ownership inventory; no assumption that the whole ICT estate is governed by retained provisions. |
| 3. Define required user support | Owners translate verified retained requirements into original operational instructions. | Source-linked expected communications, assistance and customer-control behavior approved by the responsible human. |
| 4. Gather operating evidence | Support, product and security owners provide versioned messages, procedures, interface records and case metadata. | Evidence matched to service, user/channel, date and requirement; unrelated security reports do not replace user-facing evidence. |
| 5. Exercise approved journeys | Reviewers perform synthetic support and security-communication scenarios. | Expected versus observed behavior, escalation, response and channel coverage with explicit limitations. |
| 6. Reconcile dependencies | Owners trace provider and internal handoffs supporting each required user outcome. | Named responsibility, evidence gaps and contractual dependencies; outsourcing cannot hide a failed customer journey. |
| 7. Remediate and retest | Owners correct authorized failures and reviewers repeat affected cases. | Verified user-facing outcome and historical impact review; revised policy alone does not prove operation. |
| 8. Review and sustain | Independent reviewers challenge scope and evidence; leaders approve representations. | Current-duty evidence packet, separate historical records and source-change monitoring. |

## Failure branches and decisions

- **2019 checklist presented as current:** reconcile every section with amendments before retaining its regulatory label; move historical evidence without deleting its provenance.
- **DORA applicability used to dismiss all EBA duties:** require provision-specific legal analysis of retained scope; do not assume either total replacement or full duplication.
- **Consolidated source unavailable:** preserve the exact access failure, obtain an authorized copy and keep legal conclusions unresolved.
- **Support procedure exists but users cannot reach assistance:** record failed channel and handoff evidence, assign remediation and retest the actual journey.
- **Generic supplier assurance offered for a user-facing duty:** request the particular service outcome and responsibility evidence.
- **Public representation exceeds verified scope:** return it for revision to the named approver; an internal review does not establish supervisory acceptance.

## Evidence and test plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the current [EBA consolidated EBA/GL/2019/04 publication](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-05/c8fb6168-0aef-42f6-8da9-114a8baf21b9/Consolidated%20version%20of%20EBA%20ameding%20Guidelines%20on%20ICT%20and%20security%20risk%20management.pdf); prior snapshot 2026-07-31; verify amended scope before use. Confirm national implementation and the interaction with DORA with qualified counsel. This original plan does not reproduce guidance, make a prudential judgment, decide applicability, or support a supervisory assertion.

### 1. ICT governance, service, and risk-management record

- **Request and owner:** ICT governance/committee records, business-service and ICT asset inventory, criticality/dependency information, risk-assessment records, role assignments, policy approvals, and management-review evidence from risk, ICT, security, and service owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected material service to ownership, dependency context, risk record, governance decision, and review date. This can support a source-linked readiness record; it cannot establish regulatory scope, prove complete inventory coverage, or substitute for a prudential decision.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may correlate authorized metadata and flag missing owners, outdated assessments, or conflicting dates. Humans determine materiality, approve policy and risk treatment, and decide regulatory applicability. Refresh after material product, service, outsourcing, technology, or regulatory changes.

### 2. Security operations, change, and incident evidence

- **Request and owner:** Access and security-operation records, approved change/release evidence, monitoring and test outputs, vulnerability/remediation records, ICT incident logs/escalation decisions, continuity/recovery exercise results, and outsourced-service monitoring records from security, ICT, operations, and supplier owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a human-selected change, event, or test through evidence source, accountable action, escalation, corrective action, and retest. This can demonstrate limited traceability for the selected item; it cannot establish continuous effectiveness, determine reportability, or authorize a production change.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may make a non-destructive evidence index and flag missing handoffs or overdue actions. Humans authorize changes, classify/escalate incidents, and approve communications. Recollect after an incident, failed test, significant control change, or supplier/service disruption.

### 3. Outsourcing, assurance, and management-decision workpaper

- **Request and owner:** Supplier/outsourcing inventory and oversight evidence, assurance/audit results, exception and remediation register, management decisions, resources/priority records, and closure/retest evidence from procurement, legal, risk, ICT, and independent-review owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected provider-related finding or exception to contractual/oversight context, accountable decision, mitigation, due date, and retest. This can support oversight transparency; it cannot interpret legal obligations, accept residual risk, or replace internal-audit work.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may identify stale provider reviews and prepare factual challenge questions. Humans approve outsourcing decisions, exceptions, closure, and supervisory communications. Review on the management cadence, challenge annually, and revisit after material provider, audit, or legal/regulatory changes.


## Cadence and renewal

Set reviews from verified retained duties, supervisory commitments and service change. Annual challenge is an engagement convention. Reopen applicability after amendments, permission changes, new payment services or DORA-related decisions. Preserve earlier legal analyses with their effective periods so old evidence is not mistaken for current coverage.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver the amended-scope decision, complete current-duty register, payment-service journeys, user-facing evidence, dependency records and retests. Identify separate DORA work and historical material explicitly. Name the next owner, action and missing source/evidence without prior chat. No current-compliance conclusion while controlling scope or text remains unresolved.
