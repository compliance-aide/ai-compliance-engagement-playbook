# ISO/IEC 27561:2024 privacy engineering engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not ISO or IEC text, a privacy-design approval, a deployment authorization, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through the [ISO/IEC 27561:2024 publication record](https://www.iso.org/standard/80394.html).

## Engagement focus

Make privacy operational in system engineering by keeping a traceable design record for each material service, feature, interface, and data flow involving personal information. Connect the intended outcome to accountable owners, declared privacy outcomes, architecture decisions, functional capabilities, data-flow diagrams, design assumptions, implementation evidence, test evidence, unresolved trade-offs, exceptions, release decisions, and post-release review triggers. The engagement should expose where stated privacy intent is not yet reflected in a verifiable system behavior.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Engineering, product, privacy, security, architecture, data, legal, and operations owners retain decisions within their authority. Privacy and product owners set intended outcomes and acceptable trade-offs; engineering and architecture owners maintain design and implementation evidence; security and operations owners attest to relevant technical behavior; and release authorities decide whether the documented readiness is sufficient. Independent reviewers sample changes from intent through deployed evidence, test whether material interfaces and data flows are represented, challenge unsupported design claims, and track exceptions to an accountable owner and review date.

AI may organize authorized design artifacts, surface inconsistent terminology, draft evidence requests, create change-review checklists, and prepare traceability workpapers. AI cannot decide which privacy outcome applies, approve architecture or deployment, decide that an implementation meets an intended outcome, change production systems, accept an exception, make a legal interpretation, or claim compliance. Use approved, least-privilege access to project artifacts; do not provide customer data or secrets merely to improve AI analysis. Reassess on material product, interface, data-flow, supplier, or deployment changes; perform quarterly change-traceability reviews and an annual independent readiness review.
