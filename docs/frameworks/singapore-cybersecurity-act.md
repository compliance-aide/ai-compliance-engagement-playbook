# Singapore Cybersecurity Act — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not a designation or reporting decision. Check the [CSA legislation hub](https://www.csa.gov.sg/legislation/cybersecurity-act/).

## Engagement focus

Determine regulated-system status, accountable contacts, security-operations, testing, supplier, material-change, and incident-escalation evidence; reconcile actual systems to any designations.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Operators maintain controls and records; independent reviewers sample evidence and reporting readiness. AI can identify timing gaps, but cannot interpret designations, decide reportability, notify CSA, or submit responses. Review quarterly and scope annually.


## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [Cyber Security Agency legislation hub](https://www.csa.gov.sg/legislation/cybersecurity-act/), checked 2026-07-31, and have authorized Singapore legal/regulatory owners confirm the current Act, subsidiary instruments, designation status, and reporting pathway. This original plan does not reproduce legislation or decide coverage, reportability, or notification.

### 1. Regulated-system and accountable-contact package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain the organization and system inventory, designation or correspondence record where applicable, critical-service and dependency context, accountable contact roster, and approved scope/exclusion decisions from legal, regulatory, technology, and service owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected in-scope or uncertain system to its owner, business purpose, hosting/dependency context, authoritative status record, and next decision date. This supports scope discipline; it cannot determine whether a system is covered or designated.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved inventories and identify missing contacts or review dates. Authorized human owners and counsel decide status and escalation. Trigger on a new service, material architecture change, official correspondence, acquisition, or annual review.

### 2. Operational assurance and event-readiness package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain approved security-operation procedures, asset and change records, test/exercise references, supplier responsibilities, incident chronology, escalation contacts, and remediation evidence from operations, security, engineering, and vendor-management owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected operational event or test from system identity through time-bounded records, assigned owner, escalation evidence, exception, and corrective action. This supports observable process traceability; it cannot prove security effectiveness, statutory compliance, or incident reportability.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index permitted metadata and surface missing event links; it may not access restricted systems, override operations, or notify CSA. Human security and operations leads decide containment and escalation. Trigger on a material event, failed exercise, supplier change, or expired exception.

### 3. Regulatory-communication and renewal package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain authorized communications drafts, counsel reviews, submission authority record, findings/actions register, retention record, and management-review decisions from regulatory, legal, and accountable leaders.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample a proposed communication or finding through its evidence basis, review authority, decision record, and approved follow-up. This supports orderly preparation; it cannot submit a response, represent the organization, or close a regulatory matter.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a non-sensitive action queue and flag stale approvals. Named humans approve risk decisions and all external communications. Review after source or designation changes and at least annually.
