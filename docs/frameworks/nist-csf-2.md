# NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not an official NIST publication or a claim of
> conformance. Review the current [NIST CSF source](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework)
> before each engagement milestone.

## Engagement focus

Establish the organization's business-risk context and desired outcome; maintain
a source-linked current-state narrative; prioritize remediation; test whether
governance and operating practices work; record risk decisions; refresh the
profile after a material change.

## Roles

Operators own systems, evidence, and corrective work. An assessor challenges
scope, traceability, and conclusions independently. AI may inventory authorized artifacts,
identify gaps and conflicts, and draft questions and evidence indexes, but cannot
attest to conformance, accept risk, approve remediation, or alter systems.

## Annual rhythm

Set scope and risk priorities; run quarterly evidence and change reviews; test
the most consequential practices; track remediation to a human-reviewed outcome;
reassess after incidents, major system changes, supplier changes, or changed
business objectives.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [NIST CSF source](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework), which identifies CSF 2.0 and its profile resources, as the official source. Reviewed 2026-07-31. This is original operational guidance, not a copied CSF outcome list or a conformance statement. A risk owner approves the organization profile, scope, and use of informative references.

**Plan status:** Independently reviewed; see the [review receipt](../evidence-plan-reviews/nist-csf-2.md).

### 1. Organizational profile and risk-decision record

- **Request and owner:** Approved current and target profiles, business objectives, material services, risk appetite, dependencies, and open risk decisions from the enterprise-risk and security owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace selected profile assertions to an approved business objective, risk record, accountable owner, and dated decision. This can support that cybersecurity work is connected to business risk; it cannot prove every CSF outcome is achieved or that risk is acceptable.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize profile deltas and unresolved dependencies. A named executive owner approves priorities and risk acceptance. Refresh for business-objective, service, supplier, or threat changes and at annual planning.

### 2. Service and operational-practice evidence index

- **Request and owner:** An authorized index of material services/assets, operating procedures, configuration or monitoring outputs, access/change records, incident/recovery exercises, and supplier responsibilities, from service and security owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Independently trace a human-approved service selection from the profile to its owners and time-bounded evidence; note population coverage, access limits, and contradictory records. This can support a reasoned current-state narrative, not continuous operating effectiveness or a technical certification.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may perform read-only evidence tracing and flag stale records; it may not change systems or decide a finding is closed. Recollect after material architecture, provider, incident, or control-operation change.

### 3. Measurement, remediation, and challenge workpaper

- **Request and owner:** Approved measures, test methods, results and limitations, corrective-action register, due dates, and management-review records from the program owner and independent reviewer.
- **Validate and limit:** Reperform the approved calculation or trace a selected metric to its source records, then test that an exception has a human decision and retest criterion. This can support transparent improvement tracking; it cannot establish conformance or replace independent assessment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may draft a gap or trend analysis while preserving raw-source pointers. Humans approve measures, remediation closure, and external statements. Refresh after metric-method, risk-priority, material incident, or source change.
