# Cybersecurity Capability Maturity Model (C2M2) — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not a DOE maturity rating. Check the
> [DOE C2M2 source](https://www.energy.gov/ceser/cybersecurity-capability-maturity-model-c2m2)
> before each cycle.

## Engagement focus

Choose a self-evaluation boundary and accountable subject-matter owners. Use
business and operational risk to define desired capability outcomes; collect
operating narratives rather than document-only checklists; challenge whether
work is repeatable; then fund and sequence verified improvements.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Management owns targets and accepted risk. Independent reviewers retain
judgment on capability claims. AI may organize interview and evidence themes,
track provenance and confidence, and identify dependencies; it cannot assign
a maturity result, accept risk, approve targets, or alter systems. Run periodic health reviews and a year-end current-versus-
target review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [DOE C2M2 source](https://www.energy.gov/ceser/cybersecurity-capability-maturity-model-c2m2), applicable owner-approved organizational context, and authorized source material; checked 2026-07-31. This is original evidence-planning guidance, not reproduced DOE model content, a C2M2 score, or a maturity determination. Management confirms scope, source rights, and any rating or risk decision.

### 1. Mission, scope, and capability-target package

- **Request and owner:** Executive, operational, risk, and cybersecurity owners provide mission/service context, approved evaluation boundary, dependency and criticality records, desired capability outcomes, target-setting rationale, ownership roster, and dated scope or target changes.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected target outcome to a named accountable owner, mission or risk input, approved scope record, and review date. This supports a governed evaluation starting point; it cannot assign a maturity level, establish that every dependency is known, or accept risk.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize authorized records, relate stated dependencies, and flag missing owner, rationale, or review-date fields. Humans approve boundary, targets, and risk decisions. Refresh after a mission, service, ownership, threat, or material dependency change and at annual target review.

### 2. Operating-practice and repeatability package

- **Request and owner:** Process owners provide original operating narratives, role and handoff records, dated work outputs, training or exercise records, change histories, performance signals, and evidence-location metadata for selected capability areas.
- **Validate and limit:** Use a human-approved walkthrough to trace one stated practice from accountable role through a bounded period of operating records, including a change or disruption handoff where available. This can support an observation about traceability and repeatability; it cannot score capability, prove sustained performance, or replace management judgment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index evidence metadata, identify gaps between narratives and records, and draft review questions. Humans select samples, judge whether practice is repeatable, direct remediation, and approve conclusions. Recollect after a failed handoff, major process change, exercise, incident, or scheduled health review.

### 3. Improvement, challenge, and target-renewal package

- **Request and owner:** Program and risk owners provide observed gaps, improvement portfolio, priority and funding decisions, accountable-action records, milestones, retest or follow-up evidence, management reviews, and independent-review workpapers.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected improvement item from evidence-backed observation through human prioritization, owner assignment, target date, follow-up, and unresolved limitation. This supports accountable follow-through; it cannot close a risk, validate enterprise-wide improvement, or create a DOE maturity result.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag overdue milestones and prepare a non-authoritative review packet. Humans approve investment, risk treatment, closure, targets, and external representations; independent reviewers challenge the evidence trail without setting management targets. Refresh after a missed milestone, material event, target change, and year-end current-versus-target review.
