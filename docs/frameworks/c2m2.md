# Cybersecurity Capability Maturity Model — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not a DOE maturity determination or certification.

## Source and applicability

Use the [DOE C2M2 program](https://www.energy.gov/ceser/cybersecurity-capability-maturity-model-c2m2)
and its model and self-evaluation guide. Checked 2026-09-04: DOE identifies version
2.1, June 2022, as latest and describes applicability to IT and OT. Practices are
organised into objectives and domains with maturity indicator levels. Use the
actual model's evaluation rules; do not invent an average score or translate a
C2M2 result into another framework's certification.

The [official tool](https://c2m2.doe.gov/) supports self-evaluation. Pin model and
tool versions and verify imported results before reuse. DOE's suggested workshop
duration is not a ceiling on evidence review. See the
[author record](../refresh-reviews/c2m2.md).

## Engagement focus

Evaluate the defined function's current practices, preserve evidence and disagreement,
then prioritise improvements against management's desired capability. Distinguish
self-evaluation judgments from independently observed operating effectiveness.

## Roles

Sponsor approves scope, targets and investment. Facilitator coordinates the workshop;
subject-matter owners describe actual practices. Reviewers challenge evidence and
response consistency. Management retains residual-risk decisions. AI may index
records, draft questions and reconcile approved responses; it cannot independently
assign maturity, approve targets, accept risk or claim DOE endorsement.

## Before starting

Obtain function/organisation boundary, model/tool version, participant roster,
mission/risk context, prior results, evidence permissions and evaluation method.
Identify IT/OT dependencies without authorising active OT tests. Save a protected
work item using the [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md). Complete the whole approved
evaluation population; evidence batching is not permission to omit practices.

## Ordered workflow

| Step | Action and owner | Output and exit check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Define evaluation | Sponsor/facilitator confirm function, boundary, versions and participants. | Dated scope and domain/practice coverage plan. |
| 2. Prepare factual inputs | Owners supply operating narratives, records and known gaps; AI indexes provenance. | Every planned discussion has an owner and available evidence or explicit gap. |
| 3. Facilitate responses | Participants examine actual practice using model definitions; facilitator records rationale and disagreement. | Approved response record with supporting facts, not AI guesses. |
| 4. Reconcile results | Reviewer checks scope consistency, omissions and model/tool application. | Domain-level result packet and unresolved judgments; no fabricated aggregate maturity. |
| 5. Set targets and priorities | Management compares results with mission/risk needs and decides desired outcomes. | Approved targets and rationale separate from current results. |
| 6. Implement improvements | Owners propose dependencies, resources and milestones; authorised operators execute approved work. | Decision-to-implementation receipts and remaining gaps. |
| 7. Re-evaluate and renew | Owners provide fresh evidence; facilitator/reviewer revisit affected practices and changes. | Current-versus-target comparison with version/scope differences explained. |

## Evidence and test plan

| Evidence and custodian | Check | Expected observation | Failure or limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scope/roster, facilitator | Reconcile function dependencies and practice coverage with accountable participants. | Missing expertise or coverage is visible. | One team's answer cannot represent an unrelated business function. |
| Practice records, owner | Trace narrative to dated actions and a handoff/change example where relevant. | Claimed repeatability has operating support. | A policy alone cannot establish repeated execution. |
| Response/result file, facilitator | Compare approved responses with saved/exported results and unresolved items. | Same version and responses survive readback. | Import success cannot prove every practice retained its response. |
| Improvement record, sponsor/owner | Follow gap to target, approved work and renewed evidence. | Improvement addresses the original capability gap. | Funding approval is not achieved capability. |

## Failure branches and decisions

Missing evidence or disputed answers require facilitator review, not a favourable
AI default. Scope changes invalidate unsupported comparisons with previous results.
Unexpected import omissions require response-by-response reconciliation before
reuse. Failed improvement work remains open. On interruption preserve responses,
versions, disagreements and last verified export; do not restart completed work
or overwrite a prior evaluation. OT testing needs separately approved safety scope.

## Cadence and renewal

Use risk-driven evaluation and improvement milestones. Quarterly health checks and
annual current-versus-target review are operating defaults, not DOE requirements.
Re-evaluate after major mission, ownership, dependency, process or source changes.
Explain any version migration before claiming progress across evaluations.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver scope, participant and evidence records, approved responses, result export,
disagreements, management targets and improvement/re-evaluation queue. Name untested
operational claims. Independent source, engagement and skeptical review and named
human publication approval remain pending. Original instructions and links only;
no model practice tables or private evaluation data reproduced.
