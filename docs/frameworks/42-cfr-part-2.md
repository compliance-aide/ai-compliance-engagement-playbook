# 42 CFR Part 2 — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not legal advice or a disclosure authorization. Consult the [current eCFR text](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-2).

## Engagement focus

Determine whether programs and data are in scope; maintain consent, disclosure, recipient, workflow, workforce, complaint, breach, and legal-request escalation evidence. Test authorized and blocked paths without exposing records.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Privacy and legal owners decide scope and disclosures. Independent reviewers test selected paths using approved methods. AI uses only approved redacted metadata and cannot infer coverage, authorize disclosure, answer legal requests, handle patient decisions, or send data to unapproved tools.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the [current eCFR Part 2 text](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-2), with program counsel confirming current applicability; checked 2026-07-31. This is original operational guidance and does not reproduce regulatory text or decide whether a program, record, consent, disclosure, redisclosure, or emergency request is permitted.

### 1. Program scope and Part 2 record boundary

- **Request and owner:** Program inventory, service/location and legal-entity map, system/data-flow diagram, record classifications, and accountable program/privacy owner attestations.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected record route from program intake through systems and recipients, preserving only approved metadata. This supports a factual scope trail; it cannot determine Part 2 applicability or whether a record is covered.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index approved metadata and flag unmapped routes. Counsel and privacy owners decide scope. Refresh for a program, system, affiliation, or data-flow change.

### 2. Consent, disclosure, and recipient-control evidence

- **Request and owner:** Consent/authorization workflow and version history, disclosure/accounting log, recipient identity and purpose fields, minimum-necessary access configuration, and workforce procedure/training records owned by privacy, records, and operations.
- **Validate and limit:** Independently trace a redacted sample from request and identity checks to human authorization, disclosure outcome, recipient record, and any documented refusal or escalation. This evidences workflow traceability; it cannot authorize disclosure or decide consent validity, redisclosure limits, or exceptions.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag incomplete routing fields but cannot release data or decide exceptions. Recollect after a form, recipient, workflow, access-control, or policy change.

### 3. Incident, legal request, and assurance record

- **Request and owner:** Security-event timeline, legal/subpoena request escalation records, complaint records, training completion, access-review evidence, exercise results, and remediation register.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected event or request to preservation, counsel review, decision authority, communications approval, corrective action, and retest. This supports accountable handling; it cannot determine breach/reporting duties, respond to legal process, or make an audit conclusion.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a redacted evidence packet and identify overdue actions. Legal, privacy, and incident owners approve conclusions and external communications. Refresh after an event, request, complaint trend, or annual exercise.
