# FAR 52.204-25 / Section 889 — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not legal advice or a contractual representation. Confirm current text at the [Acquisition.gov clause record](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/52.204-25?searchTerms=section+889).

## Engagement focus

Maintain solicitation and contract scope, product and service inventory, supplier attestations, due-diligence evidence, exception and waiver records, incident/escalation decisions, disclosures, and renewal reviews.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Procurement, legal, supply-chain, and authorized contract officials determine applicability and representations; independent reviewers test evidence. AI compiles approved records and flags missing artifacts, but cannot determine coverage, certify, disclose, or submit. Review per solicitation and annually.


## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the current [Acquisition.gov clause record](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/52.204-25?searchTerms=section+889), the solicitation or award, and approved procurement records; checked 2026-07-31. This is original operational guidance, not FAR text, legal advice, a coverage finding, or a contractual representation. Procurement, legal, supply-chain, and authorized contract officials decide applicability, representation, exception, waiver, disclosure, and notification actions.

### 1. Solicitation, award, and representation-scope package

- **Request and owner:** Contracts and procurement owners provide solicitation/award versions, amendment history, clause applicability and representation decision records, bidder/offeror scope, deadlines, named approvers, and any agency-directed instructions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one selected representation or applicability record to the relevant contract artifact, version/date, owner, approval authority, and stated scope. This tests record linkage; it cannot decide covered use/equipment/services or make a representation.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare supplied versions, calendar deadlines, and flag missing authorities. Humans interpret the award and authorize all representations. Refresh upon solicitation, amendment, award, renewal, or direction change.

### 2. Product, service, and supplier due-diligence package

- **Request and owner:** Supply-chain, asset, IT, and vendor-management owners provide scoped product/service inventories, supplier identity and due-diligence records, procurement records, supporting attestations where authorized, lifecycle/change information, and unresolved-item register.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample a selected inventory item to its supplier/source record, acquisition/use context, owner, diligence evidence, limitation, and disposition date. This does not independently determine manufacturer ownership, coverage, or eligibility.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved inventory and supplier data, flag gaps or outdated diligence, and prepare questions. Humans investigate, decide disposition, and approve supplier actions. Refresh after new procurement, supplier change, or inventory discovery.

### 3. Exception, escalation, and disclosure-readiness package

- **Request and owner:** Legal, contracts, procurement, security, and executive owners provide exception/waiver records, escalation paths, incident or discovery log, disclosure decision evidence, authorized contacts, corrective-action tracking, and periodic management review.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one selected exception or discovery through source record, responsible authority, timing, decision rationale, approved communication path, remediation owner, and follow-up. This cannot determine reportability, request a waiver, or submit a disclosure.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag stale exceptions, incomplete ownership, and approaching deadlines. Authorized humans decide waiver, disclosure, and external communication; independent reviewers challenge the trail. Refresh after a discovery, exception change, or solicitation/award event.
