# UK Cyber Governance Code of Practice engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not UK government text, board advice, a legal determination, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through the [UK Cyber Governance Code of Practice](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cyber-governance-code-of-practice) and applicable legal or regulatory authorities.

## Engagement focus

Maintain a board-ready cyber-governance record that connects material digital risks, critical technology and services, accountable leadership, risk appetite and tolerances approved by accountable people, strategic decisions, supplier oversight, reporting metrics, assurance activity, material incidents, management actions, and review dates. Keep management reporting distinguishable from independent assurance. Record metric definitions, source limitations, scope, and decision use so leadership can challenge a report rather than merely receive it.

## Roles and annual rhythm

The board, directors, senior management, risk, security, technology, operations, procurement, legal, and internal-audit owners retain accountable decisions. Executives prepare management information; risk and security owners maintain evidence and escalation routes; the board decides governance direction; and auditors evaluate evidence in their independent role. Independent reviewers sample reported risks and metrics through source records, management challenge, decision logs, supplier evidence, remediation, and follow-up, challenging optimism bias, ambiguous tolerances, missing escalation, and unsupported assurance claims.

AI may organize authorized governance metadata, identify missing sources or review fields, compare approved reporting definitions with records, draft questions, and prepare non-authoritative workpapers. AI cannot set risk appetite, determine materiality, advise a board as an authority, approve strategy, authorize a disclosure, accept risk, alter evidence, or claim compliance. Reassess before material business, technology, supplier, threat, incident, regulatory, or leadership changes; provide routine management reporting and conduct an annual independent readiness review.


## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the current [UK Cyber Governance Code of Practice record](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cyber-governance-code-of-practice); checked 2026-07-31. This plan is original operational planning, not government text, board advice, a governance determination, or a compliance claim. Keep board papers, privileged advice, sensitive incident detail, and confidential supplier material in approved access-controlled systems.

### 1. Leadership accountability and risk-direction package

- **Request and owner:** Board/committee terms and minutes, leadership assignments, approved cyber-risk appetite/tolerance decisions, strategic risk records, critical-service context, escalation routes, and review schedules from directors, executive management, risk, security, and technology owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one material cyber-risk topic from management evidence through leadership challenge, decision, action owner, and review date. This cannot set materiality, establish board effectiveness, or accept risk.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index authorized decisions and identify absent owners, approvals, or review dates. Humans set risk direction, determine materiality, approve strategy, and accept risk. Refresh after material business, technology, leadership, threat, or regulatory change.

### 2. Management information, resilience, and incident-oversight package

- **Request and owner:** Defined cyber metrics and source records, management reports, vulnerability/remediation summaries, resilience/test evidence, incident records, communications/escalation decisions, and management actions from security, risk, technology, operations, and communications owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one reported metric, test outcome, or material incident through quality checks, management challenge, decision, and follow-up. This cannot prove metric completeness, control effectiveness, or disclosure sufficiency.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare approved metric definitions with supplied records and flag missing sources or stale actions. Humans validate reporting, direct incident response, decide disclosures, and approve priorities. Recollect after a material incident, failed exercise, metric change, or resilience concern.

### 3. Supplier oversight, assurance, and exception package

- **Request and owner:** Material supplier/dependency records, assurance reports, security decisions, exception register, independent-review results, management remediation decisions, and retest/closure evidence from procurement, security, risk, technology, and audit owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one supplier concern, assurance observation, or exception to accountable evaluation, decision, mitigation, due date, and independent challenge. This cannot certify a supplier, close an exception, or replace independent assurance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may identify stale exceptions and draft factual challenge questions. Humans approve supplier decisions, exceptions, resources, closure, and external statements. Review routinely and conduct an annual independent readiness challenge.
