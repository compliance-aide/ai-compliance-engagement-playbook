# NIST SP 800-190 application-container security engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST text, a production-deployment approval, a technical assurance conclusion, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through the [NIST SP 800-190 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/190/final).

## Engagement focus

Maintain an application-container security record that connects workload, image, registry, runtime, orchestrator, host and platform dependencies, accountable owners, allowed configuration, build and deployment evidence, monitoring, vulnerabilities, exceptions, and end-of-life actions. Maintain separate evidence for each material layer and handoff so a secure image or a hardened runtime is not mistaken for assurance of the entire deployment.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Application, platform, infrastructure, cloud, security, operations, privacy, risk, supplier, and release owners retain accountable decisions. Workload owners document service context; platform owners maintain authorized environment evidence; security owners govern risk and verification; operations own runtime response; and release authorities decide deployment. Independent reviewers sample workloads from build through registry, environment, runtime, monitoring, vulnerability handling, and retirement to challenge unowned dependencies and unsupported assurance claims.

AI may organize authorized container metadata, compare approved configuration records, flag incomplete provenance or aging vulnerabilities, draft review questions, and prepare non-authoritative workpapers. AI cannot build or alter images, modify registry or runtime configuration, deploy a workload, create credentials, initiate scans, approve exceptions, authorize release, or claim compliance. Reassess before material workload, image, registry, platform, orchestration, supplier, vulnerability, or deployment changes; review exceptions quarterly and complete an annual independent readiness review.
