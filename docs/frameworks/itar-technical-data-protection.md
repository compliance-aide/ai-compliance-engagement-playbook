# ITAR technical-data protection — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not export classification or legal advice. Consult [DDTC compliance guidance](https://www.pmddtc.state.gov/sys_attachment.do?sys_id=35c9a068db995f00d0a370131f9619bb).

## Engagement focus

Maintain controlled-data assets, authorization, storage, collaboration, cloud, remote, foreign-person, third-party, travel, incident, and suspected-release evidence.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Export officers decide jurisdiction and authorization; independent reviewers challenge assumptions and access records. AI uses only approved sanitized metadata and cannot classify, screen conclusively, release technical data, or file with DDTC. Review quarterly and annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the linked [DDTC compliance guidance](https://www.pmddtc.state.gov/sys_attachment.do?sys_id=35c9a068db995f00d0a370131f9619bb), current official export-control sources, approved authorizations, and organization records; checked 2026-07-31. This original plan does not reproduce regulatory text, classify an item or technical data, determine jurisdiction, screen a person conclusively, authorize a release, or provide legal advice.

### 1. Technical-data boundary and authorization package

- **Request and owner:** Export compliance, legal, engineering, program, and data owners provide controlled-data inventory, program/project boundaries, jurisdiction/classification determinations made by authorized personnel, applicable authorizations, handling labels, repository/transfer maps, and review approvals.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected repository, transfer path, or data package to its named owner, approved determination/authorization reference, access boundary, handling designation, and review date. This checks an approved record trail; it cannot decide export status or authorize access or transfer.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index sanitized approved metadata and flag missing ownership, expiration dates, or unsupported handling labels. Export officials and counsel decide classification, jurisdiction, authorization, and release. Recollect after a new program, data type, collaboration tool, transfer route, or authorization change.

### 2. Access, collaboration, and third-party package

- **Request and owner:** Identity/security, HR, export compliance, procurement, travel, and program owners provide role/access approvals, onboarding/offboarding evidence, collaboration and remote-work rules, third-party agreements, subcontractor controls, foreign-person review records, and exception decisions.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample a selected access grant or third-party engagement to its approved role, authorization boundary, approving official, date, revocation path, and exception record. This cannot screen eligibility, approve a foreign-person access decision, or validate every access event.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may surface expiring approvals, missing offboarding dates, or mismatched sanitized metadata; it may not make access decisions or expose controlled content. Authorized humans approve access, supplier sharing, travel handling, and exceptions. Refresh after staffing, supplier, location, or collaboration-platform changes.

### 3. Incident, governance, and correction packet

- **Request and owner:** Export officers, legal, security incident response, executive sponsors, and independent reviewers provide suspected-release escalation procedures, incident/exercise records, investigation preservation decisions, governance minutes, training records, corrective actions, and retest/closure evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected exercise, incident record, or finding to its owner, confidentiality boundary, escalation authority, preservation decision, action plan, and retest. It cannot determine whether a violation occurred, make a voluntary disclosure decision, or communicate externally.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize approved case metadata and flag unowned actions or overdue reviews. Humans decide investigation, notification, disclosure, risk acceptance, and closure. Review quarterly and after any suspected release, authorization change, material finding, or program change.
