# FINRA cybersecurity supervisory readiness — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not a FINRA framework, rule interpretation, or supervisory approval. Consult the [FINRA cybersecurity hub](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/key-topics/cybersecurity).

## Engagement focus

With qualified compliance ownership, establish firm-specific supervisory obligations and processes in scope. Maintain evidence for governance, risk, access, change, vendor, branch, data-protection, training, and incident communications. Test consequential scenarios and keep escalations traceable.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Business and technology owners operate and evidence processes. Independent reviewers sample records and challenge escalations. AI may normalize authorized inventories, surface missing evidence, and prepare review packets; it cannot interpret FINRA rules, approve a program, decide reporting, or file anything. Review signals quarterly and scope annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Consult the official [FINRA cybersecurity topic hub](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/key-topics/cybersecurity), applicable rules, notices, and qualified counsel; checked 2026-07-31. This original readiness plan does not interpret FINRA obligations, replace firm-specific supervisory procedures, or determine a filing or reporting duty.

### 1. Firm scope and supervisory-procedure package

- **Request and owner:** Compliance, legal, and supervisory owners provide the approved firm/business scope, applicable-process analysis, written supervisory procedure references, role assignments, branch or affiliate boundaries, and recorded policy exceptions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected business activity to its designated supervisor, procedure reference, implementation owner, and exception/escalation route. This supports accountability evidence; it cannot establish rule applicability, procedure adequacy, or supervisory compliance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may normalize approved inventories and highlight missing owners. Qualified humans interpret rules and approve procedures. Refresh after a business, product, branch, affiliate, rule, or supervisory change.

### 2. Cybersecurity operation and escalation package

- **Request and owner:** Technology, security, and operations owners provide access/privilege review evidence, material change records, vendor-risk inputs, security-event tickets, training completion populations, and escalation/decision records for the selected period.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect integrity and time coverage, then trace a selected event, change, or access review to responsible owner and documented follow-up. This supports selected-record traceability; it cannot prove continuous effectiveness or decide whether an event is reportable.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may assemble a restricted evidence index and flag late reviews; it may not access production systems beyond authorization, decide materiality, or submit reports. Refresh after an incident, material vendor issue, failed control, or evidence-period rollover.

### 3. Representation, issue, and annual oversight package

- **Request and owner:** Compliance and executive owners provide issue logs, remediation status, risk acceptances, management reporting, public/customer representation approvals, and annual supervisory review records.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected issue or representation to source evidence, accountable authority, approval, remediation, and recheck record. This supports defensible governance; it cannot approve a representation, accept risk, or make an examination conclusion.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare source-linked workpapers and flag expired approvals. Humans approve communications, risk decisions, and regulatory engagement. Refresh before material representations, annual review, or supervisory examination activity.
