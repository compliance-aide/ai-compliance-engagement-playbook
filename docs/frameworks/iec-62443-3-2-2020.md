# IEC 62443-3-2:2020 IACS system-design risk assessment engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not IEC text, a safety decision or a security-level determination.

## Source and applicability

The [IEC publication record](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/30727), checked 2026-09-04, identifies system definition, partition into zones and conduits, risk assessment for each, target security levels and documented security requirements as the scope. Full licensed requirements, assessment method and sector-specific obligations remain unverified for this draft. Obtain authorized source access before detailed assessment; do not reproduce protected requirements in this repository.

The system under consideration is the approved industrial-system boundary being assessed. Use the edition required by the engagement and record deviations from current source requirements for qualified review. This guide organizes design work; it does not establish achieved security or authorize implementation.

## Engagement focus

Connect each design decision to an explicit boundary, risk scenario, evidence source, accountable authority and verification objective. Treat the target security level as a design target requiring qualified determination, not a test result or certificate.

## Roles

Asset owners approve scope and risk decisions. OT engineering, operations, safety, maintenance and security specialists provide design and consequence expertise. Suppliers explain dependencies and technical constraints. An independent reviewer challenges assumptions and traceability. AI may organize permitted records and draft questions; it cannot initiate discovery traffic, scan IACS, determine security levels, accept risk, approve safety decisions or change industrial configurations.

## Before starting

Record process purpose, operating modes, site, assets, external interfaces, stakeholders and decision deadline. Identify safety constraints and operational consequences with qualified owners. Collect approved diagrams, inventories and existing evidence without connecting to control systems. Define permitted workspace, licensed-source access and decision authority. Any future technical testing needs separate site authorization, safe conditions, stop criteria and recovery planning.

## Ordered workflow

1. **Define the system.** Reconcile all scoped assets, communication paths, remote maintenance, external dependencies and exclusions. Resolve conflicting diagrams with owners. Record unknown connections as gaps; do not infer absence from an incomplete drawing.
2. **Establish risk context.** Qualified owners approve criteria, consequence definitions, threat assumptions and acceptable decision methods. Include relevant operating modes and safety/process consequences. Missing evidence is uncertainty, not a zero-risk input.
3. **Develop the partition.** Engineers propose zones and conduits with rationale and asset/interface membership. Reconcile each connection with the representation. A VLAN name alone does not demonstrate a justified security boundary or effective separation.
4. **Assess every zone and conduit.** Experts apply the authorized method, preserving scenarios, inputs, existing safeguards, assumptions and limitations. Record shared dependencies and paths that cross boundaries. Revisit the partition where assessment results show that it no longer fits the risk context.
5. **Approve targets and requirements.** Capture qualified target security-level decisions and their rationale for each zone and conduit. Translate approved decisions into traceable design requirements with owners and acceptance evidence. Do not claim a target has been achieved because a supplier component advertises a capability.
6. **Challenge the design and resolve gaps.** Review completeness, feasibility, safety interactions, legacy limitations and conflicting requirements. Record compensating-measure proposals and exceptions for authorized decisions. Define permitted verification work and untested assumptions; no production changes follow automatically from design approval.
7. **Hand off and control changes.** Provide the approved boundary, risk records, targets, requirements and verification plan to implementation owners. Record acceptance and unresolved actions separately. Reassess when assets, communication paths, threats, suppliers or operating conditions change; preserve previous versions and reasons for revisions.

## Evidence and test plan

Account for all scoped assets, zones, conduits, scenarios, targets, requirements and exceptions. Preserve all evidence supplied for assessment without silent trimming or sampling. Complete inventory accounting does not prove exhaustive hazard discovery or technical effectiveness; report those limits explicitly.

### 1. System-context and approved-boundary package

- **Request and owner:** Human-approved system purpose/boundary, asset and interface inventory, operations and engineering owners, process/safety constraints, supplier dependencies, and approved zone/conduit representation.
- **Validate and limit:** Reconcile all scoped assets and interfaces and trace each to its owner, represented boundary, dependency, and stated constraint using authorized records only. This supports a scoped evidence population; it cannot validate design completeness or establish a security level.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize supplied metadata and flag missing owners. Humans approve all boundaries. Revisit before material asset, process, interface, or supplier change.

### 2. Human risk-design input package

- **Request and owner:** Authorized risk inputs, threat/concern records, assumptions, safety/operations review inputs, human-approved security-level decision references, and documented design constraints.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace every scoped design input to its source, accountable authority, date, boundary, and stated limitation. This supports decision traceability; it cannot perform a risk assessment, set a security level, or decide a design outcome.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index authorized inputs and surface unlinked assumptions; it cannot infer decisions. Reopen after an incident, material vulnerability, or design change.

### 3. Exception, change, and independent-challenge package

- **Request and owner:** Change/exception records, compensating-measure documentation, safety/risk approvals, remediation ownership, retest/review criteria, and independent-review workpapers.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace every scoped exception to authority, duration, operational constraint, evidence link, and follow-up. This supports governed follow-through; it cannot accept risk or replace independent engineering review.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare read-only workpapers and flag expired exceptions; it cannot initiate scanning or configuration changes. Review at approved intervals and before material zone, conduit, safety, or system change.

## Failure branches and decisions

- Connection has no owner or conduit representation: reconcile the boundary before dependent completeness claims.
- Target exists without supporting risk rationale: route it to qualified review; do not infer justification from a copied template.
- Risk decision depends on an unverified safeguard: retain the assumption and require evidence or reassessment.
- Design conflicts with safe operation: escalate to engineering, operations and safety authorities before implementation.
- Supplier capability is substituted for system verification: separate the claims and retain the missing system evidence.

Use [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md) statuses for factual assertions: supported, not_supported, inconclusive, not_applicable and not_tested. These are not security levels or risk-acceptance states.

## Cadence and renewal

Review at approved risk-based intervals and before material boundary or design changes. Reopen affected assessments after incidents, new vulnerabilities or invalidated assumptions. Do not invent a universal annual certification requirement.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver a versioned system definition, complete zone/conduit register, risk rationale, approved targets, design requirements, verification objectives and open actions with owners and due dates. Full licensed-source, independent skeptical and rights reviews remain pending. No real system assessment, industrial test or conformity decision occurred in preparing this draft.

Fictional author desk case: two zones are drawn as isolated, but a shared engineering workstation has connections to both. The isolated-boundary claim is unsupported until the path is evaluated and the design records reconciled. Do not probe or disconnect the workstation; qualified owners decide the response.
