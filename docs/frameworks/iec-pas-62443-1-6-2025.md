# IEC PAS 62443-1-6:2025 IIoT engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not IEC text, architecture approval or a conformity conclusion.

## Source and applicability

The [IEC publication record](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/102885), checked 2026-09-04, describes guidance for applying the 62443 series to new and existing Industrial Internet of Things arrangements, principally for asset owners and service providers. It is a Publicly Available Specification, not a substitute for the applicable parts of the series. ## Engagement focus

Trace industrial devices, gateways, hosted services and remote operators as one connected service chain. Identify where data, commands, identity and maintenance functions cross organizational or technical boundaries. Keep architectural intent separate from demonstrated behavior.

## Roles

Asset owners approve scope and risk. Engineering, operations and safety owners decide permitted connectivity and operational behavior. Security, privacy, service and supplier owners explain controls and obligations. An independent reviewer challenges dependency and failure coverage. AI may organize authorized records and draft questions; it cannot onboard devices, connect cloud services, initiate scans, change OT, select safeguards, accept risk or approve architecture.

## Before starting

Record business purpose, industrial process, sites, devices, gateways, services, tenants, interfaces, data types and owners. Identify whether each path carries telemetry, commands, updates or administrative access. Establish authorized evidence sources, confidentiality and safety constraints. No live discovery or remote login follows from this documentation workflow.

## Ordered workflow

1. **Reconcile the connected boundary.** Account for all scoped devices, edge functions, hosted services, remote support and subcontractors. Record unknown interfaces and shared dependencies. A device inventory alone is not a complete IIoT service boundary.
2. **Assign responsibilities and source requirements.** Map each function to its asset-owner, provider or product-owner obligation using qualified review of authorized sources. Record exact editions and scope decisions. Resolve responsibilities that both parties assume the other performs.
3. **Trace data and control paths.** Preserve source, destination, purpose, permissions and trust assumptions for every scoped path. Separate outbound telemetry from inbound commands and maintenance channels. Inspect configuration records rather than assuming that a “read-only” service cannot affect the process.
4. **Review lifecycle and failure scenarios.** Qualified owners consider enrollment, identity renewal, updates, lost connectivity, provider outages, compromised credentials and retirement. Record expected behavior and consequences, including dependencies needed to recover. Do not assume local operation continues safely when a remote service fails.
5. **Plan and examine permitted verification.** Define approved objectives, representative environments, negative cases and limits. Use existing evidence or separately authorized isolated exercises; never test an outage on live industrial equipment under this guide. Distinguish a diagram, supplier statement and observed end-to-end result.
6. **Resolve gaps through controlled decisions.** Assign remediation, compensating measures and customer/provider actions to owners. Obtain safety, change and risk approvals before execution. Retest affected paths and preserve failed or unavailable evidence.
7. **Hand off and monitor changes.** Deliver operating assumptions, response contacts, support commitments, dependency register and unresolved work. Reopen affected decisions after device, tenant, interface, supplier, identity or service changes. Retirement must address access, credentials, data and remaining dependencies through authorized actions.

## Evidence and test plan

### 1. Boundary and responsibility package

Collect the complete device/service/interface inventory, owners, contracts and role decisions. Trace every scoped path to an accountable party. Mark excluded and unknown functions explicitly; outsourcing does not erase the customer's remaining obligations.

### 2. Connectivity and lifecycle package

Collect permitted configurations, identity and remote-access records, update decisions, data-flow records and onboarding/offboarding evidence. Compare actual paths with the approved architecture. Preserve all evidence supplied for assessment without silent trimming or sampling. Full inventory accounting does not establish exhaustive technical testing.

### 3. Failure, recovery and change package

Collect approved scenarios, exercise results, supplier dependencies, recovery procedures, incidents and retest evidence. Trace customer and provider handoffs. A cloud service returning to normal does not prove that industrial devices reconnected correctly or that queued commands were handled as intended.

## Failure branches and decisions

- Telemetry-only claim conflicts with an enabled command path: record the contradiction and seek qualified architecture review.
- Unknown service tenant or owner: stop dependent access or assurance conclusions until the boundary is resolved.
- Supplier outage behavior is undocumented: retain uncertainty and assign an approved evidence or exercise plan.
- Credentials remain active after retirement: record incomplete offboarding and request authorized correction and readback.
- Recovery evidence covers only the hosted service: keep device and process outcomes separate and unverified.

Use [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md) assertions: supported, not_supported, inconclusive, not_applicable and not_tested. Keep architecture approval, configuration evidence and operational effectiveness distinct.

## Cadence and renewal

Set review intervals through approved risk and operational obligations. Trigger review on new connectivity, updates, supplier changes, incidents and invalidated assumptions. Do not invent universal quarterly reviews or annual certification duties.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver the full connected-service boundary, responsibility decisions, path evidence, approved failure assumptions, test coverage and open actions with owners and dates. Fictional author desk case: a gateway is described as telemetry-only, but its hosted management service can send configuration commands. The telemetry-only assertion is not_supported. Record the command path and request authorized risk/design review rather than disabling it without operational approval.
