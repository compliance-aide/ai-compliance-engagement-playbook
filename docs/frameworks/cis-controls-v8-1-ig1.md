# CIS Controls v8.1 Implementation Group 1 engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not CIS Controls text, a safeguard selection, a security decision, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through CIS’s [Implementation Group 1 resource](https://www.cisecurity.org/controls/implementation-groups/ig1).

## Engagement focus

Maintain a right-sized foundational-security engagement record that connects enterprise scope, accountable business and technology owners, asset and service evidence, prioritized improvement decisions, exceptions, remediation, operating evidence, and independent review. Center the annual rhythm on practical evidence of operating security while preventing a generated summary from being mistaken for a control assessment or implementation approval.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Business leadership, IT, security, privacy, risk, and service owners retain accountable decisions; evidence owners preserve authorized records; independent reviewers test scope, priority rationale, evidence traceability, and unresolved exceptions. AI may organize authorized evidence, identify stale records, draft questions, and prepare non-authoritative workpapers, but cannot choose an implementation group, select or approve safeguards, determine sufficiency, accept risk, alter production configuration, initiate scans or changes, or claim compliance. Review evidence quarterly, reassess the organization’s fit annually, and reopen the plan before material asset, service, threat, incident, business, or technology changes.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use CIS’s official [Implementation Group 1 resource](https://www.cisecurity.org/controls/implementation-groups/ig1) as the source locator; a human source owner confirms current status, version, and permitted use before each cycle. Retrieved 2026-07-31. Do not copy CIS Controls language, safeguard lists, mappings, or branded assessment material; this plan uses only original evidence objectives and organization-created records.

### 1. Foundational-scope and accountable-service record

- **Request and owner:** A right-sized organization/service inventory, accountable business and technology owners, asset/service criticality inputs, user/workforce boundary, and documented rationale for the engagement boundary from IT and business owners. Cover active in-scope services and additions since the prior review.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected service to an owner, current inventory record, boundary rationale, and date of human review. This can support a transparent foundational scope; it cannot prove every asset is captured, select an implementation group, or determine that the scope is sufficient.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved inventories and flag missing ownership or stale review dates. Humans approve scope and prioritization. Refresh for material asset, service, workforce, business, or technology change and annually.

### 2. Practical-operating-evidence trace

- **Request and owner:** Original operational records appropriate to the approved scope—such as asset-accountability, identity-administration, update/change, backup/recovery, security-event follow-up, or workforce-awareness records—plus period, population, custodian, and integrity metadata from operational owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect a human-approved sample across the declared period, tracing it from the service and responsible owner to dated source evidence and any follow-up. Use least-privilege, redacted access. This can support that the stated practices leave observable evidence; it cannot prove uninterrupted effectiveness, all safeguards, or a compliance result.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize approved metadata, detect gaps in dates or ownership, and draft follow-up questions. Humans set methods, approve changes, and interpret results. Recollect after a failed test, incident, material change, or quarterly review.

### 3. Improvement-decision and exception record

- **Request and owner:** Prioritized improvement backlog, decision rationale, named owner, due date, exception/risk record, management approval, and retest or closure evidence from security, IT, and business leaders.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected deferred item or exception to its human decision, time limit, stated boundary, mitigation, and follow-up outcome. This can support accountable follow-through; it cannot accept risk, establish adequacy, or replace independent review.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag overdue items and prepare a read-only status view. Humans approve priorities, exceptions, and closure. Trigger on overdue remediation, exception expiry, material incident, or annual reassessment.
