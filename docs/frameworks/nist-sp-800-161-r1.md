# NIST SP 800-161 Revision 1 — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not supplier certification. Check the current
> [NIST supply-chain publication](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/161/r1/upd1/final)
> before each cycle.

## Engagement focus

Treat suppliers, components, services, and dependencies as a governed business
process. Map material dependencies, distinguish verified facts from supplier
assertions, define escalation thresholds, review lifecycle exposure, collect
proportionate assurance, and exercise a meaningful disruption scenario.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Procurement, security, and service teams own dependency records and mitigation.
Independent reviewers test inventory and criticality logic. AI may organize authorized evidence,
flag concentration and stale assertions, and prepare renewal questions; it
cannot infer supplier compliance from a logo or questionnaire, approve a supplier, accept risk, or alter a dependency. Reassess the
portfolio annually and before critical renewals.


## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [NIST SP 800-161 Rev. 1 publication page](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/161/r1/upd1/final); checked 2026-07-31. This original plan does not reproduce NIST publication text or determine supplier compliance, authorization, or risk acceptance.

### 1. Dependency inventory and criticality evidence

- **Request and owner:** Procurement, architecture, and service owners provide material supplier, component, service, and dependency records; service maps; criticality rationale; ownership; and records distinguishing verified facts from supplier assertions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace selected critical dependencies to authoritative service ownership, use context, source of assertion, criticality rationale, and last review. This can test traceability of the inventory; it cannot prove that every dependency is known or safe.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare authorized inventory fields, flag unknown owners or stale attestations, and group concentration signals. Humans approve criticality and inventory dispositions. Refresh for new critical suppliers, components, service dependencies, acquisitions, or architecture changes.

### 2. Supplier assurance and flow-down evidence

- **Request and owner:** Procurement and security supply proportionate due-diligence records, contractual/security requirements, subcontractor or flow-down evidence where applicable, supplier notices, assessment results, and open issue owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample a supplier relationship from requirement to assurance artifact, evaluation date, owner, limitation, and follow-up. This supports a bounded record review; it cannot validate a supplier's real-world security or make a procurement decision.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index permitted evidence and flag expiring reviews or unassigned issues. Procurement, security, and legal humans decide supplier eligibility, contract language, and exceptions. Recollect before critical renewals and after material supplier changes.

### 3. Disruption, vulnerability, and governance evidence

- **Request and owner:** Resilience and security owners provide supplier vulnerability/advisory handling, incident/escalation records, alternate-source or continuity plans, disruption-exercise results, mitigation register, and risk decisions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected disruption or advisory from intake through impact analysis, responsible decision-maker, mitigation, communication, and exercise or retest evidence. This can show a governed response path; it cannot predict disruption or accept supply-chain risk.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag concentration, advisory, and deadline signals in approved data; it cannot contact suppliers, alter dependencies, or close a risk. Reassess annually, before critical renewals, and after a material disruption or vulnerability.
