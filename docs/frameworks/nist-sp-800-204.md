# NIST SP 800-204 microservices security engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST text, an architecture approval, a production-release decision, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through the [NIST SP 800-204 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/204/final).

## Engagement focus

Maintain a microservices security engagement record that connects service inventory, ownership, interfaces, identity and access assumptions, communication paths, platform dependencies, deployment and runtime evidence, monitoring, resilience behaviors, third-party components, exceptions, and change decisions. Treat an architecture diagram or a platform feature as context—not proof that all service interactions are protected or that an application is safe to release.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Product, application, platform, cloud, security, reliability, privacy, data, risk, and supplier owners retain accountable decisions. Service owners maintain business and interface context; platform owners preserve deployment evidence; security owners govern architecture and risk decisions; release authorities approve changes; and operations owners handle runtime response. Independent reviewers sample material service paths from owner and interface through deployment evidence, runtime monitoring, change history, exceptions, and corrective actions, then challenge unverified assumptions and shadow dependencies.

AI may inventory authorized service metadata, trace approved interface records, identify missing ownership or evidence, summarize changes, draft review questions, and prepare non-authoritative workpapers. AI cannot alter configuration, create service credentials, modify traffic paths, deploy code, trigger production changes, decide architecture, approve a release, accept risk, or claim compliance. Reassess before material service, interface, identity, platform, supplier, deployment, incident, or data-flow changes; conduct quarterly reviews and an annual independent readiness review.
