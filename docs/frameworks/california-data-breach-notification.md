# California data-breach-notification engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not California statutory text, legal advice, a breach determination, notice advice, or a compliance claim. Confirm current law through [California Civil Code section 1798.82](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&medium=ump&sectionNum=1798.82), the [California Attorney General reporting guidance](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/databreach/reporting), and qualified counsel.

## Engagement focus

Maintain an incident-readiness record that connects relevant data systems and owners, event intake, investigation scope, evidence preservation, decision authority, affected-population analysis, communications, regulator-facing records, recovery actions, and review dates. Preserve a dated facts-and-decisions timeline, including uncertainty and source limitations. Do not let a template, timeline calculation, or a draft notice stand in for a human legal decision about an event, scope, timing, content, recipient, or submission.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Legal, privacy, security, incident response, technology, records, communications, business, and executive owners retain accountable decisions. Incident leaders coordinate fact collection; system owners maintain technical evidence; counsel directs legal interpretation and notification decisions; authorized signatories submit required materials; and auditors evaluate evidence in their independent role. Independent reviewers exercise the workflow and sample incidents from alert through investigation, evidence preservation, decision records, communications, recovery, lessons learned, and retained support, challenging missing facts, unclear authority, stale contact paths, and unsupported closure.

AI may organize authorized metadata, identify missing owners or timeline fields, draft factual questions, compare retained evidence with an approved workflow, and prepare non-authoritative workpapers. AI cannot determine whether an event is a breach, decide notification or reporting duties, calculate a legal deadline as a conclusion, submit a notice, give legal advice, transmit personal information, alter evidence, or claim compliance. Reassess before material data, system, provider, incident, contact, or legal changes; exercise incident readiness periodically and conduct an annual independent readiness review.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use [California Civil Code section 1798.82](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&medium=ump&sectionNum=1798.82), [California Attorney General reporting guidance](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/databreach/reporting), and qualified counsel; checked 2026-07-31. This is original incident-readiness planning, not statutory text, a breach conclusion, a notification timetable, or a regulator submission.

### 1. Data-system and incident-intake record

- **Request and owner:** System/data inventory, data-classification and owner records, security-alert intake, triage criteria, incident roles/contact roster, preservation procedure, and exercise evidence from security, privacy, records, and incident-response owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected alert from intake to system owner, known data context, investigation assignment, preservation action, and dated fact log. This supports reconstruction of an investigation; it cannot establish that the event meets a legal threshold or that the data population is complete.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index redacted metadata and flag missing timestamps or owners. Counsel and incident leaders decide investigation scope and legal characterization. Refresh after a material system/data change, exercise, or event.

### 2. Investigation, population, and decision record

- **Request and owner:** Investigation notes, access/log-preservation references, affected-population methodology and assumptions, decision log, counsel-directed questions, escalation records, and uncertainty register from the incident leader and counsel.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected decision to dated facts, evidence locations, accountable authority, assumptions, and follow-up. This supports a defensible decision trail; it cannot calculate a legal deadline, determine notice duty, or validate each affected person.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare approved fields for gaps and prepare redacted chronology workpapers. Humans approve scope, notification/reporting decisions, and any external communication. Revisit when evidence changes or an authority requests clarification.

### 3. Notice, reporting, recovery, and review record

- **Request and owner:** Approved communication versions, recipient/source records, submission authorization and receipt where applicable, recovery/remediation plan, exercise or incident after-action report, and management-review actions from communications, legal, security, and executive owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected communication or corrective action to approval, evidence basis, audience, completion evidence, and retest. This can show governed execution; it cannot prove delivery, cure a defect, or make a compliance claim.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag stale contacts and open actions but may not send notices, submit reports, or close the incident. Authorized humans approve communications, submissions, risk decisions, and closure; review after every event and annually.
