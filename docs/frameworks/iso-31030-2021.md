# ISO 31030:2021 travel-risk management — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not ISO content, safety advice, or a travel authorization. Check the [ISO record](https://www.iso.org/standard/54204.html); do not place ISO text in AI prompts.

## Engagement focus

Maintain travel scope, traveller categories, destinations, threat and health inputs, risk decisions, advisories, vendor dependencies, emergency contacts, incident records, and improvement evidence.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Travel, safety, security, HR, and authorized managers make risk and travel decisions; independent reviewers test programme evidence. AI tracks approved inputs and flags outdated information, but cannot direct travel, determine safety, or make emergency decisions. Review destination risks continuously and programme annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Confirm the edition at the [ISO 31030:2021 record](https://www.iso.org/standard/54204.html); checked 2026-07-31. ISO material is licensed. This original plan identifies evidence categories only; it is not travel, security, medical, or legal advice and does not authorize travel or claim conformity.

### 1. Programme scope and traveller-decision package

- **Request and owner:** Travel, safety, security, HR, and authorized business owners provide the programme boundary, traveller categories, approval authorities, destination and trip categories, applicable policy records, and material-change register.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one trip category or traveller class to its defined scope, accountable decision owner, and stated approval boundary. This supports governance traceability; it cannot determine travel suitability, duty of care, or an individual's fitness to travel.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved traveller and trip metadata and flag missing ownership. Humans define scope, approve travel decisions, and resolve exceptions. Refresh after changes to operating regions, traveller populations, travel providers, or applicable policy.

### 2. Destination assessment and preparation package

- **Request and owner:** Security and travel owners provide approved destination-information sources, assessment records, traveller briefing/acknowledgement evidence, itinerary/contact records where appropriately protected, supplier support arrangements, and exceptions.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect a selected destination or trip record for a current approved information source, human assessment or approval, preparation evidence, and protected escalation path. This tests record linkage; it cannot validate threat accuracy, direct a traveller, or make an emergency decision.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag stale source dates or missing acknowledgements within approved access limits, but may not issue safety advice, approve a trip, or expose sensitive itinerary data. Recollect after a material destination event, changed itinerary, or source-status change.

### 3. Incident, assistance, and improvement package

- **Request and owner:** Travel, security, HR, and leadership owners provide incident/assistance records, escalation logs, decision authorities, post-event reviews, corrective actions, supplier follow-up, and programme-review evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one incident or assistance case from intake through accountable human decisions, support/escalation records, protected follow-up, and action status. This supports accountability; it cannot judge emergency response quality, determine safety, or authorize closure.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize non-sensitive case metadata and flag overdue actions. Humans direct assistance, communicate with travellers, accept residual risk, and approve closure or public statements. Review after material events and close the annual cycle with independent sampling.
