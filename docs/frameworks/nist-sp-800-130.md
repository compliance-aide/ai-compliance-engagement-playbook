# NIST SP 800-130 cryptographic key-management-system engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST CKMS content, a key-management-system design specification, a cryptographic approval, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-130](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/130/final) and the organization’s approved security, cryptography, architecture, procurement, legal, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Operate a governed design, implementation, and lifecycle-assurance process for an in-scope cryptographic key management system. Maintain traceability from business and data-protection needs through governance, policies, architecture, key and metadata flows, components, interfaces, access, audit records, supplier dependencies, change control, incidents, and lifecycle decisions. A design document, architecture diagram, test result, or supplier statement is evidence to assess; it does not prove a system is correctly designed, keys are protected, requirements are met, or compliance.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, security, cryptography or platform engineering, architecture, key-management operations, identity, application and data owners, procurement, supplier-management, legal and privacy where relevant, change-management, incident-response, and records-management roles. Operators maintain scoped service and component inventories, approved design and policy records, interface and key-lifecycle traceability, access and audit evidence, test and review artifacts, supplier assurance, change approvals, exception decisions, incident records, and lifecycle-retirement evidence. Reconcile components, interfaces, owners, and suppliers quarterly; review access, key-lifecycle anomalies, design or policy changes, supplier notices, exceptions, incidents, and unresolved risks at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material architecture, system, provider, cryptographic, or threat changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples design-to-operation-to-review traceability; auditors test the evidence trail without designing a CKMS, accessing or operating keys, changing production configuration, approving exceptions, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied design, inventory, lifecycle, supplier, and review evidence, identify missing ownership or stale decisions, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot design or operate a CKMS, access or derive keys, change production configuration, approve an exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.
