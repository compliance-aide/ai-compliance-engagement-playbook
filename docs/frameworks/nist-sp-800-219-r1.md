# NIST SP 800-219 Revision 1 macOS-configuration engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST or mSCP content, a configuration baseline, an authorization to change endpoints, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-219 Revision 1](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/219/r1/final), current platform information, and the organization's approved security, privacy, legal, operational, and change decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round engagement for in-scope macOS endpoint configuration and assessment. Maintain the relationship among device populations, operating-system versions, owners, approved baselines, local tailoring decisions, configuration artifacts, deployment tools, assessment outputs, exceptions, changes, support status, and retirement. Treat automated configuration and assessment as a controlled operating capability, not as an automatic proof of secure or compliant endpoints.

Preserve the human rationale behind every baseline selection and local deviation. A generated profile, script output, assessment score, inventory export, management-console report, or remediation ticket is evidence to evaluate against actual scope, version, device state, user impact, and authorized decisions. Keep records of how findings are confirmed, prioritized, changed, deferred, or closed, including where an endpoint is unavailable or cannot safely receive a change.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Endpoint, security, IT operations, system, privacy, legal, accessibility, help-desk, change, asset, and records owners establish scope and make accountable decisions. Operators maintain inventory and ownership, baseline and tailoring records, version coverage, deployment and assessment evidence, exception approvals, remediation history, user-impact records, support status, and disposal evidence. Reconcile device inventory, platform versions, baseline applicability, tool coverage, and exceptions at least quarterly; review new operating-system releases, high-impact findings, and configuration changes as they occur; and perform management review annually.

Independent reviewers sample devices and records from approved baseline through deployment, assessment, exception, and corrective action. Auditors evaluate supplied evidence without changing endpoint settings, running unapproved tools, approving tailoring, accepting risk, authorizing production rollout, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize records, identify stale versions or incomplete evidence, compare supplied results with approved baselines, draft workpapers, and prepare review questions. AI cannot set or approve baselines, alter endpoints, execute remediation, approve exceptions, accept risk, make legal or privacy conclusions, or attest to conformance.


## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** At engagement start, retain the retrieved version and applicable use terms for the official [NIST SP 800-219 Revision 1 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/219/r1/final), current approved platform information, and approved endpoint, privacy, accessibility, legal, operational, and change direction. This is original evidence planning, not reproduced NIST or mSCP content, an endpoint baseline, or authorization to modify a device.

### 1. Population, platform-version, and tailoring package

- **Request and owner:** Endpoint, asset, service, security, privacy, accessibility, help-desk, and system owners provide in-scope device inventory, ownership and use context, operating-system/version coverage, approved baseline selection, local tailoring rationale, applicability decisions, and exception register.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one device cohort or representative device from inventory and platform version through approved baseline applicability, tailoring decision, responsible owner, and current exception state. This can test documented scope and tailoring traceability; it cannot establish that the baseline suits every user or authorizes a configuration change.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile supplied inventory and assessment metadata and flag stale versions, absent owners, or unsupported tailoring links. Humans select baselines, decide applicability, approve tailoring, and evaluate user impact. Refresh after a platform release, device-population change, new management method, accessibility/privacy issue, or baseline change.

### 2. Deployment, assessment, and remediation-observation package

- **Request and owner:** Endpoint engineering, IT operations, security, system, help-desk, and change owners provide deployment records, approved profiles or scripts, change approvals, assessment outputs, device-state evidence, findings, remediation tickets, rollback/support records, and deferred-change decisions.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample one approved setting or finding from deployment evidence through assessment output, human confirmation, remediation or deferral decision, and resulting device/support record. This can assess the supplied operating trail; it cannot run a tool, alter endpoint settings, prove all devices match a baseline, or validate the safety of a remediation.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may sort supplied results, identify incomplete evidence chains, and draft questions about unconfirmed findings. Humans authorize rollout, validate findings, perform remediation, and decide rollback. Recollect after failed deployment, material assessment change, recurring finding, endpoint incident, or user-impact escalation.

### 3. Exception, lifecycle, and independent-challenge package

- **Request and owner:** Endpoint, security, risk, privacy, legal, asset, records, support, and independent-review owners provide exception approvals, compensating-action records, support/end-of-life status, retirement evidence, corrective-action register, retest records, and management review decisions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected exception, unsupported version, or retirement action to accountable human authority, stated limitation, expiry or target date, completion/retest evidence, and disposition. This can support lifecycle governance review; it cannot accept risk, make legal/privacy conclusions, or attest to configuration conformance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag expiring exceptions, unsupported versions, and overdue remediation while preparing a source-linked review packet. Humans approve risk, exception renewal, retirement, closure, and external statements. Review quarterly and after platform support, incident, privacy/legal, asset, or material operating change.
