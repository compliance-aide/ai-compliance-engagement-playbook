# NIST SP 800-232 lightweight-cryptography engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST lightweight-cryptography content, a cryptographic design, a device authorization, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-232](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/232/final) and the organization's approved security, cryptographic, engineering, safety, legal, operational, and change decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round engagement for approved lightweight cryptography in constrained devices, embedded systems, and related services. Connect each use to its device and service scope, mission, data and communication paths, cryptographic design decision, implementation and version, key or secret handling, resource constraints, software and hardware dependencies, supplier support, validation, monitoring, incident response, and eventual replacement or retirement.

Treat a hardware specification, implementation record, library inventory, test output, configuration, validation report, supplier statement, or device telemetry as evidence to evaluate in context. None alone establishes algorithm suitability, implementation correctness, safe key handling, resilience under actual constraints, or authorization for deployment. Preserve the decision trail from design and threat considerations through implementation, testing, rollout, operation, change, exception, and remediation.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Cryptographic, security, device engineering, product, operations, key-management, safety, supplier, legal, privacy, incident-response, and records owners establish scope and make accountable decisions. Operators maintain device and service inventories, approved designs, implementation and version traceability, key-handling evidence, validation and test records, supplier support records, configuration changes, exceptions, incidents, and transition plans. Reconcile active uses, device versions, implementations, owners, suppliers, and evidence currency at least quarterly; review material vulnerabilities, device changes, and cryptographic guidance as they arise; and conduct management review annually.

Independent reviewers sample a use from approved purpose and design through implementation, device deployment, and operating evidence. Auditors assess supplied materials without accessing secret key material, changing device settings, approving cryptographic designs, authorizing deployment, accepting risk, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize evidence, identify stale versions or missing traceability, draft workpapers, compare supplied records with approved decisions, and prepare review questions. AI cannot select or approve cryptographic designs, generate or access key material, alter devices, validate cryptographic correctness, accept risk, make safety or legal conclusions, or attest to conformance.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [NIST SP 800-232 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/232/final), retaining the retrieved version and applicable use terms; checked 2026-07-31. This original evidence plan does not reproduce NIST material, select or validate an algorithm, prescribe device parameters, handle key material, authorize deployment, or make a conformance claim.

### 1. Device mission, constraint, and design-boundary package

- **Request and owner:** Device engineering, product, system, security, safety, and cryptography owners provide device/service inventory, mission and deployment context, non-secret resource-constraint record, communication/data-flow reference, approved design-boundary decision, and accountable owner list.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one device class or service use from mission and deployment context through its stated constraints, data/communication path, design decision reference, and owners. This checks documentary linkage; it cannot determine suitability, safety, cryptographic strength, or deployment approval.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize supplied metadata and flag missing device versions, constraints, or owners. Humans decide architecture, safety boundaries, and approved cryptographic design. Refresh after device revision, deployment environment, mission, communication path, or constraint change.

### 2. Implementation, key-boundary, and deployment package

- **Request and owner:** Engineering, platform, key-management, manufacturing, and operations owners provide implementation/version provenance, build/release identifiers, non-secret configuration references, key-management interface records, acceptance/test references, and controlled deployment evidence.
- **Validate and limit:** Link one deployed version to its approved change, implementation provenance, declared key boundary, acceptance evidence, and deployment record. This cannot inspect or generate secrets, execute cryptography, validate implementation correctness, or authorize deployment.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag absent provenance or stale acceptance records and draft questions. Humans approve builds, device settings, deployments, and access to protected material. Recollect after a firmware/software release, key-service change, failed acceptance check, or manufacturing change.

### 3. Support, incident, and end-of-life package

- **Request and owner:** Asset, security, supplier, device operations, incident-response, safety, records, and risk owners provide support/end-of-support records, vulnerability and incident references, update/change evidence, exception decisions, device-replacement plans, disposal/retirement records, and management-review minutes.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample a deployed device lifecycle event from support status through update, incident, exception, or retirement evidence and its accountable decision. This supports lifecycle traceability; it cannot prove device security, safe operation, vulnerability absence, or acceptable residual risk.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may identify unsupported versions, aging exceptions, and missing replacement owners. Humans decide continued use, remediation, safety actions, notification, retirement, and risk acceptance. Review quarterly and after vulnerability, incident, supplier-support change, device failure, or material cryptographic guidance change.
