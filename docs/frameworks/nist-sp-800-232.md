# NIST SP 800-232 lightweight-cryptography engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST lightweight-cryptography content, a cryptographic design, a device authorization, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-232](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/232/final) and the organization's approved security, cryptographic, engineering, safety, legal, operational, and change decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round engagement for approved lightweight cryptography in constrained devices, embedded systems, and related services. Connect each use to its device and service scope, mission, data and communication paths, cryptographic design decision, implementation and version, key or secret handling, resource constraints, software and hardware dependencies, supplier support, validation, monitoring, incident response, and eventual replacement or retirement.

Treat a hardware specification, implementation record, library inventory, test output, configuration, validation report, supplier statement, or device telemetry as evidence to evaluate in context. None alone establishes algorithm suitability, implementation correctness, safe key handling, resilience under actual constraints, or authorization for deployment. Preserve the decision trail from design and threat considerations through implementation, testing, rollout, operation, change, exception, and remediation.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Cryptographic, security, device engineering, product, operations, key-management, safety, supplier, legal, privacy, incident-response, and records owners establish scope and make accountable decisions. Operators maintain device and service inventories, approved designs, implementation and version traceability, key-handling evidence, validation and test records, supplier support records, configuration changes, exceptions, incidents, and transition plans. Reconcile active uses, device versions, implementations, owners, suppliers, and evidence currency at least quarterly; review material vulnerabilities, device changes, and cryptographic guidance as they arise; and conduct management review annually.

Independent reviewers sample a use from approved purpose and design through implementation, device deployment, and operating evidence. Auditors assess supplied materials without accessing secret key material, changing device settings, approving cryptographic designs, authorizing deployment, accepting risk, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize evidence, identify stale versions or missing traceability, draft workpapers, compare supplied records with approved decisions, and prepare review questions. AI cannot select or approve cryptographic designs, generate or access key material, alter devices, validate cryptographic correctness, accept risk, make safety or legal conclusions, or attest to conformance.
