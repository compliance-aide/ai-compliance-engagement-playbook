# NIST SP 800-126 Revision 4 security-content-automation engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST SCAP content, an automated assessment result, a configuration authorization, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-126 Revision 4](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/126/r4/final), associated current specifications, and the organization's approved security, privacy, legal, operational, and change decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round engagement for the controlled use of machine-readable security configuration and vulnerability content. Maintain the relationship among scope, assets, content sources, versions, provenance, integrity, evaluation tools, target environments, execution conditions, output records, error handling, findings workflow, exceptions, and retention. Treat automation as a repeatable evidence channel that needs governance and validation, not as a substitute for human assessment or an automatic determination of compliance.

Preserve how security content is selected, approved, tested, distributed, executed, interpreted, and retired. A data stream, schema-validation result, tool log, automated finding, content signature, report, or configuration comparison is evidence to evaluate in context. Record target coverage, collection failures, version drift, false-positive or false-negative review, access restrictions, remediation decisions, and the limitations that affect how results may be used.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Security, configuration, vulnerability-management, asset, platform, engineering, operations, privacy, legal, supplier, assessment, change, and records owners establish scope and make accountable decisions. Operators maintain asset and target inventories, approved content and tool records, provenance and integrity evidence, execution logs, output retention, finding and exception records, failure handling, remediation tracking, and deprecation plans. Reconcile assets, content versions, tools, owners, execution coverage, and open findings at least quarterly; review material changes before production use; and conduct management review annually.

Independent reviewers sample from approved content and scope through execution conditions, outputs, interpretation, and follow-up. Auditors evaluate supplied evidence without running unapproved content, changing configurations, declaring findings, approving exceptions, accepting risk, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize outputs, identify missing provenance or stale content, correlate evidence, draft workpapers, and prepare review questions. AI cannot approve or sign content, execute changes, determine final findings, authorize remediation, accept risk, make legal conclusions, or attest to conformance.
