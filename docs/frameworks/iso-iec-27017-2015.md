# ISO/IEC 27017:2015 — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance for cloud assurance, not an ISO publication or compliance claim.
> Use authorized material and monitor the [ISO version record](https://www.iso.org/standard/43757.html).

## Engagement focus

Maintain cloud-service inventory, responsibility decisions, data/service
criticality, contracts, and owners. Review architecture, access, configuration,
monitoring, incident interfaces, portability, exit, supplier artifacts, change,
and exceptions using approved organizational methods.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Operators own customer-side administration and evidence. Independent reviewers
evaluate accountability and evidence credibility. AI may link authorized inventory and evidence,
flag stale provider artifacts, and summarize change; it cannot assume provider
responsibility, alter cloud configuration, approve a supplier decision, or assert compliance. Review monthly/quarterly and re-evaluate when
the successor edition is released.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Confirm the applicable edition and successor status through the [ISO/IEC 27017:2015 record](https://www.iso.org/standard/43757.html) and use authorized material only as allowed; checked 2026-07-31. ### 1. Cloud-service scope and shared-responsibility package

- **Request and owner:** Cloud service, architecture, procurement, and security owners provide the cloud-service inventory, data/service criticality, approved responsibility decisions, account/tenant ownership, and relevant contract references.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected material service to its business owner, technical owner, provider relationship, and documented responsibility decision. This supports a usable accountability boundary; it cannot establish that a provider performs a responsibility or that every service is in scope.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile inventory and ownership records and flag gaps. Humans approve service use and responsibility decisions. Refresh on provider, architecture, contract, data-classification, or material service change.

### 2. Cloud operation and supplier-interface package

- **Request and owner:** Operations and supplier owners provide approved configuration/change records, access-review evidence, monitoring/incident handoff records, provider notices or attestations, and exception records.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect a selected operational change or incident handoff from accountable request through evidence, review, exception, and follow-up. This supports observable operation in the sample; it cannot prove provider controls, continuous operation, or contract performance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index authorized artifacts and flag stale provider evidence; it may not change configurations or decide provider performance. Recollect after an incident, provider notice, failed review, or material service change.

### 3. Portability, exit, and governance package

- **Request and owner:** Service, privacy, procurement, and continuity owners provide portability/exit assumptions, data-return or deletion expectations, tested transition records where available, open risks, and leadership decisions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected exit dependency to owner, contract reference, tested or untested status, mitigation, and decision authority. This supports planning transparency; it cannot demonstrate a successful exit or accept residual dependency risk.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag unowned exit assumptions and assemble a review briefing. Humans approve supplier strategy, residual risk, funding, and external claims. Review at least annually and after a provider or contract change.
