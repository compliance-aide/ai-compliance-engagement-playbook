# ISO/IEC 27031:2025 ICT readiness — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not ISO content or a continuity assurance conclusion. Check the [ISO record](https://www.iso.org/standard/80975.html); do not place ISO text in AI prompts.

## Engagement focus

Maintain critical-service dependencies, recovery objectives, readiness design, supplier commitments, continuity tests, incident-to-recovery records, exception decisions, improvement actions, and evidence of exercised capability.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Business and technology owners set recovery priorities and approve plans; independent reviewers test exercise evidence. AI correlates dependencies and flags stale assumptions, but cannot declare readiness or activate recovery. Test material scenarios quarterly and assess annually.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Confirm the edition through the [ISO/IEC 27031:2025 record](https://www.iso.org/standard/80975.html) and use licensed material only as authorized; checked 2026-07-31. This original plan does not reproduce ISO/IEC text or make an ICT-readiness, recovery, or certification conclusion.

### 1. Critical-service dependency and objective package

- **Request and owner:** Business and technology owners provide critical-service inventory, dependency maps, recovery priorities/objectives, designated owners, supplier commitments, and documented assumptions.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected critical service to a business owner, technical dependencies, stated recovery priority, and approved assumption. This supports planning traceability; it cannot prove the stated objective is achievable or the dependency map is complete.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare dependency records and flag missing ownership or stale assumptions. Humans approve priorities and objectives. Refresh after material architecture, supplier, service, or business change.

### 2. Readiness design and recovery exercise package

- **Request and owner:** Technology and continuity owners provide recovery design/version records, configuration or runbook references, exercise plans, participants, timestamps, observations, recovery evidence, and identified gaps.
- **Validate and limit:** Inspect a selected exercise from preconditions through human-authorized execution, observed results, exception, and corrective action. This supports performance under the tested conditions; it cannot demonstrate live recovery capability or authorize a recovery action.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize evidence and identify untested dependencies; it may not execute recovery steps or sign off outcomes. Recollect after a failed test, disruption, material design change, or supplier change.

### 3. Exception, improvement, and executive decision package

- **Request and owner:** Continuity leadership provides open readiness gaps, temporary exceptions, mitigations, retest dates, risk decisions, resource commitments, and annual management-review records.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected gap to owner, decision authority, mitigation, expiry, retest evidence, and executive review. This supports accountable governance; it cannot accept risk, close an exception, or replace independent review.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag expired exceptions and prepare a source-linked briefing. Humans approve residual risk, funding, recovery decisions, and external claims. Review quarterly and close annually.
