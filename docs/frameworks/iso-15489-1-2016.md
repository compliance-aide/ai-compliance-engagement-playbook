# ISO 15489-1:2016 records management — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not ISO text, legal advice or authority to destroy records.

## Source and applicability

Use the [official ISO edition record](https://www.iso.org/standard/62542.html). Indexed publisher content checked 2026-09-05 identifies the 2016 edition as concepts and principles for creating, capturing and managing records across forms and environments. The full licensed standard was not reviewed. Confirm source-use permission before using protected content with AI; do not place ISO text in prompts without permission.

Qualified records and legal owners must establish applicable recordkeeping obligations, retention, holds and archival requirements. This guide prescribes original operational evidence, not a universal retention period or certification test.

## Engagement focus

Preserve records with enough provenance, context, integrity and accessibility to support the business activity they document. A stored file is not necessarily a complete usable record. An unchanged checksum does not prove the original content was true, and an accurate migration count does not prove relationships and meaning survived.

## Roles

Records owners define classes, metadata and custody. Business owners identify what must evidence their activities. Legal and archival authorities decide holds, retention and disposition. Repository owners implement approved access, preservation and migration controls. An independent reviewer tests traceability and retrieval. AI may organize approved metadata and flag gaps, but cannot declare legal authenticity, change holds, approve classification, dispose of records or publish restricted material.

## Before starting

Record processes, record classes, systems, physical holdings, custodians, period and intended use. Obtain approved inventories, capture rules, metadata definitions, access models, schedules, holds and migration history. Identify sensitive information and authorized evidence repositories. Keep restricted records private; a public workpaper may reference them without reproducing their contents.

## Ordered workflow

1. **Reconcile scope and record requirements.** Account for every known scoped record-producing process and repository, including third parties, messaging, attachments and physical records where relevant. Link required evidence of each activity to its owner and approved capture point. Preserve unknown coverage without silent sampling, trimming or caps.
2. **Verify capture and context.** Map records to stable IDs, creator/source, activity, event time, capture time, relationships and version/correction history. Inspect whether attachments and metadata travel with the record. Distinguish an original, copy, derivative and corrected record. Do not infer completeness from a successful file upload.
3. **Apply approved classification and controls.** Link record classes to access, retention triggers, disposition authority and relevant holds. Have qualified owners resolve conflicts before implementation. Record exceptions and their scope; changing a class must not silently shorten retention or remove a hold. AI may flag inconsistencies but does not approve the controlling rule.
4. **Test retrieval and preservation.** Use authorized read-only or synthetic cases to confirm intended users can retrieve the correct record with its context, while unauthorized access is prevented. Record format/software dependencies, integrity evidence and recovery results. A backup job success is not proof of restoration or readability.
5. **Validate migrations before source retirement.** Establish the full source manifest, destination mapping, metadata/relationship rules, acceptance criteria and rollback plan. Reconcile missing, duplicated, transformed and failed items after approved execution. Check content usability as well as counts and hashes. Keep source retirement blocked until the authorized owner resolves acceptance and hold requirements.
6. **Prepare controlled disposition.** Reconcile each proposed disposition set with approved schedules, trigger evidence, current holds and all known copies. Obtain exact-scope authorization before destruction or archival transfer. Preserve execution evidence, failures and remaining locations. Age alone is not authorization; a deletion request is not proof of completion.
7. **Review issues and hand off.** Link loss, unauthorized access, metadata gaps and failed retrieval to corrective actions, owners and verification. Preserve original records and append traceable corrections rather than rewriting historical events. Deliver an accountable record of what is retained, transferred, disposed, unverified or still under hold.

## Evidence and test plan

The three packages preserve the earlier tailored plan with complete known population accounting.

| Package | Minimum records | Reviewer challenge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scope, ownership and classification | Processes, record classes, repositories, capture points, custodians, metadata and exclusions | Can each required activity be traced to its record and context? |
| Retention, holds and disposition | Approved schedules, trigger evidence, hold scope, exceptions, exact authorizations and outcomes | Did the controlling rule reach every affected record and copy? |
| Preservation, migration and oversight | Source/destination manifests, relationship mapping, integrity/readability checks, access, recovery and remediation | Do retained records remain usable and attributable after change? |

Apply the [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md) statuses `supported`, `not_supported`, `inconclusive`, `not_applicable` and `not_tested` to individual assertions. Split count reconciliation, content integrity, contextual completeness and legal disposition. Complete inventory accounting does not mean every format or access scenario was tested.

Fictional author desk case: a migration reports 10,000 source files and 10,000 destination files, but parent-child links between messages and attachments were lost. Count reconciliation can be supported while contextual completeness fails. Preserve the source, assign the migration owner to restore relationships and retest; do not authorize source destruction from the count alone.

## Failure branches and decisions

Conflicting schedules or holds require records/legal resolution before destruction. An inaccessible record remains a retrieval gap even if a storage dashboard shows it exists. Unknown custody requires investigation rather than invented provenance. A failed migration item stays visible in the manifest. An unauthorized disclosure follows the approved incident process without copying the record into an unrestricted issue.

## Cadence and renewal

Set reviews from obligations, format/system risk and approved records plans; this guide invents no quarterly or annual ISO mandate. Reopen after organizational, process, legal, repository, metadata or technology changes. Track disposition triggers and holds independently of periodic governance reviews.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver the scoped register, private evidence references, approved decisions, migration/preservation results and outstanding actions. State limits on authenticity, completeness and long-term readability. Independent source, skeptical and rights review remain pending before publication. Author desk cases are not real migrations, legal determinations or cross-model usability tests.
