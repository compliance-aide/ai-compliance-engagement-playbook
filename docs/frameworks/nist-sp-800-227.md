# NIST SP 800-227 key-encapsulation engagement guide

> This is original operational guidance, not NIST key-encapsulation content, a cryptographic design, a key-management authorization, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-227](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/227/final) and the organization's approved security, cryptographic, architecture, legal, operational, and change decisions.

## Engagement focus

Manage a year-round engagement for the approved use of key-encapsulation mechanisms within in-scope services, products, protocols, and integrations. Connect each use to accountable owners, protocol purpose, cryptographic design decisions, algorithm and implementation versions, key and secret handling, supporting algorithms, libraries, deployment boundaries, supplier dependencies, validation, monitoring, incident response, and retirement or transition planning.

Treat a design document, code review, library inventory, configuration record, test result, validation statement, key-management record, or supplier assertion as evidence to evaluate in context. None alone establishes that a cryptographic mechanism is suitable, correctly implemented, safely operated, or approved for the stated use. Preserve the record of design assumptions, deployment decisions, changes, vulnerabilities, transition triggers, exceptions, and remediation.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Cryptographic, security, architecture, engineering, product, operations, key-management, supplier, legal, privacy, incident-response, and records owners establish scope and make accountable decisions. Operators maintain system and dependency inventories, approved design records, implementation and version traceability, key-handling evidence, test and validation records, supplier notices, configuration changes, exceptions, incident records, and transition plans. Reconcile uses, implementations, libraries, owners, dependencies, and evidence currency at least quarterly; review material vulnerabilities, cryptographic guidance, implementation changes, and incidents as they arise; and conduct management review annually.

Independent reviewers sample a use from approved design through implementation, deployment, and operating evidence. Auditors evaluate supplied records without handling secret key material, changing cryptographic settings, approving designs, accepting risk, authorizing production use, or issuing an attestation. AI may organize evidence, identify missing traceability or aging dependencies, draft workpapers, compare supplied records with approved decisions, and prepare review questions. AI cannot choose or approve cryptographic designs, generate or access key material, change implementations, validate cryptographic correctness, accept risk, make legal conclusions, or attest to conformance.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the official [NIST SP 800-227 publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/227/final), retaining the retrieved version and applicable use terms; checked 2026-07-31. This original plan links design and operating evidence without reproducing NIST material, selecting a mechanism, validating cryptographic correctness, handling secrets, or making a conformance claim.

### 1. Protocol-purpose and design-decision package

- **Request and owner:** Cryptography, architecture, application, and security owners provide the scoped use inventory, protocol and trust-boundary description, approved design-decision record, algorithm/library/version references, key-management interface reference, dependency map, and accountable owner list.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one declared use from business/protocol purpose through its approved design record, implementation reference, key-management boundary, and owner. This tests documentary linkage; it cannot choose a mechanism, assess a protocol's security, or approve a design.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize non-secret metadata and flag missing decisions, owners, or dependency links. Humans determine cryptographic design and scope. Refresh after protocol, library, key-management, integration, or deployment-boundary change.

### 2. Implementation, deployment, and key-boundary package

- **Request and owner:** Engineering, platform, key-management, and operations owners provide code/release traceability, deployment identifiers, protected configuration-access records, implementation test/validation references, and operational runbook references that exclude secret material.
- **Validate and limit:** Link one deployment to an approved change, declared implementation version, key-management interface, controlled configuration reference, and owner. This cannot inspect keys, execute cryptographic operations, validate encapsulation/decapsulation, or establish secure implementation.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may identify stale releases or unlinked key-boundary records and draft review questions. Humans approve releases, configuration changes, and access. Recollect after deployment, key-service change, material test failure, or implementation defect.

### 3. Dependency, vulnerability, and transition package

- **Request and owner:** Security, supplier, cryptography, operations, incident-response, records, and risk owners provide component provenance, supplier/support notices, vulnerability and incident references, exception records, transition triggers, remediation records, and management-review decisions.
- **Validate and limit:** Sample a dependency or transition event from provenance through accountable review, action evidence, and next-review date. This supports lifecycle traceability; it cannot certify a component, determine exploitability, accept residual risk, or authorize continued use.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag aging support records, expiring exceptions, and absent transition owners. Humans decide remediation, exception, transition, communication, and risk acceptance. Review quarterly and after vulnerability, supplier, implementation, threat, or publication changes.
