# Saudi NCA Essential Cybersecurity Controls ECC 2-2024 engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NCA control text, an assessment, a regulatory submission, or a compliance claim. Confirm the current scope and edition through the [NCA Essential Cybersecurity Controls page](https://nca.gov.sa/en/regulatory-documents/controls-list/ecc/).

## Engagement focus

Maintain a scoped cybersecurity engagement record for the national entity: applicability decisions, accountable leadership, information and technology assets, service dependencies, operating evidence, incidents, assessment evidence, remediation, and material changes. Keep the current ECC edition, related NCA extensions, and any sector-specific regime separately identified; do not treat a broad control inventory as proof of implementation.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Accountable entity leadership owns program decisions; cybersecurity and risk teams maintain operating evidence; system and service owners retain technical records; supplier owners retain third-party proof; independent reviewers test provenance and traceability; qualified Saudi counsel interprets applicability. AI may organize approved evidence, flag stale records, and draft review questions, but cannot decide scope, score compliance, perform an assessment, approve risk, submit to NCA, or claim conformity. Review the engagement annually and before material asset, service, supplier, operating-model, or incident changes.

## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Retain the official [NCA Essential Cybersecurity Controls page](https://nca.gov.sa/en/regulatory-documents/controls-list/ecc/), retrieved 2026-07-31, with the specific ECC 2-2024 source/version, status, and use terms confirmed by an authorized entity representative before engagement use. This plan is original operational evidence planning, not NCA control text, a control mapping, a scored assessment, a regulatory submission, or a conformity statement. Do not copy restricted NCA material or publish entity evidence.

### 1. Entity applicability, asset, and accountability package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain the legal/entity and approved operating boundary, accountable leadership and cybersecurity roles, information and technology asset/service inventory, data and hosting context, applicable NCA or sector-program inputs, and counsel-led applicability questions from entity leadership, cybersecurity, legal, and service owners. Set the defined population, evidence period, and exclusions before reviewing technical material.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected asset or service to its accountable owner, approved boundary, dependency/hosting context, and recorded applicability decision or open question. Use sanitized metadata where full technical or entity information is restricted. This supports a defensible engagement scope; it cannot decide NCA applicability, establish entity coverage, or prove that every asset is recorded.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may reconcile approved inventory metadata and flag missing owner, boundary, or review date. Named human leaders and qualified counsel decide scope and escalation. Trigger on a new entity/service, material asset or hosting change, sector direction, acquisition, or annual review.

### 2. Cybersecurity-operation and third-party evidence package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain approved policy/procedure references in original entity language, risk and asset-management records, access/change/monitoring or test references, incident records, supplier/shared-responsibility evidence, exception approvals, and remediation ownership from cybersecurity, technology, operations, procurement, and supplier owners. Keep production outputs and confidential supplier material in their authorized repositories.
- **Validate and limit:** For a selected service, risk, or supplier dependency, inspect the evidence trail from accountable owner and approved decision through time-bounded operating reference, exception, incident or finding, and remediation/retest link. Check timestamps, record integrity, and authorization to access the evidence. This supports traceability of the stated process; it cannot prove operating effectiveness, control coverage, supplier compliance, or ECC conformity.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may organize permitted metadata and flag missing links or overdue dates; it cannot score controls, select treatment, access restricted systems, or modify evidence. Human cybersecurity and supplier owners decide treatment and escalation. Trigger on a material incident, control/test failure, provider change, new dependency, expired exception, or annual evidence refresh.

### 3. Assessment readiness, exception, and regulatory-communication package

- **Request and owner:** Obtain independent-review references, findings, corrective-action plans, authorized risk decisions, retest evidence, management-review records, and any proposed NCA-facing communication approvals from assurance, cybersecurity leadership, legal, and accountable entity leaders. Record unresolved disagreements and external-submission boundaries separately.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace a selected finding or exception from source evidence to independent challenge, accountable owner, approved action or risk decision, due date, and retest or documented closure rationale. Review the approval chain before any external statement. This supports governance and readiness; it cannot perform an NCA assessment, accept risk, authorize a submission, or represent compliance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may prepare a source-linked action queue and identify stale decisions. Independent reviewers challenge evidence; named humans approve risk, closure, and regulatory communications. Trigger on an assessment event, material finding, incident, planned external communication, NCA source update, or annual readiness cycle.
