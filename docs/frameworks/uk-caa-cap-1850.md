# UK CAA CAP 1850 Cyber Assessment Framework for Aviation engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not CAP 1850 framework text, a safety assessment, an aviation-authority determination, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through the [UK Civil Aviation Authority CAP 1850 publication](https://www.caa.co.uk/data-and-publications/publications/documents/content/cap1850/).

## Engagement focus

Maintain an aviation cyber-assurance record that connects critical-system scope, safety and operational context, accountable aviation and technology owners, evidence of operating safeguards, supplier dependencies, security events, corrective work, and independently reviewable readiness. Preserve the distinction between cyber-assurance support and decisions that affect aviation safety, security, airworthiness, or operations.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Accountable aviation leaders, safety and security owners, and designated cyber-responsible managers retain decision rights; system and supplier owners preserve operational evidence; independent reviewers test scope, traceability, and readiness. AI may organize authorized evidence, identify stale records, draft questions, and prepare review packets, but cannot establish the critical-system scope, decide safety or operational acceptability, make a regulatory determination, assign assessment outcomes, approve a flight or service change, or claim compliance. Review annually and before a material aviation-system, service, supplier, safety, security, incident, or regulatory change.


## Tailored evidence plan

**Source and rights snapshot.** Use the current [UK CAA CAP 1850 publication](https://www.caa.co.uk/data-and-publications/publications/documents/content/cap1850/); checked 2026-07-31. This plan is original operational planning, not CAP 1850 text, an aviation safety assessment, regulatory advice, or a compliance claim. Preserve safety-sensitive operational detail, aircraft/airport configurations, incident material, and restricted supplier evidence in approved protected systems.

### 1. Aviation cyber-governance and operational-scope package

- **Request and owner:** Approved aviation service/system context, accountable safety/security interfaces, asset/operational-dependency inventories, criticality/risk records, architecture boundaries, and governance decisions from aviation operations, technology, security, safety, and risk owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one operationally relevant system or service through ownership, dependencies, aviation boundary, risk decision, and review date. This cannot determine safety impact, operational acceptability, or regulatory applicability.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may index authorized inventories and flag missing ownership or stale reviews. Humans determine scope, safety/security interfaces, operational approvals, and risk treatment. Refresh after a material system, operational, supplier, threat, safety, or regulatory change.

### 2. Protective operation, resilience, and incident package

- **Request and owner:** Authorized access/change records, monitoring/test outputs, vulnerability/remediation records, contingency/recovery exercises, incident/occurrence records, and provider performance evidence from technology, security, resilience, and aviation-operations owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Follow one test, disruption, vulnerability, or incident through detection, accountable coordination, operational decision, corrective action, and retest. This cannot establish safe operation, recoverability, or reporting duties.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may create a read-only evidence index and flag missing timing or retests. Humans declare incidents, direct operational responses, approve recovery, and decide regulator communication. Recollect after an incident, failed exercise, material vulnerability, or disruption.

### 3. Assurance, supplier dependency, and remediation package

- **Request and owner:** Supplier assurance/change notices, assessment results, exceptions, remediation plans, management decisions, resource approvals, closure/retest evidence, and independent-review observations from procurement, security, risk, operations, and audit owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Trace one supplier issue, observation, or exception to evidence, accountable decision-maker, mitigation, due date, and independent retest/challenge. This cannot certify a supplier, close remediation, or accept aviation risk.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may identify stale findings and draft factual questions. Humans approve supplier arrangements, exceptions, closure, risk acceptance, and external statements. Review routinely and conduct an annual independent readiness challenge.
