# IDEA confidentiality — engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not legal advice or a privacy certification.

## Source and applicability

Use IDEA Part B confidentiality provisions and applicable FERPA, state and local requirements. Do not apply this Part B workflow automatically to Part C early-intervention records. Identify program, participating agency, record type and rights holder before action.

Source check 2026-09-04: read Education Department sections [300.613 access](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/regs/b/f/300.613), [300.622 consent](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/regs/b/f/300.622) and [300.624 destruction](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/regs/b/f/300.624), whose displayed modification dates are in 2017. Full current confidentiality provisions, amendments, FERPA interactions, state-law rights transfers and local overlays remain to be verified.

Section 300.613 requires access without unnecessary delay, before specified IEP/hearing/resolution events, and within 45 days of the request. The maximum is not a default waiting period. Section 300.622 distinguishes authorized disclosures from specific consent-required routes, including certain transition-service and private-school inter-LEA disclosures. Section 300.624 addresses notice when information is no longer needed for educational services and destruction on parental request, with a limited permanent-record allowance. Have counsel resolve the precise conditions and competing retention duties before execution.

## Engagement focus

Make every records action traceable from request or lifecycle trigger through authority, exact record scope, approval, execution and verified outcome. Protect student information across schools, service providers, transfers, exports and retained copies.

## Roles

Special-education and records owners establish the program and record population. Privacy/legal owners decide authority, consent, exceptions, rights transfer and retention conflicts. IT and vendor owners execute authorized controls and retain evidence. An independent reviewer challenges coverage and disclosure boundaries. AI may organize permitted metadata and draft questions; it cannot decide consent, release records, change educational records, communicate rights outcomes or make legal conclusions.

## Before starting

Record agency, program, school year, systems, record categories, owners, vendors, locations and evidence period. Establish parent/representative/eligible-student authority procedures and an escalation owner. Record relevant meeting and hearing dates at intake. Keep student identifiers and confidential records outside this public repository; use synthetic fixtures for exercises in an approved workspace.

## Ordered workflow

1. **Establish coverage.** Reconcile all scoped records, systems, paper files, exports, providers and receiving agencies. Identify who maintains each record and who may authorize access. Resolve Part B versus other program treatment before applying requirements.
2. **Check safeguards and accountability.** Record access roles, approvals, training, logging and responsible officials against current requirements. Compare actual access with approved roles; retain discrepancies. Vendor contracts alone do not prove access restriction or offboarding.
3. **Receive and route rights requests.** Preserve original request date and requested records. Record any impending IEP meeting, hearing or resolution session. Apply the verified authority procedure without creating unnecessary delay. Escalate timing conflicts immediately; internal reassignment does not restart the clock.
4. **Prepare access or correction handling.** Locate all relevant records and document unavailable sources. Protect information concerning other children. Route explanations, copies, representative access and amendment/hearing requests through the applicable verified procedure. Do not treat access completion as proof that a disputed record has been corrected.
5. **Decide disclosures separately.** Record requester, recipient, purpose, exact data, proposed transfer and governing authority. Have the authorized owner verify consent or the particular permitted route, including special transition-service and private-school conditions. Prepare only the approved record boundary and required disclosure record; obtain release approval before sending.
6. **Execute and verify lifecycle actions.** Confirm access delivery or transfer through an authorized receipt. For destruction, document notice, request, approved scope and any justified retained records before execution. Trace all affected copies and vendor actions; an instruction to delete is not deletion evidence. Preserve failed or unavailable verification.
7. **Resolve gaps and hand off.** Assign findings, retest corrected controls and document unresolved copies, requests or disclosures. Escalate suspected unauthorized access through the incident process. Legal owners determine reporting and family communications from current duties; this checklist does not decide them.

## Evidence and test plan

Maintain full scoped population accounting, including failed requests and unavailable records. Preserve all evidence provided for assessment without silent trimming or sampling. State actual test coverage separately; a complete inventory does not prove every access path works.

### 1. Student-record inventory and accountable education boundary

- **Request and owner:** Request an approved record-system inventory, student/parent or guardian rights-contact process, data-flow and role map, authorized-school/contractor boundary inputs, and named records, special-education, privacy, security, and legal owners. Define the program, school year, and system population under review; exclude student identifiers from this public guide.
- **Validate and limit:** Reconcile every scoped record category and trace it from collection/use purpose to system owner, access role, retention cue, and approved boundary decision. Inspect metadata or sanitized samples in the authorized environment. This can support a documented records-lifecycle boundary; it cannot establish legal coverage, validate each record’s accuracy, or decide an individual’s rights outcome.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may compare approved inventories and ownership metadata for gaps in a segregated approved environment. Human education and privacy owners decide record classification, access, and local applicability. Trigger on a new system, program, vendor, school-year rollover, or material data-flow change.

### 2. Access, request, and disclosure-decision lineage

- **Request and owner:** Request access-role approvals, parent/eligible-party request intake and response references, disclosure request/authorization records, recipient/contractor accountability records, access logs, and exception/escalation decisions from records, school administration, privacy, legal, and vendor-management owners. Use an approved evidence period and protected records environment.
- **Validate and limit:** Inventory all scoped access and disclosure events and trace each applicable event from request/intake through authority verification, reviewer decision, released-record boundary, audit log/reference, and response/closure record. This can support process traceability; it cannot determine consent validity, legal exception applicability, actual recipient use, or compliance.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may route redacted work items, detect missing approvals, and summarize approved metadata; it cannot verify identity, approve a disclosure, alter records, or respond to a family. Humans decide disclosure and rights outcomes. Trigger on a rights request, unusual access, proposed disclosure, contractor change, or scheduled coverage review.

### 3. Retention, vendor, incident, and corrective-action record

- **Request and owner:** Request retention/disposition schedule references, deletion/destruction approvals, education-vendor data terms and access/offboarding evidence, incident intake/escalation records, corrective-action plans, and governance-review minutes from records, IT, security, procurement, and legal owners.
- **Validate and limit:** Reconcile all scoped dispositions, vendor departures and incidents and trace each applicable record to applicable record boundary, authorization/decision record, execution evidence, follow-up action, and review. This can support accountable lifecycle handling; it cannot prove complete deletion, vendor compliance, incident absence, or notification duty.
- **AI and trigger:** AI may flag stale vendor attestations, missed review dates, or incomplete evidence links. Authorized humans decide retention, destruction, supplier action, incident communications, and remediation closure. Trigger on vendor onboarding/offboarding, retention cycle, incident, audit issue, or annual governance review.


## Failure branches and decisions

- Rights holder or authority unclear: escalate to the designated owner, preserve time-sensitive dates and continue authorized retrieval without releasing records prematurely.
- A request precedes an IEP meeting: review the meeting-linked timing requirement immediately; do not automatically schedule access on day 45.
- Consent form exists but recipient or purpose differs: obtain an authorized decision; do not extrapolate consent.
- Vendor says deletion completed but verification is unavailable: distinguish its statement from independently supported execution and keep the gap open.
- Incident or disclosure error: preserve evidence, restrict further exposure within authority and route legal communications for approval.

Use [agent runbook](../agent-runbook.md) assertions: supported, not_supported, inconclusive, not_applicable and not_tested. Keep authorization, execution and recipient outcome as separate assertions.

## Cadence and renewal

Review at approved risk-based intervals and when programs, rights holders, vendors, systems or rules change. School-year rollover does not itself authorize deletion or new disclosure. Record source-based request deadlines and approved retention decisions rather than inventing a universal annual certification cycle.

## Completion and handoff

Deliver the reconciled record population, authority and decision references, request chronology, disclosure evidence, disposition results, findings and next actions. Every unresolved item needs an owner and due date. Publication requires independent source, skeptical and rights review; no legal accuracy or operational effectiveness is established by structural checks alone.

Fictional author desk case: a parent requests records before an IEP meeting next week, but the system schedules access for day 45. Flag the timing conflict for immediate owner action. A maximum-day setting does not support the meeting-linked requirement. No actual student request or disclosure occurred.
