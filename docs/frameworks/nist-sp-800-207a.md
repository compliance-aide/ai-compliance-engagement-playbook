# NIST SP 800-207A cloud-native zero-trust-access engagement guide

> Original operational guidance, not NIST zero-trust architecture content, an access-control policy, a production architecture, or a compliance claim. Confirm current material through [NIST SP 800-207A](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/207/a/final) and the organization’s approved security, architecture, engineering, privacy, legal, and operational decisions.

## Engagement focus

Operate a governed engagement for application-level zero-trust access control across in-scope cloud-native, hybrid, or multi-cloud environments. Connect service, identity, resource, and location scope to approved architecture and policy decisions, identity and enforcement dependencies, network and application interfaces, deployment and configuration evidence, access, monitoring, tests, supplier dependencies, changes, incidents, and retirement. An architecture diagram, policy extract, configuration record, test result, or audit log is evidence to assess; none alone proves access is appropriately authorized, identity assertions are reliable, a deployment is safe, or compliance.

## Roles and annual rhythm

Assign accountable executive, security, architecture, platform and application engineering, identity and access-management operations, network operations, application and data owners, supplier-management, privacy and legal where relevant, change-management, incident-response, and records-management roles. Operators maintain scoped service, identity, resource, and environment inventories, approved architecture and authorization decisions, enforcement and interface traceability, deployment and configuration baselines, access and audit evidence, monitoring and test records, supplier materials, exceptions, incidents, and retirement evidence. Reconcile services, identities, resources, enforcement points, owners, environments, and suppliers quarterly; review authorization failures, policy or configuration changes, exceptions, incidents, and unresolved risks at least quarterly; and complete an annual management review after material architecture, identity, provider, threat, or publication changes. Before annual renewal, an independent reviewer samples scope-to-policy-to-enforcement-to-review traceability; auditors test supplied evidence without granting access, changing authorization policy, deploying workloads, approving exceptions, accepting risk, or attesting for management.

AI may organize supplied inventory, architecture, policy, deployment, monitoring, and review evidence, identify missing owners or stale decisions, and draft workpapers for human review. AI cannot define or change authorization policy, grant or revoke access, deploy workloads, approve an exception, accept risk, make a compliance conclusion, attest for management, or replace independent review.
